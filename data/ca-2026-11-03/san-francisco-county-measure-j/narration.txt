Proposition J asks San Francisco voters whether to fully tax commercial, industrial and large residential properties that lenders take through foreclosure.
Today, a lender that takes over a property through foreclosure pays transfer tax only on the price above the debt still owed.
From March 2027, Proposition J would tax those properties on their full market value.
Single-family homes and buildings with fewer than five units would keep the tax break.
The Controller's Statement estimates between $100 million and $150 million a year over the first five years, between 1 and 2 percent of the roughly $7 billion General Fund.
The statement says the yearly amount would be unpredictable, likely highest at first and then falling.
Opponents say Proposition J makes lending here riskier and costlier than anywhere else in California, raising interest rates.
Supporters say a tax break made for struggling homeowners now goes to corporate lenders foreclosing on office towers.
If you vote yes, you want to apply a transfer tax to these properties when lenders take them through foreclosure.
If you vote no, you do not want to make these changes.
