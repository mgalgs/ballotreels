Measure GG asks the voters of this city whether to make five changes to the rent control rules in the city charter.
Today, the charter limits elected rent board members to two terms, without saying whether appointed terms count.
In 2014, voters capped the yearly fee for registering each rent-controlled unit at $288.
Board members could serve three terms, with appointed terms counted.
From 2028, that cap would rise with inflation, by at most 5 percent a year.
Board rules could allow good-cause extensions of the existing 120-day deadline for rent adjustment requests.
Landlords could evict affordable housing tenants who do not qualify, or who fail to show proof of eligibility after a written request.
And the charter would say its eviction protections go further than state law, so they apply in place of it.
The impartial analysis does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say the measure affirms eviction protections as state law requires, and reserves affordable units for qualifying tenants.
Opponents say the measure makes evicting affordable housing tenants easier for developers, and raises rents through higher fees.
A yes vote makes these five changes to the city charter.
A no vote leaves the city charter as it is.
