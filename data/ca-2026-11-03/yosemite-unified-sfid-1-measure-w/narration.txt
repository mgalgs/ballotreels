Measure W asks voters in one area of this school district whether to approve $18 million in bonds for elementary schools.
Only voters in that area vote, and the district would repay the bonds from a tax on property there.
Measure W needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would repair roofs, plumbing, heating and cooling at two elementary schools, renovate classrooms, and update fire alarms.
The impartial analysis says the bonds would also fund a future elementary school in the area.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would check the spending.
The district's tax statement estimates a tax of $59.81 per $100,000 of assessed value, from 2027-2028 through 2047-2048.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $24.9 million.
Supporters say many of the schools' facilities have not been updated in decades, and urgently need renovations and repairs.
No argument against Measure W appears among the filings that the county elections office has posted for this election.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue $18 million in bonds, repaid by a yearly property tax.
A no vote does not authorize the district to issue the bonds.
