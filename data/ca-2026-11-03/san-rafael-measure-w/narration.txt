Measure W asks the voters of this city whether to raise the tax paid when property is sold from 0.2 percent to 1 percent of the price.
The City Council placed Measure W on the ballot.
The transfer tax is paid once, when property changes hands, by the seller, the buyer or both, as they agree.
Measure W would raise the rate to 1 percent, so the tax on a $1 million sale would go from $2,000 to $10,000.
The money would go into the general fund of the city and could pay for any city purpose, such as fire protection, paramedics and street repair.
The higher rate would last until voters change it, and a city tax oversight committee would review how the money is spent.
As a general tax, Measure W needs a majority of the votes to pass.
The city estimates the higher rate would raise about $6 million more a year, for a total of $7.5 million.
Supporters say Measure W maintains rapid 9-1-1 response as emergency calls rise, and repairs aging infrastructure before it costs more to fix.
Opponents say Measure W is a 400 percent tax increase on people buying or selling homes, burdening first-time buyers and seniors who downsize.
A yes vote approves the measure, raising the rate to 1 percent.
A no vote rejects the measure, and the current rate would remain in effect.
