Measure M asks this school district's voters whether to approve $359 million in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
Measure M needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would fix roofs, plumbing, heating and air conditioning, and electrical and safety systems, and upgrade aging classrooms and science labs.
The district's tax statement estimates an average tax of $49.64 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $60, through 2058-2059.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $714 million.
Supporters say Measure M repairs schools nearly 40 years old and older portables, without increasing tax rates.
Opponents say the ballot hides the true cost, and the district holds $130 million usable for repairs.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue and sell the bonds.
A no vote rejects the district issuing and selling the bonds.
