## Facts and the passages that establish them

### Ballot question

S1 PDF p. 83:

> MEASURE AA
> La Mesa Spring Valley School District Classroom Repair & Upgrade Extension Measure. To repair/upgrade deteriorating roofs, plumbing, electrical systems; install air conditioning/shade structures to protect students from extreme heat; remove asbestos/lead pipes; and upgrade schools to current health/building safety/disability access standards, shall La Mesa Spring Valley School District's measure be adopted, authorizing $131,000,000 in bonds at legal rates (without increasing current tax rates), levying $22 per $100,000 assessed value ($9,000,000 annually) while bonds are outstanding, with independent oversight and audits?
> Bonds – Yes / Bonds – No

### Vote required

S1 PDF p. 83: "This measure requires 55% of those voting on the measure."

### Who votes on it

S3, Resolution 25-26-31, Section 1 (PDF p. 5):

> hereby requests the San Diego County Registrar of Voters to conduct an election under the provisions of Proposition 39 and the Act and submit to the electors of the District the question of whether bonds of the District in the aggregate principal amount of $131,000,000 (the "Bonds") shall be issued and sold

Section 3: "the purpose of the election shall be for the voters in the District to vote on a proposition". S1 PDF p. 83 (Tax Rate Statement): "for the purpose of submitting to the electors of the District the question of incurring bonded indebtedness of the District in the principal amount of $131,000,000."

In short: the electors (voters) of the La Mesa-Spring Valley School District.

### Who placed it on the ballot

S1 PDF p. 83: "The Board of Education for the La Mesa-Spring Valley School District ("District") placed this measure on the ballot."

S3 PDF p. 7: "ADOPTED, SIGNED AND APPROVED this 16th day of June, 2026." The resolution (Section 12) takes effect on adoption "by a two-thirds vote of the members of this Board"; the copy in S3 prints no vote tally.

### Impartial analysis

S1 PDF p. 83, "COUNTY COUNSEL IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS". Key statements:

> If approved by 55% of the votes cast, this measure will authorize the District to issue and sell general obligation bonds in an amount not to exceed $131,000,000.

> The District estimates the average tax rate would be $21.70 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation, and the highest tax rate would be approximately $21.70 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation. The tax is anticipated to be collected through fiscal year 2056-2057.

> The estimated total debt service, including principal and interest, required to be repaid if the bonds are issued and sold is approximately $257,998,000.

Note: the ballot question says "$22 per $100,000"; the analysis and tax rate statement say $21.70. Both as printed.

### Tax rate statement

S1 PDF p. 83, "TAX RATE STATEMENT / LA MESA-SPRING VALLEY SCHOOL DISTRICT GENERAL OBLIGATION BONDS / MEASURE AA", "Dated: June 23, 2026", "/s/ David Feliciano, Superintendent":

> 1. The best estimate of the average annual tax rate ... is $21.70 per $100,000 of assessed valuation of all property to be taxed. It is estimated that the final fiscal year in which the tax is anticipated to be collected is fiscal year 2056-57.
> 2. The best estimate of the highest tax rate ... is $21.70 per $100,000 of assessed valuation of all property to be taxed for fiscal years 2028-29 through and including 2056-57.
> 3. The best estimate of the total debt service ... is approximately $257,998,000.

### Full text

S1 PDF pp. 84-86, "FULL TEXT OF MEASURE AA". It opens: "The following is the full proposition presented to the voters by the La Mesa-Spring Valley School District (the "District")." Findings include:

> The District provides quality education for more than 10,000 local students in preschool through eighth grade

> With an old bond measure set to expire, the District has an opportunity to issue new bonds by extending, but not increasing, the tax rate paid by local property owners.

> Voter approval of the bond extension measure will help local schools qualify for an estimated $61 million in state matching funds
