Proposition H asks San Francisco voters whether to fund Muni with a new yearly tax on parcels of real estate.
Muni, the city transit system, is paid for by fares and by local, state and federal money.
From July 2027, property owners would pay the tax for 15 years, rising with building size from $129 a year to a cap of $400,000.
Most homeowners 65 or older living in their home would pay less or nothing.
The Controller's Statement estimates the tax would raise about $177 million a year, about 3 percent of the roughly $7 billion General Fund.
Running the tax would cost between $4 million and $6 million a year, less than 1 percent of that fund.
Opponents say Proposition H requires no cost cuts or efficiency targets, and taxes square footage instead of property value.
Supporters say without Proposition H, Muni service could be cut 30 percent, and at least 20 bus routes eliminated.
If you vote yes, you want the city to fund Muni service through a new parcel tax.
If you vote no, you do not want to make these changes.
