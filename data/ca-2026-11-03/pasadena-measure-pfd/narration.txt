Measure PFD asks the voters of this city whether to levy a yearly parcel tax for fire, paramedic and wildfire services.
The City Council placed Measure PFD on the ballot.
The tax would be a fixed rate on each square foot of improved property.
Owners with very low household incomes would be exempt, as would seniors and disabled owners exempt from the utility users tax.
The money would go to a special fund, only for fire, paramedic and wildfire services, and fire stations and equipment.
Annual independent audits and public spending disclosure would be required.
The tax would expire after 14 years, and needs two-thirds of the votes to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis says the tax would raise about $22.1 million a year.
Opponents say only property owners would bear a tax for services everyone uses, and audits cannot guarantee repaired stations or faster response times.
Supporters say the city has fewer firefighters than 75 years ago while calls grew more than tenfold, and two stations fail earthquake standards.
A yes vote approves the special parcel tax for fire and emergency services, with a 14-year sunset.
A no vote rejects the tax, leaving the city to fund fire and emergency services with available resources only.
