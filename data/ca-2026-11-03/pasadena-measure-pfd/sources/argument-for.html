ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE PFD

The Eaton Fire last year nearly took our city. 130 Pasadena homes were destroyed. Wind-driven embers traveled miles and jumped house to house in seconds. Had the winds shifted a few degrees, Pasadena's losses would have been catastrophic.

Pasadena has fewer firefighters today than it did 75 years ago in the 1950s. Since then, our population has grown 30 percent, our wildfire risk has multiplied with our changing climate, and 911 calls have climbed from 1,800 a year to more than 20,000.

The result: Twenty percent of the time, a second 911 emergency comes in within five minutes of the first. Without Measure PFD, Pasadena may be forced to close two fire stations, and may not have enough firefighters and paramedics on duty to reach both emergencies at once.

Several of our stations are over 60 years old and two fail current earthquake safety standards. Two are at risk of being condemned and shut down by inspectors. Measure PFD makes those seismic repairs, keeps neighborhood stations open, puts more firefighters and paramedics in them, and upgrades the engines, trucks and lifesaving equipment today's fires demand.

Every dollar is protected under Measure PFD. The measure requires independent annual audits, citizens' oversight, and public spending disclosure. By law, politicians cannot spend this money on anything besides local fire protection and emergency medical response, and cannot be taken by the county, state, or federal government. And low-income seniors are exempt.

We know what is coming. Please don't wait for the next fire.
