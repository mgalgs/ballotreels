Measure L asks the voters of this city whether to add a 1 percent sales tax for city services.
The City Council placed Measure L on the ballot in June.
The sales tax rate in the city would rise from 7.875 percent to 8.875 percent.
The money would go into the general fund of the city, for any lawful purpose.
A seven-member citizens' oversight committee would review the spending each year.
The tax would last until ended by voters, and needs a simple majority to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis estimates that Measure L would raise about $9 million a year.
The Stanislaus County voter guide says that no argument against Measure L was filed by anyone in this election.
Supporters say Measure L keeps the city safe by retaining its experienced firefighters, and helps improve the drinking water infrastructure.
A yes vote on Measure L authorizes the 1 percent sales tax.
A no vote on Measure L does not authorize the 1 percent sales tax.
