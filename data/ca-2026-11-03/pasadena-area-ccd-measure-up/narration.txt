Measure UP asks the voters of this college district whether to approve $485 million in college bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
The district's governing board placed Measure UP on the ballot, and it needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money could build and equip career training classrooms, and fix deteriorating roofs, plumbing and electrical systems.
It could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would be required.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average and a highest tax of $9 per $100,000 of assessed value, through 2065-2066.
The statement puts the total to repay, with interest, at approximately $1,013,011,577, and says its figures are estimates only.
The ballot question says the bonds would provide $27 million a year while they are outstanding.
Supporters say Measure UP would repair aging classrooms and expand career training without increasing tax rates, continuing the current rate.
The official county list of measures, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure UP was submitted.
A yes vote approves the $485 million in college bonds.
A no vote rejects the $485 million in college bonds.
