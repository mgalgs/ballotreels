San Francisco Proposition F asks voters whether to give the mayor more authority over city departments, their heads, and commissions.
Today the mayor supervises nearly 50 departments, but cannot move duties between most of them.
The mayor usually picks department heads from a commission's candidates, and the commission, not the mayor, can usually fire them.
Proposition F would let the mayor move duties between most departments, or combine them, unless the Board of Supervisors objects.
The mayor could hire deputy mayors, hire some and fire most department heads, and officials could remove commissioners they appointed without cause.
The City Controller says Proposition F would have a minimal impact on the cost of government, at a level that cannot be determined.
Opponents say Proposition F removes essential checks and balances, and would concentrate too much power in the hands of one person.
Supporters say Proposition F tackles corruption, letting the mayor hire and remove most department heads so officials answer to the public.
A yes vote means you want to expand the authority of the mayor.
A no vote means you do not want these changes.
