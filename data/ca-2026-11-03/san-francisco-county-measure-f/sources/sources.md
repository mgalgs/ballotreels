## Ballot question (p. 102)

> Shall the City amend the Charter to expand the Mayor's authority to transfer responsibilities between departments, hire deputy mayors, and hire some and fire most department heads; and to allow City officials to remove members of boards and commissions without cause?

## Digest by the Ballot Simplification Council (pp. 102–103)

**The Way It Is Now** (p. 102):
> The Mayor is the chief executive officer of the City. The Mayor directly supervises nearly 50 City departments, which are established by Charter or ordinance. Most City departments are established by Charter.
> • Transfer of Functions: The Mayor can transfer responsibilities between departments established by ordinance, as long as the Board of Supervisors (Board) does not object. The Mayor cannot transfer responsibilities for departments established by the Charter.
> • Deputy Mayors: The Mayor cannot hire deputy mayors to supervise most departments established by the Charter.
> • Department Heads: In most cases, the Mayor must select a department head from candidates recommended by the board or commission that oversees that department. Usually, the board or commission can fire the department head, but the Mayor cannot.
> • Commissioners: In addition to the Mayor, the Board and other officials appoint members to City boards and commissions. Appointed members can usually only be suspended or removed if the member engaged in official misconduct or committed certain felonies.

**The Proposal** (p. 102):
> Proposition F would amend the Charter to allow the Mayor to do the following:
> • Transfer of Functions: Transfer responsibilities between most departments or combine departments under a single department head, regardless of whether the Department was established by ordinance or Charter and as long as the Board does not object. The Mayor cannot eliminate any responsibilities established by Charter. ...
> • Deputy Mayors: Hire deputy mayors to supervise most departments established by the Charter.
> • Department Heads: Hire some and fire most department heads without board or commission involvement. The Mayor cannot hire or fire certain department heads that are exempt (see List of Exemptions in Words You Need To Know).
> • Commissioners: In addition to the Mayor, the Board and other officials who appoint board and commission members could remove them at any time without cause. This does not apply to the Board of Appeals, Civil Service Commission, or Ethics Commission. The Mayor cannot eliminate any board or commission established by the Charter.

The departments whose responsibilities the Mayor cannot transfer:
> • City Administrator, Controller, Board of Appeals, Port, Airport, Asian Art Museum, Fine Arts Museums, San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency, Public Utilities Commission, Civil Service Commission, Department of Human Resources, Retirement Board, Health Service System, Department of Elections, Ethics Commission, or departments headed by other elected officials.

**A "YES" Vote Means** (p. 103):
> If you vote "yes," you want to expand the Mayor's authority to transfer responsibilities, hire deputy mayors, hire some and fire most department heads, and allow City officials to remove members of boards or commissions without cause.

**A "NO" Vote Means** (p. 103):
> If you vote "no," you do not want to make these changes.

## Controller's Statement on "F" (p. 103)

> City Controller Greg Wagner has issued the following statement on the fiscal impact of Proposition F:
> Should the proposed Charter amendment be approved by the voters, in my opinion, it would have a minimal impact on the cost of government, although at a level that cannot be determined at this time.

Dollar figures:
> The proposed Charter amendment authorizes the Mayor to utilize deputy mayors. The average annual cost for a deputy mayor would be approximately $250,000.

> For example, in Fiscal Year 2022-23 when the Department of Sanitation and Streets merged into the Department of Public Works, the Controller's Office estimated cost savings of $3.5 million in the first year and $2.5 million in the second year of the merger.

## How "F" Got on the Ballot (p. 103)

> On July 23, 2026, the Department of Elections certified that the initiative petition calling for Proposition F to be placed on the ballot had a sufficient number of valid signatures to qualify the measure for the ballot.
> 53,076 signatures were required to place an initiative Charter Amendment on the ballot. This number is equal to 10% of the registered voters at the time a "Notice of Intent to Circulate Petition" was published. A random check of the signatures submitted by the proponents of the initiative petition prior to the July 6, 2026, submission deadline showed that the total number of valid signatures was greater than the number required.

## Arguments (pp. 104–105)

Every argument page carries: "Arguments are the opinions of the authors and have not been checked for accuracy by any official agency." and "Arguments are printed as submitted. Spelling and grammatical errors have not been corrected."

**Proponent's Argument** (p. 104). Signers: Mayor Daniel Lurie; Board of Supervisors President Rafael Mandelman; Speaker Emerita Nancy Pelosi; Nancy Tung, San Francisco Democratic Party; Former Deputy City Attorney Jim Lazarus; SFSU Associate Professor of Political Science Jason McDaniel; Former City Controller Ben Rosenfield; Former Fire Chief Joanne Hayes-White; Firefighters Local 798; SF Building Trades; Dan Calamuci, Carpenters Local 22*; Anthony Travis Jr., Laborers Local 261*; Charles Lavery, Operating Engineers Local 3*; Teamsters Local 665; Council of District Merchant Associations; Chinatown History and Culture Association (*"For identification purposes only; author is signing as an individual and not on behalf of an organization.").
> CLEAR LINES OF ACCOUNTABILITY. Allowing the mayor to hire and remove most of their department heads will ensure city officials are accountable to the public.

> The Board of Supervisors will still have oversight over city departments and a majority of the Board can reject any changes the mayor makes.

**Rebuttal to Proponent's Argument** (p. 104). Signers: Democratic Socialists of America - San Francisco; Jackie Fielder, San Francisco Supervisor*; Dean Preston, Former San Francisco Supervisor*; Theresa Imperial, Former San Francisco Planning Commissioner*; Sophia Andary, Commissioner, San Francisco Commission on the Status of Women*.
> It concentrates power in the executive, stripping away checks and balances.

**Opponent's Argument** (p. 105). Signer: Small Business Forward (confirmed by `pdftotext -layout`: the name ends the left column, under the Opponent's Argument).
> Prop F removes essential checks & balances.

> It allows the Mayor to remove Commissioners for any reason not just cause.

**Rebuttal to Opponent's Argument** (p. 105). Signers: Mayor Daniel Lurie; Board of Supervisors President Rafael Mandelman; Former Controller Ben Rosenfield.
> CHECKS AND BALANCES STAY INTACT. Watchdog agencies like ethics and police oversight remain fully independent.
