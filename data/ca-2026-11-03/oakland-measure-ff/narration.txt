City of Oakland Measure FF asks voters in the city whether to apply its real property transfer tax to most transfers made through foreclosure.
Oakland taxes certain transfers of real property, but transfers made through foreclosure are generally exempt.
The City Council placed Measure FF on the ballot, and it needs a simple majority to pass.
From January 2027, foreclosure transfers would be taxed at the rates already in city law.
Exceptions would remain for buildings turned into certain housing or health care, for community banks, and for some homes of four units or less.
The City Council could change those exceptions, and the money could be used for any city purpose.
The City Auditor estimates first-year revenue of between $10.4 million and $13.8 million, at a cost to the city of between about $593,000 and $651,000.
The ballot question itself estimates between $4 million and $13 million a year.
Supporters say investors who take property through foreclosure avoid a tax families pay, and Measure FF would close that loophole.
No argument against Measure FF was submitted, and none appears in the Alameda County Composite Voter Information Guide for this election.
A yes vote means most transfers made through foreclosure in Oakland would be taxed.
A no vote means the foreclosure exception stays as it is.
