## Facts with quotes

- **Ballot question (Resolution 2026-32 §1):** "Shall Sections 310 and 405 of the Eureka City Charter be amended to authorize the Eureka City Council to establish compensation for the Mayor and Councilmembers by ordinance, including an initial salary of $1,250 per month for the Mayor and $1,000 per month for Councilmembers, with future annual adjustments subject to review by the Finance Advisory Committee?"
- **Vote needed:** Resolution 2026-32 §3: "the vote requirement for the measure to pass is a majority (50%+1) of the votes cast."
- **Adopted:** "PASSED, APPROVED AND ADOPTED by the City Council of the City of Eureka ... on the 16th day of June 2026" (Resolution 2026-32 p. 2).
- **Current pay (guide p. 21):** "Currently, the Charter of the City of Eureka fixes the salary of the Mayor at $625 per month and that of each of the five Councilmembers at $500 per month. These levels were enacted by Charter amendment on June 7, 1988 and have not been changed since then."
- **Effect:** "If adopted by the voters, Measure H would allow the City Council to adopt an ordinance that may initially increase the salary levels to $1250 per month for the Mayor and $1000 per month for Councilmembers. Because of the timelines for the effective date of any new ordinance, these increases could take effect in January, 2027." / "Measure H would also permit future annual adjustments to the salaries by adoption of new ordinances, subject to prior review by the City's Finance Advisory Committee."
- **Yes/No:** "A "yes" vote on Measure H is a vote to enact a Charter amendment that would allow the Mayor's and Councilmembers' salaries to increase by ordinance new limits and thereafter to be adjusted annually following input from the Finance Advisory Committee. A "no" vote is a vote against the enactment of the Charter amendment and to retain the current salary limits."
- **Author:** "The following impartial analysis of the measure was prepared by the Eureka City Attorney" (guide p. 21); Resolution 2026-32 approved as to form by Robert N. Black, City Attorney.
