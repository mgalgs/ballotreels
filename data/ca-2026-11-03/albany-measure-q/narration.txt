City of Albany Measure Q asks Albany voters whether to update the parcel tax that pays for fire protection and emergency medical services.
Albany voters approved the tax in 2022; it is charged by the square foot of land, and very low-income residents are exempt or get a rebate.
Measure Q would cut the tax in half for low-income homeowners, and give low-income renters a rebate of half the tax on their unit.
Yearly increases for inflation would be capped at 3 percent, down from 5.
If any parcel of 500,000 square feet or more became tax exempt, the tax could instead be charged by the square foot of building, or of undeveloped land.
Leaseholders on exempt land would pay the tax, and Measure Q needs two-thirds of Albany voters to pass.
The City Attorney's analysis estimates the tax would raise $2,500,000 a year, kept in a fund for paramedics, firefighting and ambulances.
Supporters say Measure Q protects fire funding if Golden Gate Fields becomes a park, and closes a tax loophole.
The Alameda County Composite Voter Information Guide says that no argument against Measure Q was submitted for this election.
A yes vote approves these changes to the fire and emergency medical parcel tax.
A no vote does not approve these changes to the fire and emergency medical parcel tax.
