Measure U

MEASURE U

CITY OF WALNUT CREEK

Shall the measure amending the City of Walnut Creek General Plan, the North Downtown Specific Plan, and the Municipal Code to redesignate a 2.4 acre site at the northeast corner of North California Boulevard and Ygnacio Valley Road from office uses to mixed use residential uses, and to create a new Mixed Use Residential Senior & Commercial zoning district to limit allowable uses on the site to multifamily senior housing (primarily 55 years of age or older) and commercial uses compatible with senior housing, be adopted?

CITY ATTORNEY’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF

MEASURE U

Measure U was placed on the ballot through a voter-sponsored petition signed by the requisite number of Walnut Creek voters. It proposes to amend the city’s General Plan, North Downtown Specific Plan (NDSP), and the zoning ordinance/zoning map to establish a new “Mixed Use – Residential Senior & Commercial” (MU-RSC) zoning district and to specifically rezone a 2.4-acre parcel at the northeast corner of North California Boulevard and Ygnacio Valley Road across from the Walnut Creek BART station to MU-RSC. The MU-RSC district would change the site’s current use from office to mixed-use residential, consisting primarily of senior housing serving primarily persons 55 years or older, residential care and congregate living facilities, supportive and accessory housing required by state law, and compatible commercial retail uses. Future projects in MU-RSC district may, but are not required to, develop any or all of these uses, with required senior housing.

The Measure does not propose a specific project in the MU-RSC, but sets a base density at 1 unit per 425 square feet of net lot area and increases the maximum floor area ratio for commercial uses to 0.6 from the current 0.3. The city’s inclusionary ordinance would require future senior residential projects to develop affordable units or pay an in-lieu fee. These inclusionary units can be used to qualify the project for additional density and incentives/concessions. Future senior housing will likely qualify for a density bonus pursuant to Government Code section 65915, which grants a default 20 percent bonus. If density-bonus eligible, the project can also request waivers of applicable city development and design standards, including reducing setbacks and exceeding Measure A height limit applicable to the 2.4-acre property. A future project may be subject to California Environmental Quality Act (“CEQA”) review or may qualify for a CEQA exemption.

Absent voter approval or city approval of the landowner’s application, the city cannot substantively amend the MU-RSC or reduce its development capacities as they apply to 2.4-acre site. While the MU-RSC only includes the 2.4-acre site currently, other Walnut Creek properties could be rezoned to this zoning designation to utilize its development allowances, if approved by the city council.

A “Yes” vote is a vote to approve Measure U and its proposed amendments. A “No” vote is a vote against Measure U and the proposed amendments. Measure U will be approved if it receives a simple majority of “Yes” votes. If approved, Measure U would take effect on the earliest date legally possible. If the city has already made the maximum four general plan amendments allowed in a calendar year under state law, Measure U’s General Plan amendments would take effect on January 1 of the next year and its other amendments will take effect subject to this effective date as applicable.

The above statement is an impartial analysis of Measure U. If you desire a copy of Measure U, please contact the city clerk at (925) 943-5818 or cityclerk@walnutcreekca.gov and a copy will be mailed at no cost to you.

ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF

MEASURE U

Walnut Creek is an amazing place to call home – and Measure U will help us keep it that way.

Seniors make up nearly half of Walnut Creek’s population, but with the lack of emphasis for new housing designated for seniors, many seniors and older adults are being pushed out further and further into the East Bay, away from their families, healthcare providers and communities, at an age where they need to find a place that supports their changing needs.

An independent review recently found that 44% of the households within 2 miles of North Downtown are occupied by seniors but only 5% of the total future homes in the area are specifically designated for seniors.

Passage of Measure U will be a crucial step forward by updating zoning in North Downtown Walnut Creek to permit over 200 units of high-quality senior housing and community-serving retail near BART – without raising taxes.

The project’s prime location next door to the Walnut Creek BART station offers easy access to regional destinations, local services, and the Downtown Trolley system. This promotes walkability, wellness, and independence, ensuring our seniors can stay connected without relying solely on a car.

Measure U facilitates a vibrant, mixed-use center which could include a multi-use market similar to the Rockridge Public Market/San Francsico Ferry Building or a full-service specialty market.

This initiative aligns with Walnut Creek’s General Plan, including the City’s recently adopted Housing Element and North Downtown Specific Plan. It advances Walnut Creek’s goals for sustainable growth, reduced traffic, and enhanced quality of life, and to begin to balance the supply of housing in Walnut Creek designated for seniors.

Measure U is a responsible, forward-thinking investment in Walnut Creek’s future. It provides the housing our seniors need while creating a thriving mixed-use area for everyone – without raising taxes.

Please join us, Yes on Measure U – https://www.wcseniorhousing.org/

Marlene R. Farrell, Friends of Walnut Creek Senior Housing

Stephen L. Kostka, Advocate for Senior Housing

Hailey Murphy, Walnut Creek Resident
