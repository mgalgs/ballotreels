## Quoted facts

- Ballot question: "With funds that cannot be taken by the State and spent elsewhere, shall Mammoth Unified School District's replacement parcel tax measure to attract and retain experienced teachers, preserve smaller class sizes, and maintain and enhance high quality programs including technology, art, music, math, science, and athletics at Mammoth Elementary School, Mammoth Middle School, Mammoth High School, and Sierra High School be adopted at $99 per parcel raising $1.1 million annually for six years with independent oversight, audits, senior exemptions, and public disclosure of all spending?"
- What it replaces: analysis, "Measure P would authorize the Mammoth Unified School District (“the District”) to levy a replacement education parcel tax, succeeding the District’s existing Measure G parcel tax, which expires on June 30, 2027."
- Origin: "This measure was placed on the ballot by the Board of Education of the Mammoth Unified School District".
- Rate and term: "If approved, Measure P would authorize a tax of $99 per parcel, per year, for six years, beginning with fiscal year 2027-28 and ending with fiscal year 2032-33. The District estimates the tax would generate approximately $1.1 million annually."
- Vote needed: "Passage of Measure P requires approval by at least two-thirds (66.67%) of the voters voting on the measure."
- If it fails: "If Measure P fails, the existing Measure G tax will continue in effect until it expires on June 30, 2027."
- Earmark: "The measure specifically requires that at least 10% of annual proceeds be used for maintaining extracurricular and athletic programs."
- Exemptions: "(1) an exemption for any parcel owned and occupied as a primary residence by a person aged 65 or older, with property owners who already hold a senior exemption under Measure G automatically exempted from Measure P without reapplying; and (2) an exemption for any property that is otherwise exempt from ad valorem property taxes".
- Argument in favor signers: "Chelsea Nash, Alonso Escobar, Marcy Castro, Marty Thompson, Sarah Keith" (each "MUSD Board Member").
