Measure P asks the voters of this school district whether to approve a parcel tax of $99 a year for six years.
Today, the district has a parcel tax that voters approved in 2020, and that tax expires on June 30, 2027.
Measure P needs two-thirds of the vote to pass.
Measure P would replace the 2020 tax with a tax of $99 per parcel each year, from 2027-2028 through 2032-2033.
The measure lists what the money may pay for, including keeping qualified teachers, small early-grade classes, and music, arts and athletics.
Owners aged 65 or older who live on their parcel could be exempt, and so could property already exempt from property taxes.
If Measure P fails, the 2020 tax would still end on June 30, 2027.
The impartial analysis says the district estimates the tax would bring in about $1.1 million a year.
Supporters say Measure P helps keep dedicated, qualified teachers and protects programs students rely on, and every dollar stays local.
No argument against Measure P was filed, so the county voter guide prints only the argument in favor of the measure.
A yes vote is a vote to authorize the tax.
A no vote is a vote not to authorize the tax.
