## Facts and quotes

**Ballot question** (Notice of Measures, 8/5/2026, https://content.solanocounty.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/Notice_of_Measures.pdf): "Shall the citizens of Vacaville adopt a measure maintaining the City's financial stability and essential services--including 911 emergency response; fire and ambulance protection; crime prevention; parks and recreation; safe public spaces; supporting local businesses; repairing streets; and other general city services--by establishing an additional one-cent sales tax providing approximately twenty-eight million dollars annually, with local control, citizens oversight, annual audits, and public reporting, until ended by voters?"

**Impartial analysis** ("City Attorney's Impartial Analysis of Measure V", signed Andria Borba, City Attorney), key statements:
- "On June 23, 2026, the City Council of the City of Vacaville unanimously voted to place Measure V on the November 3, 2026 statewide general election ballot." (p1)
- "The City's General Fund has a projected deficit of approximately $12 million for the current fiscal year and has already reduced General Fund expenditures by approximately $9 million through cost-saving measures, as reflected in the City's adopted budget." (p1)
- "If approved by a majority of the voters voting on the measure, this measure would add Chapter 3.15 to the Vacaville Municipal Code, authorizing the City to impose a new transactions and use tax, commonly referred to as a "sales tax", of one percent (1%) on the sale and use of tangible personal property within the City." (p1)
- "The City's current combined sales tax rate is 8.125% ... If approved Measure V would increase the combined rate to 9.125%, increasing the City's share to 2.75%." (p1)
- "This tax would be separate from, and in addition to, the City's existing three-quarter percent (.75%) sales tax, "Measure M"; approval would not modify, reduce, or repeal that tax." (p1)
- "If approved, the one-cent sales tax would take effect April 1, 2027. The tax would not expire on a fixed date and would remain in effect until repealed by a future vote of the City's qualified voters." (p1)
- "This is a local general tax. ... The ordinance would establish a Citizens Oversight Committee to review and annually report to the Council on receipt and expenditure of the revenue and would require periodic independent audits." (p2)
- "This measure requires a simple majority of "yes" votes for the measure to pass and become effective." (p2)

**Who votes:** voters of the City of Vacaville (analysis: tax "within the City"; repeal by "the City's qualified voters").
