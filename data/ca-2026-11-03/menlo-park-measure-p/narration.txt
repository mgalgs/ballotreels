Menlo Park Measure P asks city voters whether to require a public vote before changes that reduce parking on downtown city lots.
Today, the City Council decides how its eight downtown parking plazas are used.
The housing plan of Menlo Park calls for at least 345 affordable homes on some of these plazas.
Measure P would require a public vote to sell or lease a plaza, or to build on one if that permanently reduces parking.
That includes the affordable homes, if they reduced parking.
Repairs, or work that keeps or adds parking, would not.
Measure P would reach back to May 15, 2025, and only voters could repeal it.
The published measure documents of San Mateo County do not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Opponents say Measure P blocks new garages with affordable homes above, and likely breaks state law, risking legal fees and delay.
Supporters say Measure P gives voters a say before plazas become high-rises, and the city could be on the hook for garages.
A yes vote means these changes to the plazas would need a public vote.
A no vote means the City Council keeps deciding how the plazas are used.
