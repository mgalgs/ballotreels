# Measure F — City of San Mateo — Charter amendment (district elections, vacancies, meetings, clean-up)

- **Jurisdiction:** City of San Mateo.
- **Kind:** City Charter amendment, placed by the City Council, Resolution No. 85 (2026).
- **Who votes:** City of San Mateo voters. Resolution Sec. 1, PDF p. 1: the Council "orders the measure proposing to amend the Charter to be submitted to the qualified voters of the City of San Mateo at the general municipal election consolidated with the statewide general election on November 3, 2026."
- **Threshold:** simple majority. Resolution Sec. 8, PDF pp. 2-3: "The measure proposes to amend the City Charter and shall be approved if a majority (50% +1) of those voting on the measure approve it." Continues: "If approved by the voters, the Charter amendment shall become effective upon acceptance and filing by the Secretary of State."

## Ballot question (verbatim)

Resolution Sec. 2 ("Ballot Question"), PDF p. 2 (scan):

> Shall the measure amending the City of San Mateo's Charter to reflect the City's current district-based election system, update the process for scheduling Council meetings and filling Council vacancies, update or remove obsolete provisions, and make other technical and conforming changes, be adopted?

## Full text

- **Resolution No. 85 (2026)**, City Council of the City of San Mateo. PDF p. 3: "RESOLUTION NO. 85 (2026) adopted by the City Council of the City of San Mateo, California, at a regular meeting held on July 20, 2026". AYES: "Council Members Loraine, Fernandez, Diaz Nash, and Newsom"; NOES: "None"; ABSENT: "Council Member Cwirko-Godycki". Attested by Martin McTaggart, City Clerk; signed Adam Loraine, Mayor. Filed with the Chief Elections Officer "JUL 27 2026" (stamp, p. 1).
- Recitals, PDF p. 1: "the Charter requires amending in order to reflect the City's transition to district elections in 2021 following a demand letter claiming a violation of the California Voting Rights Act"; and "additional amendments to the Charter are appropriate in order to clarify the process for scheduling Council meetings and filling Council vacancies, remove obsolete provisions, and make other technical and conforming amendments". Hearings: "a study session on March 16, 2026 and public hearings on April 6, 2026 and May 18, 2026".
- Sec. 5, PDF p. 2: the Council authorizes "Deputy Mayor Nicole Fernandez and Council Member Cwirko-Godycki, to file written argument and rebuttal in favor of the measure."
- Sec. 7, PDF p. 2: "the full text of the proposed amendments shall not appear in the voter pamphlet given its length and resulting expense, but the City Clerk shall post the proposed measure on the City's website, provide any person a copy of the measure upon request, and publish a synopsis of the measure."
- **Exhibit A**, PDF p. 4 on: e.g. Sec. 2.01(a) becomes "There shall be a city council of five members elected by the voters of the city ~~at large~~ by districts. Any recall election or special election or appointment to fill a vacancy shall also be by district." (deletions struck through, additions double-underlined in the original).

## Impartial analysis

`impartial-analysis.pdf`: "City Attorney's Impartial Analysis of Measure __", signed (signature redacted) "Prasanna W. Rasiah, City Attorney" (p. 2).

- "Changes to the Charter must be approved by a majority of City voters." (p. 1)
- "The Charter currently states that Councilmembers are elected by the voters at large. However, the Council adopted an ordinance in 2021 converting to district elections in order to comply with the California Voting Rights Act." (p. 1)
- "The proposal would also provide for district boundaries to be redrawn following the federal census that is produced every ten years, consistent with federal and state law." (p. 1)
- "The proposal would extend the period of time that the City has to fill a vacancy on the Council from 30 days to 60 days." (p. 1)
- "The proposal would remove language specifying the day and time for regular Council meetings and would allow the Council to do so instead by resolution." (p. 1)
- "A YES vote approves the measure. A NO vote rejects the measure." (p. 2)

Other listed changes (p. 1-2): removing outdated 2015/2017 term provisions; extending the absence-forfeiture period from 30 to 60 days; summary publication of emergency ordinances; removing the candidate statement requirement; removing sealed-bid newspaper selection; "clarifying the title and appointment process for the Finance Director, and adding gender-neutral wording."
