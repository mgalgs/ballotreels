CITY OF SAN MATEO
RESOLUTION NO. 85 (2026)

SUBMITTING TO THE VOTERS A MEASURE TO AMEND THE CITY CHARTER TO REFLECT THE CHANGE TO DISTRICT ELECTIONS AND UPDATE CERTAIN PROVISIONS TO BE VOTED ON AT THE GENERAL MUNICIPAL ELECTION HELD ON TUESDAY, NOVEMBER 3, 2026

WHEREAS, the Charter of the City of San Mateo is the governing document establishing the powers and providing guidance for the City's organization and rule; and

WHEREAS, following adoption of a Charter by local voters in 1922, a new Charter was adopted by the voters in 1970 and ratified by the Legislature of the State of California via Resolution Chapter 10 — Statutes of 1971; and

WHEREAS, the Charter was revised in its entirety on November 4, 2002 and chaptered in Charter Chapter 62 – Statutes of 2002 and was further amended by a vote of the people on November 8, 2016; and

WHEREAS, the Charter requires amending in order to reflect the City's transition to district elections in 2021 following a demand letter claiming a violation of the California Voting Rights Act; and

WHEREAS, additional amendments to the Charter are appropriate in order to clarify the process for scheduling Council meetings and filling Council vacancies, remove obsolete provisions, and make other technical and conforming amendments; and

WHEREAS, the City Council held a study session on March 16, 2026 and public hearings on April 6, 2026 and May 18, 2026 to discuss the proposed amendments, in accordance with state law; and

WHEREAS, on June 15, 2026, the San Mateo City Council adopted Resolution No. 60 (2026), calling for the holding of a General Municipal Election for certain Council seats and requesting the Board of Supervisors of the County of San Mateo to consolidate a general municipal election with the statewide general election on November 3, 2026; and

WHEREAS, the City seeks to add this measure on the ballot at the same election for which services have been called; and

WHEREAS, the City Council is authorized and directed by statute to submit the proposed charter amendment to the voters.

NOW, THEREFORE, THE COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF SAN MATEO, CALIFORNIA, RESOLVES AS FOLLOWS:

1. Submission of Measure to the Voters. Pursuant to Elections Code sections 9255, 10201, and 10403, the City Council hereby orders the measure proposing to amend the Charter to be submitted to the qualified voters of the City of San Mateo at the general municipal election consolidated with the statewide general election on November 3, 2026. The consolidated election shall be held and conducted in the manner prescribed by Elections Code section 10418. The full text of the Charter amendments that shall be voted on is attached hereto as Exhibit A.

[PDF page 2]

2. Ballot Question. The City Council approves the proposed charter amendments set forth in Exhibit A, attached hereto and incorporated in this Resolution by this reference, for submission to the voters of the City, and orders that the ballots for the election shall be in the form provided by law, and that the ballot question shall appear on the ballot in the following form and shall be submitted to the voters of the City:

Shall the measure amending the City of San Mateo's Charter to reflect the City's current district-based election system, update the process for scheduling Council meetings and filling Council vacancies, update or remove obsolete provisions, and make other technical and conforming changes, be adopted?
YES
NO

3. The City Clerk is directed to forward the proposed measure to the City Attorney for preparation of an impartial analysis in accordance with Section 9280 of the Elections Code.

4. Arguments for and against the proposition may be submitted to the qualified voters of the City in accordance with sections 9282 through 9287 of the California Elections Code. The deadline date for submitting ballot arguments for or against the proposition shall be set by the City Clerk. Proposed arguments shall not exceed 300 words and shall be submitted to the Office of the City Clerk. The deadline for submitting rebuttal arguments shall be set by the City Clerk. Proposed rebuttal arguments shall not exceed 250 words and shall be submitted to the office of the City Clerk. The provisions of Section 9285(a) of the California Elections Code shall apply to the submittal of rebuttal arguments.

5. The City Council authorizes two members of the City Council, Deputy Mayor Nicole Fernandez and Council Member Cwirko-Godycki, to file written argument and rebuttal in favor of the measure.

6. Any argument filed for or against said proposition not exceeding 300 words shall be filed with the City Clerk, signed, with the printed name(s) and signature(s) of the author(s) submitting it, or if filed on behalf of an organization, the name of the organization, and the printed name and signature of at least one of its principal officers who is the author of the argument.

7. The City Clerk is hereby directed to file a certified copy of this Resolution with the San Mateo Clerk of the Board of Supervisors and the County Registrar of Voters in sufficient time so that the measure may be included on the November 3, 2026, City municipal election and statewide general election ballot. The City Clerk is hereby directed to perform all other acts that are required for submitting the measure to be voted on at the November 3, 2026, general municipal election and statewide general election. Pursuant to Elections Code sections 9280 and 12111, the full text of the proposed amendments shall not appear in the voter pamphlet given its length and resulting expense, but the City Clerk shall post the proposed measure on the City's website, provide any person a copy of the measure upon request, and publish a synopsis of the measure.

8. Majority Vote. The measure proposes to amend the City Charter and shall be approved if a

[PDF page 3]

majority (50% +1) of those voting on the measure approve it. If approved by the voters, the Charter amendment shall become effective upon acceptance and filing by the Secretary of State. The City Clerk is hereby directed to transmit and file all documents required by law with the Secretary of State following certification of the election results.

9. The City Clerk shall certify to the passage and adoption of this resolution and enter it into the book of original resolutions.

10. The City Council authorizes the City Clerk to administer said election and all reasonable and actual election expenses shall be paid by the City upon presentation of a properly submitted bill.

11. Severability. In the event any section, clause or provision of this resolution shall be determined invalid or unconstitutional, such section, clause or provision shall be deemed severable and all other sections or portions hereof shall remain in full force and effect. It is the intent of the City Council that it would have adopted all other portions of this resolution irrespective of any such portion declared to be invalid or unconstitutional.

12. Environmental Determination. In accordance with California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) Guidelines section 15378(b)(5), submission of the proposed Charter amendments to the voters is categorically exempt from CEQA because it involves administrative activity that will not result in direct or indirect physical changes in the environment.

13. Effective Date. This Resolution shall take effect upon its adoption.

RESOLUTION NO. 85 (2026) adopted by the City Council of the City of San Mateo, California, at a regular meeting held on July 20, 2026, by the following vote of the City Council:

AYES: Council Members Loraine, Fernandez, Diaz Nash, and Newsom
NOES: None
ABSENT: Council Member Cwirko-Godycki
