# Measure BB — City of Pacifica — Transactions and use (sales) tax, 1%, general tax ("Pacifica City Services Measure")

- **Jurisdiction:** City of Pacifica.
- **Kind:** ordinance adding Chapter 20, "Transactions and Use Tax of 2026", to Title 3 of the Pacifica Municipal Code: a 1% (one-cent) general sales tax, until ended by voters. Placed by City Council Resolution No. 61-2026.
- **Who votes:** Pacifica voters. Resolution Sec. 2, PDF p. 2: the Council "hereby orders the submission to the qualified voters of the City of Pacifica at the General Municipal Election to be held on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, a measure that, if approved, establishes a transactions and use (sales) tax at the rate of 1%".
- **Threshold:** simple majority. Resolution Sec. 5, PDF p. 3: "The vote requirement for the Measure to pass is a majority (50% + 1) of the votes cast."

## Ballot question (verbatim)

Resolution Sec. 3 ("Ballot Language"), PDF p. 2 (scan):

> Pacifica City Services Measure. To protect Pacifica's long-term financial stability; fund police, fire, 911 emergency response; prioritize paving streets in the worst conditions; keep parks, beaches and public spaces safe and maintained; and for general government use, shall the City of Pacifica's measure establishing a 1% (1¢) transactions and use (sales) tax, generating approximately $5,000,000 annually until ended by voters, with public disclosure and local control of funds, and independent annual audits, be adopted?

## Impartial analysis

`impartial-analysis.pdf`, p. 1: "CITY ATTORNEY IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE BB", signed "/s/ Michelle Marchetta Kenyon, City Attorney, City of Pacifica".

- "The City Council of the City of Pacifica placed Measure BB on the November 3, 2026, ballot which if approved by a majority of the City's voters would enact an ordinance authorizing a 1% (1¢) transactions and use tax in the City of Pacifica."
- "If approved, Measure BB would impose a City tax that adds 1¢ to the cost of a $1.00 tangible personal property item purchased in Pacifica."
- "This tax would be a general tax, meaning that revenues raised from the tax can be used for any general municipal purpose and will be deposited in the City's general fund."
- "Certain necessities of life, such as groceries or prescription medicine, would be exempt from the tax."
- "The City estimates that Measure BB, if approved, would generate approximately $5,000,000 in annual tax revenue until ended by voters."
- "In order for Measure BB to pass, the City's voters must approve the Measure by a simple majority vote (50% + 1) vote. A "YES" vote is a vote to approve the tax. A "NO" vote is a vote against the tax."
