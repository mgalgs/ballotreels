RESOLUTION NO. 2026 - 075

A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF SAN BRUNO SUBMITTING TO THE VOTERS AT THE NOVEMBER 3, 2026 GENERAL MUNICIPAL ELECTION A MEASURE THAT PROPOSES ADOPTION OF AN ORDINANCE REPEALING INITIATIVE ORDINANCE NO. 1284 RELATING TO VOTER APPROVAL TO PERMIT BUILDINGS EXCEEDING THREE STORIES OR FIFTY FEET IN HEIGHT, ANY INCREASE IN RESIDENTIAL UNITS OR OCCUPANCY IN RESIDENTIAL ZONING DISTRICTS IN EXCESS OF LIMITS PERMITTED ON OCTOBER 10, 1974, MULTISTORY PARKING STRUCTURES, AND DEVELOPMENT THAT ENCROACHES UPON, MODIFIES, WIDENS OR REALIGNS CRYSTAL SPRINGS ROAD BETWEEN OAK AVENUE AND JUNIPERO SERRA FREEWAY, AND SNEATH LANE FROM EL CAMINO REAL TO THE WESTERLY CITY LIMITS; REQUESTING THAT SUCH ELECTION BE CONSOLIDATED WITH THE STATEWIDE GENERAL ELECTION HELD ON THAT DATE; TAKING CERTAIN OTHER ACTIONS; AND FINDING SUCH ACTION EXEMPT FROM CEQA

WHEREAS, by prior resolution, the City Council called a general municipal election (the "Election") for November 3, 2026, to be consolidated with the statewide general election to be held on that same date;

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to have the matter of an ordinance that would repeal Initiative Ordinance No. 1284, an initiative measure to require an affirmative vote of the voters of the City of San Bruno prior to any City approvals that allow: (i) buildings exceeding three stories or fifty feet in height, (ii) any increase in residential units or occupancy in residential zoning districts in excess of limits permitted on October 10, 1974, (iii) multistory parking structures, and (iv) development that encroaches upon, modifies, widens or realigns Crystal Springs Road between Oak Avenue and Junipero Serra Freeway, and Sneath lane from El Camino Real to the westerly City limits; be presented to the voters on the November 3, 2026 ballot; and

WHEREAS, since the adoption of Ordinance No. 1284, the California Legislature has enacted a broad array of State housing laws that override local zoning regulations, including voter-approved height and density limits, reducing the City's local control over land use decisions and such that many of the development scenarios the Ordinance was designed to subject to voter approval may now proceed under State law regardless of local voter sentiment; and

WHEREAS, State law, including but not limited to the State Density Bonus Law, SB 9 (2021), and SB 79 (2025), has significantly pre-empted the housing-related provisions of Ordinance No. 1284, further eroding local control over residential development while the Ordinance continues to impose meaningful constraints on commercial and economic development; and

WHEREAS, the practical effect of Ordinance No. 1284 on San Bruno's economic development goals has been documented, including the loss of the Crossings Hotel development opportunity in 1999 and the decision by a major employer to redirect campus expansion plans to other jurisdictions due in part to the constraints imposed by the Ordinance, representing significant foregone property tax, business license tax, and transient occupancy tax revenue; and

WHEREAS, the City Council has established strengthening the City's fiscal base and attracting private investment as core economic development goals, including through the

[PDF page 2]

Elevate San Bruno initiative launched in January 2026, which seeks to make San Bruno more competitive through updates to the General Plan, Specific Plans, and Municipal Code; and

WHEREAS, adoption of this Resolution and approval of this ballot measure would modify only the approval process for such developments and projects, with any future proposal for a development or project remaining subject to all applicable General Plan policies, zoning and development standards, discretionary review requirements, and environmental review under California Environmental Quality Act ("CEQA").

NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF SAN BRUNO, CALIFORNIA, AS FOLLOWS:

1. Pursuant to Election Code Sections 9222 and 10201, the City Council directs that the ordinance attached hereto as Exhibit "A" and incorporated herein by reference (the "Measure") be submitted to the qualified voters of the City of San Bruno at the Election.

2. The question shall appear on the ballot as follows:

Shall City of San Bruno Ordinance No. 1284, the 1977 zoning initiative that requires voter approval before the City Council can approve buildings exceeding specified height limits, residential density increases beyond 1974 zoning, multi-story parking structures, and certain roadway changes, be repealed given that State housing laws supersede local height limits and the ordinance interacts with current City economic development goals?
Yes
No

3. The Measure shall only take effect if at least a majority of those voting on the question vote in favor of it at the Election.

4. The full text of the Measure, attached to this Resolution as Exhibit A, being the proposed ordinance repealing Initiative Ordinance No. 1284, shall be completely printed in the voter pamphlet.

5. The City Council hereby directs the City Clerk to transmit a copy of the Measure to the City Attorney. The City Attorney shall prepare an impartial analysis of the Measure, not to exceed 500 words in length, showing the effect of the Measure on the existing law and the operation of the measure, and transmit such impartial analysis to the City Clerk no later than August 18, 2026 by 5:00 p.m.

6. Pursuant to Elections Code Section 10403, the City Council requests that the San Mateo County Board of Supervisors consent to consolidation of the election for the Measure with the general election to be conducted by the San Mateo County Registrar of voters on November 3, 2026. The election shall be held in all respects as if there were only one election, and only one form of ballot shall be used. The election will be held and conducted in accordance with the provisions of law regulating a special election consolidated with a statewide election. The City will bear all costs customarily charged by the County to a city in connection with a ballot question submitted at a municipal election that is consolidated with an election conducted by the County.

7. The City Council directs the City Clerk to file a certified copy of this Resolution with the Registrar of Voters of San Mateo County.

8. Notice of the time and place of holding of the election is given and the City Clerk is authorized, instructed, and directed to give further or additional notice of the election in the time, form, and manner as required by law.

9. Pursuant to Section 10400 et seq. of the Elections Code, the Board of Supervisors of San Mateo County is requested to consolidate the election on this ballot measure with other elections held on the same day in the County.

10. The Board of Supervisors is authorized to canvass the returns of the Election pursuant to Section 10411 of the Elections Code.

11. Pursuant to Section 10002 of the Elections Code, the Board of Supervisors is requested to permit the County Clerk to render all services specified by Section 10418 of the Elections Code relating to the election, for which services the City agrees to reimburse the County, in accordance with current County pro-rations and allocation procedures.

12. The City Clerk shall file a certified copy of this Resolution with the Clerk of the County Board of Supervisors.

13. The City Clerk is hereby authorized to do all things necessary and proper to implement the provisions of this Resolution.

14. This Measure is exempt from CEQA pursuant to CEQA Guidelines Section 15061(b)(3) because it can be seen with certainty that there is no possibility that this ballot measure will have a significant effect on the environment. Adoption of this Resolution and approval of this ballot measure would modify only the approval process for certain development projects, but any future proposal for such development projects would remain subject to all applicable General Plan policies, zoning and development standards, discretionary review requirements, and environmental review under CEQA. Therefore, approval of the ballot measure would not result in a direct or reasonably foreseeable indirect physical change in the environment.

EXHIBIT A

Proposed Ordinance

AN ORDINANCE OF THE PEOPLE OF THE CITY OF SAN BRUNO, CALIFORNIA TO REPEAL INITIATIVE ORDINANCE NO. 1284 CODIFIED IN CHAPTER 12.26 OF THE SAN BRUNO MUNICIPAL CODE

THE PEOPLE OF CITY OF SAN BRUNO ORDAIN AS FOLLOWS:

SECTION 1. The purpose of this Ordinance is to repeal Initiative Ordinance No. 1284, an initiative measure to require an affirmative vote of the voters of the City of San Bruno prior to City approvals that allow buildings exceeding three stories or fifty feet in height, any increase in residential units or occupancy in residential zoning districts in excess of limits permitted on October 10, 1974, multi-story parking structures, and development that encroaches upon, modifies, widens or realigns Crystal Springs Road between Oak Avenue and Junipero Serra Freeway, and Sneath Lane from El Camino Real to the westerly City limits.

SECTION 2. Chapter 12.26 of the San Bruno Municipal Code is hereby repealed in its entirety.

SECTION 3. If any provision, clause, sentence or paragraph of this Ordinance or the application thereof to any person or circumstance shall be held invalid, such invalidity shall not affect the other provisions of this Ordinance which can be given effect without the invalid provisions or application, and to this end the provision of this Ordinance are declared to be severable.

SECTION 4. This ordinance shall take effect 10 days after the City Council declares the results of the vote at the November 3, 2026, election.

Approved by the People of the City of San Bruno at the General Municipal Election Held on November 3, 2026.

Rico E. Medina
Mayor
