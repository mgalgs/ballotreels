# Measure S — City of San Bruno — Ordinance repealing Ordinance No. 1284 (1977 voter-approval zoning initiative)

- **Jurisdiction:** City of San Bruno.
- **Kind:** Council-placed ordinance that repeals Initiative Ordinance No. 1284 (Municipal Code Chapter 12.26), which requires a citywide vote before approving buildings over three stories or fifty feet, residential density above 1974 limits, multistory parking structures, and changes to two scenic corridors. Placed by City Council Resolution No. 2026-075.
- **Who votes:** San Bruno voters. Resolution Sec. 1, PDF p. 2: the Council "directs that the ordinance attached hereto as Exhibit "A" ... (the "Measure") be submitted to the qualified voters of the City of San Bruno at the Election."
- **Threshold:** simple majority. Resolution Sec. 3, PDF p. 2: "The Measure shall only take effect if at least a majority of those voting on the question vote in favor of it at the Election."

## Ballot question (verbatim)

Resolution Sec. 2, PDF p. 2 (scan):

> Shall City of San Bruno Ordinance No. 1284, the 1977 zoning initiative that requires voter approval before the City Council can approve buildings exceeding specified height limits, residential density increases beyond 1974 zoning, multi-story parking structures, and certain roadway changes, be repealed given that State housing laws supersede local height limits and the ordinance interacts with current City economic development goals?

## Full text

- **Resolution No. 2026-075**, City Council of the City of San Bruno. PDF p. 3: "I hereby certify that foregoing Resolution No. 2026 - 075 was introduced and adopted by the San Bruno City Council at a regular meeting on July 28, 2026, by the following vote: AYES: Councilmembers: Hamilton, M. Medina, Salazar, Alvarez, Mayor R. Medina NOES: Councilmembers: None ABSENT: Councilmembers: None". Signed Lupita Huerta, City Clerk. Filed with the Chief Elections Officer "JUL 30 2026" (stamp, p. 1). (The handwritten date on the p. 3 certified-copy stamp reads like "7/30/2022"; not relied on.)
- Placed under Elections Code "Sections 9222 and 10201" (Sec. 1, p. 2) — the Council, not a petition, put this repeal on the ballot.
- Recital, PDF p. 1: "since the adoption of Ordinance No. 1284, the California Legislature has enacted a broad array of State housing laws that override local zoning regulations, including voter-approved height and density limits". Another recital cites "the loss of the Crossings Hotel development opportunity in 1999 and the decision by a major employer to redirect campus expansion plans to other jurisdictions".
- Sec. 4, p. 2: "The full text of the Measure ... shall be completely printed in the voter pamphlet."
- **Exhibit A**, PDF p. 4: "SECTION 2. Chapter 12.26 of the San Bruno Municipal Code is hereby repealed in its entirety." "SECTION 4. This ordinance shall take effect 10 days after the City Council declares the results of the vote at the November 3, 2026, election."

## Impartial analysis

`impartial-analysis.pdf`, p. 1: "CITY ATTORNEY IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE S", signed "/s/ Trisha Ortiz, City Attorney for the City of San Bruno".

- "Measure S asks the voters of the City of San Bruno whether they want to repeal Ordinance No. 1284, a zoning ordinance that requires voter approval for certain development projects in the City of San Bruno."
- "Chapter 12.26 requires approval by a majority of the voters of the City of San Bruno before the City can issue building permits, grading permits or other approvals" for buildings "exceeding fifty feet in height" or "exceeding three stories in height", density increases beyond "limits permitted on October 10, 1974", "Multistory parking structures or buildings", and changes to the Crystal Springs Road and Sneath Lane scenic corridors.
- "Measure S was placed on the ballot by the San Bruno City Council."
- "the process for considering a development project that meets one or more of the criteria listed above would be completed by City staff, the City Planning Commission, the City Council, or a combination thereof"
- "Measure S will become effective only if approved by a majority of the voters voting on the measure. A "yes" vote on Measure S is a vote to remove the voter approval requirements in Chapter 12.26 of the San Bruno Municipal Code. A "no" vote on Measure S is a vote to continue the voter approval requirements".

Key text (p. 3):
- "In 1977, San Bruno voters approved Ordinance 1284, requiring a citywide vote for new buildings over three stories, increases in residential density and multistory parking garages. Nearly fifty years later, new state housing laws overrule these local restrictions."
- "Ordinance 1284 limits our ability to attract commercial and market-rate housing projects — the only types of development that generate significant property tax revenue, impact fees, and community benefit funds."
- "Repealing it restores local control, supports responsible development, and ensures we can fund the services our community depends on."
