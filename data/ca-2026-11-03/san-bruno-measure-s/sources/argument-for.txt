Argument in Favor of Measure __

In 1977, San Bruno voters approved Ordinance 1284, requiring a citywide vote for new buildings over three stories, increases in residential density and multistory parking garages. Nearly fifty years later, new state housing laws overrule these local restrictions.

Today, these new state housing laws allow major projects to be built in San Bruno without approval from the planning commission, city council, or a public vote, and to bypass our local height limits when they are 100% affordable. While affordable housing projects are needed and remain a City priority, they generate no property tax revenue to pay for the police, fire, parks, streets, and other services their residents rely on, so they cannot remain the only viable path for new development in San Bruno.

Ordinance 1284 limits our ability to attract commercial and market-rate housing projects — the only types of development that generate significant property tax revenue, impact fees, and community benefit funds. You can see the consequences all around you. A ten-story, 341-unit, 100% affordable housing development is under construction on San Bruno Avenue near El Camino, the tallest building in San Bruno’s history. Two major economic opportunities were lost because Ordinance 1284 made investment uncertain: the Crossings hotel project, which would have generated up to $4 million annually, failed at the ballot box and the deal collapsed; and future phases of the Google/YouTube Bayhill Master Plan are now on indefinite hold. Meanwhile, neighboring cities continue attracting investment and tax revenue that should be coming here.

Ordinance 1284 no longer protects us. It only prevents San Bruno from shaping growth to strengthen our economy. Repealing it restores local control, supports responsible development, and ensures we can fund the services our community depends on.

Help keep San Bruno fiscally strong and economically competitive. Vote YES on Measure __.
