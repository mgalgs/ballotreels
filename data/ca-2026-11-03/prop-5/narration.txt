Proposition 5 is about recalls, which let voters remove state officials early.
Today, a recall ballot asks two questions: remove the official, and who replaces them?
Proposition 5 would drop the second question.
If the Governor is recalled, the Lieutenant Governor would serve until voters elect a new one.
Other offices would be filled by special election or appointment, as today.
The Legislative Analyst's Office says special elections could cost millions and shorter ballots could save millions, netting less than one-tenth of 1 percent of the state's General Fund.
Supporters say Proposition 5 stops replacements winning office with a small fraction of the vote.
Opponents say Proposition 5 takes away voters' right to choose who replaces a recalled official.
A yes vote means recall elections would no longer include a question about who should fill the vacancy.
A no vote means voters would continue to be asked who should fill the vacancy as part of recall elections.
