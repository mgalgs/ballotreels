Measure N asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $70 million in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
Measure N needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would update science and math classrooms, improve campus security, repair restrooms and plumbing, and upgrade drop-off, shade and play areas.
Bond money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee would review spending.
The district's tax statement estimates a tax of $25 per $100,000 of assessed value, from 2027-2028 through 2058-2059.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $148 million.
Supporters say some schools were built in the 1960s, and the state does not adequately fund updates without Measure N.
The Stanislaus County voter guide says that no argument against Measure N was filed by anyone in this election.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue up to $70 million in bonds.
A no vote does not authorize the district to issue the bonds.
