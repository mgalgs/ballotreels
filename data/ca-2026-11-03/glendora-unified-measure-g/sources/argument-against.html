ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE G

No Legally Binding Tax Caps — Vote No

Proponents call this an "extension." The numbers say otherwise. This measure authorizes $86 million in new debt with no binding cap on your tax rate.

Zero Rate Protection. The District's own ballot materials admit tax estimates are "informational only" and "not maximum amounts." These bonds carry an ad valorem tax unlimited as to rate or amount. Declining assessed values — a documented risk — can push levies above the promised $33 per $100,000. There is no ceiling.

You're Already Paying for Two Existing Refunding Bonds. Using GUSD's 2025-26 taxable valuation of $8,365,331,172 reported by LA County, this is what GUSD receives:

Two existing refunding bonds: 0.036605% — $3,062,129.48 annually = approximately $8,389 per day
Proposed new bond/"extension": 0.033% — $2,760,559.29 annually = approximately $7,563 per day
Total combined: 0.069605% (approximately 0.070%) — $5,822,688.76 annually = approximately $15,953 per day

This proposed "extension" would be one of three bonds, meant for GUSD building facilities.

A Major Asset the District Hasn't Weighed. GUSD is in escrow to exchange Whitcomb for $28,450,000 in income-producing property. At a conservative 5% cap rate, that portfolio throws off roughly $1.4 million yearly in passive income — enough to cover nearly the District's entire est. annual maintenance load. That revenue comes regardless of this bond, by its own admission, the District has never analyzed whether it could reduce the need for this $86 million bond.

$161 Million, Three Decades. Debt service on this measure totals $161,348,832 through fiscal year 2056-57.

Unsecured State Match. The District leans on $50 million in state funds not guaranteed to arrive, while conceding its "capital needs currently exceed" what this measure funds.

Sound financial management starts with using what's already on the table. Vote No.

MEL BENNETT
Founder, We Are Glendora (WAG)
