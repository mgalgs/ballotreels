San Diego Unified Measure M asks the district's voters whether to approve $3.5 billion in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
Measure M needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would repair roofs, plumbing and restrooms, remove asbestos and lead paint, and upgrade career and science classrooms.
The district would set aside $567 million of it, about 16 percent, for charter school projects.
The district's tax statement estimates an average tax of $32 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $59.18, through 2062-63.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $8.1 billion.
Supporters say Measure M makes critically needed school repairs and renovations without raising taxes.
No argument against Measure M was filed for the San Diego County voter guide.
A yes vote is in favor of letting the district sell the bonds.
A no vote is against letting the district sell the bonds.
