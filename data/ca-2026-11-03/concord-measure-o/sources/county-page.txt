Measure O

MEASURE O

CITY OF CONCORD

To protect City of Concord services/ensure smaller businesses pay less than larger businesses, shall the measure be adopted to: repair potholes on neighborhood and major streets; maintain 9-1-1 emergency response; keep parks/playgrounds safe/clean; relieve traffic congestion along major corridors; maintain senior/youth programs; for general government use; by updating Concord’s 37-year-old business license rates to 0.1% of gross receipts until ended by voters, providing an additional $2,900,000 annually, requiring independent citizen oversight?

CITY ATTORNEY’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF

MEASURE O

The Concord City Council has placed Measure O (“Business License Tax Simplification Ballot Measure”) on the ballot. Concord’s existing gross receipts business license tax rate schedule, based on graduated tiers of gross receipts, with rates that become progressively lower from 0.1% to 0.03% as business gross receipts increase, has remained substantially unchanged for approximately 40 years. Measure O would replace those tiered rates with a single uniform rate of one tenth of one percent (0.1%) of gross receipts for gross receipts-based businesses. Measure O would expand the small business exemption for students and seniors making less than $3,600 to any business making less than $5,000. Neither the existing gross receipts tax rates nor the rates as amended by Measure O contain inflation adjustments. Measure O also clarifies the initial due and delinquency dates for tax payments; clarifies that refund claims must be submitted within one year of payment consistent with state law; subjects the existing $25 minimum tax to inflationary adjustments; and imposes interest of 1.5% per month on delinquent amounts.

Because Measure O does not legally restrict use of the tax revenues to any specific purpose, it is a “general tax.” The tax proceeds would be deposited in Concord’s general fund and may be used for any valid governmental purpose, including: pothole repair and street maintenance, 911 emergency response, park and playground upkeep, traffic congestion relief, and senior and youth programs. It is an independent source of revenue for Concord that neither Sacramento nor the federal government can take.

Measure O requires the City’s independent auditors to prepare a publicly available annual report on the collection, management, and expenditure of $2.9 million of the additional revenue generated by Measure O. Concord’s existing Measure V Citizens’ Oversight Committee would review/report on the receipt/expenditure of those funds.

Business license tax is paid only by businesses operating in Concord and not by residents who are not business owners. Under current law, smaller businesses subject to the general gross receipts tax with lower gross receipts pay a higher effective tax rate than larger businesses with higher gross receipts because the tax rate decreases at each successive tier.

Measure O would eliminate that tiered structure and apply the same 0.1% rate to all general gross receipts taxed businesses, regardless of size. Measure O would not change the rates for manufacturing, specified professions and trades, apartments, hotels, motels, contractors, or other specifically identified business categories.

Measure O will be approved if it receives a simple majority of “Yes” votes. Measure O would terminate when repealed by Concord voters. If Measure O is not approved, the existing tiered gross receipts business license tax rates and exemption for only student/senior businesses making under $3,600 (rather than any business making $5,000 or less) in annual gross receipts would remain in place; due-date, delinquency date, and refund claim date clarifications would not be made; the $25 minimum tax would not be subject to inflationary adjustments; and interest on delinquent amounts would not be imposed.

A “Yes” vote is a vote in favor of Measure O.

A “No” vote is a vote against Measure O.

Dated: August 13, 2026

ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF

MEASURE O

Yes on O simplifies and updates Concord’s nearly 40-year-old business license structure to ensure that large businesses are paying their fair share, and smaller businesses are not unfairly shouldering the cost of keeping Concord a safe, well-maintained community. Measure O is not a tax on homeowners or residents. Measure O simply updates an outdated business tax structure.

Yes on O will:

• Repair potholes, neighborhood and major city streets

• Maintain 9-1-1 emergency response

• Keep parks/playgrounds safe and clean for kids

• Relieve traffic congestion along major corridors

• Maintain senior and youth programs

Yes on O requires strict transparency and accountability – including Independent Citizens Oversight, public disclosure of all spending, financial audits, and yearly reports to the community to ensure that all funds are spent locally and as promised.

Yes on O ensures local control over local dollars for our own local needs. Not a penny can be taken by the state.

We need Yes on O. Did you know? Concord has approximately 65 million square feet of public streets to maintain and keep safe! In an accident, disaster or medical emergency, Measure O helps ensure rapid 9-1-1 emergency services are ready when we need them the most, including first responders who must safely and quickly reach people that need help.

Vote Yes on O! By simplifying the business license structure, Measure O ensures smaller businesses, homeowners, and residents aren’t unfairly shouldering the cost of vital local services like public safety, roads, parks, and emergency response.

Let’s keep Concord safe and maintain our quality of life – Join a unanimous City Council, local businesses, community leaders, and neighbors in voting Yes on O!

REBUTTAL TO ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF

MEASURE O

Don’t be fooled by the “simplification” label

• This is a permanent tax increase with no sunset date. Once enacted, it never expires unless voters act again, and voters rarely do.

A gross-receipts tax ignores profit margin entirely.

• Low-margin businesses — grocery stores, gas stations, and other retailers could see this tax eat up a massive share of their actual earnings.

• High-margin businesses like consultants or law firms pay the same rate on far more profitable revenue.

• That’s not fairness; it’s a new form of discrimination that punishes the very businesses Concord residents rely on most.

The city’s own consultant found Concord’s business license tax already outperforms peer cities. This isn’t a broken system needing rescue; it’s a healthy one the city wants to overdose by $2.9 million a year.

Calling it “not a tax on homeowners” ignores basic economics.

• Businesses don’t absorb new costs — they pass them to customers through higher prices, or cut jobs and hours to cover the difference.

• Every Concord resident who shops, eats, or does business locally would feel this.

“General government use” means the money isn’t legally required to go to potholes or 911 response at all — it can be spent on anything the Council chooses. Citizen oversight committees can review spending, but they can’t stop it.

Vote No on Measure O. Tell the Council to fix its priorities first, not tax struggling low-margin businesses to backfill them.

Contra Costa Taxpayers Assn, Cynthia Johnson, Co-Treasurer Jennifer Wang-Filomeo, Concord Resident

Eric Rotsten, Real Estate Broker

Tom McDougall, School Board Member; Retired Businessman

ARGUMENT AGAINST

MEASURE O

Who Pays?

• The City claims residents who aren’t business owners won’t pay more. Businesses rarely absorb new costs passively — they pass them along through higher prices at the shops,

restaurants, and services you use every day.

• A tax on business is, in actual effect, a tax on everyone who spends money in Concord.

Where does the money go?

• This measure funds the General Fund, not a locked account earmarked specifically for roads or emergency response.

• Like past measures, future City Councils retain full discretion over how the money is actually spent, regardless of what’s advertised now.

Right time?

• Concord voters are already facing a regional transit sales tax and other county measures on this same ballot. Stacking another new tax on top — even one branded as

“modernization” — adds to a real cumulative burden on households and businesses at a moment when affordability is already strained.

Startup Penalties?

• New companies losing money still owe the tax because the levy ignores production costs

Well Written?

• This overhaul of a 40-year-old structure is significant and deserves more scrutiny than a single election cycle allows.

• The rewrite must include a truly independent, binding oversight and sunset mechanism without which voters are being asked to trust the outcome rather than control it.

Vote No, and ask the City to return with a plan that includes real accountability — a hard spending lock, a sunset date, and full transparency — before asking Concord to pay more.

No on O!

Contra Costa Taxpayers Assn, Cynthia Johnson, Co-Treasurer

Jennifer Wang-Filomeo, Concord Resident
