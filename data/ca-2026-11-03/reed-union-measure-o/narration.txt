Measure O asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $115 million in bonds for school repairs and upgrades.
The board of trustees placed Measure O on the ballot on June 8, 2026; as a Proposition 39 bond, it needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would repair and upgrade the district's three schools, including classrooms, bathrooms, leaky roofs, heating and cooling, and fire, security and earthquake safety.
The bond money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, or other operating costs.
A citizens' oversight committee and yearly independent audits would check how the money is spent.
The tax rate statement of the district estimates an average tax of $26.63 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $30, through 2060-2061.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, from a tax on property in the district, at $213 million.
The elections office of the county says on its page for this measure that no argument against Measure O was filed.
Supporters say the district's buildings are deteriorating, with leaking roofs and moldy portables, and that all bond money must be spent locally.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue up to $115 million in bonds.
A no vote does not authorize the bonds.
