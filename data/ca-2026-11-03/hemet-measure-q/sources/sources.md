## Quoted passages

- Question (p.22): "Hemet Public Safety, Essential City Services Measure. Shall a measure funding general city services such as 9-1-1 emergency response, public safety; reducing drug-related crime; keeping public areas safe/clean; addressing homelessness; reducing wildfire risks; providing safe, clean drinking water; repairing streets/potholes; maintaining library services; attracting/retaining businesses, by establishing a 1¢ sales tax providing approximately $16,000,000 annually until ended by voters, subject to oversight, public spending disclosure, all funds controlled locally, be adopted?"
- Who placed it (p.43): "On July 14, 2026, the City Council voted to place Measure “Q” on the November 3, 2026 general municipal election ballot."
- Threshold (p.43): "If approved by a majority of Hemet voters, this Measure would authorize a one percent (1%) retail transactions and use (sales) tax within the City of Hemet."
- Analysis (p.43): "It is estimated that Measure “Q” will provide an additional $16,000,000 in annual local funding"
- Analysis (p.43): "This tax would be a “general tax,” meaning that revenues raised from the tax would go into the City’s General Fund to pay for any lawful City program, improvement, or service."
- Analysis (p.43): "Measure Q would not amend, extend, replace, or otherwise change the existing Measure H transactions and use tax which would remain in effect."
