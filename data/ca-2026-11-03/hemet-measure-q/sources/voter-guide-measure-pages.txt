               IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE “Q”
On July 14, 2026, the City Council voted to place Measure “Q” on
the November 3, 2026 general municipal election ballot. By placing
Measure “Q” on the ballot, the City of Hemet complies with the California
Constitution, which requires a majority of the voters to approve an
ordinance that establishes a general tax.
If approved by a majority of Hemet voters, this Measure would authorize
a one percent (1%) retail transactions and use (sales) tax within the
City of Hemet. A one percent (1%) rate equates to an extra 1¢ per $1
purchased of taxable goods. Exempted from sales tax are unprocessed
groceries, rent, prescription medicine and personal services such as
medical, dental, and legal. It is estimated that Measure “Q” will provide
an additional $16,000,000 in annual local funding that could be used for
general City services, including services such as police, fire, paramedic,
911 response, reducing drug-related crime, keeping public areas clean
and safe, addressing homelessness, reducing wildfire risks, providing
safe and clean drinking water, repairing streets and potholes, maintaining
library services, and attracting and retaining businesses. This tax would be
a “general tax,” meaning that revenues raised from the tax would go into
the City’s General Fund to pay for any lawful City program, improvement,
or service.
California Revenue and Taxation Code section 7285.9 authorizes the City
to levy a general transactions and use tax so long as the tax is approved
by a majority of the voters voting in an election on that issue. If approved,
the tax would remain in effect until ended by Hemet voters voting at a
subsequent election. The tax would be paid in addition to sales taxes and
would be collected at the same time and in the same manner as existing
sales taxes. Measure Q would not amend, extend, replace, or otherwise
change the existing Measure H transactions and use tax which would
remain in effect. All revenues raised by the tax would remain in the City
and would not be shared with the State, County, or any other agency. The
receipt and expenditure of tax revenues would be subject to the City’s
budget process, public financial reporting, and independent annual audits.
A “yes” vote on Measure “Q” will authorize the 1% transactions and use
(sales) tax.
A “no” vote on Measure “Q” will not authorize the 1% transactions and
use (sales) tax.
The above statement is an impartial analysis of Measure “Q”. If you
desire a copy of the Measure, please visit https://www.hemetca.
gov/1376/Information-About-Measure-Q or call the City Clerk’s Office
at (951) 765-2395 and a copy will be mailed at no cost to you.
