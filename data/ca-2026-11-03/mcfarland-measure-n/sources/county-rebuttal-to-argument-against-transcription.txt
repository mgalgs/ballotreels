The argument against Measure N was not written by McFarland residents. It comes from outside special-interest groups based in Orange County and the Bay Area, organizations that have no stake in our community's needs, priorities, or future. Their claims misrepresent what the proposed Charter does.
Measure N does not eliminate competitive bidding, taxpayer protections, or ethical safeguards. State and federal laws still apply, including requirements for transparency, public meetings, financial reporting, and anti-corruption rules. In fact, the Charter adds additional accountability measures that go beyond state minimums, requiring clearer public reporting, stronger disclosure standards, and direct voter oversight of key decisions.
The opponents criticize the Charter for providing flexibility, but flexibility is exactly what McFarland needs to improve public works, streamline contracting, and respond quickly to local infrastructure and economic-development opportunities. Local control means local accountability, your elected City Council remains fully answerable to McFarland voters, not Sacramento bureaucracies or outside advocacy groups.
The Charter does not grant "sweeping powers" without limits. It establishes a framework that still requires ordinances, public hearings, and community oversight. Any future rules must be adopted publicly, with voter input and full transparency. That is how local democracy works, and Measure N enhances these requirements, ensuring more openness than the state currently mandates.
Don't let organizations from hundreds of miles away tell McFarland how to govern itself. Measure N strengthens local decision-making and ensures that McFarland, not outside interests, sets the rules for its own future.
Vote YES on Measure N for real local control.
