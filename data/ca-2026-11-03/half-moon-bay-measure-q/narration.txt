City of Half Moon Bay Measure Q asks the voters of Half Moon Bay whether to adopt an ordinance for 40 affordable homes for senior agricultural workers.
In May, the City Council adopted the ordinance, approving agreements with Mercy Housing California to build on land the city owns on Kelly Avenue.
Voters then filed a referendum petition, which suspended the ordinance and sent it to the ballot.
Today, the ordinance is on hold, and takes effect only if a majority of voters approve it.
Adopting the ordinance would lease the land to Mercy Housing for 99 years, at $1 a year.
Mercy Housing would have to build and maintain the project, which includes 2,653 square feet of community space.
The city manager would review the lease at least every seven years.
The published measure documents of San Mateo County do not include a cost estimate for the measure.
No argument against Measure Q appears in the published measure documents of San Mateo County.
Supporters say the apartments cost taxpayers nothing; state law bars rejecting them, even by referendum.
A yes vote is in favor of adopting the ordinance and its agreements for the project.
A no vote is against adopting the ordinance and its agreements for the project.
