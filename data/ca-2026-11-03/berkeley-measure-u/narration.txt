City of Berkeley Measure U asks voters in the city whether to approve $300 million in bonds.
The city would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in Berkeley.
Measure U needs two-thirds of the vote to pass.
The money could pay for fire stations, emergency response facilities, parks, earthquake upgrades and other city facilities.
The tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $22 a year per $100,000 of assessed value, through 2066-2067.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at $610 million.
Opponents say Measure U is a $300 million blank check, with no binding commitment to specific projects.
Supporters say Measure U keeps Berkeley safe, replacing unsafe fire stations and building seawalls for sea-level rise.
A yes vote means the city could issue the $300 million in bonds.
A no vote means the city could not issue these bonds.
