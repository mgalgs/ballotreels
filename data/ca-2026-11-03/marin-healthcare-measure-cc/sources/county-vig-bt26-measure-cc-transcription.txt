MEASURES SUBMITTED TO THE VOTERS
DISTRICT
MARIN HEALTHCARE DISTRICT
MEASURE CC

Marin Healthcare District - Emergency Healthcare Funding Measure Shall the measure funding expanded and upgraded emergency room, intensive care, hospital, and other medical facilities to care for trauma, heart attack, stroke, and other patients and attract qualified doctors and nurses, with no funds for administrator salaries, by enacting a parcel tax of 14¢ per square foot of improved building area annually, generating approximately $12,400,000 annually for 30 years, subject to annual audits and citizens’ committee oversight, be adopted?

YES    NO

IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS BY COUNTY COUNSEL
MARIN HEALTHCARE DISTRICT
MEASURE CC

This Measure was placed on the ballot following submission of a voter initiative petition containing the number of signatures required by law.

Because Measure CC was placed on the ballot through the initiative process, it will be adopted if approved by a majority of the votes cast in this election. If it is adopted, the Marin Healthcare District will levy a parcel tax beginning the first full fiscal year after the effective date. The Measure will remain in effect for thirty years from the beginning of the parcel tax. The rate of the parcel tax shall be $0.14 per square foot of improved building area, which is defined as the area on a parcel of taxable real property on which there exists any building. The Marin Healthcare District estimates revenue from this Measure will average approximately $12,400,000 annually.

The revenue raised by this parcel tax must be placed in a special account and can only be used for the purposes stated in this Measure. The stated purposes for the funds raised by this special tax are the expansion and modernization of intensive care, trauma, triage, and hospital facilities, as well as additional capacity for the emergency room and patient rooms. The funds may also be spent to hire and retain doctors and nurses, with no funds for administrator salaries.

s/BRANDON W. HALTER

ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE CC

Vote YES on Measure CC to protect local access to life-saving emergency care and hospital services close to home by keeping MarinHealth staffed, equipped, and ready!

For victims of heart attacks, strokes, and accidents, seconds mean the difference between life and death. Measure CC ensures that we have access to life-saving medical care when we need it most.

In Marin County, access to emergency care is especially critical — we have only one major freeway, and traffic delays to the nearest hospital in San Francisco or Sonoma can exceed an hour.

MarinHealth Medical Center is the only designated trauma center in Marin County — and a full-service community hospital providing emergency care, heart and stroke treatment, cancer care, surgery, and mental health services. Studies show that trauma center care can increase survival rates by up to 25%.

Emergency room visits are increasing, and demand for emergency care is growing. Without investment, our hospital risks falling behind safety standards and losing critical services.

Vote YES on Measure CC to:
• Upgrade medical technology for heart attacks, strokes, and cancer
• Modernize intensive care, trauma, and triage facilities
• Expand ER capacity to reduce wait times and overcrowding
• Improve outdated operating rooms, labs, and imaging facilities
• Improve mental health, addiction, and suicide prevention services
• Modernize hospital facilities for earthquake safety
• Attract and retain qualified doctors, nurses, and specialists

Measure CC requires strict fiscal accountability:
• Ensures that MarinHealth Medical Center remains a public, community-owned hospital
• Every penny stays local and cannot be taken by State or Federal governments
• All funding must be spent as promised and not on administrators’ salaries
• Independent oversight and audits

When an emergency strikes, we need quick access to excellent medical care.

Join local first responders and doctors who rely on MarinHealth every day to save lives. Vote YES on Measure CC!

REBUTTAL TO ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE CC

Marin residents support needed public services. We draw the line at “straight up dishonest” tactics that manufacture panic to fund a wish list through generational taxation.

Proponents claim they need tax to attract staff and save emergency care. The district’s own August 2026 metrics confirm no such crisis: nursing headcount rose, primary care waiting list fell from 3,000 to zero, and turnover dropped to 2%.

MarinHealth is nowhere near ruin, nor is its emergency room. It reported $661 million in revenue, a $24 million surplus, $23.6 million in gifts, and CEO pay in the top 5% nationwide. Taxpayers should fund needs, not wants. Let donors fund wish lists.

Proponents promise strict fiscal accountability. In the fine print, oversight is advisory only, without enforcement, relying on routine district audits. Worse, the district may pledge this 30-year tax to issue bonds, borrowing cash upfront, piling debt and locking revenue so repeal is impossible.

Their record: taxpayers approved $394 million in 2013 and got a front-loaded, interest-heavy structure. More than borrowed ($593 million) remains owed through 2047. Fiscally irresponsible.

The district is extending its seismic deadline while the measure does not even require spending on seismic work.

Measure CC charges per square foot, so modest homes pays like mansions, with no exemption for seniors or low-income residents already afraid of hospital bills. It will take from everyone just to pad their budget.

Examine the numbers. Vote no on CC. It’s double crazy.

CLAY HESS, MD, MPH
Independent Public Health Physician

[Guide page 39, MCCMHD-5: excerpts of the ordinance's Section 3, "Adoption of Marin Healthcare District Parcel Tax", Section 1 - Imposition]

B. The Parcel Tax shall hereby be established and levied each year, commencing July 1st after the Effective Date, on each parcel of taxable real property within the boundaries of the District at the uniform rate of fourteen cents ($0.14) per square foot of improved building area.

C. The collection of the Parcel Tax shall commence on July 1st after the Effective Date and continue for thirty (30) years thereafter.

F. The District may incur bonded indebtedness for eligible capital costs related to the planning and construction of hospital improvements and upgrades, utilizing the proceeds from the Parcel Tax to make principal and interest payments to service such debt.

[Guide page 40, MCCMHD-6: excerpt of the ordinance's Section 3, Section 6]

Section 6 - Fiscal Accountability Protections and Oversight.

Starting with the first operative year of this Parcel Tax, the District shall establish an oversight committee comprised of District residents to submit a report on at least an annual basis to the Board of Directors evaluating whether the proceeds from the Parcel Tax are being properly expended for the purposes set forth herein. Proceeds from the Parcel Tax shall be subject to the District’s generally applicable independent annual audit procedures and requirements.
