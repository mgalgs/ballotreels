Measure MM asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $396 million in school bonds.
The district's governing board placed Measure MM on the ballot, and it needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay to upgrade classrooms and labs, repair gas lines, remove asbestos, lead paint and mold, and provide safe drinking water.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would be required.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average and a highest tax of $60 per $100,000 of assessed value, through 2057-2058.
The statement puts the total to repay, with interest, at about $813 million, and says its figures are estimates only, which may vary.
Supporters say many schools built in the 1920s and 1930s are in dire need of repairs, and the state cannot take the funding.
The official list of measures from the county, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure MM was submitted.
A yes vote approves the $396 million in school bonds.
A no vote rejects the $396 million in school bonds.
