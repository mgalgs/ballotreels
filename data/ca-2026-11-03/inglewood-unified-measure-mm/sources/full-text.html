FULL TEXT OF BALLOT MEASURE MM

The following is the full proposition presented to the voters by the Inglewood Unified School District.

Inglewood Unified School District Safety, Repair, Upgrade Measure. To repair/upgrade classrooms/labs including science, engineering, career/skilled trades to help recruit/retain teachers; repair deteriorating gas lines; remove asbestos, lead paint, mold; provide safe drinking water; construct, repair equip sites, facilities; shall Inglewood Unified School District's measure authorizing $396,000,000 in bonds, at legal rates, levying $60 per $100,000 of assessed valuation ($26,000,000 annually) while bonds are outstanding, be adopted, requiring oversight, audits, spending disclosure, all funds for local schools?

PROJECT LIST

The Board of Education and County Administrator of the Inglewood Unified School District have determined that the District's public schools, many of which were built in the 1920s and 30s, are in urgent need of basic health and safety repairs. To that end, the Board evaluated the District's critical facility needs, including safety issues, class size reduction, computer and information technology in developing the types of projects to be funded by this measure as described below.

In approving this Project List, the Board of Education and the County Administrator determine that the District must:

(i) Provide safe drinking water; and

(ii) Improve and modernize school facilities to recruit and retain quality teachers; and

(iii) Keep schools safe and clean; and

(iv) Adhere to specific fiscal accountability safeguards such as:

(a) Complete public disclosure of all spending shall be required;

(b) Prohibit Sacramento, the State or the federal government from taking any of the funds;

(c) Require annual independent financial and performance audits; and

(d) Every dollar must be used to upgrade local schools and cannot be used for inappropriate administrator salaries, pensions or benefits.

The Project List includes the following types of upgrades and improvements at District schools, facilities and sites:

CAREER TECHNICAL EDUCATION AND SKILLED TRADES Local Funding to Provide a 21st Century Education

Goals and Purpose: This measure will create our own local source of funding and community investment to help train our students for careers in health, emergency medical response, skilled trades, robotics and engineering and other in-demand careers. This helps students, including those whose paths do not lead to college, to learn the skills necessary to get good-paying jobs and have more economic opportunity. Schools in the District have less funding that schools in some other nearby communities, and the federal government continues to threaten cuts to education funding. Local funding helps protect against unpredictable federal decisions, provides cleaner, safer and more up-to-date schools and helps retain and attract quality teachers to provide 21st century education to our young people.

- Improve vocational and college and career preparation classrooms and facilities

- Ensure accessibility for disabled students

- Repair and upgrade computer, engineering and science classrooms and labs

- Improve instructional and classroom technology for 21st century learning

- Improve security camera systems and equipment

- Upgrade early childhood educational classrooms and facilities

LOCALLY CONTROLLED FUNDING FOR BASIC IMPROVEMENTS School Repair, Safety and Upgrade Projects

Goals and Purpose: Many of our local public schools were built in the 1920s and 30s and are in urgent need of basic health and safety repairs. This measure will provide funding to remove asbestos, lead paint and mold, provide safe drinking water and repair broken plumbing, bathrooms, gas lines, leaky roofs and heating, ventilation and air conditioning systems. In addition, many of our local schools have outdated security and safety features, including smoke alarms and fire safety and communications systems, and inadequate lighting and fencing. This measure will allow our schools to make basic security upgrades that treat our young people with respect and keep students and teachers safe.

- Repair deteriorating gas lines and heating, ventilation and cooling systems

- Remove asbestos, lead paint and mold

- Repair and upgrade deteriorating roofs

- Improve classrooms for children with special needs

- Improve fire safety systems

- Repair and upgrade deteriorating plumbing and restrooms

- Repair outdated and deteriorating electrical systems and wiring

The upgrading of technology infrastructure includes, but is not limited to, network rewiring, cabling, computers, portable interface devices, servers, switches, routers, modules, sound projection systems, laser printers, scanners and copiers, digital white boards and smart boards, document projectors, upgrade voice-over-IP, clock/telephone/ intercom systems, call manager and network security/firewall, wireless technology systems, refresh classroom technology, miscellaneous IT and instructional equipment, data storage, archiving and preservation, phones, identity cards and the construction and installation of a data center in the cloud for the District's enterprise systems, such as resource planning, websites, domain name systems, cloud applications and information security.

The budget for each project is an estimate and may be affected by factors beyond the District's control. Some projects throughout the District may be undertaken as joint use projects in cooperation with other local public or non-profit agencies. The final cost of each project will be determined as plans and construction documents are finalized, construction bids are received, construction contracts are awarded and projects are completed. Based on the final costs of each project, certain of the projects described above may be delayed or may not be completed. Demolition of portable classrooms and existing facilities and reconstruction of facilities scheduled for repair and upgrade may occur, if the Board determines that such an approach would be more cost-effective in creating more enhanced and operationally efficient campuses. Necessary site preparation/restoration may occur in connection with new construction, renovation or remodeling, or installation or removal of relocatable or modular classrooms, including ingress and egress, removing, replacing, or installing irrigation or drainage systems, traffic signals, sewer or utility lines, trees and landscaping, relocating fire access roads, road widening, and acquiring any necessary easements, licenses, permits, or rights of way to the property. The listed projects will be completed as needed. Each project is assumed to include its share of furniture, equipment, architectural, engineering, legal fees and similar planning costs, connection fees, program and project management, staff training expenses and a customary contingency for unforeseen design and construction costs. Proceeds of the bonds may be used to pay or reimburse the District for the cost of District bond audits and staff when performing work on or necessary and incidental to bond projects. Bond proceeds shall only be expended for the types of projects and purposes identified herein. The District shall create an account into which proceeds of the bonds shall be deposited and comply with the reporting requirements of Government Code § 53410.

FISCAL ACCOUNTABILITY

This Measure requires a clear system of accountability, including a citizen's oversight committee to ensure all funds are spent properly. By law, every penny of this Measure must be spent on our local schools and no funds can be taken by Sacramento, the County or the federal government and spent elsewhere.

In accordance with Education Code Section 15272, the Board of Education will appoint a citizens' oversight committee and conduct annual independent audits to ensure that funds are spent only on District projects and for no other purpose. The expenditure of bond money on these projects is subject to stringent financial accountability requirements. By law, performance and financial audits will be performed annually, and all bond expenditures will be monitored by an independent citizens' oversight committee to ensure that funds are spent as promised and specified. The citizens' oversight committee must include, among others, representation of a bona fide taxpayers association, a business organization and a senior citizens organization. No District employees or vendors are allowed to serve on the citizens' oversight committee.

No Administrator Salaries: Proceeds from the sale of the bonds authorized by this proposition shall be used only for the acquisition, construction, reconstruction, rehabilitation, or replacement of school facilities, including the furnishing and equipping of school facilities, and not for any other purpose, including inappropriate teacher and school administrator salaries and other operating expenses.
