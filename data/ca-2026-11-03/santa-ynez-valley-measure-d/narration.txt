Measure D asks the voters of this high school district whether to approve $80 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them from property taxes, on top of the taxes owners pay now.
Measure D needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would go to listed projects, such as upgrading classrooms, labs and career training facilities, and fixing roofs, plumbing and electrical systems.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $21.73 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $23.38, through 2060-2061.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $142.1 million.
No argument against Measure D was filed, so the county voter guide prints only the argument in favor of the measure.
Supporters say the high school was built ninety years ago, and its 27 portable classrooms over 25 years old are falling apart.
A yes vote is in favor of letting the district issue up to $80 million in bonds.
A no vote is against letting the district issue the bonds.
