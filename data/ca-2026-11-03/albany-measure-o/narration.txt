City of Albany Measure O asks Albany voters whether the City Council may change zoning in single-family areas without a neighborhood petition.
Under Measure D, which Albany voters passed in 1978, a zoning change in a single-family area needs a petition from its neighbors.
At least half of the resident voters within 300 feet of the change must sign it.
Measure O would let the City Council make those changes without the petition, after public hearings and the steps state law and city code require.
Only voters can change Measure D, so Measure O needs a majority of Albany voters to pass.
The Alameda County guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say an outdated rule makes it harder to meet housing needs, and Measure O approves no project itself.
The Alameda County Composite Voter Information Guide says that no argument against Measure O was submitted for this election.
A yes vote authorizes the City Council to make those zoning changes without the neighborhood petition.
A no vote does not authorize the City Council to make those zoning changes without the neighborhood petition.
