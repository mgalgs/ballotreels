Understanding the Budget and Utility Users Tax (UUT)

INFORMACIÓN EN ESPAÑOL

The City of South Gate faces an ongoing structural budget gap of approximately $9 million, with ongoing expenditures exceeding ongoing revenues. The gap is driven in large part by increasing costs, including pension obligations, health insurance, workers’ compensation, and liability insurance.

Federal relief funds and City reserves have helped maintain current services, but these resources are nearly exhausted. Without a sustainable long-term revenue solution, future budgets may require reductions in services, staffing, response times, programs, events, and facility hours.

To address this ongoing fiscal challenge, the City Council is considering whether to place a Utility Users Tax (UUT) measure on a future ballot for voters to consider. 

Budget and Fiscal Challenges Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the City of South Gate facing a budget deficit?

South Gate operates with a lean budget and has already implemented cost-saving measures. However, several factors are contributing to a growing budget gap:

  • Inflation driven cost increases for maintenance and utilities Steep increases for both general and health insurance
  • A growing backlog of needed maintenance and repairs to streets, facilities, and equipment
  • State mandates and retirement system obligations


Has the City done everything it can to balance the budget?

Yes. The City has taken steps over the years to control costs, reduce staﬃng levels, consolidate services, and pursue revenue development opportunities. However, without identifying a new local revenue source, cuts to services may become unavoidable.


What other revenue options has the City explored?

The City considered several alternatives, including a parcel tax, business license tax increase, and higher transient occupancy tax, but none would generate enough revenue to close the gap. A parcel tax would burden only property owners, a business license tax would yield limited funds and strain small businesses, and South Gate lacks enough hotels for a TOT increase to make a real impact.

After careful review, the City found that a Utility Users Tax is the most practical and equitable option, providing stable, locally controlled funding from a broad base of users.


 

Utility Users Tax Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Utility Users Tax (UUT)?

The Utility Users Tax (UUT) is a local tax applied to certain utility services, including electricity, gas, water, sewer, cable, and telephone services (landline and mobile). The tax is collected monthly through utility bills and must be approved by local voters.

More than 160 cities and public agencies across California have adopted a UUT to provide locally controlled funding for essential public services. South Gate is among the few cities in Los Angeles County without a UUT. A UUT could provide the City with a dedicated source of local revenue to help support essential services, including public safety, street maintenance, and emergency response.

Do other neighboring cities have a UUT?

Nearby cities rely on a UUT to help fund essential services:

  • Downey: 5% on electricity and gas, and 4.8% on telecommunications
  • Bell: 10% on electricity, gas, water, telephone, and cable
  • Bellﬂower: 5% on electricity, gas, water, telephone, and cable
  • Lynwood: 9% on electricity, gas, water, telephone, and cable

If passed, a UUT in South Gate would reﬂect a similar structure and rate to those already in place in neighboring communities.

 

Why is the City considering a Utility Users Tax?

South Gate is facing growing budget challenges due to rising costs and maintenance needs. Like many cities in California, South Gate is experiencing structural budget pressures, costs for public safety, infrastructure maintenance, and community services are increasing faster than current revenue streams allow. The City’s General Fund, which pays for most day-to-day services, is under strain.

The UUT is a critical, locally controlled revenue stream that helps South Gate maintain services for our residents.


What services would the UUT help fund?

The Utility Users Tax would help South Gate invest in the services that keep our city safe, clean, and thriving:

  • Maintain reliable 911 emergency response and police staﬃng levels
  • Keep our school crossing guards at our schools
  • Keep our recreation facilities open and available for youth sports
  • Repair and maintain city streets, including pothole repairs
  • Maintain senior and youth services


Who pays the UUT?

The UUT is paid by anyone who uses taxed utilities in South Gate, this includes both residents and businesses. The tax is included on utility bills and collected by service providers, who then remit it to the City.


Will there be any exemptions available?

Senior Exemption — Do You Qualify?

If you are a low-income senior resident, you may be fully exempt from the UUT — meaning you pay $0 in utility users tax.

You qualify if you meet all three of the following:

  1. Age — You are 65 years of age or older.
  2. Account Holder – You (qualifying senior) are the named account holder.
  3. Residence — The utility service address is your primary residence (where you live most of the time).
  4. Household income — The combined gross income of everyone living in your home does not exceed the City’s income limit. Income limits are set annually using the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) low-income utility assistance standards. Current limits are available at City Hall and on this page (see below).

How to Apply

Applying is free. The exact process will be communicated to the public if the ballot measure is approved. In general, it involves the following steps:

  1. Completing an application.

    The application must include:

    • –Proof of age (driver’s license, passport, birth certificate, or other government-issued ID)
    • –Documentation of all household members’ income for the prior calendar year (tax return, Social Security award letter, pension statement, or equivalent)
  2. Sign and submit. The form must be signed under penalty of perjury. Return the completed application to the Finance Department by mail or in person.

Once approved, your exemption stays in effect as long as you continue to qualify. The City will ask you to re-verify your eligibility each year.

What Happens After You Apply?

The City will review your application and notify you in writing of the decision. If approved, your utility service provider will be notified and the UUT will no longer appear on your utility bills.

Important: If your circumstances change — for example, you move, your income increases beyond the limit, or another household member’s income changes — you must notify the Finance Department in writing within 30 days.

If you do not report a change in time, you will not be entitled to a refund of taxes already collected during the period you no longer qualified.

 

Will there be public input or a vote?

Measure SG is a proposed Utility Users Tax (UUT) measure that will appear on the November 3, 2026 ballot. The measure will allow the community to decide if new revenue will be added to the city budget.

School Crossing Guard Program Update 

Why are crossing guards being reduced?

The program is part of more than $9 million in City service reductions resulting from the City's ongoing structural budget deficit.

 

How much does the program cost?

$750,000 annually.

Who has paid for it?

The City of South Gate has historically fully funded the program.

Is it permanent?

It will remain suspended until additional revenue becomes available.

Will it come back if UUT passes?

The City intends to evaluate restoring services, including crossing guards, as part of the budget restoring process.

What other cuts are happening?

PUBLIC SAFETY

  • Fewer officers per shift causing slower response
  • Reduced low-priority call responses
  • Crossing guard program eliminated
  • Reduced crime prevention programs

MAINTENANCE SERVICES

  • Slower graffiti removal
  •  Longer streetlight repair times
  • Less frequent tree trimming

PARKS & RECREATION

  • Reduced recreation programs
  • Fewer community events
  • Reduced pool operations
  • Pool and sports fields scheduled to be closed in November.
