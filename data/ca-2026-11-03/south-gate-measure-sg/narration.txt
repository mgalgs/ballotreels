Measure SG asks the voters of this city whether to add an 8 percent tax to utility bills, for general city services.
The city does not charge a utility users tax now, its August budget presentation says.
Four of the five City Council members voted to place Measure SG on the ballot.
The measure's own text declares a financial state of emergency, citing an ongoing deficit in the general fund.
Phone, video, electricity, gas and water bills in the city would carry an 8 percent tax, collected by the utilities.
People 65 or older with low household incomes could apply to be exempt at their home.
The money would go to the general fund for any lawful city purpose, with no end date unless voters repeal the tax.
The City Council could amend the tax without a vote, but not to increase it.
The ballot question estimates the tax would raise about $10.4 million a year.
Two taxpayers associations, one statewide and one for the county, filed an argument against Measure SG; its text was not gathered.
A council member, a former council member and three local groups filed an argument for Measure SG; its text was not gathered.
A yes vote adopts the 8 percent utility users tax.
A no vote rejects the 8 percent utility users tax.
