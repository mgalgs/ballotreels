City of Los Angeles Measure LA asks voters in the city whether to change the charter rules on the budget, infrastructure, public works and city contracts.
The City Charter sets the rules for how the city manages its budget, contracts, property and Department of Public Works.
Today, the city budgets one year at a time, and needs no capital plan for infrastructure.
A full-time Board of Public Works oversees the department, and the charter bars the city from mortgaging property or running commercial businesses.
Measure LA would require two-year budgets and a capital plan for roads, parks, bridges and buildings.
The Director of Public Works would gain powers, and the City Council could change or eliminate the board.
The city could also mortgage property, run commercial businesses, and change some contracting rules.
The City Administrative Officer estimates one-time General Fund costs between $4 million and $6 million, and says other effects cannot be quantified yet.
Opponents say letting the council eliminate the board removes independent review of contracts and weakens public accountability.
Supporters say long-term planning and two-year budgets let the city fix infrastructure before problems become more expensive.
A yes vote means you want the charter amended to make these changes.
A no vote means you do not want the charter amended.
