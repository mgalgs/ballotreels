Measure R asks the voters of this city whether to add a three-eighths of 1 percent sales tax for 10 years.
Today the city charges its own sales tax of 1.5 percent, on top of state and other local sales taxes.
Measure R would raise that city rate to 1.875 percent.
The increase would last 10 years, then end on its own.
As a general tax, the money would go to the general fund, for purposes including public safety, repairs, and upkeep of streets, beaches and parks.
Measure R needs a simple majority to pass.
The ballot question says the tax would raise about $1 million a year.
The city clerk's election page lists the argument in favor, and says no argument against Measure R was submitted.
Supporters say putting off repairs only makes them cost more, and that visitors, not just residents, would help pay.
A yes vote approves the sales tax increase for 10 years.
A no vote does not approve the sales tax increase.
