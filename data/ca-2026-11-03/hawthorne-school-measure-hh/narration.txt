Measure HH asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $110 million in school bonds.
The district's governing board placed Measure HH on the ballot, and it needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay to repair and build classrooms and facilities, such as plumbing, electrical, heating and security systems.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would be required.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average and a highest tax of $30 per $100,000 of assessed value, through 2068-2069.
The statement puts the total to repay, with interest, at about $226.2 million, and says its figures are estimates only, which may vary.
Supporters say the measure would repair and modernize local schools, and not a penny can be taken by the state or other districts.
The official list of measures from the county, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure HH was submitted.
A yes vote approves the $110 million in school bonds.
A no vote rejects the $110 million in school bonds.
