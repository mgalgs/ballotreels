Modesto City High School District Measure K asks the district's voters whether to approve $250 million in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
The bonds would extend an earlier bond's tax, with the rate not projected to rise above today's rate.
Measure K needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would upgrade classrooms, labs and career training facilities, and repair roofs, plumbing, and heating and cooling.
Bond money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee would review spending.
The district's tax statement estimates an average tax of $24 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $29.
The statement puts the total to repay, with interest, at $480 million.
Supporters say many high schools were built between the 1940s and 1960s, and Measure K would lower the tax rate.
The Stanislaus County voter guide says that no argument against Measure K was filed by anyone in this election.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue up to $250 million in bonds.
A no vote does not authorize the district to issue the bonds.
