## Quoted facts

- Who votes / vote needed: notice, "an election will be held on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, within the District ... This measure will become effective only if approved by 55% of those voters voting on the measure."
- Ballot question: "High School Career Training/ Classroom Repair Extension Measure. To upgrade classrooms/ labs for quality math, science, writing instruction; update career training facilities preparing students for the workforce; and repair deteriorating roofs, plumbing, heating/ cooling systems, shall Modesto City High School District’s measure be adopted authorizing $250,000,000 in bonds at legal rates, extending the current tax rate of $24 per $100,000 assessed value (no increase), providing approximately $14,000,000 annually while bonds are outstanding, with independent oversight/ all funds locally controlled?"
- Analysis: "The Board of Education (Board) of the Modesto City High School District (District) placed this Measure on the ballot to authorize the issuance of bonds of up to $250 million in aggregate principal amount".
- Analysis: "the estimated average annual tax needed to repay the principal and interest on the bonds would be $24 per $100,000 ... The estimated maximum tax rate is $29.00 per $100,000 ... with a levy starting in fiscal year 2031-2032 through 2060-2061. The District estimates that the total amount repayable during the life of the bonds, including principal and interest, will be approximately $480,000,000."
- Analysis: "No administrator or teacher salaries may be paid from bond proceeds."
- Tax rate statement: average "$24 per $100,000 of assessed valuation. The final fiscal year in which the tax is anticipated to be collected is 2060-61."; highest "$29 per $100,000 of assessed valuation first occurring in fiscal year 2031-32"; total debt service "$480,000,000"; "the District expects to sell the bonds in multiple series as a “bond extension” to the prior bond authorization, with bond maturities structured so that tax rate in each year is not projected to increase as compared to the current tax rate."
