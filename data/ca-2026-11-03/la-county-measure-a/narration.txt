Los Angeles County Measure A asks county voters whether to set up binding arbitration for public safety labor disputes.
Today, firefighters and deputy sheriffs may not strike, and the county must negotiate with their unions in good faith.
Measure A would also bar strikes by district attorney investigators, medical examiners and lifeguards.
When talks fail, a three-member panel would pick one side's final offer on each issue.
The panel's decision would be binding, without approval from the Board of Supervisors or voters.
The Auditor-Controller says each arbitration could cost tens of thousands to millions of dollars, and has no basis to predict the effect on labor costs.
Supporters say neutral experts would decide pay, not politics, helping departments recruit and keep safety workers.
No argument against Measure A was submitted, so the official materials print only the supporters' argument.
A yes vote means the charter would set up binding arbitration for these disputes.
A no vote means no binding arbitration, and the negotiation process stays as it is.
