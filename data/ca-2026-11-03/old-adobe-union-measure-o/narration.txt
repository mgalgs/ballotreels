Measure O asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $48 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds over time and repay them from property taxes in the district, in addition to current property taxes.
Measure O needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money could repair leaky roofs, replace outdated heating and cooling systems, make safety and accessibility improvements, and modernize classrooms.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $24.15 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $25, until 2059-2060.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $97 million.
No argument against Measure O was submitted for the county Registrar of Voters' printed voter guide pages on this measure.
Supporters say many older classrooms and buildings no longer meet modern standards, and Measure O has strong taxpayer protections.
A yes vote will authorize the district to issue the bonds and levy taxes to repay them.
A no vote will not authorize the bonds or the taxes to repay them.
