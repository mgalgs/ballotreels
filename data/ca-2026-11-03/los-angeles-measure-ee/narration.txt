City of Los Angeles Measure EE asks voters in the city whether to change charter rules on ethics, elections, and how often the City Council meets.
Today, the charter requires the City Council to meet at least three days a week.
The police and fire commissions can direct their own investigators not to start or continue an investigation or audit.
Measure EE would require the council to meet at least weekly, and end the power of the commissions to stop those investigations.
Ethics commissioners could not run for city or school board office for five years after leaving.
The top campaign finance penalty would rise to $15,000, or three times the amount involved if that is more.
A referendum petition would need signatures equal to 15 percent of the votes in the last election for mayor, up from 10 percent.
The City Administrative Officer says fewer meetings may save on translation and captioning, while more oversight staff may bring costs that cannot be determined.
Supporters say Measure EE strengthens independent oversight of police and fire, and frees council members to work in their districts.
Opponents say Measure EE mainly cuts the public meetings where residents can watch, give comment, and hold elected officials accountable.
A yes vote means you want the charter amended to make these changes.
A no vote means you do not want the charter amended.
