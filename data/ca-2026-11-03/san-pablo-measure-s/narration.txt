Measure S asks the voters of this city whether to adopt limits on rent increases and on the reasons for evicting tenants.
Measure S reached the ballot by a citizens' petition, and it needs a majority of the vote to pass.
Today, local rules do not limit rent increases beyond what state law does.
For existing tenants, rents would roll back to what was charged on July 1, 2025.
After that, yearly increases would generally be held to 60 percent of the rise in consumer prices, and no more than 3 percent.
Landlords would need a specific legal reason to end a tenancy, and relocation payments would generally be at least $12,000.
The city attorney's analysis says running the program would cost between $1.08 million and $2.36 million a year, paid by fees on rental property owners.
Opponents say Measure S creates a costly new regulatory program, and that it does not create new housing or increase the housing supply.
Supporters say that limiting rent increases and preventing evictions without a reason will reduce homelessness and help families stay in their homes.
A yes vote approves the new rules, which effectively replace the current tenant ordinances of the city.
A no vote leaves the current tenant ordinances of the city in place.
