City of Los Angeles Measure PRK asks voters in the city whether to raise the minimum budget the charter sets for the Department of Recreation and Parks.
Today, the charter requires the General Fund, the main operating fund of the city, to give parks a minimum share of the assessed value of city property.
Measure PRK would double that share over ten years, starting in the 2027-2028 budget year.
In a declared fiscal emergency, the City Council could cut the added money by up to 30 percent, but never below the share required today.
The City Administrative Officer estimates Measure PRK would add $32 million to parks in its first year, rising to $497 million by the 2036-2037 budget year.
With no new revenue, the officer says the city must move money from other services, including public safety, public works and homeless services.
No argument against Measure PRK was submitted, so the City Clerk's voter pamphlet prints only arguments in favor of it.
Supporters say Measure PRK would keep parks clean, repair park facilities, and keep recreation and senior centers open without raising taxes.
A yes vote means you want to increase the minimum budget for the Department of Recreation and Parks.
A no vote means you do not want to increase that minimum budget.
