City of Los Angeles Measure TE asks voters in the city whether to exempt sales of homes damaged in the Palisades Fire from the Proposition ULA tax.
In 2022, city voters approved Proposition ULA, a tax on property sales over $5 million that pays for affordable housing and help for tenants.
The Palisades Fire began on January 7, 2025, and damaged or destroyed about 5,495 residential structures.
Today, a damaged home that sells for over the threshold owes the tax.
Measure TE would exempt the sale once, if the owner on the day the fire began sells by January 6, 2030.
Owners who already paid the tax on such a sale could get a refund.
The City Administrative Officer says the General Fund is not affected, but the housing and homelessness prevention fund would lose between $35 million and $66 million through January 2030.
Supporters say fire survivors who must sell should not pay the ULA tax, and the purpose of ULA stays.
No argument against Measure TE was submitted, so the voter pamphlet prints only the argument in favor of it.
A yes vote means you want these sales to qualify for a one-time refund or exemption from the tax.
A no vote means you do not want these sales to qualify.
