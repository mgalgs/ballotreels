## Facts, with quotes

- **Question** (p. 11): "Shall an ordinance be adopted to provide a one-time exemption from the City's special documentary transfer tax (Proposition ULA) for residential properties that were damaged or destroyed in the Palisades Fire and are sold within five years of the date of the Fire?"
- **Who votes / threshold:** City of Los Angeles voters (RR/CC list p. 11, "CITY OF LOS ANGELES – (8) VOTE PASSAGE – Majority Vote 50%+1"). Impartial Summary: "This measure will become effective if approved by a majority of voters."
- **Impartial Summary:** "Proposition ULA established a four percent tax on the sale or transfer of real property over $5 million and a 5.5 percent tax on sales or transfers at $10 million or more." "the Palisades Fire led to the damage and destruction of approximately 5,620 structures, of which an estimated 5,495 were residential structures." "If a damaged or destroyed residential property is sold or transferred between January 7, 2025 and January 6, 2030 by the owner of record on the date the Palisades Fire began, the sale or transfer would qualify for a one-time refund or exemption from the Proposition ULA tax." "This measure may lead to a reduction in Proposition ULA tax revenues, which would result in a decrease of funding for affordable housing and tenant assistance programs."
- **Financial Impact:** "This exemption will not impact the City's General Fund, but will reduce revenue to the House LA Fund … The estimated reduction in ULA revenue is between $35 million and $66 million through January 2030."
- **Argument in favor signers** (p. 14) include Traci Park (Councilmember, 11th District), Karen Bass (Mayor), Marqueece Harris-Dawson (Council President), Monica Rodriguez, Ben Allen, Jacqui Erwin, Nick Melvoin, LA/OC Building & Construction Trades Council, Western States Regional Council of Carpenters, Pacific Palisades Community Council.
