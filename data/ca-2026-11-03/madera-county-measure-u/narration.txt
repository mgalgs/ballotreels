Measure U asks the voters of this county whether to raise the tax on short hotel and rental stays from 9 to 11 percent.
Today, guests on short stays at lodging outside city limits pay a 9 percent tax, which brings in about $8 million for the county.
Measure U would raise the rate to 11 percent, with no end date.
The added money could pay for any governmental purpose, and Measure U needs a simple majority to pass.
The impartial analysis estimates $1.78 million a year in added revenue.
The fiscal impact statement estimates $1.85 million a year, and $25,000 a year in added costs.
No argument against Measure U appears among the filings that the county elections office posted for this election.
Supporters say visitors use local roads and emergency services, and the tax recovers those costs without burdening residents.
A yes vote raises the tax on short stays to 11 percent.
A no vote keeps the tax at 9 percent.
