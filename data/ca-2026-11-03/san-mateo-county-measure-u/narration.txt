San Mateo County Measure U asks county voters whether an independent commission should redraw the districts of the Board of Supervisors after each census.
Today, the Board of Supervisors itself adopts the ordinance that sets the boundaries of its five districts.
Measure U would have an independent redistricting commission draw the five districts after each federal census, instead of the board.
State law defines that commission as a body other than the board, and limits who may serve on it.
Supervisors would still be elected by district, live in their districts and serve at most three terms in a row.
The Board of Supervisors placed Measure U on the ballot, and it needs a majority of the vote to pass.
The published measure documents of San Mateo County do not include a cost estimate for the measure.
No argument against Measure U appears in the published measure documents of San Mateo County, only the supporters' argument.
Supporters say the board has overridden citizen members of a redistricting commission, and a truly independent one keeps districts competitive.
A yes vote means the charter would be amended so an independent redistricting commission draws the districts.
A no vote means the charter would not be amended, and the board keeps drawing the districts.
