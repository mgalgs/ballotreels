Measure V asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $7.3 million in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
Measure V needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would build a gymnasium for school and community use, upgrade classrooms, repair roofs and building systems, and add safety improvements.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would check the spending.
The district's tax statement estimates an average tax of $28.25 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $30, through 2060-2061.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $16.1 million.
No argument against Measure V appears among the filings that the county elections office has posted for this election.
Supporters say facilities are over 100 years old, and the district is among the few without a school gym.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue $7.3 million in bonds, repaid by a yearly property tax.
A no vote does not authorize the district to issue the bonds.
