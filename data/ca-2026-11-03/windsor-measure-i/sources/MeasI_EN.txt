    Town Attorney’s Impartial Analysis of Measure I
 The Town Council of the Town of Windsor has placed Measure I on the
 November 3, 2026, ballot to ask the Town’s voters to approve a one
 percent (1%) local transactions and use (sales) tax. If approved, the tax
 would add $1 to the price of an item that costs $100.
 Currently, the tax on retail sales in Windsor is 9.25% of the purchase
 price. Of the 9.25%, the Town directly receives 1%, with the remaining
 8.25% going to the State, Sonoma County, the Sonoma County
 Transportation Authority, and SMART. The Town is the only municipality
 in Sonoma County that has not previously implemented a local
 transactions and use tax.
 Measure I would authorize a 1% transactions and use tax, which would
 increase the total sales tax rate in Windsor to 10.25% (subject to the
 adoption of other countywide taxes). The Town’s portion would increase
 from 1% to 2%.
 According to the Council Resolution placing Measure I on the ballot and
 the report prepared by Town staff, the tax would provide approximately           Continue to next page for arguments
 $5.7 million annually for the Town’s general fund, which could be used
 for services such as repairing potholes and maintaining streets,
 neighborhood police patrols, emergency response, traffic enforcement,
 preserving parks, community events, disaster preparedness, and fire
 prevention. Because Measure I does not limit the use of tax revenue, it
 is a “general tax,” not a “special tax” that restricts the funds to specific
 purposes. Therefore, the Town may use the funds for any legitimate
 governmental purpose.
 The Measure requires the establishment of an independent Citizens’
 Oversight Commission that will review and report on the revenue and
 expenditure of funds collected under the tax “to ensure compliance with
 the public safety, infrastructure, and local service priorities approved by
 voters.” The Measure also requires the Town’s independent auditors to
 prepare a publicly available report annually on the revenue and
 expenditure of funds generated from the tax.
 If Measure I is approved, the tax would continue until ended by Town of
 Windsor voters.
 Like other sales taxes, retailers collect the tax at the time of sale and
 remit the funds to the California Department of Tax and Fee
 Administration (“CDTFA”), which administers the tax. The proposed tax
 would be collected at the same time and in the same manner as existing
 sales taxes. The CDTFA then transmits the funds collected to the Town.
 A “Yes” vote is a vote to approve a one percent (1%) sales tax in Windsor.
 A “No” vote is a vote against the tax. Measure I will pass if it receives a
 simple majority of “Yes” votes.
 The above statement is an impartial analysis of Measure I. If you desire
 a copy of the Ordinance or Measure, please call the Town Clerk at (707)
 838-5315, and a copy will be mailed at no cost to you.

2-6-210      AMENDMENTS.            All amendments subsequent to the
effective date of this ordinance to Part 1 of Division 2 of the Revenue and
Taxation Code relating to sales and use taxes and which are not
inconsistent with Part 1.6 and Part 1.7 of Division 2 of the Revenue and
Taxation Code, and all amendments to Part 1.6 and Part 1.7 of Division 2
of the Revenue and Taxation Code, shall automatically become a part of
this ordinance, provided however, that no such amendment shall operate
so as to affect the rate of tax imposed by this ordinance.
2-6-220       ENJOINING COLLECTION FORBIDDEN.                 No injunction
or writ of mandate or other legal or equitable process shall issue in any
suit, action or proceeding in any court against the State or the Town, or
against any officer of the State or the Town, to prevent or enjoin the
collection under this ordinance, or Part 1.6 of Division 2 of the Revenue
and Taxation Code, of any tax or any amount of tax required to be
collected.
2-6-230     AMENDMENTS BY TOWN COUNCIL.                  Chapter 2-6 of
the Windsor Municipal Code may be repealed or amended by the City
Council without a vote of the People. However, as required by Article XIII
C of the California Constitution, voter approval is required for any
amendment of any provision that would increase the tax rate, revise the
methodology for calculating the tax such that a tax increase would result,
or impose the tax on transactions and uses not previously subject to the

tax (unless such amendment occurs automatically by operation of
California law). The People of the Town of Windsor affirm that the
following actions shall not constitute an increase of the rate of a tax:
             A.      The restoration of the rate of the tax to a rate that is
no higher than that set by this Ordinance if the City Council has acted to
reduce the rate of the tax; and
              B. The collection of the tax imposed by this Ordinance, even
if the City had for some period of time failed to collect the tax.
2-6-240      CITIZENS' OVERSIGHT COMMISSION. Prior to the
Operative Date of this ordinance, the Town Council shall establish and
appoint an independent Citizens' Oversight Commission. The purpose of
the Committee shall be to review and report on the revenue and
expenditure of funds collected under this ordinance to ensure compliance
with the public safety, infrastructure, and local service priorities approved
by voters.
2-6-250       ANNUAL INDEPENDENT AUDIT. The Town's independent
external auditors shall prepare an annual audit report on the revenues
generated by this tax and the expenditures of those funds. This audit
report shall be presented publicly to the Town Council and made available
to the public in conjunction with the Citizens' Oversight Committee report.
