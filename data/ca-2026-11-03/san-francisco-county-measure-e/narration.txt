San Francisco Proposition E asks voters whether to give the City Administrator more authority over city purchasing and contracts.
Today, the City Administrator handles most city purchasing, but the Board of Supervisors sets the rules for choosing contractors.
The board must approve contracts that cost the city $10 million or more.
Proposition E would raise the approval threshold to $25 million, and let only the City Administrator propose purchasing rules, which the board and mayor could reject but not amend.
The City Administrator would set purchasing rules for all departments, and serve a ten-year term instead of five.
The City Controller says Proposition E would have a minimal impact on the cost of government, at a level that cannot be determined.
Opponents say Proposition E concentrates power and weakens checks and balances, raising the threshold for supervisors to approve major contracts.
Supporters say Proposition E slashes waste in contracting, with transparent standards so contracts go to whoever is best for the job.
A yes vote means you want to give the City Administrator more authority, and make these changes.
A no vote means you do not want to make these changes.
