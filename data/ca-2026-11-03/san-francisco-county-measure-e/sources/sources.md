## Ballot question (p. 96)

> Shall the City amend the Charter to give the City Administrator more authority by centralizing the City's purchasing rules and processes, changing how the City approves purchasing rules, changing when contracts and leases require Board of Supervisors approval, and extending the City Administrator's term?

## Digest by the Ballot Simplification Council (pp. 96–97)

**The Way It Is Now** (p. 96):
> The City Administrator's Office is responsible for most of the City's purchasing. However, authority for the City's purchasing and technology is shared among the City Administrator's Office, the Board of Supervisors, the Mayor, and some City departments.

> • Board Approval of Various Agreements: With some exceptions, the City's Charter requires the Board to approve:
> • Contracts and leases that bring in $1,000,000 or more in revenue to the City, and
> • Contracts that cost the City $10,000,000 or more.

> • City Administrator's Term: The Mayor appoints the City Administrator to a five-year term, subject to Board approval.

**The Proposal** (pp. 96–97):
> Proposition E would:
> • Rules Regulating Purchasing: Give the City Administrator exclusive authority to propose ordinances that change how the City selects contractors and enters into contracts for goods, services, construction, and grants (Purchasing). The Board and Mayor may not amend the ordinances, but they can:
> • Approve or reject the ordinances within 60 days, or
> • If no action is taken within 60 days, the ordinances would be approved.
>
> Proposition E does not change the current Board authority for Purchasing ordinances that mainly address wages, workplace health and safety, utilization of local business, or labor agreements.
> • Board Approval of Various Agreements: Increase the dollar amount requiring Board approval to:
> • Contracts and leases that bring in $4,500,000 or more in revenue to the City,
> • Contracts that cost the City $25,000,000 or more.
>
> Starting in 2032, the City's Controller would adjust these dollar amounts every five years for inflation.
>
> Proposition E would require Board approval for major amendments to these agreements.
> • City Administrator's Term: Increase the City Administrator's term from five to ten years.
> • City Administrator's Authority: The City Administrator would set Purchasing rules and processes for all goods, services, construction, and grants that all City departments must follow, except as limited by other laws. Most departments would now be subject to the City Administrator's authority regarding technology policies and the disposal of City personal property. These rules would override those departments' conflicting rules and policies.

**A "YES" Vote Means** (p. 97):
> If you vote "yes," you want to give the City Administrator more authority by centralizing the City's Purchasing rules and processes, changing how the City approves Purchasing rules, changing when contracts and leases require Board approval, and extending the City Administrator's term.

**A "NO" Vote Means** (p. 97):
> If you vote "no," you do not want to make these changes.

## Controller's Statement on "E" (p. 97)

> City Controller Greg Wagner has issued the following statement on the fiscal impact of Proposition E:
> Should the proposed Charter amendment be approved by the voters, in my opinion, it would have a minimal impact on the cost of government, although at a level that cannot be determined at this time.

Dollar figures:
> ... (5) increase the Board of Supervisors' approval threshold from $1 million to $4.5 million for revenue generating contracts and real property lease agreements; and (6) increase in the Board of Supervisors' approval threshold from $10 million to $25 million for expenditure contracts.

> The proposed amendment establishes a more unified City procurement approach, which may result in reduced costs for the purchase of goods or services and staff time savings in the long run.

## How "E" Got on the Ballot (p. 97)

> On July 23, 2026, the Department of Elections certified that the initiative petition calling for Proposition E to be placed on the ballot had a sufficient number of valid signatures to qualify the measure for the ballot.
> 53,076 signatures were required to place an initiative Charter Amendment on the ballot. This number is equal to 10% of the registered voters at the time a "Notice of Intent to Circulate Petition" was published. A random check of the signatures submitted by the proponents of the initiative petition prior to the July 6, 2026, submission deadline showed that the total number of valid signatures was greater than the number required.

**Rebuttal to Proponent's Argument** (p. 98). Signer: "Larry S. Marso, Esq." ("Mr. Marso, a Nob Hill resident, was ballot opponent of the 2024 $400 million MUNI bond—rejected by voters in a tremendous victory for taxpayers!").

**Opponent's Argument** (p. 99). Signer: "Larry S. Marso, Esq."
> One measure expands City Administrator authority, raising the dollar threshold for Supervisor approval of major contracts.

> Most of what Lurie claims these changes would deliver could be achieved through legislation and regular order ...

**Rebuttal to Opponent's Argument** (p. 99). Signers: Mayor Daniel Lurie; Board of Supervisors President Rafael Mandelman; Former Controller Ben Rosenfield.
> Prop E empowers a professional City Administrator to set clear, transparent rules for who gets city contracts.
