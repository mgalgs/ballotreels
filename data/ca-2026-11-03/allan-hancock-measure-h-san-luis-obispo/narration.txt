Measure H asks the voters of this community college district whether to approve $290 million in college bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them from property taxes, on top of the taxes owners pay now.
Measure H needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would go to listed projects, such as upgrading aging classrooms, labs and career training facilities, fixing roofs and plumbing, and removing asbestos.
The district's tax rate statement estimates a tax of $19 per $100,000 of assessed value, on average and at most, through 2067-2068.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $618 million.
Supporters say two in five local high school graduates rely on the college, which trains nurses, firefighters and skilled workers.
No argument against Measure H was filed, so the county voter guide prints only the argument in favor of the measure.
A yes vote is in favor of letting the district issue up to $290 million in bonds.
A no vote is against letting the district issue the bonds.
