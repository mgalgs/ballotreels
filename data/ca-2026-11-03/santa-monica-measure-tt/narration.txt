Measure TT asks the voters of this city whether to change how the hotel tax applies to rooms that companies book for long stays.
Today, the hotel tax is 15 percent of the room charge at hotels, and 17 percent at home-shares.
The tax applies to stays of 30 days or less, and companies have asked whether room blocks booked for longer are taxed.
The City Council says the measure clarifies the tax, and puts it to voters to avoid doubt about enforcing it.
The tax rates would stay the same.
The Council could declare major event periods, like the 2028 Olympics, when rooms companies book for over 30 days would be taxed.
During those periods, the Council could set the rate on rooms booked by companies anywhere from 15 percent down to zero.
A refund of the tax on a long stay would require the same person to stay in the room at least 31 days in a row.
The official list of measures from the county does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
The official county list of measures for this election says that no argument against Measure TT was submitted, by any group or person.
A local firefighters union, a former mayor and three other people filed an argument for Measure TT; its text was not gathered.
A yes vote adopts these changes to the hotel tax.
A no vote leaves the hotel tax rules as they are.
