Measure Z asks voters in one area of this high school district whether to approve $129 million in school bonds.
Only voters in that area vote, and a tax on property there would repay the bonds.
Measure Z needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would modernize classrooms, make safety improvements, replace leaky roofs, and renovate plumbing.
The money could not pay salaries, and yearly audits and a citizens' committee would oversee the spending.
The district's tax statement estimates an average tax of $29.27 per $100,000 of assessed value each year, through 2062-2063.
The statement puts the total to repay, with interest, at about $281,756,000.
Supporters say many classrooms and buildings no longer meet modern standards, and passing qualifies the district for state matching funds.
No argument against Measure Z appears among the documents that the county elections office has posted for this election.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue up to $129 million in bonds, repaid by a property tax in that area.
A no vote is against authorizing the district to issue the bonds.
