Public Comment Process:

The Board of Education provides an opportunity for members of the public to directly address the legislative body on any item on the agenda or topic of interest to the public that is within the subject matter jurisdiction of the Board. In order to discuss District business in an orderly manner, the public is asked to comply with the procedures listed below. Per Board Bylaw 9323, public comment is limited to three (3) minutes per speaker. The total time allotted to any one topic is 20 minutes. As a reminder, the Board requests but does not require public speakers to identify themselves. Per the Brown Act, no action may be taken, and no discussion held on non-agenda items raised during public comment. The Board may ask for clarification and/or refer the item to staff.

 

In-Person Public Comment:

In-person: Please fill out a Speaker Card available on the back counter of the Board Room and return it to the Executive Secretary to the Superintendent before the start of the meeting.

- Speakers to agenda items will be called upon to address the Board after the presentation of that item.

- Speakers to non-agenda items will be called upon to address the Board during public comment on non-agenda items.

 

Online Submission Public Comment:

Online Submissions: Please visit the CUSD website to access the Public Comment Google Form provided along with the links to the agenda and Zoom live stream. Online submissions for public comment must be submitted by 4:00 p.m. the day of the Board meeting. A copy of the public comments will be distributed to the Board of Education and posted on the CUSD website prior to the start of the Board Meeting.

      15.01

      Public Hearing in Connection with the Local Control Accountability Plan (LCAP)

      FOCUS AREA(S): THE CUSD COMMITMENT - ALL FOCUS AREAS ARE APPLICABLE

      SUBMITTED BY: Julie Olesniewicz, Ed.D., Assistant Superintendent,

      Educational Services

      BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION: In 2014, California Assembly Bill 97 (AB 97) dictated a new funding formula for California school districts called the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF). One goal of AB 97 is to improve academic outcomes by providing more money to school districts that serve high-needs students. Another goal is to give local school districts and stakeholders more authority to determine how best to spend education dollars. Under AB 97, all school districts and charter schools are required to prepare a Local Control Accountability Plan (LCAP), which describes how they intend to meet annual goals for all pupils along with specific activities to address state and local priorities. The LCAP plan is to be developed with input from community stakeholder groups. Together with all other districts in the state, the Claremont Unified School Board has to hold a public hearing and adopt an LCAP on or before July 1, 2026.

      The LCAP is to be written and developed with feedback and input from community stakeholder groups. In order to meet this expectation, an LCAP Advisory Council and District Parent Advisory Committee were formed consisting of various stakeholders, including parents from every school site. Multiple meetings with the LCAP Advisory Council, District Parent Advisory Committee, District English Language Advisory Council, and student focus groups from El Roble Intermediate School, Claremont High School, and San Antonio High School have provided the District with opportunities to solicit input from all stakeholders. A draft of the amended 2024-2027 LCAP has been posted to the District’s website.

      FINANCIAL IMPACT: All fiscal implications have been included in the 2026-2027 budget.

      eLCAP060926.pdf

      NEW LCAP Changes - 2026-27 UntrackedActions-Services - Executive Summary 060826.pdf

    1.  

      18.01

      Adoption of Resolution No. 23-2026 Resolution of the Board of Education of the Claremont Unified School District Ordering an Election, and Establishing Specifications of the Election Order

      FOCUS AREA(S): THE CUSD COMMITMENT - ALL FOCUS AREAS ARE APPLICABLE

      SUBMITTED BY: James Elsasser, Superintendent of Schools

      BACKGROUND/DISCUSSION: The resolution before the Board (i) calls an election within the Claremont Unified School District (the “District”) for the purpose of approving general obligation bonds, (ii) requests that the Los Angeles County Registrar of Voters to conduct the election on behalf of the District, and (iii) authorizes the preparation of election materials including ballot arguments and tax rate statement, to be included in the ballot pamphlet.

      State law requires the governing board of a school district to order a bond election. The Los Angeles County Registrar of Voters will conduct the election on behalf of the District, including publishing all required notices. This resolution meets the statutory requirements for describing the types of projects to be funded with the proceeds of the bonds, which is included as Exhibit B to the resolution. A 75-word summary of the measure, as it will appear on the ballot, is also included in the resolution as Exhibit A. The resolution also authorizes the preparation and filing of a tax rate statement, which must be included in the ballot pamphlet, describing the anticipated rates of tax throughout the life of the bond issue.

      This election will be called under constitutional and statutory provisions that require fifty-five percent (55%) voter approval, and certain accountability requirements, including annual independent financial and performance audits of how funds are spent, and the formation of a Citizens’ Bond Oversight Committee. Following adoption, the resolution (including the signed tax rate statement) must be delivered to the County Registrar of Voters and the County Board of Supervisors. State law requires that 2/3rd of a school board support the resolution calling an election requiring 55% voter approval. Therefore, at least four (4) Board members must be present and vote “Yes” in order to call the election.

      ATTACHMENT: Resolution 23-2026 Ordering Election Claremont USD.pdf

       

       

       

      To adopt Resolution 23-2026: Resolution of the Claremont Unified School District Ordering an Election and Establishing Specifications of the Election Order.
