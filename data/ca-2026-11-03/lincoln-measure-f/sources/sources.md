## Key facts, quoted

- **Ballot question** (MF1): "Shall the measure to prevent cuts to public safety services (911 emergency/medical response, police/fire protection, qualified police/firefighters); add life-saving equipment for medical emergencies; keep public areas/streets/parks safe and clean; and other general government uses, by establishing a one-cent sales tax, providing $6,000,000 annually with all funds spent locally, until ended by voters, with a citizen oversight committee, and annual audits/public spending disclosure, be adopted?"
- **Placed by / who votes / threshold:** "On June 23, 2026, the City Council of the City of Lincoln ("City"), acting on its own motion, not by voter petition, voted to place Measure F on the ballot for the November 3, 2026, general municipal election. If approved by a majority of City voters (50% + 1), this measure would authorize an additional one cent (1¢) transactions and use tax within the City."
- **Rate:** "The City's current sales tax rate is 7.250%. If the measure passes, the new sales tax rate would be 8.250%."
- **Revenue:** "It is estimated that the proposed sales tax will provide an additional Six Million Dollars ($6,000,000) in annual local funding. The sales tax revenue would remain in the City and would not be shared with the State or County."
- **General tax:** "This sales tax is a "general tax", meaning that revenues raised from the tax would go into the City's general fund to pay for any lawful City program, service, or improvement."
- **What sales tax covers:** "Sales taxes do not apply to many foods and groceries, prescription medicine, utilities, or payment for services."
- **Oversight:** "The measure would require an annual audit. The measure would also require the City Council to establish a five-member Citizen Oversight Committee to annually review expenditure of tax revenues and prepare an annual accountability report".
- **Yes/No:** "A "yes" vote on Measure F will authorize the one cent (1¢) sales tax." / "A "no" vote on Measure F will not authorize the one cent (1¢) sales tax."
- **Analysis signed:** "/s/ Doug White, Interim City Attorney".
- **Argument in favor signers** (MF2): Evan Faddis, Resident, Lincoln Firefighter; Antonio Virga, Resident, Lincoln Police Officer; Kris Knutson, Resident, Lincoln Community Foundation President; Victoria Borst, Executive Assistant, Chamber of Commerce.
- **Argument against** (MF2): "NO ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE F WAS SUBMITTED".
