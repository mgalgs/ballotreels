Measure W asks the voters of this city whether to let the city sell the water system it owns and runs.
State law says a city must hold a public hearing and get its voters' approval before selling its water system.
The City Council held that hearing in June, and voted to ask for that approval.
Today, the city system serves about one-third of residents, and the Golden State Water Company serves the other two-thirds.
Golden State would get the whole system, with its water rights, and serve every resident.
Rates would stay at current levels until January 2028; after that, the state utilities commission must approve any increase.
The commission would first review the sale, and could approve it, deny it, or add conditions.
The impartial analysis says Golden State would pay the city $41 million, for any public purpose.
The official county list of measures for this election says that no argument for Measure W was submitted, by any group or person.
The official county list of measures for this election says that no argument against Measure W was submitted, by any group or person.
A yes vote would permit the city to sell its water system to Golden State.
A no vote would prohibit the sale, unless voters later approve a new and different proposal.
