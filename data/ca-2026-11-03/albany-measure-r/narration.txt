City of Albany Measure R asks Albany voters whether to raise the business license tax and base it on what a business takes in.
Today, businesses in Albany pay a yearly license tax set at a flat rate or by their average number of employees.
The money goes to the General Fund and may pay for any city expense.
Under Measure R, a business taking in up to $200,000 a year would pay a flat $50 or $200.
Above $200,000, retail businesses would pay $1 for each $1,000 they take in, and most other businesses $5.
Residential landlords would pay $120 per rental unit, and Measure R needs a majority of Albany voters to pass.
The City Attorney's analysis estimates the new rates would raise an additional $600,000 a year, starting in 2027.
Supporters say the current tax charges many small businesses as much as larger ones, and Measure R would lower it for most businesses.
Opponents say a tax on all revenue ignores costs like rent and payroll, so thin-margin businesses would pay a much higher tax.
A yes vote approves these changes to the business license tax.
A no vote does not approve these changes to the business license tax.
