Measure S asks the voters of this city whether to continue an existing 1 percent sales tax for street repair for 8 more years.
The city attorney's impartial analysis says voters first approved the tax in 2014, and extended it in 2018.
Today the tax is set to expire on March 31, 2027, unless voters extend it.
Measure S would keep the tax at the same rate until March 31, 2035.
As a special tax, its money could be spent only on streets, sidewalks, storm drains, and access improvements like curb ramps.
Measure S needs two-thirds of the vote to pass.
The ballot question says the measure renews the tax without increasing tax rates, providing $12 million a year.
Supporters say preserving the progress with ongoing investment is five times cheaper than rebuilding later, and that most of the tax would be paid by non-residents.
Opponents say homeowners must pay to fix the public sidewalks beside their homes while paying a tax for sidewalk repairs, and that sidewalk spending is down.
A yes vote continues the 1 percent sales tax for 8 more years.
A no vote does not extend the tax, which would no longer be collected after March 31, 2027.
