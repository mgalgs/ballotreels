MEASURE S SAYS IT WILL FIX SIDEWALKS. WHICH ONES?

In 2018, Monterey voters approved Measure S after the official ballot analysis promised the tax would be used "exclusively" for the rehabilitation, repair and improvement of streets, sidewalks, storm drains and ADA improvements.

We paid the tax. Then City Hall moved the goalposts.

In late 2023, the City was issuing notices requiring homeowners to repair adjoining public sidewalks, with residents responsible for hiring contractors, obtaining City permits and paying City fees.

When City Hall paints an orange line and requires a repair, homeowners must personally pay to fix the adjoining public sidewalk — while continuing to pay a tax that funds sidewalk repairs.

Effective July 1, 2026, the City's Master Fee Schedule lists a new $389 "Sidewalk Maintenance and Repairs" fee. If a repair encompasses a driveway apron, the City says the $1,009 "Driveway or Sidewalk Encroachment Fee" applies — up 246% from $291.26 — plus $404 for traffic control: $1,413 before construction costs.

Meanwhile, the latest Measure P/S Oversight report shows a $27.5 million fund balance through June 15, of which the City now says $9.32 million was appropriated or encumbered, leaving approximately $18.26 million not appropriated. Sidewalk spending remains down year-over-year: $233,846 — 42% below the comparable prior-year period.

Now City Hall wants another eight years of this one-cent tax, projected to collect $96 million. The City Attorney's impartial analysis again says Measure S funds the repair and improvement of streets, sidewalks, storm drains and ADA improvements.

But which sidewalks? The analysis doesn't say. When homeowners are required to repair adjoining public sidewalks, they must pay the bill themselves.

Before approving another eight years, taxpayers deserve to know where Measure S sidewalk funding ends and their own sidewalk bill begins.

VOTE NO ON MEASURE S.

[signed] 8/17/26
