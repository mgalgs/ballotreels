San Diego County Measure B asks county voters about a half-cent sales tax for health and safety services.
Measure B is a citizen initiative, and the guide says it needs a simple majority to pass.
The tax would apply countywide and would last until voters end it.
The money would go to Tijuana River sewage cleanup, to public safety and wildfire prevention, and to health care, child care and nutrition.
An oversight committee would audit the spending, and all spending would be made public.
The county attorney's impartial analysis estimates the tax would raise between $400 million and $450 million a year.
Opponents say Measure B raises average household taxes by hundreds of dollars yearly, without a sunset clause.
Supporters say Measure B prevents cuts: slower emergency response, fewer hospital staff, and families without health care.
A yes vote would impose the half-cent sales tax for these services.
A no vote would reject the half-cent sales tax for these services.
