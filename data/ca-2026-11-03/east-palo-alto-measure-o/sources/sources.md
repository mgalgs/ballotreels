# Measure O — City of East Palo Alto (general obligation bond, $125,000,000)

- **Jurisdiction:** City of East Palo Alto.
- **Kind:** General obligation bond, up to $125,000,000, for a civic center (library, City Hall, police facility with Emergency Command Center), a recreational/sports park, and water and stormwater projects.
- **Who votes:** the voters of the City of East Palo Alto. Resolution No. 57-2026, Sec. 1 (resolution-full-text.pdf p. 2): "The City Council hereby orders submitted to the voters at an election to be held in the City on November 3, 2026, a measure ... authorizing the sale of general obligation bonds by the City to finance the costs of Improvements". Ordinance Sec. 3 (p. 8): the Measure is "submitted to the qualified voters of the City".
- **Vote threshold:** two-thirds. Resolution Sec. 2 (p. 2): "The Measure shall not take effect unless approved by a two-thirds vote of the voters voting on the question." Ordinance Sec. 9 (p. 10): bonds may be issued "if two-thirds of all qualified voters voting on the proposition set forth above vote in favor thereof."
- **Placed by:** the City Council (resolution of necessity adopted July 7, 2026 "by a two-thirds vote of all its members", recital p. 1).
- **Election:** November 3, 2026.

## Ballot question

> East Palo Alto Community/Water/Public Safety Infrastructure Measure. Shall the measure constructing a Library, Emergency Command Center, civic center, youth sports park; replacing deteriorating water pipes to provide safe/ clean drinking water; repairing storm drains to prevent flooding; addressing earthquake safety, by authorizing East Palo Alto to issue $125,000,000 in general obligation bonds, with annual levies averaging $105.26 per $100,000 of assessed value (raising approximately $8,940,000 annually) while bonds are outstanding, with independent audits/ public disclosure, be adopted?

(resolution-full-text.pdf p. 2, Sec. 1; the OCR layer reads "CommunityNVater", read from the page as "Community/Water". The resolution says the measure will appear "in substantially the following form", and spaces "safe/ clean" and "audits/ public" as shown.)

## Full text (resolution, ordinance and Measure)

- **Resolution No. 57-2026**, City Council of the City of East Palo Alto, "PASSED AND ADOPTED this 21st day of July 2026". AYES: Abrica, Barragan, Dinan, Lincoln, Romero. NOES, ABSENT, ABSTAIN: blank. Signed Webster Lincoln, Mayor; James Colin, City Clerk; approved as to form for John D. Lê, City Attorney. Filed with the Chief Elections Officer July 24, 2026 (p. 5).
- Recital (p. 2): "on the date hereof, the City Council adopted, by a two-thirds vote of all its members, an ordinance ... ordering the submission of a proposition of incurring a bonded indebtedness to the qualified voters of the City".
- **Ordinance No. XX-2026** (pp. 6-12; number and vote tally left blank on this copy, p. 12). Recital (p. 6): "the City estimates the cost of the improvements to total $205,000,000", of which about $20,000,000 is for the police facility, $120,000,000 for the library, City Hall and civic facilities, $30,000,000 for recreation and parks, and $35,000,000 for water and stormwater (pp. 6-7).
- Recitals (p. 7): owners of about four acres and about six acres on Pulgas Avenue have indicated an intent to donate them "if the issuance of the Bonds is approved by two-thirds of all voters voting on the proposition"; the City plans to fund the remaining "$176,000,000" from the Bonds ($125,000,000), "funds on hand in the City's General Fund ($20,000,000 expected to be appropriated), private cash donations ($21,000,000 goal), and local, state and federal grants ($10,000,000 goal)"; "no assurance can be given that any of said donations or grants will be received by the City".
- **Exhibit A, the Measure** (pp. 13-15). Sec. 4 (p. 15): "The aggregate principal amount of the Bonds to be issued is not to exceed $125,000,000." Sec. 5 (p. 15): "The maximum rate of interest to be paid on the Bonds shall be 12% per annum."
- Exhibit A Sec. 6 (p. 15), accountability: "The proceeds of the Bonds will be deposited into a separate account to be created and held by the City"; the Director of Finance files an annual report; "All expenditures also will be subject to an annual independent financial audit to confirm that Bond expenditures are consistent with the intent of this Measure."
- Exhibit A Sec. 2 (p. 15): "The City Council cannot guarantee that the Bonds will provide sufficient funds to allow completion of all needed Improvements."

## Impartial analysis

"CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE O", signed John D. Lê, City Attorney (impartial-analysis.pdf).

- "Measure O would authorize the City to issue up to $125,000,000 in general obligation bonds to finance the acquisition, construction, and improvement of the following".
- "The bonds would be payable from ad valorem taxes levied on taxable property in the City. The maximum interest rate to be paid on the bonds shall not exceed 12%, though the prevailing interest rates could be less."
- "The City estimates that the highest tax rate that would be required to be levied is approximately $114.41 per $100,000 of assessed value, which the City projects would begin to apply in fiscal year 2028-2029. The City also estimates that the average annual tax rate that would be required to be levied is $105.26 per $100,000 of assessed value, with the final fiscal year in which the tax would be collected being 2057-2058."
- "The City also estimates that the total debt service, including the principal and interest, that would be required to be repaid if all the bonds are issued and sold is $268,205,658."
- "This measure was placed on the ballot by the City Council of the City of East Palo Alto. It requires a two-thirds vote to pass."
- "Voting "Yes" would mean authorizing the City to issue up to $125,000,000 in general obligation bonds for use on the public improvements stated in the measure and briefly summarized above. Voting "No" would mean the City would not be authorized to issue the bonds."

## Tax rate statement / fiscal

No tax rate statement is among ACRE's files for Measure O. Ordinance Sec. 15 (p. 11) refers to "the Tax Rate Statement required by Section 9401 of the California Elections Code presented at this meeting and on file with the City Clerk", and authorizes the City Manager and Director of Finance "to file the tax rate statement with the San Mateo County Registrar of Voters"; the statement itself is not in the file. Its figures appear in the impartial analysis (quoted above): highest rate "approximately $114.41 per $100,000 of assessed value" from FY 2028-2029; average "$105.26 per $100,000"; last year of collection FY 2057-2058; total debt service "$268,205,658". The ballot question gives "approximately $8,940,000 annually".

## Notes

- The argument in favor was written before the letter was assigned ("Measure "__"") and once says "Yes on "X""; the argument against says "Measure __". The rebuttal says "Measure O". The printed guide presumably substitutes "O"; unconfirmed, since the per-voter sample ballot pamphlet was not checked.
- The argument in favor speaks of "Independent OVERSIGHT"; the Measure text (Exhibit A Sec. 6) provides for a separate account, an annual Director of Finance report and an annual independent audit, and does not create an oversight committee.
- The argument against's "$250,000,000 or more" is its own estimate; the City's estimate of total debt service is $268,205,658 (impartial analysis).
