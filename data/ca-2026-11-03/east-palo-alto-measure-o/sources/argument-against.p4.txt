Argument Against
East Palo Alto $125,000,000 Bond Issue, Measure __

When voters are asked to go into debt with bond measures like Measure __, what are they saying? They are admitting that everything in the current budget is more important than the projects listed in this ballot measure.

Budgets reflect priorities. Actions speak louder than words: every tax dollar spent today is going to something they consider a higher priority than “replacing deteriorating water pipes to provide safe/ clean drinking water” and “constructing a Library.”

Do you agree?

A bond is much like a mortgage. When you buy a home, truth in lending laws mandate that banks must tell the real cost (the loan amount AND 30 years of interest payments).

$125,000,000 could cost us $250,000,000 or more (depending on the interests’ rates).

Shouldn’t consumer protection laws apply to bond debt too? We deserve to know the full truth about Measure __. We don’t even know when this debt will begin, nor what market interest rate will be, when it does.

Shouldn’t consumer protection laws apply to bond measures? Taxpayers deserve all the full facts. We do not know when this debt will begin, or what the market interest rate will be at that time.

Which is more important to you?

1. Paying for essential safety repairs now, by using dollars already in the budget\.

2. Sending new tax dollars -- in the form of principal and interest payments -- to big banks, investment brokers, and other wealthy people, for use in tax shelters.

Is #2 really the best use of our taxes?

If you value clean drinking water more than funding tax shelters with tax free bonds, vote NO on Measure __.

If you value clean water more than making interest payments for 25 years, vote NO on Measure __.
