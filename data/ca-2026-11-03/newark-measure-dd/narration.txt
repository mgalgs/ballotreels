City of Newark Measure DD asks voters in the city whether to replace the business license tax with a new one.
Today, Newark sorts businesses into many categories, taxed by a flat amount, by employees, by vehicles, by payroll or by gross receipts.
Measure DD would put businesses in seven categories, each taxed at a share of its gross receipts, up to 0.3 percent.
The minimum tax would drop from $39 to $25 on the first $100,000 of receipts, a cut for some businesses.
As a general tax with no end date, its money could go to any legitimate city purpose, and it needs a simple majority to pass.
The City Attorney's impartial analysis says Measure DD would generate about $6.6 million a year.
Supporters say Measure DD lowers taxes for most small businesses, which now pay higher rates than large ones, and spares residents.
Opponents say the tax increase would burden struggling local businesses and raise prices, with no guarantee of the promised services.
A yes vote approves the ordinance replacing the business license tax.
A no vote is against adopting the ordinance.
