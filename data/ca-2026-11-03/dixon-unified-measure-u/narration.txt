Measure U asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $49 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them, with interest, from property taxes in the district.
Measure U needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money could replace aging portable classrooms, fix roofs, pipes and heating, and make health and safety improvements to school buildings.
The district would decide which projects come first, and passing the measure does not guarantee every listed project.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $29 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $30, until 2061-2062.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $105 million.
Opponents say the district has not properly maintained its schools, and the oversight committee for its 2016 bond has not done its job.
Supporters say most district schools are 60 to 70 years old, with failing pipes causing leaks, and all the money stays local.
A yes vote means up to $49 million in bonds may be issued for the school projects in the measure.
A no vote means the bonds will not be authorized.
