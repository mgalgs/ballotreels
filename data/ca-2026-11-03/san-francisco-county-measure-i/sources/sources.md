# Proposition I — Changes to Real Property Transfer Tax

- **Jurisdiction:** City and County of San Francisco
- **Kind:** Real property transfer tax ordinance (initiative, "Affordable Housing Guarantee Act"), placed on the ballot by initiative petition
- **Who votes:** all registered San Francisco voters (consolidated city and county; one electorate)
- **Vote threshold** (VIP PDF p. 134): "This measure requires 50%+1 affirmative votes to pass."

## Ballot question (p. 134)

> Shall the City permanently dedicate half of the transfer tax revenues collected when certain real estate with a value or sales price of more than $10,000,000 changes hands, which will continue to be taxed at rates of 5.5% to 6%, to be spent only on affordable housing, including social housing developments and housing assistance, and exempt certain sales from the dedicated portion of the tax, for an estimated annual revenue decrease of approximately $1 million?

## Digest by the Ballot Simplification Council (pp. 134–135)

The digest is printed in two columns; `pdftotext` extracts its pieces out of order, so the order below follows the headings.

**The Way It Is Now** (p. 134):
> The City collects a transfer tax when most real estate changes hands. The tax rates depend on the property's sales price or value and the two highest tax rates are:
> • 5.5% for properties between $10,000,000 and under $25,000,000, and
> • 6% for properties at $25,000,000 or more.
>
> Some real estate transfers are exempt from the tax or are subject to reduced rates.
>
> Revenues generated from the transfer tax may be spent for any governmental purpose.
>
> The Board of Supervisors (Board) may amend, reduce, suspend, or repeal the transfer tax without voter approval. Voters must approve any increase of the transfer tax.
>
> State law limits the total revenue, including tax revenue, the City may spend each year. The voters may approve increases to this limit for up to four years.

**The Proposal** (p. 134):
> Proposition I would dedicate half of the transfer tax revenues when certain real estate with a value or sales price of more than $10,000,000 changes hands, to be spent only on affordable housing, including social housing developments and housing assistance.

> For properties with a sales price or value between $10,000,000 and under $25,000,000:
> • Reduce the current transfer tax from 5.5% to 2.75%
> • Impose a new transfer tax of 2.75%
> For properties with a sales price or value at $25,000,000 or more:
> • Reduce the transfer tax from 6% to 3%
> • Impose a new transfer tax of 3%
> This measure will not increase the tax rate on the transfer of any property above what is charged today.

> The new transfer tax would also exempt the first sale of certain multifamily real estate if it is sold within 5 years of completion of new residential units.

> The City could spend revenue from the new transfer tax only on:
> • At least 60% for affordable housing production, with at least half to social housing developments;
> • At least 25% towards the acquisition or rehabilitation of affordable housing, with at least 60% to acquisition of existing housing units; and
> • At least 10% to tenant stabilization/homeless prevention programs, with at least half to eviction defense and prevention;

> The Board could only change the transfer tax consistent with the purpose of the tax. All other changes would require voter approval.

> Proposition I would increase the City's spending limit for four years.

**A "YES" Vote Means** (p. 135):
> If you vote "yes," you want to dedicate half of the transfer tax revenues when certain real estate with a value or sales price of more than $10,000,000 changes hands, to be spent only on affordable housing, including social housing developments and housing assistance.

**A "NO" Vote Means** (p. 135):
> If you vote "no," you do not want to make these changes.

## Controller's Statement on "I" (p. 135)

> City Controller Greg Wagner has issued the following statement on the fiscal impact of Proposition I:
> Should the proposed ordinance be approved by the voters, in my opinion, it would represent a moderate annual decrease of approximately $1 million to the City's overall revenues starting in Fiscal Year (FY) 2027-28. However, the ordinance would also shift $120 million per year of existing revenue from the more flexible General Fund to a special revenue fund dedicated to affordable housing programming and support. Because the ordinance would remove existing revenue from the General Fund, it would increase the City's projected General Fund deficit by $120 million annually.

Rates and dollar figures:
> The ordinance would reduce the Real Property Transfer Tax rate on transfers of properties with consideration or value from $10 million to under $25 million from 5.5% to 2.75%, and on transfers of properties with consideration or value of $25 million or more from 6% to 3%.

> The House SF Tax would tax transfers of properties with consideration or value between $10 million and $25 million at a rate of 2.75%, and transfers of properties with consideration or value of $25 million or more at 3%. The ordinance also includes a new exemption from the House SF Tax for certain recently constructed multi-family housing properties, reducing the collected revenue by approximately $1 million annually, so the proposed House SF Fund would generate approximately $119 million annually.

> The House SF special revenue fund and tax would represent the City's 24th voter-mandated funding requirement that limits the City's budget flexibility.

> ... it would limit the City's fiscal flexibility and increase the City's General Fund deficit, most recently projected at $741.7 million and $1.1 billion in FY 2028-29 and FY 2029-30, respectively.

> Note that the proposed measure would change the duties of the Controller's Office, which has prepared this statement.

## How "I" Got on the Ballot (p. 135)

> On July 23, 2026, the Department of Elections certified that the initiative petition calling for Proposition I to be placed on the ballot had a sufficient number of valid signatures to qualify the measure for the ballot.
> 10,644 signatures were required to place an initiative ordinance on the ballot. This number is equal to 2% of the registered voters at the time a "Notice of Intent to Circulate Petition" was published. A random check of the signatures submitted by the proponents of the initiative petition prior to the July 6, 2026, submission deadline showed that the total number of valid signatures was greater than the number required.

## Tax rates in the legal text (p. 236)

Sec. 1102 (existing tax, amended). The printed text shows the old and new rates side by side; the old rate is in strike-through italics, which plain text cannot show:
> (e) at least $10,000,000 and less than $25,000,000, a tax at the rate of $27.50 $13.75 for each $500 or fractional part thereof ... or (f) at least $25,000,000, a tax at the rate of $30 $15 for each $500 or fractional part thereof ...

Sec. 1102.3 HOUSE SF TAX IMPOSED (new):
> In addition to and separate from any tax imposed under Section 1102, there is hereby imposed a tax known as the "House SF Tax" ... (a) exceeds $10,000,000 and less than $25,000,000, a tax at the rate of $13.75 for each $500 or fractional part thereof ... or (b) at least $25,000,000, a tax at the rate of $15 for each $500 or fractional part thereof ...

($13.75 per $500 is 2.75%; $15 per $500 is 3%, matching the digest and Controller's Statement.)

## Arguments (pp. 136–137)

Every argument page carries: "Arguments are the opinions of the authors and have not been checked for accuracy by any official agency." and "Arguments are printed as submitted. Spelling and grammatical errors have not been corrected."

**Proponent's Argument** (p. 136). Signers: Affordable Housing Alliance; San Francisco Tenants Union; Small Business Forward; United Educators of San Francisco; Service Employees International Union SEIU 1021; Council of Community Housing Coalitions; Faith in Action Bay Area; Senior and Disability Action; Anti Displacement Coalition; Community Tenants Association; Housing Rights Committee; Tenants Together; Democratic Socialist of America - San Francisco; San Franciscans for Social Housing.
> In 2020, voters approved a tax on the sale of properties worth more than $10 million, specifically so that money could fund affordable housing and rent relief.

> But it wasn't guaranteed. In the past two years, none of this revenue has gone to affordable housing.

**Rebuttal to Proponent's Argument** (p. 136). Signer: San Francisco Building and Construction Trades Council.
> Prop I would lock roughly $120 million a year away from the General Fund without requiring that a single new unit of housing be built.

**Opponent's Argument** (p. 137). Signers: Judge Quentin L Kopp; San Francisco Taxpayers Association.
> While affordable housing is an important priority, locking transfer tax revenues into rigid set-asides is fiscal mismanagement that threatens San Francisco's financial stability.

**Rebuttal to Opponent's Argument** (p. 137). Signers: Council of Community Housing Organizations; San Francisco Tenants Union; Small Business Forward; Affordable Housing Alliance; Service Employees International Union SEIU 1021; Senior and Disability Action; SF Anti Displacement Coalition; Housing Rights Committee; Democratic Socialists of America - San Francisco; San Franciscans for Social Housing.
> For the first few years, politicians kept the promise. But over the last two years, not one dollar of this revenue has gone to housing.

## Legal text (pp. 235–241)

Text of the initiative ordinance, amending Article 12-C of the Business and Tax Regulations Code and adding Administrative Code Sec. 10.100-12 ("HOUSE SF FUND"). First lines (p. 235):
> Proposition I
> Affordable Housing Guarantee Act
> Ordinance dedicating revenue from the existing real estate transfer tax on properties valued at $10 million or more exclusively to the protection, preservation, and production of affordable housing in San Francisco. It guarantees that funds cannot be diverted to other uses and creates new accountability requirements for how the money is spent.
