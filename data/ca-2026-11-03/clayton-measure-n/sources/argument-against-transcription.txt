Argument Against Measure N

We know from experience or hearsay (depending on age) that last midcentury, two dimes would buy a gallon of gas or two scoops of ice cream; ten dimes could buy 6 basic hamburgers. If the dimes are silver, they still do. But the digital and paper currencies aren't doing as well.

You may be aware that Japan was bailed out by the U.S. because they are our biggest buyer of Treasurys and need money, but if they sell our debt, it devalues faster.

What does this have to do with this proposed Special Tax? It would rise with inflation. The United States of America is inflating like crazy, and they're just getting started.

The next ten years are likely to be challenging for everyone.

Perhaps the landscaping budget could be funded by sponsorships, voluntary neighborhood workdays, fundraisers.

Also bad: spending public funds to defend against violations of election code.

California Elections Code section 13119(a) requires ballots to use this language:

"Shall the measure (stating the nature thereof) be adopted?"

"The nature thereof" does not mean "why," or "how," or "for whom," or anything other than "what is the nature" of the measure being adopted.

Contra Costa County wrote a ballot question recently. It was ruled "prejudicial and argumentative" in Superior Court. The court ordered offending language removed, including "independent citizens oversight."

Section 9051(e) requires it "shall neither be an argument, nor be likely to create prejudice, for or against the proposed measure."

Clayton's Measure N ballot question lists benefits, not costs like pay-raises; it is not neutral.

If you don't like public funds spent defending violations, the best way to save court costs and stop cheating, is vote "No" every time you see a ballot question containing sales pitches. Save that for the argument.

Sandra Kallander
Chair, Libertarian Party of Contra Costa County
