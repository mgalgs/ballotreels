Measure S asks the voters of this city whether to rewrite the charter section on the Park Commission.
Today the charter sets up a five-member Park Commission, appointed by the City Council to unpaid five-year terms.
The charter also gives the commission and the parks department powers over park fees, funds and property.
The City Council placed Measure S on the ballot, and its question says it affirms the current and previous advisory practice of the commission.
Measure S would keep the commission and its terms, but remove the charter's provisions on park operations, finances and property, leaving them to other city laws.
The commission would advise the council on parks, and its recommendations would not bind the council.
Before acting on city parks, the council would have to receive the commission's recommendation.
Measure S would not itself spend money, sell park land or abolish the commission, and needs a simple majority to pass.
The Local Voter Information Guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
The county voter guide says that no argument for Measure S was filed.
The county voter guide says that no argument against Measure S was filed.
A yes vote is a vote to amend the charter as the impartial analysis describes.
A no vote is a vote to leave the existing charter language unchanged.
