## Facts with quotes

- **What it is:** "The Oroville City Council placed Measure S on the ballot. Measure S would amend Article X, Section 2 of the Oroville City Charter, concerning the Park Commission." (guide p. 73)
- **Ballot label (guide p. 73):** "City of Oroville – Resolution 9465 – Charter Amendment: Park Commission. Shall the City of Oroville Charter be amended to affirm the current and previous practice of the Park Commission performing in an Advisory capacity to the City Council? Yes or No"
- **Current law:** "The Charter currently establishes a five-member Park Commission. Members are appointed by the City Council, serve five-year terms, and receive no compensation. The Charter also assigns specified powers and duties concerning City parks to the Department of Parks and the Park Commission." (guide p. 73)
- **Change:** "Measure S would retain the Commission's five-member composition, appointment by the City Council, five-year terms, and service without compensation. It would replace the remaining provisions with language requiring the Park Commission to advise the City Council on matters concerning the operation and regulation of City parks. Commission recommendations would be advisory and would not bind the City Council. However, before the City Council takes action related to City parks, the measure would require the Council to receive a recommendation from the Commission." (guide p. 73)
- **Limits:** "The measure would not itself appropriate or transfer money, sell or change the use of park property, or abolish the Park Commission." (guide p. 74)
- **Yes/No:** "A "yes" vote is a vote to amend the Charter as described above. A "no" vote is a vote to leave the existing Charter language unchanged." (guide p. 74)
- **Vote needed:** Resolution 9465 §3: "the vote requirement for the measures each to pass are a majority (50%+1) of the votes cast." (The guide's Measure S analysis does not restate the threshold.)
- **Analysis author:** "s/Scott E. Huber, City Attorney, City of Oroville, CA" (guide p. 74)
