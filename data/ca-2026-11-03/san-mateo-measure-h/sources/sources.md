# Measure H — City of San Mateo — Transactions and use (sales) tax, ¼ cent, general tax

- **Jurisdiction:** City of San Mateo.
- **Kind:** ordinance adding a 0.25% transactions and use (sales) tax (on top of the existing 0.25%), general tax, until ended by voters. Placed by the City Council, Resolution No. 84 (2026).
- **Who votes:** City of San Mateo voters. Resolution Sec. 1, PDF p. 2: the Council "calls for an election at which it shall submit to the qualified voters of the city a measure". Sec. 3: the ballot question "shall be submitted to the voters of the City".
- **Threshold:** simple majority. Resolution Sec. 4, PDF p. 3: the taxes are "for general municipal purposes and accordingly the taxes shall be approved upon a majority vote of the qualified voters of the City of San Mateo voting in the election on the Ordinance."

## Ballot question (verbatim)

Resolution Sec. 3, PDF p. 2 (scan):

> To provide funding for San Mateo city services, such as keeping local streets, sidewalks, parks, infrastructure, and community facilities safe, clean, and well-maintained, fixing potholes, providing fire protection, paramedic, crime prevention, and 9-1-1 emergency response, and improvements to traffic safety/traffic management, shall the City of San Mateo's ordinance establishing a one-quarter cent sales tax be adopted, providing 7 million dollars annually for general government use until ended by voters, with independent audits and all funds locally-controlled?

## Full text

- **Resolution No. 84 (2026)**, City Council of the City of San Mateo. PDF p. 4: "RESOLUTION NO. 84 (2026) adopted by the City Council of the City of San Mateo, California, at a regular meeting held on July 20, 2026". AYES: "Council Members Loraine, Fernandez, Diaz Nash, and Newsom"; NOES: "None"; ABSENT: "Council Member Cwirko-Godycki". Sec. 12 (p. 4) appropriates "$158,000" for election costs. Filed with the Chief Elections Officer "JUL 27 2026" (stamp, p. 1).
- Recital, PDF p. 1: "San Mateo Municipal Code (SMMC) Chapter 3.50 currently imposes a Transactions and Use Tax of one quarter percent (0.25%) that expires on March 31, 2048". The new Ch. 3.51 "would increase the Transactions and Use Tax rate by an additional one quarter percent (0.25%) until ended by voters, thereby increasing the total Transactions and Use Tax rate to one half percent (0.50%)".
- Recital, PDF pp. 1-2: the tax "is estimated to generate approximately $7 million annually in locally controlled funding for general governmental purposes".
- Sec. 2, PDF p. 2: "the rate of the sales tax shall be one quarter percent (0.25%) of the gross receipts of any retailer from the sale of all tangible personal property sold at retail in the city".
- Sec. 8, PDF p. 3: the Council authorizes "Deputy Mayor Nicole Fernandez and Council Member Cwirko-Godycki, to file written arguments in favor of the proposition".
- **Exhibit A**, PDF p. 5: "CITY OF SAN MATEO ORDINANCE NO. 2026-__"; Sec. 1: the People "hereby adopt Chapter 3.51 to impose a 0.25 percent transactions and use tax until ended by voters". Sec. 3.51.020: operative on "the first day of the first calendar quarter commencing more than 110 days after the adoption of this Chapter".

## Impartial analysis

`impartial-analysis.pdf`, p. 1: "City Attorney's Impartial Analysis of Measure __", signed (signature redacted) "Prasanna W. Rasiah, City Attorney".

- "The City of San Mateo currently imposes a transactions and use tax, commonly known as a sales tax, of 0.25%. If voters approve Measure __, it would increase this tax from 0.25% to 0.50%."
- "A 0.25% increase amounts to an extra 25 cents per $100 purchased. The additional 0.25% sales tax would remain in effect until voters end it."
- "If voters approve it, the proposed tax is estimated to generate approximately $7 million a year in locally controlled funding."
- "Measure __ would be a general tax, which means that the tax revenue could be spent for any City purpose"
- "Measure __ would require an annual audit and provide for a committee of five members of the public to review and report on receipts and spending of tax proceeds."
- "Measure __ will be adopted if approved by a majority of voters voting on it." "A YES vote approves the measure. A NO vote rejects the measure."

## Tax rate statement / fiscal

No tax rate statement or separate fiscal analysis was found among ACRE's files (none is required for a sales tax). Fiscal figures in the official files: "approximately $7 million annually" (resolution recital, PDF pp. 1-2; ballot question "7 million dollars annually"); the impartial analysis repeats "approximately $7 million a year". The arguments cite different deficit figures ($16.8 million against; $18.2 million in the proponents' rebuttal); neither figure appears in the resolution or the impartial analysis.
