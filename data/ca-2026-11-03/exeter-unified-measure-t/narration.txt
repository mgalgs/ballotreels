Measure T asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $23 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them from a tax on property in the district.
Measure T needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money could build an agricultural shop, renovate science labs, modernize classrooms and restrooms, and repair roofs, plumbing, heating and cooling.
Bond money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would check the spending.
The district's tax statement estimates $38.70 per $100,000 of assessed value each year, through 2060-2061.
The statement says the district expects its total bond tax rate to stay at or below the 2025-2026 rate of $93.20 per $100,000.
The full text says that expectation is not a limit, and that the measure extends the final repayment date of the district's bonds.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $48.4 million.

No argument against Measure T appears among the official documents gathered for this video.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue the bonds.
A no vote does not authorize the district to issue the bonds.
