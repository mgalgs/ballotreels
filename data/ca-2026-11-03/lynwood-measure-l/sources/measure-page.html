The basics

How a Transient Occupancy Tax works

A Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT), sometimes called a “hotel tax,” is paid by guests who stay in hotels, motels, and similar lodging for 30 days or less. It is not a tax on Lynwood residents or local businesses. It is collected by the lodging operator and remitted to the City.

Lynwood is one of the few cities in Los Angeles County that do not levy a TOT today. Visitors who stay here use city services during their stay, such as police and fire response, emergency medical services, streets, and code enforcement. Meanwhile, every neighboring city already collects a lodging tax, at rates ranging from 5% to 12%.

Background

Why a Transient Occupancy Tax?

Why is Measure L on the ballot?

Like many California cities, Lynwood faces rising costs to provide city services. The cost of goods, labor, and contract services has outpaced available revenue, even after steps the City has taken to reduce spending and improve efficiency. The City Council placed Measure L on the November 3, 2026 ballot for voters to decide.

Who would pay it?

Measure L would apply to guests staying in Lynwood hotels and motels, not to residents, property owners, or local businesses. It is collected by lodging operators and remitted to the City. Nearly every neighboring city already levies a similar tax.

General fund

What the revenue could help support

Because it is a general tax, the revenue would go into the General Fund and could help support local services such as:

911 emergency response
Crime, gang & trafficking prevention
Addressing homelessness
Safe, clean parks & public areas
Addressing speeding & unsafe driving
Street & pothole repair

Safeguards

Local control & accountability

  • All revenue stays local, deposited in Lynwood's General Fund and controlled by the City for the benefit of Lynwood residents.
  • An independent financial audit every year, with public reporting posted on the City's website.
  • Exemptions, including:
    • Emergency shelter, interim housing or homelessness-referral stays paid by a government agency, or by a nonprofit under contract with a government agency
    • Stays funded through the Emergency Food and Shelter National Board Program
    • Lynwood residents temporarily displaced from their homes by fire, flood, earthquake or another qualifying emergency
  • The tax takes effect only if approved by a majority of Lynwood voters.
  • The rate is capped at 12%.
  • Online travel companies and booking platforms must also collect and remit the tax.

On the ballot

The ballot question

As it will appear on the ballot

“To maintain 911 emergency response; prevent crime, including gang/juvenile crime and human trafficking; address homelessness; keep parks/public areas safe/clean; address speeding/unsafe driving; repair streets/potholes; support youth/senior programs, other general city services, shall the City of Lynwood ordinance establishing a 12% transient occupancy tax, (paid only by hotel/motel guests), be adopted, providing approximately $751,315 annually until ended by voters, requiring audits/public spending disclosure, all funds locally controlled/benefitting Lynwood residents?”

The vote

How the measure is decided

50% + 1

A simple majority of Lynwood voters is required to approve the measure, as a general tax.

Nov. 3, 2026

General Municipal Election, when Lynwood voters decide.
