## Ballot question and supporters/opponents (verbatim, from the county's final measures list)

> Lynwood Public Safety/Community Protection/City Services Measure To maintain 911 emergency response; prevent crime, including gang/juvenile crime and human trafficking; address homelessness; keep parks/public areas safe/clean; address speeding/unsafe driving; repair streets/potholes; support youth/senior programs, other general city services, shall the City of Lynwood ordinance establishing a 12% transient occupancy tax, (paid only by hotel/motel guests), be adopted, providing approximately $751,315 annually until ended by voters, requiring audits/public spending disclosure, all funds locally controlled/benefitting Lynwood residents?
>
> Supporters: Yolanda Rodriguez Gonzalez, Former Treasurer; Gary Hardie, Board Member; Rene Luna, Business Owner; Aura Quijada
>
> Opponents: None submitted.

Vote threshold: "50% + 1 — A simple majority of Lynwood voters is required to approve the measure, as a general tax" (city's own Measure L page; matches the county's "Majority Vote 50%+1").

## What's present and what's missing

- **Full ordinance text**: present (within `staff-report.pdf`, proposed §6-9 "Transient Occupancy Tax").
- **Resolution placing the measure on the ballot**: present only as a *proposed* resolution text, within the Aug 4 staff-report packet's attachments — not found as a standalone adopted/signed document. The city's own Measure L page has a "Resolution" link that is explicitly commented out in the page's HTML as "document not yet available."
- **Impartial analysis**: missing — as of the Aug 4, 2026 staff report, not yet written (the staff report's own recommendation #5 directs the City Attorney to prepare one *after* that meeting); not found anywhere else.
- **Argument for**: **confirmed filed** — four named supporters in the county's final list, after the filing deadline. The filed text is missing, not found.
- **Argument against**: **confirmed not filed** — the county's official list states "Opponents: None submitted."
- **Rebuttals**: not applicable to either side. California law lets each side rebut only the opposing side's filed argument; since no argument against was filed, there is no argument against for proponents to rebut, and no official opponent authors exist to file a rebuttal to the argument for.
- **Tax rate statement**: not applicable — L is a flat-rate transient occupancy tax, not a property-tax-secured bond measure.
- A separate "Measure L Fact Sheet" PDF linked from the city page (`/DocumentCenter/View/2635/measure-l-fact-sheet`) could not be retrieved — the URL returns a redirect loop (`...?bidId=`) that never resolves, likely requiring a browser session/cookie the CivicPlus document viewer sets via JavaScript.
