Measure L asks the voters of this city whether to tax hotel and motel stays at 12 percent, for general city services.
Today, the city does not tax hotel and motel stays.
Guests staying 30 days or less would pay 12 percent of what they pay for the room.
The tax would apply to the full price, including fees, and online travel companies would collect it too.
Government travelers, emergency shelter stays and residents displaced from their homes by a disaster would be exempt.
The money would go to the general fund for any city purpose, with yearly audits, until voters repeal the tax.
The City Council could lower the rate and raise it again, but never above 12 percent without another vote.
The ballot question estimates about $751,315 a year, which the city staff report puts at approximately 1.69 percent of the general fund.
The official county list of measures for this election says that no argument against Measure L was submitted, by any group or person.
A former treasurer, a board member, a business owner and one other person filed an argument for Measure L; its text was not gathered.
A yes vote adopts the 12 percent hotel tax.
A no vote rejects the 12 percent hotel tax.
