City of Los Angeles Measure PL asks voters in the city whether to change the charter rules on who decides planning and land use matters.
The City Charter, the constitution of the city, sets the powers of planning commissions, the Planning Department and the City Council.
Today, seven Area Planning Commissions, with 35 commissioners in all, have been in place since 2000.
The charter limits floor area to 13 times the buildable area of a site.
Measure PL would replace them with one Neighborhood Appeals Commission of at least seven members, to hear appeals anew.
Planning Department staff would decide some applications, and some approval rules would move from the charter into ordinances.
Changes to the general plan would be approved, not rejected, if the City Council does not act within 75 days, and the council could change the floor area limit by ordinance.
The City Administrative Officer says commission costs would fall by $360,000 a year, from $1.1 million to $740,000, with no expected effect on revenue.
No argument against Measure PL was submitted, so the voter pamphlet prints only the argument in favor of it.
Supporters say one appeals commission and clearer rules make planning more consistent and harder to manipulate for special treatment.
A yes vote means you want to amend the charter with these planning changes.
A no vote means you do not want to amend the charter.
