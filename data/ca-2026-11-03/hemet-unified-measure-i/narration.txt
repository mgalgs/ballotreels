Hemet Unified School District Measure I asks the district's voters whether to approve $225 million in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
Measure I needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would fix roofs, plumbing and electrical systems, upgrade aging classrooms and labs, and improve school safety and air conditioning.
The district's tax statement estimates an average tax of $49 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $60, through 2051-2052.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $382 million.
Supporters say Measure I fixes leaky roofs and adds air conditioning and shade, without raising tax rates.
Opponents say Measure I taxes struggling families for 25 years without first exhausting grants or other funding.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue and sell the bonds.
A no vote rejects the district issuing and selling the bonds.
