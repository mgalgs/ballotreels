Measure M asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $295 million in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
Measure M needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
State law lets school bonds pay only for building, repairing and equipping schools, or for land.
The listed projects include replacing portable classrooms with permanent ones, and modernizing classrooms, restrooms, libraries and playfields.
The district's tax statement estimates an average tax of $59.14 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $60, through 2059-2060.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $623.3 million.
Supporters say Measure M would fix old classrooms, plumbing and safety systems, and a citizens' committee would audit all of the spending each year.
Opponents say Measure M means about 30 years of taxes to repay the debt, while most students are behind grade level in English and math.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue and sell up to $295 million in bonds for school facilities.
A no vote does not authorize the district to issue and sell the bonds.
