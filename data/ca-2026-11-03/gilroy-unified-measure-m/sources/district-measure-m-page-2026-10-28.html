Measure M

The Gilroy Unified School District is preparing to modernize school facilities and address urgent facility needs. Through the support of local bond measures, like Measure M, our campuses remain safe, secure, and equipped with up-to-date technology and modern classrooms.

Measure M is a $295 million initiative on the November 2026 ballot.  If passed, funds from Measure M will support existing facilities renovations and new construction throughout the Gilroy Unified School District.  Some top priorities for Measure M funds include:

Facilities Upgrades for Our Youngest Learners

One possible use of Measure M funds will enhance transitional kinder and kindergarten students' educational experiences by improving aging facilities and transitioning from existing portable classrooms to new state-of-the-art classrooms throughout the District.

School Modernization

Another possible use of Measure M funds will target our oldest school sites to elevate current buildings and create new and innovative 21st century learning spaces.

Enhancing Outdoor Spaces

Another possible use of Measure M funds will identify outside areas at our schools in need of enhancement. This includes installation of shade structures, improvements to athletics stadiums and other outdoor spaces.

Focus on Career Technical Education

Measure M funds would help develop Career Technical Education (CTE) facilities at Gilroy High School for culinary arts, welding and HVAC. These modern spaces would provide cutting-edge hands-on training and career preparation.

From Superintendent Munshi to the Gilroy community

Dear Community Member,

The Gilroy Unified School District has served the families of Gilroy and the south Santa Clara Valley since 1966. Today approximately 10,000 students in transitional kindergarten through twelfth grade attend our fourteen schools. It is through the hard work and dedication of teachers and staff that our children succeed.

Faced with aging schools across the District and the need to bring school facilities up to current standards, the District and Board of Education have decided to place Measure M, a school improvement, general obligation (G.O.) bond measure, on the November 3, 2026 ballot. Measure M will allow us to modernize and renovate our aging schools. Below are Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) to assist voters in understanding the facts behind Measure M and how its passage will affect the District, our students, and the community.

Please take a moment to review the FAQ, and feel free to reach out to us should you have any questions on this important issue.

Sincerely,
Anisha Munshi, Ed.D.
Superintendent

What is Measure M?

Measure M is a $295.0 million general obligation (G.O.) bond program. This measure is intended to address the needs of the student population through modernization projects at the District's fourteen schools.

What is a G.O. bond?

G.O. bonds fund projects such as the renovation of existing classrooms and school facilities, as well as construction of new schools and classrooms. Similar to a home loan, G.O. bonds are typically repaid over 25 to 30 years. The loan repayment comes from a tax on all taxable property residential, commercial, agricultural, and industrial - located within the District's boundaries.

Why has the District placed Measure M on the ballot?

Our school facilities are outdated, and upgrades and renovations need to be made. While facilities have been well maintained, old classrooms must be upgraded since many do not meet 21st-century educational and technological standards. A local school improvement measure would allow the District to improve the quality of the school facilities and education provided to local students.

Why can't the District meet its facilities needs with its current budget?

Today, the scope of improvements needed in the Gilroy Unified School District is far more than the current funding sources available. The per pupil funding which the District receives from the state is intended to be used for the day-today business of educating students and not the cost of upgrading, modernizing, and repairing facilities.

How did the District come up with the project list for Measure M?

Over the last several months, with input from staff, teachers, parents and community leaders, the District has prepared a School Facilities Needs Analysis. The analysis identifies the major repairs and upgrades that need to be made. Specific projects identified include:

  • Repairing or replacing leaky roofs
  • Renovating and constructing career technical education classrooms to better prepare students for jobs
  • Improving student safety at drop-off and pick-up areas
  • Upgrading or replacing deteriorating plumbing and sewer systems
  • Making safety improvements including fire alarms and security cameras

What will the passage of Measure M mean for our students and the community?

Measure M will provide our students with a better learning environment by making repairs and upgrades to existing classrooms and school facilities, many of which are also used and available to the community.

What will happen if Measure M does not pass?

If Measure M does not pass, our classrooms and school facilities will continue to deteriorate. In addition, funds that would otherwise go to classroom instruction will be needed to make critical safety repairs and improvements at the schools. Major repairs will need to be postponed and, as a result, will likely be more expensive to make.

What will Measure M cost?

The tax rate per property owner is estimated to be $60 per $100,000 of assessed valuation. (Do not confuse assessed valuation with market value. Assessed valuations are the value placed on property by the County and are almost always lower than market values.) Check your property tax statement for your current assessed valuation.

How can I be sure that funds will be spent on improving our local schools?

By law, all funds have to be spent locally and cannot be taken by the state. Furthermore, an independent citizens' oversight committee will be established to ensure that bond funds are properly spent. Also, by law, there must be annual audits and no bond money can be used for teacher or administrative salaries.

Who has oversight over Bond fund expenditures?

By law, spending must be reviewed and annually audited by an independent citizens' oversight committee.

Can the District use Measure M funds to give GUSD employees a raise?

No.  Bond funds are required to be spent only on schools, not for administrator or teacher salaries.

Can bond funds be taken by the State of California?

No.  All bond funds must be spent locally and cannot be taken by the State.

Additional Resources

The Gilroy Unified School District is committed to transparency, strategic planning, and responsible stewardship of taxpayer funds. Every dollar is managed with the highest level of fiscal discipline, as evidenced by our financial and performance audits and bond credit ratings. Gilroy Unified has earned the highest possible credit ratings from both Standard & Poor’s (Aa3) and Moody’s (Aa3). These premier ratings reflect our robust financial health and ensure that we secure the lowest possible interest rates when issuing bonds, saving significant money for local taxpayers.

Facilities Master Plan

In 2025, Gilroy Unified and industry experts developed a comprehensive Facilities Master Plan (FMP).

A FMP is a strategic, long-term blueprint that assesses the educational adequacy of every campus, evaluates structural conditions, identifies critical infrastructure needs, and incorporates input from the community to align our facility investments with our educational goals and priorities of our community.
