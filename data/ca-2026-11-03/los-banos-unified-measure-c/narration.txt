Measure C asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $99 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them from a tax on property in the district.
Measure C needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The ballot question lists projects such as upgrading classrooms and labs, fixing roofs and plumbing, and building classrooms to relieve overcrowding.
The district's tax rate statement estimates $32 a year per $100,000 of assessed value, through 2059-2060.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $178 million.
No argument against Measure C appears among the official documents gathered for this video; only the argument in favor does.
Supporters say many district schools are 50 to over 100 years old, and new classrooms are needed to relieve severe overcrowding.
A yes vote is in favor of authorizing the district to issue and sell up to $99 million in bonds.
A no vote is against authorizing the district to issue and sell the bonds.
