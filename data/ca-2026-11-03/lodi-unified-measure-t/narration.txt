Lodi Unified School District Measure T asks the district's voters whether to approve $195 million in school bonds.
Voters approved school bonds for the district in 2002 and 2016, taxed at $54.50 per $100,000 of assessed value in the 2025-2026 fiscal year.
Measure T needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would repair roofs, plumbing, electrical and gas lines, and heating and cooling, improve security, and upgrade career training classrooms.
The district's tax statement says the bonds would be sold so the property tax rate is not projected to rise above today's.
The statement estimates an average tax of $21.13 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $30.87, through 2060-2061.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $388 million.
No argument against Measure T was printed in the San Joaquin County voter guide for this election.
Supporters say every dollar stays with local schools, and repairing them now costs far less than waiting.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue up to $195 million in bonds.
A no vote does not authorize the district to issue the bonds.
