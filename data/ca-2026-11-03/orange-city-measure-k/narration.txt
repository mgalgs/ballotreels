City of Orange Measure K asks city voters whether to adopt a city charter, making Orange a charter city.
Today, Orange is a general law city, governed mainly by state law.
A charter city still follows state law on statewide matters, but has authority over its own municipal affairs.
The City Council placed Measure K on the ballot, and it needs a majority to pass.
The charter would set lifetime limits of two four-year terms for mayor and three for council members, not counting earlier terms.
The charter would create an open space reserve of city land, and taking land out of it would need voter approval.
The charter also covers city contracting, the taking of private property, balanced budgets and yearly independent audits.
The Orange County Voter Information Guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say Measure K gives voters the final say on open space and sets lifetime limits for mayor and council.
Opponents say Measure K lets the current council reset and extend its terms, and charter cities face higher legal costs.
A yes vote approves the charter and makes Orange a charter city.
A no vote rejects the charter, and Orange stays a general law city governed by state law.
