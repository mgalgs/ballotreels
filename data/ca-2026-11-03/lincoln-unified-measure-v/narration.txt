Lincoln Unified School District Measure V asks the district's voters whether to approve $79 million in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
Measure V needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would pay for safety and security improvements, classrooms and labs for job training, and new roofs and heating and cooling systems.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would check the spending.
The district's tax statement estimates an average tax of $39.07 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $39.50, through 2061-2062.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $156.5 million.
No argument for Measure V was printed in the San Joaquin County voter guide for this election.
No argument against Measure V was printed in the San Joaquin County voter guide for this election.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue up to $79 million in bonds.
A no vote does not authorize the district to issue the bonds.
