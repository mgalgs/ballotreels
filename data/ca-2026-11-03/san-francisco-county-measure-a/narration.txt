San Francisco Proposition A asks voters whether to update the City Charter, and eliminate or reorganize some commissions.
The charter is the constitution of San Francisco, and only voters can change it.
The charter and other voter-approved laws create many city commissions, and the voter pamphlet says some of these laws have become obsolete.
Proposition A would update the charter to match how some departments work now, and change some processes.
Proposition A would also eliminate, combine, or move out of the charter some commissions and advisory bodies.
The Board of Supervisors would then amend some voter-approved laws to carry out the changes.
The City Controller says Proposition A would save $365,000 to $450,000 a year, less than 1 percent of the General Fund of about $7 billion.
Opponents say Proposition A threatens democracy by removing commissions voters created, and lets the board remove independent oversight by majority vote.
Supporters say Proposition A makes small fixes to an outdated charter, like ending a commission overseeing a department that no longer exists.
A yes vote means you want to amend the charter in these ways.
A no vote means you do not want to make these changes.
