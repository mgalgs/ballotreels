## Ballot question (p. 66)

> Shall the City amend the Charter to align it with how some City departments currently operate, change some processes, eliminate or reorganize certain commissions and advisory bodies, and require the Board of Supervisors to amend voter-approved laws to enact some of these changes?

## Digest by the Ballot Simplification Council (p. 66)

**The Way It Is Now:**
> The Charter is the City's constitution and establishes the organization, powers, and responsibilities for many City departments. The Charter and other voter-approved laws also create many City commissions and advisory bodies and describe their powers, duties, membership and removal rules, and reporting requirements.
>
> Over time, some of these laws have become obsolete. The Charter and most laws passed by the voters can only be changed with voter approval.

**The Proposal:**
> Proposition A would align the Charter with how some City departments currently operate, change some processes, and eliminate or reorganize certain commissions and advisory bodies. Proposition A would require the Board of Supervisors to amend voter-approved laws to enact some of these changes.

> Proposition A would:
> • change the language of the Charter to reflect how some City departments currently operate in order to clarify responsibilities and update rules, procedures, and reporting requirements.
> • change some processes. For example: allowing legal settlements to be approved by resolution instead of by ordinance.
> • require a task force to meet every ten years to recommend changes to commissions.
> • eliminate or reorganize certain commissions and advisory bodies by, for example: eliminating some commissions and advisory bodies, combining and renaming some advisory bodies, and moving some commissions and advisory bodies from the Charter into the Municipal Code.

**A "YES" Vote Means:**
> If you vote "yes," you want to amend the Charter to align it with how some City departments currently operate, change some processes, eliminate or reorganize certain commissions and advisory bodies, and require the Board of Supervisors to amend voter-approved laws to enact some of these changes.

**A "NO" Vote Means:**
> If you vote "no," you do not want to make these changes.

## Controller's Statement on "A" (pp. 66–67)

> City Controller Greg Wagner has issued the following statement on the fiscal impact of Proposition A:
> Should the proposed Charter amendment be approved by the voters, in my opinion, it would have a moderate impact on the cost of government, saving $365,000 to $450,000 annually. Additional costs and savings are dependent on the decisions that the Mayor and Board of Supervisors make through the normal budget process. (p. 66)

Dollar figures:
> The proposed Charter amendment creates cost savings ranging from $365,000 to $450,000 annually by eliminating the Sanitation and Streets Commission, Public Works Commission, and the Street Artists and Craftsmen Examiners Advisory Committee. The Sanitation and Streets Commission and Public Works Commission shared two full-time staff members with annual costs of $336,000. (pp. 66–67)

> For context, convening the CSTF in 2025 required approximately 5,900 hours of staff time. (p. 67)

> For example, the proposed Charter amendment removes the restriction on compensating Youth Commission members which may lead to future costs, likely ranging from $5,000 to $85,000 annually, should policymakers seek compensation for the members. (p. 67)

> Note that the proposed amendment would change the duties of the Controller's Office, which has prepared this statement. (p. 67)

## Arguments (pp. 68–69)

Every argument page carries: "Arguments are the opinions of the authors and have not been checked for accuracy by any official agency." and "Arguments are printed as submitted. Spelling and grammatical errors have not been corrected."

**Proponent's Argument** (p. 68). Signers: Mayor Daniel Lurie; Board of Supervisors President Rafael Mandelman; Supervisor Alan Wong; Supervisor Bilal Mahmood; Supervisor Chyanne Chen; Supervisor Danny Sauter; Supervisor Matt Dorsey; Supervisor Myrna Melgar; Supervisor Steven Sherrill; State Senator Scott Wiener; Assemblywoman Catherine Stefani; Assemblyman Matt Haney.
> The mayor and a supermajority of the Board of Supervisors agreed on 110 small fixes that will help city government serve you better.

> For example, the Sanitation and Streets Commission oversees a department that doesn't exist, and the charter still references a Parking Authority that voters removed a decade ago.

**Rebuttal to Proponent's Argument** (p. 68). Signer: Coalition on Homelessness.
> A healthy democracy requires a balance of power. A healthy government has plenty of citizen participation. Debate leads us to the best answers. These are all reasons to vote No on A.

**Opponent's Argument** (p. 69). Signer: Coalition on Homelessness.
> There are grievous mistakes in Prop A that threaten participatory democracy by removing commissions put in place by voters.

> The measure allows the Board of Supervisors to remove independent oversight with a majority vote.

(The argument concerns the oversight body for November 2018 Proposition C, "Our City, Our Home".)

**Rebuttal to Opponent's Argument** (p. 69). Signers: Board of Supervisors President Rafael Mandelman; Mayor Daniel Lurie.
> Currently five separate, overlapping commissions oversee homeless services in San Francisco. None of them are removed by Prop A.

> Prop A lets the Board handle those small fixes through ordinary legislation instead.
