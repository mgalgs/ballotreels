Measure B asks the voters of this city whether to raise the tax on hotel and short-term rental stays from 4 to 12 percent.
Today, guests who stay 30 days or less pay a tax of 4 percent of the rent.
Measure B would raise the rate to 12 percent, paid by the guest rather than the lodging operator.
The tax would expressly cover vacation rentals and home-sharing, and online booking platforms may have to collect it.
The money would go to the general fund of the city, for any lawful purpose, until ended by the voters.
Measure B needs a simple majority of the votes to pass.
The ballot question says that Measure B would provide about $55,037 a year for general city services.
No argument in favor of Measure B appears among the filings that the county elections office posted for this election.
Opponents say a 12 percent tax is a substantial burden, and local short-term rental owners would end up shouldering the costs.
A yes vote increases the hotel tax rate from 4 to 12 percent, and adopts the amended ordinance.
A no vote is a vote against the measure, and the existing 4 percent tax would remain in effect.
