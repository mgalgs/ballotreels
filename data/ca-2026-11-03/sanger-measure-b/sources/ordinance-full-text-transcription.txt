EXHIBIT A

ORDINANCE SUBMITTED TO THE VOTERS

ORDINANCE NO. 2026-06

AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF SANGER, CALIFORNIA, AMENDING AND RESTATING ARTICLE V OF CHAPTER 66 OF THE SANGER MUNICIPAL CODE RELATING TO THE TRANSIENT OCCUPANCY TAX

WHEREAS, Revenue and Taxation Code section 7280 authorizes a city to levy a tax on the privilege of occupying a room or other living space in a hotel, inn, tourist home or house, motel, or other lodging for a period of 30 days or less; and

WHEREAS, the City of Sanger currently imposes a transient occupancy tax pursuant to Article V of Chapter 66 of the Sanger Municipal Code; and

WHEREAS, the City's existing transient occupancy tax ordinance is based on a long-standing municipal tax structure and contains administrative provisions that remain useful for tax collection, enforcement, appeals, refunds, confidentiality, and successor liability; and

WHEREAS, the manner in which transient lodging is advertised, reserved, paid for, and administered has changed substantially with the growth of short-term rentals and online booking platforms, including platforms commonly known as Airbnb, VRBO, and similar marketplaces; and

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to amend and restate Article V in its entirety in order to preserve the City's existing transient occupancy tax framework while modernizing the ordinance to apply expressly to short-term rentals, vacation rentals, home-sharing arrangements, and other similar transient lodging accommodations; and

WHEREAS, Senate Bill 346, Chapter 751 of the Statutes of 2025, added Government Code section 50990 et seq., known as the Short-Term Rental Facilitator Act of 2025, and authorizes a city that adopts an ordinance making that Act applicable within its jurisdiction to require short-term rental facilitators to provide certain information, include local license and transient occupancy tax certification information in listings, and submit to audit or examination where the facilitator is responsible for collecting and remitting transient occupancy tax; and

WHEREAS, the Legislature expressly recognized in Government Code section 50996 that the Short-Term Rental Facilitator Act does not preempt local agencies from adopting ordinances regulating short-term rentals, short-term rental facilitators, or the payment and collection of transient occupancy taxes in a manner different from the procedures prescribed in that Act; and

[Page 2]
WHEREAS, Business and Professions Code section 17568.6, added by Assembly Bill 537 and operative July 1, 2024, requires short-term lodging providers, internet websites, applications, centralized platforms, and other persons to advertise or display short-term lodging rates in a manner that includes all required fees and charges except government-imposed taxes and fees, and to include all government-imposed taxes and fees in the total price to be paid before the consumer reserves the stay; and

WHEREAS, Business and Professions Code section 17568.8, added by Assembly Bill 2202 and operative July 1, 2025, requires disclosures regarding certain additional cleaning-task fees, charges, or penalties for short-term lodging and authorizes enforcement by a city attorney, district attorney, county counsel, or the Attorney General; and

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to include a limited state-law compliance provision in this Article to reference those short-term lodging transparency statutes; and

WHEREAS, the City Council desires to increase the transient occupancy tax rate by eight percentage points, from four percent (4%) to twelve percent (12%), and to expand the type of short-term rental properties it applies to, subject to approval by the voters if and to the extent required by Article XIII C of the California Constitution; and

WHEREAS, the City Council further desires to make clear that payment, collection, or remittance of transient occupancy tax does not authorize, legalize, establish a lawful nonconforming use, or otherwise permit any use of property that is prohibited by the Sanger Municipal Code or any other applicable law.

NOW, THEREFORE, THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF SANGER DOES ORDAIN AS FOLLOWS:

SECTION 1. Amendment and Restatement of Article V of Chapter 66.

Article V of Chapter 66 of the Sanger Municipal Code is hereby amended and restated in its entirety to read as follows:

"ARTICLE V. TRANSIENT OCCUPANCY TAX

Sec. 66-126. - Short title; purpose and intent.

This article shall be known as the Transient Occupancy Tax of the City.

[Page 3]
The purpose of this article is to impose a tax upon the privilege of transient occupancy in hotels and other lodging accommodations within the City and to provide for the collection, reporting, administration, and enforcement of that tax.

This article is adopted solely for purposes of taxation and tax administration. Nothing in this article is intended to authorize, legalize, establish a lawful nonconforming use, expand, impair, or alter any land use entitlement, building permit, certificate of occupancy, business license, short-term rental permit, or other approval required by the Sanger Municipal Code or other applicable law.

Sec. 66-126.5 - Authority; State Law.

This article is adopted pursuant to Revenue and Taxation Code section 7280 et seq., Article XIII C of the California Constitution, Government Code section 53724, Elections Code section 9222, and all other applicable law. This article shall be administered consistently with applicable state law. If any provision of this article conflicts with state law, state law shall control.

Sec. 66-127. - Definitions.

Except where the context otherwise requires, the following definitions govern the construction of this article:

"Booking transaction" means any reservation, booking, payment, payment processing, listing, advertising, offer, acceptance, or other transaction involving transient occupancy that is facilitated, directly or indirectly, by a short-term rental facilitator or marketplace.

"City" means the City of Sanger.

"Electronic notice" means notice transmitted by electronic mail or through an electronic filing, payment, or reporting system approved by the tax administrator, to the extent permitted by law.

"Hotel" means any structure, or any portion of any structure, occupied or intended or designed for occupancy by transients for dwelling, lodging, or sleeping purposes, including any hotel, inn, tourist home or house, motel, studio hotel, bachelor hotel, lodging house, rooming house, apartment house, dormitory, public or private club, mobile home or house trailer at a fixed location, campground, campsite, recreational vehicle space, bed and breakfast establishment, single-family residence, dwelling unit, condominium, townhouse, accessory dwelling unit, guest house, home-sharing unit, vacation rental, short-term rental, or other lodging or living accommodation, or portion thereof, offered for transient occupancy for consideration. For purposes of this article, the term hotel is used as a tax term and shall not determine whether a use is permitted under any zoning, building, housing, business license, or other regulatory provision. "Hotel" does not include any facility, occupancy lodging, campsite, space, interest or transaction that the City is prohibited from taxing under Revenue and Taxation Code Section 7280 et seq. or other applicable law, including, without limitation, any campsite in a unit of the state park system to the extent exempt from transient occupancy tax under Revenue and Taxation Code Section 7282.

"Legislative body" means the City Council of the City.

"Marketplace" has the meaning set forth in Government Code section 50991, as that section may be amended, supplemented, renumbered, or succeeded, and includes an internet website, application, software application, online platform, or other physical or electronic place through which the occupancy of a short-term rental is facilitated.

[Page 4]
"Occupancy" means the use or possession, or the right to use or possess, any room, rooms, dwelling unit, lodging, campsite, space, accommodation, or portion thereof in any hotel for dwelling, lodging, or sleeping purposes.

"Operator" means the person who is proprietor of the hotel, whether in the capacity of owner, lessee, sublessee, mortgagee in possession, licensee, host, property manager, managing agent, or any other capacity. Where the operator performs functions through a managing agent of any type or character other than an employee, the managing agent shall also be deemed an operator for purposes of this article and shall have the same duties and liabilities as the principal. Compliance with this article by either the principal or the managing agent shall be considered compliance by both to the extent of the compliance actually made.

"Person" means any individual, firm, partnership, joint venture, association, social club, fraternal organization, joint stock company, corporation, limited liability company, estate, trust, business trust, receiver, trustee, syndicate, or any other group or combination acting as a unit.

"Rent" means the total consideration charged, whether or not received, for occupancy of space in a hotel, valued in money, whether to be received in money, goods, labor, credits, property, services, or otherwise, without deduction. Rent includes all mandatory fees and charges required as a condition of occupancy, including mandatory cleaning fees, mandatory resort fees, mandatory linen fees, required utility charges, booking fees charged by the operator, and other mandatory charges imposed by the operator or collected on behalf of the operator as a condition of occupancy. Rent does not include refundable security deposits to the extent actually refundable, separately stated taxes or assessments imposed by a governmental agency on the stay, or separately stated service fees charged by a short-term rental facilitator for marketplace services if the service fee is not paid to or retained by the operator as consideration for occupancy. "Rent" does not include any amount subject to tax under the Sales and Use Tax Law with respect to the sale of food products, including food and beverage products, to the extent excluded from transient occupancy taxation by Revenue and Taxation Code Section 7282.3 or any successor statute.

"Short-term rental" means the occupancy of a home, house, room in a home or house, campsite, dwelling unit, accessory dwelling unit, guest house, condominium, apartment, or other lodging that is not a commercially operated hotel, motel, bed and breakfast inn, or similar transient lodging establishment, for a period of 30 consecutive calendar days or less, and under any other circumstances specified in this article, when facilitated by a short-term rental facilitator or otherwise offered for transient occupancy.

"Short-term rental facilitator" means a person or entity that satisfies the definition of short-term rental facilitator in Government Code section 50991, as that section may be amended, supplemented, renumbered, or succeeded.

"Tax administrator" means the Director of Administrative Services of the City, or the Director's designee, and includes any officer, employee, agent, or contractor authorized by the City to administer or enforce this article.

"Transient" means any person who exercises occupancy or is entitled to occupancy in a hotel by reason of a lease, concession, permit, right of access, license, booking transaction, reservation, or other agreement for a period of 30 consecutive calendar days or less, counting portions of calendar days as full days. Any such person occupying space in a hotel shall be deemed to be a transient until the period of 30 days has expired, unless there is an agreement in writing between the operator and the occupant

[Page 5]
providing for a longer period of occupancy and the occupancy is actually for a period longer than 30 consecutive calendar days. In determining whether a person is a transient, uninterrupted periods of time extending both before and after the effective date of this article may be considered.

"Transient occupancy tax review committee" means the committee established pursuant to section 66-155.

Sec. 66-128. - Imposition generally; tax deemed debt to City.

For the privilege of occupancy in any hotel, each transient is subject to and shall pay a tax in the amount of twelve percent (12%) of the rent charged by the operator.

The tax constitutes a debt owed by the transient to the City. The debt is extinguished only by payment to the operator, a short-term rental facilitator authorized or required to collect the tax, or the City.

The tax imposed by this article is a general tax, the proceeds of which shall be deposited in the City's general fund and may be used for any lawful municipal purpose.

The increased rate of the tax and expanded coverage approved by the voters at the November 3, 2026 election shall become operative on the first day of the first calendar quarter beginning after the date on which the ordinance increasing the rate is approved by the voters, unless the City Council establishes a later operative date by resolution.

The tax imposed hereunder shall continue until ended by voters.

Sec. 66-129. - Exemptions from tax; written claim for exemption.

No tax imposed under this article shall be imposed upon:

(1) Any person as to whom, or any occupancy as to which, it is beyond the power of the City to impose the tax provided in this article;

(2) Any officer or employee of a foreign government who is exempt by reason of express provision of federal law or international treaty;

(3) Any occupant for more than 30 successive calendar days, provided that the requirements of this article for nontransient occupancy are satisfied; or

(4) Any occupant whose rent is paid for a hospital room or to a medical clinic, convalescent home, or home for aged people; or

(5) Any occupancy of a campsite in a unit of the state park system to the extent prohibited by Revenue & Taxation Code Section 7282 or any successor statute.

No exemption shall be granted under subsections (1) or (2) except upon a claim made at the time rent is collected, under penalty of perjury, upon a form prescribed by the tax administrator.

Sec. 66-130. - When and to whom payable.

The transient shall pay the tax to the operator or to a short-term rental facilitator that is required or authorized to collect the tax at the time rent is paid. If the rent is paid in installments, a proportionate share of the tax shall be paid with each installment. Any unpaid tax shall be due upon the transient's ceasing to occupy space in the hotel.

If for any reason the tax due is not paid to the operator or short-term rental facilitator, the tax administrator may require that the tax be paid directly to the tax administrator.

Sec. 66-149.5. - Lien.

(a) Any tax, penalty, interest, fee or other amount required to be paid under this Article shall constitute a debt owed to the City.

(b) If any amount required to be paid under this Article remains unpaid after it becomes final, the tax administrator may record with the county recorder a certificate of lien specifying the amount of the tax, penalties, interest, fees, and other charges due, the name and last known address of the person liable for the amount, and such other information as the tax administrator determines is appropriate.

(c) From the time of recording, the amount required to be paid shall constitute a lien upon all real property in the county of Fresno owned by the person liable or thereafter acquired by that person before the lien expires. The lien shall have the force and effect, and priority of a judgment lien and shall continue for ten (10) years from the time of recording unless sooner released or otherwise discharged, and may be extended in the manner provided by law.

(d) The Tax Administrator may release the lien when the amount due has been paid or when the lien was recorded in error.

Sec. 66-150. - Disposition of tax proceeds.

The tax administrator shall deposit all moneys collected pursuant to this article to the credit of the general fund of the City.

Sec. 66-151. - Records; examination by tax administrator.

Every operator, short-term rental facilitator, and other person liable for tax or required to collect or remit tax under this article shall keep records, receipts, invoices, booking records, platform reports, exemption certificates, tax collection records, payment records, and other pertinent papers in such form as the tax administrator may require.

All records shall be retained for a period of not less than four years and six months after they come into being, or for such longer period as may be required by written notice from the tax administrator, this article, or other applicable law.

The remedies provided in this article are cumulative and in addition to any other remedies available at law or in equity, including civil actions, administrative citations, collection actions, injunctions, and any remedies authorized by the Sanger Municipal Code or state law.

Sec. 66-158. - Short-Term Rental Facilitator Act; future amendments; no change to tax without required approval.

The provisions of Government Code section 50990 et seq., known as the Short-Term Rental Facilitator Act of 2025, as amended, renumbered or succeeded, from time to time, are hereby incorporated into this article to the fullest extent permitted by law and made applicable within the jurisdictional boundaries of the City to the fullest extent permitted by law.

The reporting, collection, remittance, audit, examination, listing, administrative, and enforcement provisions of Government Code section 50990 et seq., as amended, supplemented, renumbered, or succeeded from time to time, are incorporated into this article to the extent they relate to transient occupancy tax administration and short-term rental facilitators.

No future amendment, supplement, renumbering, or successor statute to Government Code section 50990 et seq. shall, without further action by the City and any voter approval required by applicable law, alter the rate of tax imposed by this article, or expand the transactions or persons subject to tax beyond those authorized by this article.

Sec. 66-159. - State short-term lodging disclosure laws.

Each operator, short-term rental facilitator, marketplace, internet website, application, or other similar centralized platform, and any other person advertising, displaying, offering, facilitating, or processing payment for transient occupancy within the City shall comply with Business and Professions Code section 17568.6, Business and Professions Code section 17568.8, and any successor or related state law governing short-term lodging price transparency, disclosure of government-imposed taxes and fees before reservation, and disclosures regarding cleaning-task fees, charges, or penalties.

This section is declaratory of independently applicable state law and is intended to support local administration and enforcement. Nothing in this section shall alter the rate of tax imposed by this article, expand the tax base, impose a tax not otherwise imposed by this article, or require voter approval separate from any approval required for the tax imposed by this article.

The City Attorney is authorized to enforce Business and Professions Code sections 17568.6 and 17568.8, and any successor provisions, to the extent authorized by state law.

Sec. 66-160. Amendments.

The City Council may amend this article without further voter approval to the extent permitted by Article XIII C of the California Constitution and other applicable law. Without limiting the foregoing, the City Council may amend this article to clarify, administer, enforce, or implement the tax; to conform this article to state or federal law; to adopt or amend reporting, collection, audit, appeal, refund, electronic filing, or administrative procedures; and to implement or conform to Government Code section 50990 et seq., as amended, supplemented, renumbered, or succeeded. No such amendment shall increase the rate of tax, impose the tax on occupancy not previously subject to

[Page 18]
the tax, or otherwise constitute a tax increase requiring voter approval unless approved in accordance with applicable law."

SECTION 2. Voter Approval; Effective Date of Tax Increase.

The increase in the transient occupancy tax to twelve percent (12%) shall take effect only upon approval by a majority of the voters of the City to the extent required by Article XIII C of the California Constitution.

SECTION 3. CEQA.

The City Council finds that this Ordinance is not a project within the meaning of the California Environmental Quality Act pursuant to State CEQA Guidelines sections 15060(c)(2), 15060(c)(3), and 15378 because it involves fiscal and administrative governmental activity that will not result in a direct or reasonably foreseeable indirect physical change in the environment. To the extent this Ordinance is deemed a project, it is exempt from CEQA pursuant to State CEQA Guidelines section 15061(b)(3).

SECTION 4. Severability.

If any section, subsection, sentence, clause, phrase, or portion of this Ordinance is held invalid or unconstitutional by a court of competent jurisdiction, that decision shall not affect the validity of the remaining portions of this Ordinance. The City Council declares that it would have adopted this Ordinance and each section, subsection, sentence, clause, phrase, and portion thereof irrespective of the fact that any one or more sections, subsections, sentences, clauses, phrases, or portions may be declared invalid or unconstitutional.

SECTION 5. Publication and Codification.

The City Clerk shall certify to the adoption of this Ordinance and shall cause the same to be published or posted in the manner required by law. The City Clerk is authorized to make non-substantive formatting, numbering, and typographical corrections necessary for codification.

SECTION 6. Effective Date.

This Ordinance shall take effect only if approved by a majority of the voters voting on the measure at the November 3, 2026 election and upon certification of the election results by the City Council. The increased tax rate shall become operative on the first day of the first calendar quarter beginning after certification of the election results, unless the City Council establishes a later operative date by resolution.
