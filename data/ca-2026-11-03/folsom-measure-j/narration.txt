City of Folsom Measure J asks voters in the city whether the City Council may give city boards and commissions the power to make decisions.
Today, the City Charter says boards and commissions shall only advise the council, so they cannot make binding decisions.
The City Council placed Measure J on the ballot, and it needs a simple majority.
Measure J would remove the advisory-only rule, so the council could grant a commission decision-making authority by ordinance.
The City Attorney's analysis says certain development projects reviewed by one commission may then proceed more quickly, without separate City Council approval.
The Sacramento County Current Measure Information page does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Opponents say Measure J would let the council give unlimited authority to unelected commissioners, reducing accountability to voters.
Supporters say Measure J resolves conflicting charter language, and commission decisions could still be appealed to the council.
A yes vote authorizes a charter amendment letting the council grant decision-making authority to boards and commissions.
A no vote does not authorize the charter amendment, and boards and commissions would remain advisory.
