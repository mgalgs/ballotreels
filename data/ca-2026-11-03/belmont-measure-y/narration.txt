Belmont Measure Y asks city voters whether to approve a special tax on building space to fund a new community center.
A citizen petition put Measure Y on the ballot, and it needs a simple majority to pass.
The tax would start July 1, 2027, charged by the square foot of each building, with a higher rate for buildings that are not homes.
Owners 65 and older who live in their homes would be exempt.
The money would go to a special fund to build and run the center, until voters repeal the tax.
The City Council could raise the rate each year by the growth in inflation or income indexes.
The impartial analysis estimates about $7.1 million for the fund in the first year.
Opponents say everyone would be forced to pay this special tax increase, and that Belmont must live within its budget.
Supporters say Belmont has outgrown its 75-year-old center, and that every dollar would be protected in a special fund.
A yes vote would approve Measure Y and the tax.
A no vote would reject Measure Y.
