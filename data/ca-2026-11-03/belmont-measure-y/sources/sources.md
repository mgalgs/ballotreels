# Measure Y — City of Belmont (special tax on improved square footage, citizen initiative)

- Jurisdiction: City of Belmont.
- Kind: special tax on the square footage of improvements, to fund a new community center ("City of Belmont Community Center Tax Measure"; the ordinance calls itself the "Build Belmont Together Measure"). Citizen initiative.
- Who votes: the voters of the City of Belmont. Resolution, p. 2, Sec. 2(a): "A municipal election in the City of Belmont is called for Tuesday, November, 3, 2026, a general election, for the purpose of submitting to the electorate a ballot measure approving an ordinance levying a special tax on the square footage of improved property within the City of Belmont". Sec. 5(b), p. 3: "its submission to the voters of the City at the November 3, 2026 election."
- Vote threshold: majority. Resolution, p. 2, under the question: "This question requires the approval of the majority of qualified electors casting votes." Impartial analysis: "California courts have held that a special tax measure proposed by citizen initiative requires only a simple majority vote to pass."
- Placed by: citizen initiative petition; submitted to the voters by City Council Resolution No. 2026-69.
- Election: November 3, 2026.

## Ballot question

Heading: "City of Belmont Community Center Tax Measure"

> Shall the ordinance providing funding for and maintaining supervised children's play, youth, teen and adult programs, childcare, afterschool and senior programs in a disabled accessible, fire and earthquake resistant community center, usable as an emergency health and disaster relief facility, levying 33¢ per square foot of residential improvements and 54¢ per square foot of nonresidential improvements, providing $7,100,000 annually until ended by voters, with senior exemptions and independent audits, increasing the appropriation limit, be adopted?

(resolution-full-text.pdf p. 2, Sec. 3(c); transcribed from the scan.)

## Full text (resolution / ordinance)

- Resolution No. 2026-69, "A Resolution of the City Council of the City of Belmont Calling for the Holding of a General Municipal Election on Tuesday, November, 3, 2026, for the Submission of an Ordinance Proposed by Citizen Initiative" (p. 1). Stamped AUG 04 2026 (p. 1).
- Adopted: "ADOPTED July 14, 2026 by the City of Belmont City Council by the following vote: Ayes: Pang-Maganaris, McCune, Latimerlo, Jordan, Mates" (p. 5). No Noes/Absent lines are printed.
- Petition history (recitals, p. 1): Notice of Intention filed "on January 28, 2026"; petitions "filed with the City Clerk on June 3, 2026"; the County found "2084 of the 2702 signatures on the petition were examined of which 1799 were found to be sufficient", above the "at least 1750 (10%)" needed. "Elections Code Section 9215 requires the legislative body of the city to submit the proposed ordinance to the voters".
- What the ordinance does (Sec. 5(c), p. 3): "The ordinance authorizes the tax beginning July 1, 2027, and establishes rates of $0.33 per square foot of improvements for dwelling units and $0.54 per square foot of improvements for all other property for that tax year, and requires revenue from the tax be deposited into a "Community Center Special Fund"".
- Ordinance (adds Article VII, "Community Center Special Tax", to Chapter 23 of the Belmont City Code; pp. 6-12). Sec. 1, p. 6: "This measure shall be known and may be cited as the Build Belmont Together Measure".
- Sec. 23-302(c), pp. 7-8: "Each tax year, the City Council may increase the tax rate by up to the greater of either (1) the percentage increase in the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U) for San Francisco-Oakland-Hayward ... or (2) the percentage increase in per capita personal income in the State of California".
- Sec. 23-306(a)(2), p. 8: funds "shall be used for the construction, operations, staffing, maintenance, repair, and capital improvements of the new Barrett Community Center".

## Impartial analysis

Heading: "Impartial Analysis Measure "Y" Belmont Community Center Measure", Office of the City Attorney. Signed "Scott M. Rennie City Attorney". Single page (impartial-analysis.pdf p. 1).

- "Measure "Y" asks Belmont voters whether to approve an ordinance levying a special tax for constructing and operating a new community center in the City of Belmont."
- "Measure "Y" was placed on the ballot by a citizen initiative petition proposed by the group "Friends of Belmont Community Center" and signed by more than 10% of registered Belmont voters."
- "the measure authorizes a tax on the square footage of improvements in the city beginning July 1, 2027, and establishes rates of 33¢ per square foot of improvements for dwelling units and 54¢ per square foot of improvements for all other property."
- "The tax does not apply to parcels and improvements exempt from taxation by law and to the dwelling unit of persons age 65 and older who own and reside in the dwelling unit."
- "The measure is estimated to generate approximately $7.1 million for the Special Community Center Fund in the first tax year." ... "the tax authorization would continue until ended by voters."
- "A "yes" vote would approve the measure and the levy. A "no" vote would reject the measure."

## Tax rate statement / fiscal

Special tax, not a bond; no tax rate statement is among ACRE's files. Key figures from the ordinance (resolution-full-text.pdf):
- Rate, Sec. 23-302(b), p. 7: "For all dwelling units, the tax shall be imposed at the rate of $0.33 per square foot of improvements." / "For all other property, the tax shall be imposed at the rate of $0.54 per square foot of improvements." (tax year 2027-2028).
- Exemptions, Sec. 23-303, p. 8: "(b) The tax imposed by this article shall not apply to the owner of a dwelling unit that resides in such dwelling unit and who is sixty-five (65) years of age or older on or before June 30 of the tax year immediately preceding the year in which the tax would apply." Claimants "shall submit an application ... prior to May 1st of the tax year".
- Duration, Sec. 23-304, p. 8: "The tax imposed by this article shall be operative from July 1, 2027, until ended by voters."
- Revenue: "approximately $7 million for the first tax year" (Sec. 5(c), p. 3); the ballot question says "$7,100,000 annually".
