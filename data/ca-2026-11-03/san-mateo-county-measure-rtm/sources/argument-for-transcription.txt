Every day, thousands of San Mateo County residents rely on public transit to get to work, school, medical appointments, and local businesses. Our public transit system reduces traffic, supports our local economy, and ensures everyone has an affordable way to get where they need to go - including families, seniors, people with disabilities, students, and workers.

Today, Bay Area transit faces a turning point. Without new funding from the Regional Transit Measure, we will experience significant and deep cuts to our public transit systems -- setting us back decades. Failure of this measure could result in major station closures and service cuts for Caltrain, BART, Muni, and AC Transit.

Without the Regional Transit Measure, Caltrain could close up to one-third of stations, reduce frequency, and stop late night and weekend service entirely. BART could cut 70% of their service and shut down 15 stations including Colma, San Bruno, South San Francisco, and Millbrae. These cuts would disrupt daily life and push thousands more cars onto Highways 101, 280, 92, and local streets.

The consequences would impact everyone. This would worsen air pollution, make traffic jams start earlier and last longer, and result in more time behind the wheel and less time home with our families.

The Regional Transit Measure invests directly in San Mateo County, protecting Caltrain, SamTrans, and paratransit service, while preserving regional connections that Peninsula residents rely on. The measure also invests money directly into San Mateo County communities, making transit more reliable and efficient, repairing potholes, reducing traffic, and improving safety.

The Regional Transit Measure protects the public transit residents depend on while requiring local transportation investments - with strong accountability and oversight that requires large transit agencies to show cost savings to continue receiving dedicated operating funding. And it doesn’t tax rent, utilities, prescription medicines, or most groceries.

Please join us and vote Yes on the Regional Transit Measure.
