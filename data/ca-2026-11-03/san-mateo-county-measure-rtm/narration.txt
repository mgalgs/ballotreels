San Mateo County Measure RTM, the Regional Transit Measure, asks voters in a five-county transit district whether to add a sales tax for transit.
A 2025 state law lets voters in that district, San Mateo, Alameda, Contra Costa and Santa Clara counties and San Francisco, approve a sales tax for transit.
Measure RTM would add a 0.5 percent sales tax for 14 years, or 1 percent in San Francisco.
The measure splits the money among agencies such as BART, Caltrain and Muni, by shares it sets for each county.
Measure RTM needs a majority of all the votes cast in the five counties combined, not in each county.
The impartial analysis estimates the tax would raise about $980 million a year across the district.
Opponents say it is a $14 billion bailout for a broken system, sending most San Mateo County taxes to agencies serving other counties.
Supporters say that without it, Caltrain could close up to a third of its stations, and that it invests directly in San Mateo County.
A yes vote is a vote to impose the sales tax for 14 years.
A no vote is a vote against imposing the tax.
