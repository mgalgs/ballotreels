Measure U asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $26 million in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
Measure U needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would upgrade and repair classrooms, science labs and career technology facilities, and fix roofs, plumbing, heating and cooling, and electrical systems.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would check the spending.
The district's tax statement estimates a tax of $18 per $100,000 of assessed value, from 2027-2028 through 2057-2058.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $61 million.
No argument against Measure U was printed in the San Joaquin County voter guide for this election.
Supporters say most district schools were built over 70 years ago and don't meet current safety codes.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue up to $26 million in bonds.
A no vote does not authorize the district to issue the bonds.
