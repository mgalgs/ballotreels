Temecula Valley Unified School District Measure S asks the district's voters whether to approve $215 million in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it with a tax on property, based on its assessed value.
Measure S needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would repair roofs, plumbing, gas and electrical systems, and upgrade aging classrooms, science labs and career technology labs.
The district's tax statement estimates a tax of $20 per $100,000 of assessed value, collected until 2055-2056.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $383 million.
Opponents say the ballot hides the true cost, and the district holds $120 million usable for repairs.
Supporters say Measure S replaces aging portable classrooms with permanent ones and upgrades cooling for 100-degree summers.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue and sell the bonds.
A no vote rejects the district issuing and selling the bonds.
