Measure D asks the voters of this city whether selling, leasing or changing the city-owned downtown parking lots should need a public vote.
The city owns ten open surface parking lots downtown, known as Plazas 1 through 10.
Measure D is a citizen-sponsored initiative, and it needs a majority to pass.
Without a majority public vote, the city could not sell or lease the lots, or declare them surplus land.
Nor could the city cut public parking, add levels, or harm protected trees at the plazas.
Two of the lots could be developed for affordable housing without a vote, and upkeep and the farmers' market could go on.
The Registrar of Voters' List of Local Measures does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say residents, not any one city council, should decide on permanent changes to the downtown parking plazas.
Opponents say Measure D would cost taxpayers millions in legal battles, and would endanger the state-approved housing plan.
A yes vote requires a public vote before the plazas are sold, leased or permanently repurposed.
A no vote leaves the city free to use the plazas as the law allows.
