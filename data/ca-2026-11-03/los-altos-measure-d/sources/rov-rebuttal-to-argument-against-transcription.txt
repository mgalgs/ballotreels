City Council is proposing projects on city-owned parking plazas that are not housing. Measure D lets the community vote on these “non-housing” projects and leaves open options for housing.

Measure D does not cause changes to the state-certified Housing Element nor trigger Builder’s Remedy. Measure D does not put residential neighborhoods at risk of rezoning; Los Altos has already identified 30% more potential housing units than state mandate requires.

Measure D protects taxpayers and public assets. City Councils have previously given away or sold public land without voter approval. Measure D safeguards against third-party deals, strengthens accountability, and encourages smart planning before committing substantial public funds.

California law protects voter initiatives since government officials may not defend them. Measure D provides that safeguard; it does not authorize proponents to initiate taxpayer-funded lawsuits.

Measure D was developed with legal and land-use expertise, and is supported by former mayors, city commissioners, educators, and current downtown property and business owners.

Measure D respects representative government. It leaves ordinary City governing to elected officials and reserves voter approval only for consequential, irreversible actions spelled out in the ordinance.

Measure D is consistent with Los Altos policies. Voter approval is already required for the disposition of parks, open space, and the Civic Center property. Measure D applies the same voter-approval principle to our invaluable parking plazas.

Your Downtown.Your Decision. Vote Yes on D.

Yeslosaltos.org
