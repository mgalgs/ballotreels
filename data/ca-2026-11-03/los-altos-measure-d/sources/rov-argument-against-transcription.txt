Measure D is a costly mistake. Its supporters promise a simple solution, but the reality will be years of expensive unintended consequences. It will cost taxpayers millions in legal battles, discourage investment in our downtown, put local businesses at a disadvantage, and risk our community’s control over where and how housing is built.

Measure D won't improve parking or support local businesses. Instead, it requires a costly city-wide election before any future changes can move forward, delaying improvements and making it harder to attract investment and new customers.

Measure D is a sledgehammer. Even if you oppose a downtown park you should still oppose this measure. The park is only a proposal, and the City Council is required to hold public meetings and hear community input. Measure D permanently locks broad restrictions into law, limiting future options for parking, housing, and solutions we haven't even imagined. It ties our hands for decades.

Measure D is poorly drafted and legally ambiguous. It jeopardizes Los Altos’ state-approved housing plan. If the State finds us out of compliance, the City may have to identify housing sites elsewhere, including near neighborhood shopping areas or along major corridors such as El Camino, San Antonio, or Foothill. We could face Builder’s Remedy, losing local control over development throughout neighborhoods in Los Altos. Builder’s Remedy is the cost when this measures gambles with neighborhoods across Los Altos.

Measure D weakens accountability. Once adopted, even future City Councils can't fix its flaws. This measure will require taxpayers to fund the lawsuits and appeals supported by its proponents, who are private citizens we can't vote out and are accountable to no one at the ballot box.

Protect local control. Join us and the local League of Women Voters to VOTE NO on Measure D.
