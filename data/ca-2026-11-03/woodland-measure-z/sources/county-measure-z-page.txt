Measure Z - City of Woodland
Last Content Update:

City of Woodland Measure
Shall the measure amending the policy establishing the City’s Urban Limit Line to permit the extension of sewer, water, and recycled water facilities to serve existing commercial facilities located on parcels with a boundary that falls wholly or partially within one mile of the Urban Limit Line and in operation on or prior to November 3, 2026, be adopted?
To pass, a majority (50% + 1) of the votes cast by City of Woodland voters must approve the measure.

[#tabfd22f24a-36e8-4b9d-8175-77ad82527dde_0] Impartial Analysis of Measure Z
CITY OF WOODLAND
CITY ATTORNEY’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE “Z”
On June 16, 2026, the City Council voted to place Measure “Z” on the November 3, 2026 general municipal election ballot, which will be held and consolidated with the Statewide Primary Election of the same date.
If approved by a majority of Woodland voters, Measure would amend Policy 2.A.1 of Goal 2.A of the City of Woodland’s 2035 General Plan Land Use, Community Design and Historic Preservation Element to authorize the City to extend potable water, recycled water and sewer services from the City to existing commercial properties that are located within one mile of the City’s Urban Limit Line.
In 2006, a majority of Woodland voters passed Measure “A”, which established a permanent Urban Limit Line (ULL) to circumscribe urban development and protect agricultural lands surrounding the City. The City is prohibited from extending public services and facilities to properties located beyond the ULL. This prohibition on the extension of public services and facilities beyond the ULL is codified in Policy 2.A.1 of Goal 2.A of the City’s 2035 General Plan Land Use, Community Design and Historic Preservation Element. Measure “Z” would amend Policy 2.A.1 as previously enacted by Measure “A” to create an exception to the prohibition on extending public services and facilities beyond the ULL. Measure “Z” would allow the City to extend sewer, potable water and recycled water facilities to serve existing commercial facilities located on parcels with a boundary that falls wholly or partially within one mile of the ULL, so long as those facilities were in operation on or prior to November 3, 2026. The City would not be required to extend service to any qualifying commercial property, and any extension of service would be subject to City Council approval and all other applicable regulatory approvals. This exception would not apply to new commercial development, any residential development (new or existing), or any other non-commercial facilities (new or existing).
A "yes" vote on Measure “Z” will allow the City to extend City sewer, potable water and services to existing commercial facilities within one mile of the ULL on a case by case basis, subject to City Council approval and all applicable regulatory approvals.
A "no" vote on Measure “Z” will keep the language of Policy 2.A.1 in its current form as originally enacted by Measure “A”, and will prohibit any extension of City services or facilities beyond the ULL.
The above statement is an impartial analysis of Measure “Z”. If you desire a copy of the Measure, please call the Office of the City Clerk at (530) 661-5806 and a copy will be mailed at no cost to you.

[#tabfd22f24a-36e8-4b9d-8175-77ad82527dde_2] Argument In Favor Of Measure Z
Vote YES on Measure Z to protect Woodland’s environment, secure our water resources, and help stabilize utility bills—without expanding City limits!
In 2006, Woodland voters voted to establish an Urban Limit Line to protect open-space and local agriculture from sprawl and overdevelopment. Measure Z would maintain the Urban Limit Line’s key protections while providing a common-sense, environmentally sound update to allow for the limited delivery of City water and wastewater services just outside the line, resulting in major benefits to local families.
$2 Million in Direct Community Benefits: Measure Z would allow private investment to fund the expansion of Woodland's recycled water system to serve our parks, schools, and the cemetery, offsetting potable water use and reducing the likelihood that water restrictions are ever imposed on existing residents.
Protecting Our Shared Aquifer: Measure Z protects the critical groundwater resources that residents and family farmers rely on by reducing the need for several large businesses to pump groundwater from local aquifers.
Discouraging Septic Systems: Old septic systems pose a constant risk to our local water quality. Measure Z would allow neighboring properties to connect to the City’s modern wastewater system, eliminating septic systems that could contaminate groundwater and harm our environment.
Potential for Rate Stabilization: Under Measure Z, new corporate water and wastewater customers would pay for 100% of their infrastructure and utility costs, expanding Woodland’s service base and helping share the fixed operating costs of our utility systems. As a result, Measure Z could stabilize water and wastewater fees for all current Woodland ratepayers.
Prevents Sprawl and Overdevelopment: Measure Z maintains the key Urban Limit Line protections that strictly prohibit new residential development, protecting our agricultural heritage and preventing urban sprawl.
Join Woodland families, environmental advocates, and local farmers in voting YES on Measure Z!
