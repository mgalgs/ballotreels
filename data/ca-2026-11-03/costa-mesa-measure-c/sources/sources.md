## Quoted passages

- Question (p.1): "Without taxing residents, shall the measure be adopted, updating Costa Mesa’s 41-year-old business license tax, ensuring large businesses pay their proportionate share and helping fund general city services including keeping public areas safe/clean; preventing crime/burglaries; addressing homelessness; maintaining Costa Mesa’s financial stability; revising the tax rate to 50¢ per $1,000 gross receipts, with no increase for small businesses; generating approximately $5,600,000 annually until ended by voters, requiring audits, spending disclosure, funds locally controlled?"
- Who placed it (p.3): "The Measure was placed on the ballot by the City Council."
- Analysis (p.3): "Small businesses, those making $500,000 or less, would not pay any more tax than they do currently. The Measure would update the business license tax for businesses making more than $500,000 annual gross receipts, up to a maximum amount of $15,000 per year for businesses earning $30 million per year or more. The existing maximum tax is $200 per year no matter how much a business earns"
- Analysis (p.3): "Business license tax receipts in the City are currently approximately $1 million."
- Analysis (p.3): "The Measure is anticipated to raise an additional $4.6 million dollars annually, to be placed into the general fund"
- Analysis (p.3): "A “no” vote would leave the existing business license tax in place and maintain the existing $200 cap adopted in 1985."

Note: the ballot question says "approximately $5,600,000 annually"; the impartial analysis says "an additional $4.6 million dollars annually" and that current receipts are "approximately $1 million". The official record does not reconcile the two figures further; both are quoted as printed.
