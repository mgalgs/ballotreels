Costa Mesa Measure C asks city voters whether to raise the business license tax for businesses earning more than $500,000 a year.
Today, no business pays more than $200 a year, under a cap set in 1985.
The tax is charged to businesses, not to residents, and the City Council placed Measure C on the ballot.
Businesses earning $500,000 a year or less would pay no more than now, which the impartial analysis says is over 72 percent of them.
Larger businesses would pay 50 cents per $1,000 of gross receipts, up to $15,000 a year.
The city attorney's impartial analysis says the tax brings in about $1 million today, and the measure would raise an additional $4.6 million a year.
The ballot question says the updated tax would generate about $5.6 million a year.
No argument against Measure C was submitted, so none is printed in the official Orange County Voter Information Guide.
Supporters say license costs have not risen since 1985, and a $1 million business would pay $500 a year.
A yes vote approves the reform, leaving the tax unchanged for small businesses and setting a new rate for those earning over $500,000.
A no vote leaves the existing tax and its $200 cap from 1985 in place.
