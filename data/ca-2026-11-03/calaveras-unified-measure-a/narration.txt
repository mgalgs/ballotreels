Measure A asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $66,800,000 in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them from a tax on property in the district.
Measure A needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money could go to projects such as modernizing classrooms, renovating career training facilities, and replacing leaky roofs and old heating and cooling systems.
The district's tax rate statement estimates $49.44 a year per $100,000 of assessed value, on average, through 2060-2061.
The statement estimates the highest rate at $58.03 per $100,000, starting in 2039-2040.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $161.6 million.
No argument against Measure A was filed, so the county voter pamphlet prints only the argument in favor of the measure.
Supporters say the district's schools, nearly 55 years old on average, must be upgraded, and Measure A would not raise tax rates.
A yes vote would authorize the district to finance school improvements by issuing the bonds.
A no vote is a vote against authorizing the district to issue the bonds.
