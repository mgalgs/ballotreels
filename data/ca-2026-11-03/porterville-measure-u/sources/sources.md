## Facts and quoted passages

- **Ballot question** (July 21 replacement resolution, Section 3, packet p. 832; identical wording in the June 16 resolution, p. 528):
  > Shall the City Charter be amended to authorize the City Council to determine whether public works, maintenance, repair, improvement, and construction activities are performed by City forces, through contracts, or through agreements with other public agencies, and to authorize the City to establish purchasing and contracting procedures?
- **Who votes:** voters of the City of Porterville. Section 2: "there is called and ordered to be held in the City of Porterville on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, a General Municipal Election for the purpose of submitting a City Council charter amendment proposal to the voters for approval."
- **Vote needed:** Section 5: "Passage of this measure requires a regular majority of votes."
- **History of the resolutions** (July 21 staff report, p. 829): "On June 16, 2026, the City Council adopted Resolution No. 44-2026 submitting a proposed amendment to Section 61 of the City Charter relating to Local Control of Public Works and Procurement Authority ... The attached replacement resolutions rescind and replace Resolution Nos. 44-2026 and 45-2026 in their entirety. The revised resolutions incorporate the additional election requirements requested by the Tulare County Registrar of Voters while preserving the ballot questions and Charter amendment language previously approved by the City Council."
- **Adoption of the July 21 replacement:** NOT confirmed. The minutes of July 21 were not found in the August packets I checked (Aug 4 packet; Aug 18 and Sep 1 face sheets).
- **What the new Section 61 says** (Exhibit A, "VOTER INFORMATION PAMPHLET BALLOT TEXT", p. 836):
  > The City shall have the power to perform any work of improvement, replacement, repair or maintenance by use of its own forces and is not required to contract for such work, including the construction of such work. The City may also contract with other public agencies for such work.
  > The City shall have the power to establish standards, procedures, rules, or regulations related to the purchasing of goods, property, or services.
- **What it replaces:** Section 4 (pp. 832-833) prints the existing Section 61 "with deletions of existing text shown in strike out and additions shown in underlining". The strike-out is lost in a text extraction; read the PDF. Existing text includes: "When a required expenditure exceeds five thousand dollars, it shall be contracted for and let to the lowest responsible bidder after notice."
- **Cost** (June 16 staff report, PDF p. 526): "the cost associated with placing each Charter amendment before the voters is anticipated to be approximately $35,000. Should both proposed Charter amendments be submitted to the voters, the total estimated election cost would be approximately $70,000."
- **Impartial analysis assigned to City Attorney** (Section 7): "The City Attorney is directed to prepare an impartial analysis of the proposal, not to exceed 500 words in length".
- **Council argument authorized** (Section 9): "the City Council authorizes the Mayor, or the Mayor's designee, to file a written argument on behalf of the City Council regarding the measure".
