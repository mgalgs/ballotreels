Measure RC asks the voters of this city whether to adopt a city charter.
Today, the city is a general law city, with only the powers state law gives it.
The City Council placed Measure RC on the ballot.
A charter would give the city more control over local affairs, but state law would still govern matters of statewide concern.
Council members, who have no term limits today, would be limited to five four-year terms, counting only future terms.
The city could create a city prosecutor to charge violations of city laws and certain state misdemeanors.
The city could spend general fund money on economic development.
The city could set its own contract rules, including extra points for all veterans, not only disabled veterans.
Council pay and elections would still follow state law.
The charter could be amended only by a majority of voters, the same vote Measure RC needs to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Opponents say five four-year terms, 20 years, are not real term limits, and that all of these items can be handled through city ordinances.
Supporters say the charter lets the city create its own prosecutor for city law violations, and gives it new tools to grow the economy.
A yes vote adopts the charter, making this a charter city.
A no vote rejects the charter, and the city remains a general law city.
