CITY ATTORNEY IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE RC

Measure RC, which was placed on the November 3, 2026, ballot by the City Council, asks whether voters wish to adopt a charter for the City of Rosemead (“City”). If approved, the City would become a “charter city” where the proposed charter would operate as the City’s “constitution”.

California has two kinds of cities: general law and charter. General law cities are governed by general law—meaning State law—and only have those powers defined by the State Legislature. The California Constitution authorizes charter cities. Charter cities have greater control over their “municipal affairs”, but must follow general laws regarding matters of “statewide concern”, which include laws relating to open meetings, public records, environmental protection, bargaining with employee associations, and certain housing and zoning matters. All cities must comply with U.S. and California constitutions. Although the State Legislature may define what constitutes “statewide concern”, courts ultimately decide whether a matter is a “statewide concern”.

If adopted, the proposed charter would:

1. Form of Government: Continue the City’s council-manager form of government.

2. City Council Term Limits: Impose term limits of five consecutive or non-consecutive four-year terms on City Councilmembers, which would apply prospectively, with partial terms counting as full terms. Although general law cities may impose term limits, the City currently has no term limits.

3. City Prosecutor: Authorize the City to establish the office of City Prosecutor, which would have the authority to prosecute violations of the City’s ordinances and certain State misdemeanors. As a general matter, general law cities only have the authority to prosecute violations of their ordinances and certain crimes with consent from the District Attorney.

4. Economic Development: Authorize the City to utilize its general fund revenues upon economic development in the City. General law cities have limited authority to support economic development projects.

5. Purchasing and Contracts; Veterans Preferences: Authorize the City to adopt its own purchasing and contracting procedures by ordinance, rather than following State requirements. This includes purchasing and contracting that give veterans and disabled veterans a credit of additional points when the City is awarding contracts. General law cities may only grant preferences to disabled veterans.

The charter would not affect Councilmember compensation or elections. If adopted, the charter can only be amended by a majority vote of the City’s voters.

A “YES” vote on Measure RC is a vote to adopt the charter and become a charter law city.

A “NO” vote on Measure RC is a vote against adopting the charter and the City would remain a general law city.

This Measure will be effective only if a majority (50% + 1) of voters voting on the Measure vote in its favor.
