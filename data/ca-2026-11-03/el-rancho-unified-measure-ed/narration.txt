Measure ED asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $118 million in school bonds.
The district's governing board placed Measure ED on the ballot, and it needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay to update heating and air conditioning, replace plumbing and roofs, improve school safety, and modernize classrooms.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would be required.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $54.19 per $100,000 of assessed value, and a highest of $60, through 2057-2058.
The statement puts the total to repay, with interest, at about $246.5 million, and says its figures are estimates, not maximum amounts.
Supporters say the measure keeps schools safe and functional before repairs become costly emergencies, and the funds cannot be taken by the state.
The official list of measures from the county, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure ED was submitted.
A yes vote approves the $118 million in school bonds.
A no vote rejects the $118 million in school bonds.
