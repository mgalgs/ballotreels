# Proposition G — Allowing Private Vehicles on the Upper Great Highway in Sunset Dunes Park

- **Jurisdiction:** City and County of San Francisco
- **Kind:** Initiative ordinance (amends the Park Code), placed on the ballot by initiative petition
- **Who votes:** all registered San Francisco voters (consolidated city and county; one electorate)
- **Vote threshold** (VIP PDF p. 110): "This measure requires 50%+1 affirmative votes to pass."

## Ballot question (p. 110)

> Shall the City close Sunset Dunes Park from Monday at 4:00 a.m. through Friday at 6:00 p.m., unless a holiday falls on a weekday, in order to reopen the roadway known as the Upper Great Highway to private vehicles during that time?

## Digest by the Ballot Simplification Council (p. 110)

**The Way It Is Now:**
> Sunset Dunes Park is an open recreational space that replaced the Great Highway between Lincoln Way and Sloat Boulevard (Upper Great Highway) and is closed to private vehicles at all times. Sunset Dunes Park (former Upper Great Highway) is open to emergency vehicles, official government vehicles, intra-park transit shuttle buses, and similar authorized vehicles.

**The Proposal:**
> Proposition G would close Sunset Dunes Park from Monday at 4:00 a.m. through Friday at 6:00 p.m., unless a holiday falls on a weekday, in order to reopen the roadway known as the Upper Great Highway to private vehicles during that time.
>
> The Upper Great Highway will remain open to emergency vehicles, official government vehicles, intra-park transit shuttle buses, and similar authorized vehicles.
>
> Proposition G would require the City to obtain all necessary approvals, including any permits required by the California Coastal Act or from other government agencies, to reopen the roadway known as the Upper Great Highway to private vehicles.

**A "YES" Vote Means:**
> If you vote "yes," you want to close Sunset Dunes Park from Monday at 4:00 a.m. through Friday at 6:00 p.m., unless a holiday falls on a weekday, in order to reopen the roadway known as the Upper Great Highway to private vehicles during that time.

**A "NO" Vote Means:**
> If you vote "no," you do not want the City to make these changes.

## Controller's Statement on "G" (pp. 110–111)

Note: this statement is issued by the Deputy City Controller.
> Deputy City Controller ChiaYu Ma has issued the following statement on the fiscal impact of Proposition G:
> Should the proposed Ordinance be approved by the voters and the roadway is open to private vehicles, in my opinion, it would significantly increase the cost of government by approximately $9.8 million in one-time capital costs and by approximately $500,000 to $1.9 million annually. (p. 110)

Dollar figures (p. 111):
> Depending on future operational decisions made by City departments, the proposed Ordinance could result in annual cost increases ranging from $500,000 to $1.9 million. This includes traffic signal maintenance costs, annualized roadway maintenance and replacement costs, and an additional cost for sand removal and dune reshaping.

> This would include approximately $750,000 to remove existing park elements and restore roadway segments, $8.8 million in new traffic signals at 8 intersections, and $200,000 for temporary and permanent restriping and signage.

> For context, staffing at Sunset Dunes Park costs approximately $300,000 annually for 1.5 full-time-equivalent positions.

> Finally, the proposed Ordinance would require a coastal development permit.

## How "G" Got on the Ballot (p. 111)

> On July 23, 2026, the Department of Elections certified that the initiative petition calling for Proposition G to be placed on the ballot had a sufficient number of valid signatures to qualify the measure for the ballot.
> 10,615 signatures were required to place an initiative ordinance on the ballot. This number is equal to 2% of the registered voters at the time a "Notice of Intent to Circulate Petition" was published. A random check of the signatures submitted by the proponents of the initiative petition prior to the July 6, 2026, submission deadline showed that the total number of valid signatures was greater than the number required.

## Arguments (pp. 112–113)

Every argument page carries: "Arguments are the opinions of the authors and have not been checked for accuracy by any official agency." and "Arguments are printed as submitted. Spelling and grammatical errors have not been corrected."

**Proponent's Argument** (p. 112). Signer: Richard Corriea, SFPD Commander Ret.
> Yes on G restores the compromise in place from 2021 to 2024: it keeps the Great Highway closed on weekends, allowing access to it as a park, but opens it to vehicle travel Monday through Friday.

**Rebuttal to Proponent's Argument** (p. 112). Signers: Outer Sunset Neighbors; Sunset Merchants Association; Sunset Parent Advocates.
> Prop G is a park closure, not a compromise. Prop G removes every park feature added since Sunset Dunes opened — the playground, the skate park, and every bench.

**Opponent's Argument** (p. 113). Signers: Senator Scott Wiener; Supervisor Matt Dorsey; Supervisor Bilal Mahmood; Supervisor Rafael Mandelman; Supervisor Myrna Melgar; Supervisor Danny Sauter; Sierra Club.
> Proposition G asks you to spend nearly $10 million to demolish a coastal park (plus up to $1.9 million every year for added roadway maintenance), to remove all amenities, and to end the nearly 60% of visits that are on weekdays.

**Rebuttal to Opponent's Argument** (p. 113). Signer: Richard Corriea, SFPD Commander Ret.
> More than 16,000 San Franciscans signed the petition to put Prop G on the ballot.

> Prop G doesn't "destroy" a park. It gives us both.
