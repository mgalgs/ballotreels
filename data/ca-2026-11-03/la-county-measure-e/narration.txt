Los Angeles County Measure E asks county voters whether to change the County Charter on ethics and community investment.
Today, the county has an ethics commission and sets aside at least 10 percent of locally raised general revenue for community investment.
Measure E would make the ethics commission and its office more independent.
The commission could investigate violations of county ethics laws and impose penalties.
The set-aside would continue after an elected county executive takes office in 2028.
The Auditor-Controller says the county must budget at least $14.3 million a year for ethics oversight, from new revenue, other programs, or both.
No argument against Measure E was submitted, so the official materials print only the supporters' argument.
Supporters say an independent ethics commission would have power to prevent corruption and hold officials accountable.
A yes vote means a more independent ethics commission, and the set-aside continues.
A no vote means the commission stays as it is, and the set-aside is not required after 2028.
