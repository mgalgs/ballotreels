There are many ways to contract for public construction projects, but the outdated City of Santa Clara Charter limits us to only one: Design-Bid-Build (DBB). Under DBB, a project must be fully designed before it is bid out to contractors. A fixed-price contract is awarded to the lowest responsible bidder. While this process works well for many smaller projects, it often delays complex projects because every detail must be designed before construction can begin. Contractors usually price unknown risks into their bids or decide not to bid at all. The result can be fewer bidders, higher costs, and unnecessary delays.

Measure ______ allows the City of Santa Clara to use additional proven contracting methods alongside DBB that can help deliver projects faster and more efficiently while protecting taxpayers. The most common alternative is Design-Build (DB), whereby one qualified team designs and constructs the project. Contracts are awarded based on experience, expertise, and proven performance. Construction can begin before every design detail is finalized, reducing delays. Costs remain transparent through City oversight, and project risks are shared between the contractor and the City. DB is already permitted for all California General Law cities and many Charter cities like San Jose.

The City is preparing to deliver more than $400 million in critical infrastructure improvements, many of them large and technically complex. Smaller, simpler projects would continue to use the traditional DBB process. Measure ____ simply gives Santa Clara the flexibility to choose the contracting method best suited to each project.

In 2009, former City Manager, Don Von Raesfeld, led a Charter committee that recommended design-build be put to the voters.

City Council voted 7:0 to put this measure on the ballot.

Vote YES to help Santa Clara complete major projects such as the ISC rebuild, more efficiently, more competitively, and with greater flexibility.
