Measure D asks the voters of this city whether the city may take part in the California State Water Project without voter approval.
The State Water Project is a system of reservoirs and aqueducts that stores and delivers water across California.
Under a 1990 measure, the city may not take part in the project without majority voter approval.
A 2016 measure lets the city buy state water without a vote only during a local water emergency the council declares.
Measure D would repeal and replace both, so a city council vote could approve taking part and buying state water.
Measure D needs a simple majority to pass.
The county voter guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
No argument against Measure D appears in the county voter guide, which prints only the one argument in favor of the measure.
Supporters say local water supplies are not reliable, and this gives the city flexibility to get water from different sources for dry years.
A yes vote lets the city take part in the State Water Project without voter approval or a declared water emergency.
A no vote keeps the need for voter approval or a declared water emergency.
