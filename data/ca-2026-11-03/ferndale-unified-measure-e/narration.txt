Measure E asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $7 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them, with interest, from a tax on property in the district.
Measure E needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would build and upgrade classrooms, labs and other buildings for career technical education.
State law caps this kind of tax at $60 per $100,000 of assessed value for a unified school district.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $49.70 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $49.95, until 2058-2059.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $15.4 million.
The county voter guide prints only the argument in favor of Measure E, and none against it.
Supporters say many labs and classrooms are outdated, and bonds give the local match for state grants.
A yes vote allows up to $7 million in bonds for the school projects in the measure.
A no vote does not authorize the bonds.
