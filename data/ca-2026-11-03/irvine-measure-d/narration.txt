Irvine Measure D asks city voters whether to use ranked choice voting to elect the mayor and City Council.
Today, Irvine voters pick one candidate, and whoever gets the most votes wins, even without a majority.
With ranked choice, voters rank the candidates in order of preference.
If no one has a majority, the last-place candidate is dropped and those ballots go to their next choice.
The switch would come after January 1, 2028, if voting equipment can handle it and the added cost stays under a cap.
The Orange County Voter Information Guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
The city attorney's impartial analysis says the cap is 0.23 percent of the general fund: about $717,790 of a $312.1 million budget in 2026-2027.
Supporters say ranked choice guarantees that winners have majority support, and a strict cost limit protects taxpayers.
Opponents say no one knows what ranked choice will cost, while Irvine faces a serious budget crisis.
A yes vote is in favor of moving to ranked choice after January 1, 2028, if the conditions are met.
A no vote is against adopting the measure.
