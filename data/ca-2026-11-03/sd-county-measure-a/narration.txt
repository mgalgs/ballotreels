San Diego County Measure A asks county voters whether to make several changes to the County Charter.
The charter sets out how county government is organized, and only voters can change it.
Today, a county supervisor can serve at most two terms, or eight years.
Measure A would allow three terms instead, or twelve years, counting time already served.
Measure A would also create an ethics commission, and let the board appoint an independent budget analyst and program auditor.
The board would confirm some senior county officials, and all county spending data would be posted online.
The San Diego County voter guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say Measure A fights corruption with an independent ethics commission, and adds watchdogs over county money.
Opponents say Measure A undoes the two-term limit voters set, giving supervisors twelve years instead of eight.
A yes vote is a vote in favor of the proposed charter amendments.
A no vote is a vote against amending the charter.
