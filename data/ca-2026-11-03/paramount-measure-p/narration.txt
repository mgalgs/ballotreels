Measure P asks the voters of this city whether to tax commercial and industrial property each year, by the square foot, for set purposes.
The City Council placed Measure P on the ballot by a unanimous vote.
Commercial, industrial and vacant parcels would each have a top yearly rate per square foot, and residential property would not be taxed.
The rates would phase in over three years, then could rise by up to 3 percent a year for inflation.
As a special tax, the money could pay only for roads, public safety, environmental services and business programs.
An oversight committee would monitor the money, and the tax would last until ended by voters.
Measure P needs two-thirds of the votes to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis says the city estimates about $4 million a year in new local revenue.
The mayor, the sheriff, two organizations and a business owner filed an argument for Measure P; its text was not gathered.
The official county list of measures, which names who filed each argument, says that no argument against Measure P was submitted.
A yes vote is to adopt the parcel tax ordinance.
A no vote is to not adopt the parcel tax ordinance.
