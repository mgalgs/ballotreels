Measure O asks the voters of this city whether to add a parcel tax for police, recreation and park upkeep.
Voters had approved an earlier parcel tax for police and fire, but rejected continuing it in March 2024, and it ended that November.
Each single-family home would pay $125 a year, and each apartment unit $140, with other parcels taxed by the acre.
The rates would rise 3 percent each year, and the tax would have no end date.
Seventy percent of the money would go to police services, and the rest to parks and recreation.
A citizen oversight committee would check that the money is spent as voters intended.
Measure O needs two-thirds of the votes to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis estimates that the tax would raise about $460,907 a year.
Opponents say the tax would never end and rises 3 percent every year, making life harder for families.
Supporters say Measure O prevents police cuts that could slow response times, and keeps parks and recreation going.
A yes vote adopts the parcel tax, at $125 a year for each single-family home.
A no vote does not adopt the parcel tax.
