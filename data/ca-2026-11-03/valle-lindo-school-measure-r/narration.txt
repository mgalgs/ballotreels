Measure R asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $9.2 million in school bonds.
The district's governing board placed Measure R on the ballot, and it needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay to replace heating and air conditioning, modernize classrooms and restrooms, and repair or replace roofs.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would be required.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $29 per $100,000 of assessed value, and a highest of $30, through 2060-2061.
The statement puts the total to repay, with interest, at about $18.7 million, and says its figures are estimates only, which may vary.
The official list of measures from the county, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure R was submitted.
Supporters say many classrooms and school facilities are outdated and inadequate, and bond funds must be spent locally, not taken by the state.
A yes vote approves the $9.2 million in school bonds.
A no vote rejects the $9.2 million in school bonds.
