Measure B asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $29.9 million in school bonds.
The district reaches into two counties, and its voters in both decide Measure B.
The district would repay the bonds from a tax on property in the district, and Measure B needs 55 percent to pass.
The money could go only to listed projects, such as roofs, plumbing, sewers, heating and classrooms.
The district's tax statement estimates an average tax of $46 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $49, through 2061-2062.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at $58.6 million.
No argument against Measure B was filed by the July deadline, so only an argument in favor appears.
Supporters say Measure B upgrades outdated classrooms, and its funds stay local, audited by an independent citizens' committee.
A yes vote authorizes the bonds and the property tax to repay them.
A no vote is a vote against the bonds and the property tax.
