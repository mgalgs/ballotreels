San Francisco Proposition B asks voters whether to let the city set up a municipal finance corporation and a public bank.
A municipal finance corporation is a government-owned nonprofit that makes loans but takes no deposits.
A public bank also makes loans, and takes deposits.
No local law lets San Francisco create either one.
Proposition B would let the city start the corporation if it has enough funding; after three years, it could become a public bank.
Both would lend for affordable housing, homeownership and small businesses.
The City Controller says Proposition B itself would not directly affect city costs.
If the city goes ahead, the Controller cites costs between $310 million and $460 million over eight years; the General Fund is about $7 billion a year.
Supporters say Proposition B puts the people of San Francisco over private profits, with a bank run by professionals, not politicians.
Opponents say Proposition B would create a city-controlled bank without any dedicated funding, and would leave taxpayers holding the bag later.
A yes vote means you want to authorize both.
A no vote means you do not want to authorize them.
