- **Kind:** Charter amendment, placed on the ballot by the Board of Supervisors
- **Who votes:** all registered San Francisco voters (consolidated city and county; one electorate)
- **Vote threshold** (VIP PDF p. 72): "This measure requires 50%+1 affirmative votes to pass."

## Ballot question (p. 72)

> Shall the City amend the Charter to authorize the City to establish a municipal finance corporation and a public bank?

## Digest by the Ballot Simplification Council (p. 72)

**The Way It Is Now:**
> A municipal finance corporation (MFC) is a government-owned non-profit corporation that makes loans but does not take deposits. A public bank is a government-owned non-profit corporation that both makes loans and takes deposits.
>
> There is no local law authorizing the City to create a municipal finance corporation or a public bank.

**The Proposal:**
> Proposition B would amend the City Charter to authorize the City to establish a municipal finance corporation if the City has sufficient funding and resources. After three years of operation, the municipal finance corporation may become a public bank.
>
> The MFC would make loans but would not take deposits. If the MFC becomes a public bank, the public bank would make loans and also take deposits.
>
> The MFC and public bank would provide services to support:
> • affordable housing development,
> • affordable homeownership,
> • small business development,
> • environmental sustainability, and
> • environmental justice.
>
> These services would be available to individual City residents and to projects and businesses located in the City.
>
> Under Proposition B, the MFC and public bank would each be governed by a board of directors. Each board would be appointed and overseen by its own oversight commission. The members of each oversight commission would be appointed by the Board of Supervisors, Mayor, Treasurer-Tax Collector, Controller, and City Attorney.

**A "YES" Vote Means:**
> If you vote "yes," you want to authorize the City to establish a municipal finance corporation and a public bank.

**A "NO" Vote Means:**
> If you vote "no," you do not want to authorize the City to establish a municipal finance corporation and a public bank.

## Controller's Statement on "B" (pp. 72–73)

> City Controller Greg Wagner has issued the following statement on the fiscal impact of Proposition B:
> Should the proposed Charter amendment be approved by the voters, in my opinion, it would not directly impact the cost of government as the Charter amendment does not require the City to create a Municipal Finance Corporation (MFC) that could transition into a Public Bank. However, if the City moves forward with establishing a MFC and subsequently a Public Bank, as authorized by the proposed Charter amendment, the costs would be significant, ranging from approximately $310 million to $460 million over an eight-year period, according to 2023 Reinvestment Working Group (RWG) estimates, which could be significantly higher or lower depending on interest rates and changing economic conditions. (p. 72)

Dollar figures (p. 73):
> The funding required to set up an MFC could cost approximately $4 million annually for approximately one year or longer, depending on when the MFC is set up, excluding capitalization costs.

> According to a 2023 report of the RWG, costs to capitalize and fund the MFC could cost approximately $26 million in the first year and increase to $30 million annually by the third year for a total of $90 million for the first three years of the MFC.

> The cost to operate a Public Bank would be approximately $80 million in year 4, decreasing to approximately $30 to $40 million annually for a total of approximately $220 million over years four through eight.

> Finally, the MFC and Public Bank would require an MFC Oversight Commission or Bank Oversight Commission which could cost between $550,000 to $600,000 annually for staff salaries, commissioner stipends, and operating costs. There is a high degree of uncertainty in the cost to government of establishing an MFC or Public Bank.

> Note that the proposed amendment would change the duties of the Controller's Office, which has prepared this statement.

## Arguments (pp. 74–75)

Every argument page carries: "Arguments are the opinions of the authors and have not been checked for accuracy by any official agency." and "Arguments are printed as submitted. Spelling and grammatical errors have not been corrected."

**Proponent's Argument** (p. 74). Signers: The San Francisco Foundation; San Francisco Public Bank Coalition; District 11 Supervisor Chyanne Chen; District 5 Supervisor Bilal Mahmood; District 7 Supervisor Myrna Melgar; District 8 Supervisor Rafael Mandelman; District 9 Supervisor Jackie Fielder; District 10 Supervisor Shamann Walton; San Francisco Labor Council; San Francisco Climate Emergency Coalition; Affordable Housing Alliance; San Francisco Tenants Union.
> A Public Bank puts the people of San Francisco over private profits to finance the things we need.

> Prop B establishes the purpose and structure of a public bank. Prop B is not a tax, and will not raise taxes for San Franciscans.

**Rebuttal to Proponent's Argument** (p. 74). Signer: "Larry S. Marso, Esq." (described as "a Nob Hill resident" who "ran financial institution M&A practices at two Wall Street investment banks, represented banks and insurers as a New York attorney, and served as an economic analyst at the Federal Reserve Board").
> A "public bank" is a politically directed lending machine that puts billions of taxpayer dollars at risk.

**Opponent's Argument** (p. 75). Signers: "— No on Prop B – San Franciscans for Fiscal Responsibility — Leslie Huang, Dorothy Joan Leone, Stephanie Jeong, and Kathleen Louise Trewin".
> Proposition B asks San Francisco voters to create a City Hall-controlled public bank and municipal finance corporation — without any dedicated funding, without proven demand, and at a time when the City already faces massive budget shortfalls.

**Rebuttal to Opponent's Argument** (p. 75). Signers: Matt Dorsey, Supervisor; Chyanne Chen, Supervisor; San Francisco Labor Council; San Francisco Public Bank Coalition; The San Francisco Foundation.
> Instead, it would be the most regulated bank in America, subject to all typical banking regulations of private banks, in addition to California regulations and the oversight systems of Prop B.

## Legal text (pp. 183–189)

Text of the proposed Charter amendment. First lines (p. 183):
> Proposition B
> Describing and setting forth a proposal to the voters at an election to be held on November 3, 2026, to amend the Charter of the City and County of San Francisco to authorize the establishment of a Municipal Finance Corporation and Public Bank as nonprofit corporations, and to set forth the mission, principles, and governance structure of those corporations.
