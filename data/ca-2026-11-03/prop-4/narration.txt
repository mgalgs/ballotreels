Proposition 4 is about public money for election campaigns.
Today, state law bans public money for most campaigns, though some charter cities are exempt.
Proposition 4 would lift the ban, letting governments create such programs.
Programs couldn't use education, transportation or public safety money, and candidates must show broad support and accept spending limits.
The Legislative Analyst's Office says the state would spend a few hundred thousand dollars a year.
It says any programs governments create could have significant costs.
Supporters say public funding would let qualified candidates compete without relying on wealthy donors.
Opponents say Proposition 4 is a blank check of taxpayer money for politicians' campaigns.
A yes vote means state and local governments could create public campaign financing programs, subject to limits.
A no vote means state and most local governments would remain unable to create public campaign finance programs.
