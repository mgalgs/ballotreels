Measure Y asks the voters of this city whether to adopt a limited city charter.
A charter city may govern itself on local matters, while state law still governs matters of statewide concern.
Today, the city is a general law city, so its powers are set by the general laws of the state.
The charter would give the city one new power: to set a tax charged when real property is sold.
That tax is Measure Z, which takes effect only if voters approve both Measures Y and Z.
The ballot question says the charter keeps the council-manager form of government the city has now.
Measure Y needs a simple majority to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Opponents say the charter only looks limited, opening the door to major government and tax changes, and the city can seek to expand it anytime.
Supporters say the charter is narrow, made only so voters can decide on Measure Z, and the current council-manager form of government stays in place.
A yes vote adopts the city charter.
A no vote keeps the city a general law city.
