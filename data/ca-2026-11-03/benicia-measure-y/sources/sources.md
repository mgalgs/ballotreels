## Facts and quotes

**Ballot question** (Correction to Notice of Measures – Benicia Measures, 8/10/2026, https://content.solanocounty.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/Corrected_Notice_of_Measures_Benicia_XYZ.pdf): "Shall the measure, adopting a limited City Charter for the City of Benicia that allows the real property transfer tax in Measure Z to take effect if voters approve both Measures Y and Z, and continues the City's existing council-manager form of government, be adopted?"

**Who votes / vote needed (analysis):** voters of the City of Benicia. "Measure Y will be adopted if it is approved by a majority vote of the electorate."

**Impartial analysis** ("City Attorney's Impartial Analysis of Measure Y", submitted August 11, 2026, Benjamin Stock, City Attorney), key statements:
- "Measure Y asks voters to adopt the City of Benicia Charter ... The proposed Charter specifically limits the City's new power over municipal affairs solely with respect to the power to enact a system for the imposition, levy and collection of a tax on the conveyance of real property."
- "The City is currently a general law city which means the powers of the City are governed by the general laws established by the State Legislature or enacted by voter initiative."
- "A "yes" vote on Measure Y adopts the City of Benicia Charter. A "no" vote on Measure Y will maintain the City of Benicia as a general law city."
- "If Measure Y is approved by a majority of voters and the Charter is adopted, any future amendment or expansion of the scope of the Charter would be subject to voter approval."
- "The City's existing council-manager form of government would continue unless changed by the voters, and the proposed Charter does not give the City Council any new powers to raise its compensation or that of other City officials without voter approval."
- "Measure Y was placed on the ballot by the City Council of the City of Benicia."
