Measure U asks the voters of this recreation district whether to approve a special tax on building space to fund its programs and facilities.
The district provides recreation programs, a swimming pool, parks and related services to the people within its boundaries.
Measure U needs two-thirds of the vote to pass.
Starting in 2027, each parcel with a building would pay a yearly tax on each square foot of building space, and land with no building would pay nothing.
From 2028, the board could raise the rate each year with consumer prices, by no more than 3 percent a year.
The tax would continue until the district's voters repeal it.
The money could pay only for the pool and other facilities, staffing, recreation programs, and improvements and reserves for the district's facilities.
The impartial analysis estimates the tax would raise about $292,000 a year.
Supporters say the district's main local funding dates from 1962 while costs keep rising, and the tax would help keep the pool open and programs affordable.
No argument against Measure U appears among the official documents gathered for this video; the county list of local measures links only the argument in favor.
A yes vote is in favor of a special tax to fund the district's recreation programs, pool services, upkeep, staffing and long-term needs.
A no vote is against the special tax.
