Measure Q asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $16 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them, with interest, from a tax on property in the district.
Measure Q needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money could pay only for school buildings, grounds and equipment, on the projects the measure lists.
The ballot question names roofs and windows, career technical education buildings, electrical and air-conditioning systems, and safety repairs.
The district's tax information statement estimates a tax of $16.28 per $100,000 of assessed value each year, through 2059-2060.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $33.6 million.
The Local Voter Information Guide for this election says that no argument against Measure Q was filed, and the guide prints none.
Supporters say Measure Q repairs classrooms, some over 75 years old, without raising tax rates, and qualifies the district for state matching funds.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue up to $16 million in bonds for the projects in the measure.
A no vote means the district would not be authorized to issue the bonds.
