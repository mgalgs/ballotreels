Argument in Favor of Measure ___

In 2005 San Bruno voters approved the sale and use of “Safe and Sane” fireworks. Since then, State Fire Marshal maps have identified much of San Bruno is located within high fire hazard severity zones, requiring additional precautions from property owners and increased inspection responsibilities for the Fire Department. In this environment, even state-approved fireworks present fire risks that cannot be completely eliminated.

In recent years, fireworks related incidents have resulted in between 5 and 11 fires annually, including exterior and structural fires. One incident caused an estimated $200,000 in damage to a single home. These events place strain on emergency response resources, increase risk to residents and firefighters, and create preventable hazards during a time of heightened regional wildfire concern.

Across the Bay Area, most communities have already moved away from consumer fireworks. San Bruno and Pacifica are now the only cities in San Mateo County that still permit “Safe and Sane” fireworks. Ending their sale and use would align San Bruno with current regional public safety practices.

It is important to acknowledge that several local nonprofit organizations have historically relied on fireworks sales as a seasonal fundraising opportunity. Their contributions to the community are valuable, and the loss of this revenue source is a meaningful consideration. However, the measure focuses on balancing that impact with the broader responsibility to reduce fire risk, protect homes, and support the safety of residents and first responders.

This measure simply asks voters whether the City should repeal the existing ordinance and prohibit “Safe and Sane” fireworks. It is a practical, safety-focused decision.
