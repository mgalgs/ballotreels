Regional Measure RTM asks voters in San Francisco and four other Bay Area counties whether to add a sales tax for public transit.
The measure needs a majority of the votes in all five counties combined.
In 2025, the Legislature created a five-county transit district, whose voters put this tax on the ballot by petition.
Measure RTM would add a 1 percent sales tax in San Francisco, and half a percent in the other counties, for 14 years.
Of the money raised in San Francisco, about 63 percent would go to Muni, and 29 percent to BART.
The District's impartial analysis estimates the tax would raise about $980 million a year from all five counties.
Opponents say Measure RTM makes life in San Francisco less affordable, by raising its sales tax rate to 9.625 percent.
Supporters say without Measure RTM, Muni could cut service by at least 30 percent, and BART could close 15 stations.
A yes vote is a vote to impose the tax for 14 years.
A no vote is a vote against imposing the tax.
