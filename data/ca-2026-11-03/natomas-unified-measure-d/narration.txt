Natomas Unified School District Measure D asks the district's voters whether to approve $300 million in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
Measure D needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would repair aging classrooms, electrical systems and leaky roofs, and improve school safety and access for students with disabilities.
The district's tax statement estimates an average tax of $59.38 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $60, through 2053-2054.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $600 million.
Supporters say Measure D repairs classrooms and expands career training, without raising tax rates.
No argument against Measure D was filed with Sacramento County Voter Registration and Elections.
A yes vote allows the district to issue up to $300 million in bonds.
A no vote does not allow the district to issue the proposed bonds.
