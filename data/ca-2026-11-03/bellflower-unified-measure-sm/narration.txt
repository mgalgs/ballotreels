Measure SM asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $135 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them from a yearly tax on property in the district.
Measure SM needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay only for the school building projects the measure lists, not for salaries or other operating costs.
Those include repairing roofs, plumbing, heating and electrical systems, upgrading classrooms and labs, and improving disability access.
The district's tax rate statement estimates the tax at $45.78 per $100,000 of assessed value on average, and $49 at its highest.
The statement expects the tax to be collected through 2057-2058.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $240.9 million.
According to the county registrar's official list of measures, no argument against Measure SM was submitted.
Supporters filed an argument for Measure SM; its text is not among the documents gathered here.
A yes vote approves the $135 million in school bonds.
A no vote rejects the $135 million in school bonds.
