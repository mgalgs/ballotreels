Measure Q asks the voters of this city whether the city clerk should be appointed instead of elected.
The city clerk keeps official records and meeting minutes, publishes city ordinances, and runs city elections.
Today, voters in the city elect the city clerk to a four-year term.
Measure Q would have the city council appoint the clerk, and let the council hand that choice to the city manager.
An appointed clerk would not have to live in the city, and would be paid an amount the council sets.
The current clerk, elected in 2024, would finish her term in 2028, and could then be appointed.
Measure Q needs a simple majority to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Opponents say voters would lose direct control of the clerk, and transparency suffers when the clerk answers to officials it records.
Supporters say the clerk should be chosen for qualifications, not campaign politics, since an elected clerk generally needs no training.
A yes vote makes the city clerk position appointed.
A no vote means the city clerk position will remain elected.
