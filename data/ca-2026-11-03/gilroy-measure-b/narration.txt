Measure B asks the voters of this city whether to raise the hotel tax from 9 percent to a maximum of 13 percent.
Guests of hotels, short-term rentals and other lodging pay the tax on stays of 30 days or less.
The money goes to the general fund, and may be used for any lawful purpose.
The City Council placed Measure B on the ballot, and it needs a simple majority to pass.
Measure B would first raise the rate from 9 to 11 percent.
After that, the City Council could move the rate up or down by resolution, without a public vote, up to 13 percent.
The ballot question, which allows a rate of up to 13 percent, says the measure would provide $700,000 a year.
Opponents say the tax increase would discourage visitors; core services belong in the annual budget.
Supporters say visitors, not residents, pay the tax, and the rate is unchanged since 1983.
A yes vote raises the rate to 11 percent, and lets the City Council set it as high as 13 percent.
A no vote keeps the rate at 9 percent.
