# Measure B — City of Gilroy — Transient Occupancy Tax ("hotel tax"), 9% to a maximum of 13% (general tax)

- **Letter:** B, confirmed by the City of Gilroy elections page ("Measure B - Transient Occupancy Tax Rate Measure") and the impartial analysis.
- **Jurisdiction:** City of Gilroy (charter city).
- **Kind:** ordinance amending the Transient Occupancy Tax (City Code Chapter 25A.2); general tax; placed by the City Council.
- **Who votes:** City of Gilroy voters.
- **Threshold:** Resolution p. 2, §3: "The vote requirement for the measure to pass is a majority vote of the" qualified electors (sentence continues). Impartial analysis: "The Measure will be approved if it receives a simple majority of "Yes" votes."
- **Status:** adopted. Resolution No. 2026-30, adopted May 4, 2026, DocuSigned (pp. 4–5).

## Ballot question (resolution p. 2; same text on the Notice of Election and the city page)

> To maintain critical City services such as street/pothole repair, park and recreation programs, emergency preparedness for storms, flooding and wildfires, and police and fire response shall the City of Gilroy measure raising the Transient Occupancy Tax (hotel tax) rate from 9% to a maximum of 13%, set by Council resolution, providing $700,000 annually paid only by hotel/lodging guests, with annual independent audits, that cannot be taken by Sacramento, until ended by voters, be adopted?

## Full text

Resolution No. 2026-30, "PASSED AND ADOPTED by the City Council of the City of Gilroy at a regular meeting duly held on the 4th day of May 2026" (p. 4). Vote (p. 5): "AYES: COUNCIL MEMBERS: CLINE, FUGAZZI, HILTON, MARQUES, RAMIREZ, BOZZO / NOES: COUNCIL MEMBERS: BRACCO / ABSTAIN: NONE / ABSENT: NONE". Approved by Greg Bozzo, Mayor; attested by Kim Mancera, City Clerk.

Recital (p. 1): "the TOT is a general tax, the proceeds of which shall be deposited" in the general fund. Ordinance (pp. 7–11): the rate becomes "eleven percent (11%) of the rent charged by an operator" (p. 8), and the Council may adjust it up to "AN AMOUNT NOT TO EXCEED THIRTEEN PERCENT (13%)" (p. 7, title). Resolution p. 4, §16: collection at the new rate "shall commence effective January 1, 2027, or as soon thereafter as this ordinance shall go into effect."

## Impartial analysis

"CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE B", signed "s/Andrew L. Faber, City Attorney, City of Gilroy". Key statements:

> The Transient Occupancy Tax, commonly referred to as the "Hotel Tax," is imposed on persons staying in hotels, short-term rentals, or other lodging for a period of 30 days or less.

> If approved by voters, Measure B amends the Hotel Tax Ordinance set forth in Chapter 25A.2 of the Gilroy City Code to initially increase the Hotel Tax rate by 2% from the current rate of 9% to 11%.

> ... the City Council may by resolution adjust the rate of the Hotel Tax upwards or downwards without a vote of the people provided that the rate does not exceed 13%.

> Monies received from the Hotel Tax are deposited into the City of Gilroy general fund and may be used for any lawful purpose.

## Arguments and rebuttals

- **Argument in favor** — signed "Greg Bozzo, Mayor, City of Gilroy". "Vote YES on Measure B, a fair, fiscally responsible way to support Gilroy's essential services without raising taxes on Gilroy residents." and "An 11% rate generates approximately $300,000 annually; the 13% maximum, approximately $700,000".
- **Argument against** — the posted text names no individual signer; it closes "Silicon Valley Taxpayers Association's volunteers advocate for taxpayers throughout Santa Clara County. Visit SVtaxpayers.org." Quotes: "The Gilroy City Council wants voters to approve a huge, 31 PERCENT tax hike by saying that we, the residents, won't have to pay it." and "Why aren't critical police, pothole, and fire emergency services covered by the current budget?"
- **Rebuttal to argument against** — signed "Greg Bozzo, Mayor, City of Gilroy". "The argument against Measure B has it backwards." and "The increase is modest: two percentage points, from 9% to 11%, with a strict, voter-approved ceiling of 13%."
- **Rebuttal to argument in favor:** the city page lists the heading "Rebuttals to Argument in Favor of Measure B" with no document linked. No rebuttal to the argument in favor found among the official documents fetched; the ROV voter guide could not be fetched (Cloudflare), so whether the official guide prints one is unconfirmed.

Signer names here are as they appear on the city's posted copies; the printed guide's signer lines are unconfirmed.
