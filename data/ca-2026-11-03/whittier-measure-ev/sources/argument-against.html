ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE EV

Vote NO on Measure EV.

Moving Whittier's elections from April to November protects incumbent councilmembers, costs the taxpayers more money, helps special interests and means waiting three weeks for results.

In the November election, candidates for city council will have to spend three times as much money to campaign as in April. That gives incumbents and big-money special interests extraordinary influence in the outcome of the election. Challengers will not be able to raise enough money to be competitive. The rich developers, cannabis retailers, trash haulers, activists, aggressive unions and political parties, ALL FROM OUT-OF-TOWN, will be able to dictate the outcome like they have done in neighboring southeast cities known locally as the "corridor of corruption" for good reason.

When Whittier conducts our own election in April it costs the taxpayers $450,000. Consolidating with the November County election could cost as much as $1.7 million.

In April, Whittier candidates and issues are the only items on the ballot.

Just like November, every April voter gets a ballot. But it takes just 5 minutes to mark it and mail it. All the ballots are counted in a few hours, not weeks. Most importantly, Whittier voters and candidates can focus just on Whittier issues without all the clutter of legislative races, state-wide ballot measures and judicial races.

Burying the Whittier election in the middle of a massive November ballot with dozens of other decisions is not good for Whittier. Whittier has maintained its unique character and independence because we protected our local control of our city. We shouldn't surrender that local control to out-of-town special interests.

Whittier has successfully conducted its own April elections for decades. Moving the election to November benefits the incumbents with no real advantage to the voters.

Vote wisely. Vote NO on measure EV.

MERI RAMA

ANDREW PRYOR
