Mountain Empire Unified School District Measure L asks the district's voters whether to approve $23 million in bonds for its high school.
The bonds would be repaid, with interest, by a yearly tax on the assessed value of property in the district.
Measure L needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would repair or rebuild high school classrooms and labs, remove asbestos, lead pipes and mold, and fix roofs and plumbing.
The impartial analysis says approval does not guarantee that the listed projects will be funded by the bond sales.
The district's tax information statement estimates $38 a year per $100,000 of assessed value, through fiscal year 2060-2061.
The statement puts the total repaid, with interest, at about $48 million.
Supporters say Measure L fixes failing gas and sewer lines at a high school that had 17 roof leaks last year.
No argument against Measure L was filed with the Registrar of Voters for the San Diego County Voter Information Guide.
A yes vote is in favor of authorizing the district to issue and sell the $23 million in bonds.
A no vote is against authorizing the district to issue and sell the bonds.
