## Facts and the passages that establish them

### Ballot question

S1 PDF p. 79 (same wording in S3 Section 3(b), PDF p. 6):

> MEASURE L
> To repair, replace, or reconstruct deteriorating classrooms, science labs, career technical education and school facilities at Mountain Empire High School that don't meet building safety/earthquake safety standards; remove asbestos, lead pipes, mold; and replace/fix roofs plumbing, sewer, electrical, and aging portables, shall Mountain Empire Unified School District's measure authorizing $23,000,000 in bonds at legal rates be adopted, levying approximately $38 per $100,000 of assessed valuation ($2,542,353 annually) while bonds are outstanding, with independent oversight, audits, and all money locally controlled?
> Bonds – Yes / Bonds – No

### Vote required

S1 PDF p. 79: "This measure requires 55% of those voting on the measure."

### Who votes on it

S3, Resolution 25/26-041, Section 2 (PDF pp. 5-6):

> a special election shall be held within the boundaries of the District on November 3, 2026, for the purpose of submitting to the registered voters of the District the bond measure contained in Exhibit A attached hereto

S1 PDF p. 80 (full text): "By approval of this measure by at least 55% of the voters of the Mountain Empire Unified School District (the "District") voting on the measure, the District shall be authorized to issue and sell bonds of up to $23,000,000".

In short: the registered voters of the Mountain Empire Unified School District.

### Who placed it on the ballot

S1 PDF p. 79: "The Board of Education for the Mountain Empire Unified School District ("District") placed this measure on the ballot."

S3 PDF p. 8: "PASSED AND ADOPTED this day, May 12, 2026, by the following vote:" with the AYES, NOES, ABSTAIN and ABSENT lines **left blank** in the copy filed with the ROV. The cover letter (PDF p. 1) calls it "a certified copy of a resolution adopted on May 12, 2026, by the Board of Education".

### Impartial analysis

S1 PDF p. 79, "COUNTY COUNSEL IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS". Key statements:

> If approved by 55% of the votes cast, this measure will authorize the District to issue and sell general obligation bonds in an amount not to exceed $23,000,000.

> The District estimates the average tax rate would be $38 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation, and the highest tax rate would be approximately $38 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation. The tax is anticipated to be collected through fiscal year 2060-2061.

> The estimated total debt service, including principal and interest, required to be repaid if the bonds are issued and sold is approximately $47,978,702.

### Tax rate statement

S1 PDF p. 79, "TAX INFORMATION STATEMENT", "Dated: May 12, 2026", "/s/ Dr. Patrick Keeley, Superintendent":

> 1. ... is $38.00 per $100,000 of assessed valuation. The final fiscal year in which the tax to be levied to fund this bond issue is anticipated to be collected is fiscal year 2060-61.
> 2. ... is $38.00 per $100,000 of assessed valuation in fiscal year 2027-28.
> 3. ... the total debt service ... is approximately $47,978,702.

### Arguments and rebuttals

- **Argument in favor**, S1 PDF p. 80. First line: "A YES vote on Measure L is a vote for a safe high school for our students." It says "17 roof leaks in the last year alone at Mountain Empire High School". Signers: Mark Kersey (President, San Diego County Taxpayers Association); Ed Ketchum (First Responder); Bill LeBlanc (Retired MEUSD Teacher (1995-2026)); Jeff Osborne (Local Business Owner); Trevor Garewal (Descanso Hay and Feed).
- **Argument against**: none. S1 PDF p. 80: "No Argument Against Measure L was filed with the Office of the Registrar of Voters".
- **Rebuttals**: none printed.
