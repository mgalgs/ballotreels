City of Alameda Measure M asks voters in the city whether to amend and repeal a group of City Charter sections, all as one package.
The City Charter is the highest policy and governance document of the city.
The City Council placed Measure M on the ballot, and it needs a simple majority to pass.
Department heads and other top employees would serve at the pleasure of whoever appoints them, as they do in practice now.
Only the City Council could authorize the City Attorney to file lawsuits for the city.
Other changes cover filling vacancies in top city offices, qualifications for some city boards, and duties of the City Auditor.
The Alameda County Composite Voter Information Guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Opponents say the public and the full Council did not adequately vet the changes, and some, like ending the citizenship rule for city boards, are not simple cleanup.
Supporters say the measure removes obsolete dates and references to agencies that no longer exist, and that it is careful housekeeping, not a restructuring of city government.
A yes vote would conform the City Charter to existing practices and amend and repeal outdated provisions.
A no vote means the Charter provisions in Measure M remain the same.
