Measure X - Yolo County Unincorporated Area Hotel Tax
Measure X - Yolo County
YOLO COUNTY UNINCORPORATED AREA HOTEL TAX
To fund local programs and services – such as maintaining health and human service program levels, enhancing public safety, and supporting emergency preparedness projects – shall the ordinance increasing Yolo County’s Transient Occupancy Tax, which is paid only by hotel/lodging/short-term rental guests in unincorporated areas (excluding cities), from 8% to 12% of the total rent charged, providing approximately an additional $250,000 annually until ended by voters, be adopted?
To pass, a majority (50% + 1) of the votes cast by Yolo County voters must approve the measure.

[#tabc46555ba-13f0-4aa7-beb2-fc51768115d3_0] Impartial Analysis of Measure X
COUNTY OF YOLO
County Counsel’s Impartial Analysis of Measure X
A transient occupancy tax (“TOT”) is a tax on the privilege of occupying a room or other living space in a hotel, motel, vacation rental, or other similar lodging for up to 30 days. The TOT is based on the total rent charged to the lodging guest (transient). The TOT is paid by the lodging guest.
Measure X asks whether the TOT rate in the unincorporated areas of Yolo County should be increased from 8% to 12% of the total rent charged. If Measure X is approved, Ordinance No. 1585 would be adopted and the TOT rate would increase to 12%, effective January 1, 2027, on short-term lodging stays of 30 days or less within the unincorporated areas of Yolo County. For example, if a hotel in the unincorporated area of Yolo County charges a guest $100 per night, the TOT charged would increase from $108 per night to $112 per night.
If approved, Measure X will only increase the TOT charged for short-term lodging stays in the unincorporated areas of the County. TOT rates charged in the incorporated cities of Davis, Woodland, West Sacramento, and Winters would not change.
Ordinance No. 1585 also includes additional proposed changes to the County’s TOT ordinance, including changes to clarify the definition of “rent” as the total of all consideration charged to the guest for the short-term occupancy, including the room rate, service charges, retail markup charges, commission, processing fees, cleaning fees, resort fees, pet fees, and online booking fees.
Ordinance No. 1585 also includes other changes, such as a change to the definition of a “hotel” subject to TOT, to clarify that it includes short-term rentals, vacation rentals, guest rooms, and camping sites, except to the extent exempted by State law.
Measure X was placed on the ballot by a unanimous vote of the Yolo County Board of Supervisors. TOT general tax revenues generated by Measure X would be placed into the County General Fund and would be available for appropriation through the County’s annual budget for any lawful expenditure. Such expenditures, as identified in the Measure, may include maintaining health and human service program levels, enhancing public safety, and supporting emergency preparedness projects.
A “yes” vote on Measure X approves Ordinance No. 1585 and the proposed increase in the TOT from 8% to 12%.
A “no” vote on Measure X is a vote against authorizing the proposed changes in Ordinance No. 1585 and the TOT rate would remain at 8%.
The Measure passes if it is approved by a simple majority of the qualified electors voting on the Measure. The electors entitled to vote on Measure X are qualified electors residing within Yolo County, including its incorporated and unincorporated areas.
The above statement is an impartial analysis of Measure X. The full measure (proposed Ordinance No. 1585) is also printed in the sample ballot.
/s/ Philip J. Pogledich
County Counsel, County of Yolo
[#tabc46555ba-13f0-4aa7-beb2-fc51768115d3_1] Full Text of Measure X
ORDINANCE NO. 1585
AN ORDINANCE OF THE BOARD OF SUPERVISORS OF THE COUNTY OF YOLO AMENDING TITLE 3, CHAPTER 7 OF THE YOLO COUNTY CODE OF ORDINANCES TO INCREASE THE TRANSIENT OCCUPANCY TAX RATE IN THE UNINCORPORATED AREAS OF THE COUNTY FROM EIGHT PERCENT (8%) TO TWELVE PERCENT (12%) OF THE RENT CHARGED AND OTHER CHANGES
The Board of Supervisors of the County of Yolo ordains as follows:
SECTION 1. PURPOSE
The purpose of this Ordinance is to amend Title 3, Chapter 7 of the Yolo County Code of Ordinances and known as the “Uniform Transient Occupancy Tax Law” of the County (“TOT Ordinance”) regarding the transient occupancy tax within the unincorporated areas of the County – which is a general tax on the privilege of occupying a room or rooms, or other living space, in a hotel, motel, inn, tourist home or house, vacation rental, or other lodging for a period up to 30 days. This Ordinance amends the TOT Ordinance to increase the tax rate from eight percent (8%) to twelve percent (12%) and makes other changes to the TOT Ordinance, including clarification that the rent on which the TOT is based is a percentage of the total consideration charged to the transient.
SECTION 2. AMENDMENTS TO TITLE 3, CHAPTER 7, OF THE YOLO COUNTY CODE OF ORDINANCES
The following sections of Title 3, Chapter 7, of the Yolo County Code of Ordinances are hereby amended as follows:
Sec. 3-7.02. Definitions.
For the purposes of this chapter, unless otherwise apparent from the context, certain words and phrases used in this chapter are defined as follows:
“Calendar quarter” shall mean a period of three (3) consecutive calendar months commencing on the first day of each January, April, July, and October.
“Hotel” shall mean any structure, or any portion of any structure, which is occupied, or intended or designed for occupancy, by transients for dwelling, lodging, or sleeping purposes and shall include any hotel, inn, tourist home or house, motel, studio hotel, bachelor hotel, lodging house, rooming house, apartment house, short-term rental, vacation rental, guest room, dormitory, public or private club, mobile home or house trailer at a fixed location, a camping site or a space at a campground or recreational vehicle park, or other similar structure or space, or portion thereof, except to the extent exempted by State law.
“Occupancy” shall mean the use or possession, or the right to the use or possession, of any room, or portion thereof, in any hotel for dwelling, lodging, or sleeping purposes.
“Operator” shall mean the person who is the proprietor of the hotel, whether in the capacity of owner, lessee, sublessee, mortgagee in possession, licensee, or any other capacity. Where the operator performs such functions through a managing or booking agent of any type or character other than an employee, the managing or booking agent shall also be deemed an operator for the purposes of this chapter and shall have the same duties and liabilities as his principal. Compliance with the provisions of this chapter by either the principal or the managing/booking agent shall, however, be considered to be compliance by both. For purposes of this ordinance, an online booking platform that makes referrals and reservations and processes payments shall be a managing agent of the operator.
“Person” shall mean any individual, firm, partnership, joint venture, association, social club, fraternal organization, joint stock company, corporation, estate, trust, business trust, receiver, trustee, syndicate, or any other group or combination acting as a unit.
“Rent” shall mean the total of all consideration charged to the transient, whether or not received, for the occupancy of space in a hotel valued in money (including but not limited to, room rates, service charges, retail markup, commissions, processing fees, cleaning fees, resort fees, pet fees, and online booking fees), whether to be received in money, goods, labor, or otherwise, including all receipts, cash, credits, property, and services of any kind or nature, without any deduction therefrom whatsoever.
“Transient” shall mean any person who exercises occupancy or is entitled to occupancy by reason of concession, permit, right of access, license, or other agreement for a period of thirty (30) consecutive calendar days or less, counting portions of calendar days as full days. Any such person so occupying space in a hotel shall be deemed to be a transient until the period of thirty (30) days has expired, unless there is an agreement in writing between the operator and the occupant providing for a longer period of occupancy. In determining whether a person is a transient, uninterrupted periods of time extending both prior and subsequent to the effective date of this chapter may be considered.
“Treasurer-Tax Collector” shall mean the Yolo County Chief Financial Officer, or his or her designee.
Sec. 3-7.03. Tax Imposed
For the privilege of occupancy in any hotel within the unincorporated area of the County, each transient shall be subject to and shall pay a tax in the amount of twelve (12%) percent of the rent charged. Such tax shall constitute a debt owed by the transient to the County, which debt shall be extinguished only by payment to the operator or to the County. The transient shall pay the tax to the operator of the hotel at the time the rent is paid. If the rent is paid in installments, a proportionate share of the tax shall be paid with each installment. The unpaid tax shall be due upon the transient’s ceasing to occupy space in the hotel. If for any reason the tax due is not paid to the operator of the hotel, the Treasurer-Tax Collector may require that such tax shall be paid directly to the Treasurer-Tax Collector.
Sec. 3-7.05. Operator’s duties.
Each operator shall collect the tax imposed by the provisions of this chapter to the same extent and at the same time as the rent is collected from every transient. The amount of tax shall be separately stated from the amount of the rent charged, and each transient shall receive a receipt for payment from the operator. No operator of a hotel shall advertise or state in any manner, whether directly or indirectly, that the tax, or any part thereof, will be assumed or absorbed by the operator, or that it will not be added to the rent, or that, if added, any part will be refunded except in the manner provided in this chapter. If rent is collected by a managing or booking agent, the managing or booking agent shall collect the tax at time of collecting the rent and remit the tax to the Treasurer-Tax Collector.
Sec. 3-7.06. Registration.
Thirty (30) days prior to the initial occupancy of a hotel, each operator of any hotel shall register such hotel with the Treasurer-Tax Collector and obtain a “Transient Occupancy Registration Certificate” which shall at all times be posted in a conspicuous place on the premises. Such certificate shall, among other things, set forth the following information:
The name of the operator;
The address of the hotel;
The date upon which the certificate was issued; and
A statement as follows: “This Transient Occupancy Registration Certificate signifies that the person named on the face hereof has fulfilled the requirements of the Uniform Transient Occupancy Tax Law by registering with the Treasurer-Tax Collector for the purpose of collecting from transients the Transient Occupancy Tax and remitting such tax to the Treasurer-Tax Collector. This certificate shall not authorize any person to conduct any unlawful business or to conduct any lawful business in an unlawful manner, nor to operate a hotel without strictly complying with all local applicable laws, including, but not limited to, those requiring a permit from any board, commission, department, or office of this County. This certificate shall not constitute a permit.”
Such certificates shall be nonassignable and nontransferable and shall be surrendered immediately to the Treasurer-Tax Collector upon the cessation of business at the location named upon its sale or transfer.
Sec. 3-7.07. Reporting and remitting.
Each operator and managing or booking agent having collected rent for a hotel, shall, on or before the last day of the month following the close of each calendar quarter, make a return to the Treasurer-Tax Collector, on forms provided by the Treasurer-Tax Collector, of the total rents charged and received and the amount of tax collected for transient occupancies in the calendar quarter, as well as any amounts not previously reported. At the time the return is filed, the full amount of the tax collected shall be remitted to the Treasurer-Tax Collector. Returns and payments shall be due immediately upon cessation of business for any reason. All taxes collected by operators and managing or booking agents pursuant to the provisions of this chapter shall be held in trust for the account of the County until payment thereof is made to the Treasurer-Tax Collector.
SECTION 3. GENERAL TAX
The transient occupancy tax imposed by this Ordinance is a general tax within the meaning of Government Code section 53721 and Article XIII C, section 1(a), of the California Constitution. The revenue generated by this general tax is available for general governmental purposes. To that end, revenues from the tax shall be deposited into the County General Fund. The revenue from this general tax shall be available to the Board of Supervisors for annual appropriations in the County’s budget for any lawful expenditure. Nothing in this Ordinance nor in any other ordinance, advisory measure, resolution, or policy shall be construed as limiting, in any way, the amount or the objects of the appropriations and expenditures that can be made from the revenue of the tax nor be construed as creating a continuing appropriation.
SECTION 4. CEQA FINDING
Pursuant to CEQA Guidelines Section 15378(b)(4), adoption of this ordinance as a government funding mechanism is not a project subject to the requirements of CEQA. Prior to commencement of any project that may result from the expenditure of revenues from this tax increase, any necessary environmental review required by CEQA shall be completed.
SECTION 5. SEVERABILITY
If any section, sub-section, sentence, clause, or phrase of this Ordinance is held by a court of competent jurisdiction to be invalid, such decision shall not affect the remaining portions this Ordinance. The Board of Supervisors hereby declares that it would have passed this Ordinance, and each section, sub-section, sentence, clause, and phrase hereof, irrespective of the fact that one or more sections, sub-sections, sentences, clauses, and phrases be declared invalid.
SECTION 6. EFFECTIVE DATE
If approved by a majority of the electorate voting on this Ordinance at the November 3, 2026 general election, this Ordinance shall take effect on January 1, 2027.

[#tabc46555ba-13f0-4aa7-beb2-fc51768115d3_2] Argument In Favor Of Measure X
The passage of Measure X will sustain our county's efforts to build a strong local economy and provide vital public services throughout Yolo County.
Measure X would raise our Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) from 8% to 12% of the cost of a hotel room, lodging or short-term rental. This proposed TOT increase only applies to the hotels, lodging and short-term rentals in the unincorporated areas of Yolo County. The TOT is tax paid for by tourists and visitors who stay in our hotels, motels or short-term rentals; Yolo County residents do not pay this tax unless they stay in one of these local lodging options.
Visitors use our roads, parks and emergency services as they enjoy all that Yolo County has to offer; from our vibrant Farmers Markets, to parks, wineries, shops and galleries throughout our county. Raising the TOT represents a direct and fair wayto recover the increased costs associated with visitor stays in Yolo County.
In Fiscal Year 2024-2025, the TOT in Yolo County generated $250,000 in revenue for the County. A 4% increase in the TOT currently amounts to an additional increase of $250,000 annually.
Also, a 4% TOT increase does not place Yolo County out of market for the region as most local jurisdictions are already at 12%.
The proposed increase achieves an important balance between maintaining our competitiveness for tourism dollars, while providing an opportunity to fund critical county activities.
The Board of Supervisors has pledged that Measure X revenues will help fund critical services, and because none of the money can be taken away by the State, all Measure X revenue will support services in Yolo County.
Please join us in voting YES on Measure X, and help our county build a stronger local economy and protect important county services.
/s/ Sheila Allen
Yolo County Supervisor, Chair
