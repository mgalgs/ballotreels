Measure J asks the voters of this city whether to raise the tax on hotel stays from 6 percent to 10 percent.
Today the city charges a tax of 6 percent of the rent for a room in a hotel or other lodging.
The hotel operator collects the tax from guests and sends it to the city.
Measure J would raise the rate by 4 percentage points, to 10 percent.
The increase is a general tax, so the city could spend the money on any general government purpose.
Measure J needs a simple majority of the vote to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis estimates the increase would raise about $75,000 a year.
The ballot question says the higher tax would continue in perpetuity.
No argument against Measure J appears among the official documents gathered for this video; only the argument in favor does.
Supporters say the rate has stayed at 6 percent since 2006, and visitors should help pay for the services they use.
A yes vote would allow the city to set the hotel tax at 10 percent.
A no vote would keep the hotel tax at 6 percent.
