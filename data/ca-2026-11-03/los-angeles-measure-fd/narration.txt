City of Los Angeles Measure FD asks voters in the city whether to add a 0.5 percent sales tax to fund the Los Angeles Fire Department.
Measure FD is a citizen initiative, which qualified for the ballot by petition.
Today, nearly all fire department funding comes from the General Fund, the main operating fund of the city, which supports most city departments.
The sales tax rate in the city is 10.25 percent.
Measure FD would raise the rate to 10.75 percent, with the new money kept in a separate fund for the fire department.
The fund would pay for firefighters, paramedics and other staff, vehicles, equipment, fire stations and brush clearance.
The tax could be collected only in years the city keeps fire department funding at or above its average share of the budget in the ten years before the tax begins.
The City Administrative Officer estimates the tax would raise about $345 million a year, and the tax would last until voters repeal it.
Supporters say Measure FD lets the department hire at least 1,000 more firefighters and paramedics, so that help can get to you faster.
Opponents say Measure FD is a third sales tax increase in two years, and the city should fully fund the fire department without one.
A yes vote means you want to establish a 0.5 percent sales tax to provide more funding for the fire department.
A no vote means you do not want to establish this sales tax.
