City of Emeryville Measure BB asks voters in the city whether to rewrite its business license tax.
Today, the business license tax has 56 categories, most taxed at a flat rate or by employees, days open, vehicles or other assets.
The City Council placed Measure BB on the ballot, and it needs a majority vote to pass.
Measure BB would cut the categories to seven, most taxed at 0.1, 0.3 or 0.4 percent of gross receipts.
Businesses with $100,000 or less in receipts would pay $25, and the most any business pays in a year would rise from $300,000 to $800,000.
The new rates would start on January 1, 2027, and the money could go to any legitimate city purpose.
The ballot question says Measure BB would provide $12.4 million a year; the City Attorney's impartial analysis gives no estimate.
No argument against Measure BB was submitted for this election's Alameda County Composite Voter Information Guide.
Supporters say Measure BB modernizes a 42-year-old tax, shifts more to larger businesses, and spares residents.
A yes vote adopts the measure and its new business license rates.
A no vote keeps the 42-year-old business license tax as it is.
