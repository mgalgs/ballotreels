Measure HOM asks the voters of this city whether to allow housing in certain commercial and industrial areas.
Since 1982, any change to the land use part of the general plan has needed voter approval.
The City Council placed Measure HOM on the ballot.
Measure HOM would create a housing overlay that could allow apartments, townhomes, condominiums and mixed-use buildings in commercial and industrial areas on the city map.
Measure HOM itself would approve no development.
The council would first adopt zoning rules, and each project would then need council approval after a public hearing.
The overlay could not be used in single-family or other low-density neighborhoods, and the ban on data centers would stay.
Measure HOM needs a majority yes vote to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say Measure HOM lets the city revive underused commercial and industrial land while keeping existing neighborhoods as they are.
The official county list of measures, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure HOM was submitted.
A yes vote creates the housing overlay and lets the council write its zoning rules.
A no vote leaves the land use plan and its map unchanged, with no housing overlay.
