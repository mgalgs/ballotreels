Measure A asks voters across this county whether to tax short stays at hotels and rentals outside city limits, at 12 percent of the rent.
Every voter in the county votes on Measure A, in the cities too, though the tax would apply only outside them.
Today, the county does not have a tax on hotel stays.
Guests staying 30 days or less would pay 12 percent of the rent, collected by the lodging operator.
The money would go to the general fund of the county, for any governmental purpose.
The tax would start in January 2027 with no end date, and needs a simple majority to pass.
The auditor-controller's fiscal impact statement estimates $4.5 million a year in revenue.
Setting up the tax would cost about $50,000, and running it about $130,000 a year.
Opponents say a 12 percent tax is a substantial burden, and local short-term rental owners would end up shouldering the costs.
Supporters say the tax is paid mainly by visitors, not residents, and nearly every county in the state already has one.
A yes vote establishes the 12 percent tax on short-term lodging outside city limits.
A no vote leaves the county without a tax on hotel stays.
