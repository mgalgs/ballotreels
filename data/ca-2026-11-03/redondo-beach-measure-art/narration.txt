Measure ART asks the voters of this city whether to change zoning to allow more commercial building, and less housing density in places.
The land use plans of the city were last substantially updated in 1992, and the city charter requires a public vote on these amendments.
Along two commercial boulevards, buildings are limited today to 30 feet and two stories.
Measure ART would allow 45 feet and three stories there, with more floor area and design standards for pedestrians.
Industrial areas, and some commercial areas along Pacific Coast Highway, could also build more floor area.
Allowed housing density would fall from 35 to 30 homes per acre in some areas, and rise from 28 to 30 in others.
Measure ART needs a simple majority to pass.
The county Registrar's list of measures does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Opponents say one all-or-nothing vote bundles separate changes, and added zoning capacity does not guarantee projects get built.
Supporters say modern zoning would bring new restaurants, shops and jobs, and more city revenue without taxing residents.
A yes vote approves the land use and zoning changes.
A no vote opposes the changes.
