Transcription of argument-against.pdf (Argument Against Measure ART, filed
on the Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder/County Clerk's Ballot
Label/Arguments and Rebuttals Form). Typed from the page images: the PDF's
text layer is an OCR with many misread letters ("ctearty", "atso").

Page 1 (the form) marks "Direct Argument Against (Opponents)", gives the
jurisdiction/measure name as "City of Redondo Beach - Major Changes in
Allowable Land Use", and the ballot label's opponents as "Patrick Healy";
it also starts the argument's text, which page 3 prints in full. Page 2

[Page 3]

After defending Redondo Beach's right to maintain its own municipal election calendar, the City voluntarily scheduled a $250,000 Special Municipal Election for one City measure on November 3, 2026, only four months before the March 2, 2027 General Municipal Election. Article XXVII ordinarily places major land use changes on the next regular municipal ballot while allowing a special election as an exception. The election resolutions identify no urgency that requires this additional expense. Those funds should remain available for community priorities, including crossing guards.

Measure ART increases zoning capacity, but zoning capacity is not economic development. Along Artesia and Aviation Boulevards, it raises maximum floor area ratios from 0.60 to 1.50 and heights from 30 to 45 feet, while retaining detailed standards. Rooftop dining requires an Administrative Use Permit and counts toward floor area. These changes do not guarantee feasible, approved, financed, or completed projects.

The measure also reduces housing density or removes residential uses from certain properties. It combines commercial, residential, inland, and coastal changes affecting separate corridors and neighborhoods into one all or nothing vote. A resident might support rooftop dining on Artesia but oppose greater intensity elsewhere, or support commercial improvements while opposing reduced housing opportunities. Voters cannot make those choices separately.

The official measure text spans 53 pages. Changes this complex require clear property level comparisons and, where appropriate, separate ballot questions.

Measure ART approves planning and zoning rules, not a particular development project. It should not be treated as blanket approval of future coastal proposals or as a waiver of project review, environmental laws, coastal permitting, or Measure DD and Article XXVII safeguards.

One question cannot fairly capture these distinct policy choices.

Voters deserve genuine, informed choices.

Vote NO on Measure ART. Require a clearer, fully explained proposal presented at Redondo Beach's regular municipal election.

Patrick Healy
