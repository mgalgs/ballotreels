Measure E asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $16 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds over time and repay them from an increase in property taxes in the district.
Measure E needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money could modernize classrooms, playgrounds and restrooms, fix leaky roofs, plumbing and heating systems, and improve access for students with disabilities.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $28.65 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $30, until 2060-2061.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $32 million.
No argument against Measure E was submitted for the county Registrar of Voters' printed voter guide pages on this measure.
Supporters say many aging classrooms do not meet modern educational and technology standards, and that Measure E protects taxpayers.
A yes vote will authorize the district to issue up to $16 million in bonds and levy taxes to repay them.
A no vote will disapprove the bonds and the taxes to repay them.
