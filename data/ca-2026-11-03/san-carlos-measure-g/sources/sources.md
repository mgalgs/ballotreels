# Measure G — City of San Carlos (transactions and use "sales" tax, ½ cent, 14 years, general tax)

- **Jurisdiction:** City of San Carlos.
- **Kind:** General tax: adds Municipal Code Chapter 3.18, a 0.5% transactions and use (sales) tax until March 31, 2041.
- **Who votes:** the voters of the City of San Carlos. Resolution No. 2026-088, Sec. 1 (resolution-full-text.pdf p. 2): "the City Council hereby calls for an election at which it shall submit to the qualified voters of the city a measure, that if approved by the majority of voters voting in the election, would adopt and impose a transactions and use (sales) tax of 0.5% until March 31, 2041, unless extended by action of the voters prior to that date."
- **Vote threshold:** majority. Sec. 7 (p. 4): "the taxes shall be approved upon a majority vote of the qualified voters of the City of San Carlos voting in the election on the Ordinance."
- **Placed by:** the City Council.
- **Election:** November 3, 2026.

## Ballot question

> To provide funding for San Carlos city services, such as keeping local streets, sidewalks, storm drains, parks, and community facilities safe, clean, and well-maintained; fixing streets and potholes; relieving traffic congestion; repairing aging infrastructure; and supporting fire protection, paramedic, crime prevention, and 911 emergency response; shall City of San Carlos' ordinance establishing a one-half cent sales tax be adopted, providing $6,000,000 annually for general government use for 14 years, with independent audits and all funds locally-controlled?

(resolution-full-text.pdf p. 3, Sec. 4; from the OCR layer, checked against the page.)

## Full text (resolution and ordinance)

- **Resolution No. 2026-088**, City Council of the City of San Carlos. City Clerk Crystal Mui certifies it "was duly and regularly passed and adopted by the City Council of the City of San Carlos at a scheduled meeting thereof held on the 22nd day of June 2026" (OCR reads "22"" for "22nd"). AYES: Dugan, Layton, McDowell, Venkatesh. NOES: None. ABSENT: Rak (p. 5).
- The election itself was called by Resolution 2026-062, "adopted on June 8, 2026" (recital, p. 1).
- Sec. 2 (p. 3): the Ordinance specifies a rate of "one half percent (0.5%) of the gross receipts of any retailer from the sale of all tangible personal property sold at retail in the city" and "that the tax shall be in effect for fourteen years, that is until March 31, 2041, unless extended by action of the voters prior to that date".
- Sec. 6 (p. 4): the County is to "print the full text of the Ordinance attached hereto as Exhibit A exactly as filed ... in the voter information guide".
- **Exhibit A, Ordinance No. ____ adding Chapter 3.18** (pp. 6-13). Recital (p. 6): "the transactions and use tax to be submitted to the voters is a general tax that can be used for any legitimate governmental purpose; it is not a commitment to any particular actions or purposes, and the specific purposes recited in the ballot measure are non-restricted and non-exclusive examples only."
- Sec. 3.18.020 (p. 8): "Based upon a November 3, 2026 election, the Operative Date for the transactions and use tax imposed hereunder shall be April 1, 2027."
- Sec. 3.18.140 (p. 12): the auditor "shall include an accounting of the revenue received from the tax and expenditures thereof in the audited financial statements."
- Sec. 3.18.150 (p. 13): "The authority to levy the taxes imposed by this Ordinance shall terminate and be of no further force or effect on March 31, 2041, unless extended by action of the voters prior to that date."

## Impartial analysis

"IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE G", signed "/s/ Gregory J. Rubens, City Attorney" (impartial-analysis.pdf p. 2; p. 1 is the same text with hand word counts, 335 words).

- "On June 22, 2026, the City Council unanimously voted to place Measure G on the November 3, 2026, general municipal election ballot. If approved by a majority of San Carlos voters, Measure G would authorize a one-half cent general retail transactions and use (sales) tax within the City of San Carlos ("Proposed Tax")."
- "A one-half cent rate equates to an extra $0.50 per $100 purchased. The City estimates that the Proposed Tax will provide an additional $6,000,000 in annual revenue."
- "The Proposed Tax would be imposed starting on April 1, 2027 and remain in effect for fourteen (14) years until March 31, 2041 unless extended by a majority of San Carlos voters voting at a subsequent election."
- "The Proposed Tax would be paid in addition to the existing 9.375% sales tax, for a total sales tax rate of 9.875% in the City of San Carlos."
- "there would be no change to exemptions such as groceries, prescription medications and diapers."
- "A "yes" vote on Measure G is a vote in favor of the Proposed Tax. A "no" vote on Measure G is a vote against the Proposed Tax."

## Tax rate statement / fiscal

General tax; no tax rate statement applies. Rate: "one-half percent (0.5%)" (Sec. 3.18.050, p. 9; use tax Sec. 3.18.070, p. 9). Duration: until March 31, 2041 (Sec. 3.18.150, p. 13). Revenue: "$6,000,000 annually" (ballot question); "an additional $6,000,000 in annual revenue" (impartial analysis). No separate fiscal analysis was found among ACRE's files.

## Notes

- The argument in favor, argument against and rebuttal to the argument against were written before the letter was assigned ("Measure __" / "Measure ___"); only the rebuttal to the argument in favor says "Measure G". The printed guide presumably substitutes "G"; unconfirmed, since the per-voter sample ballot pamphlet was not checked.
- The impartial analysis says the Council "unanimously voted"; the resolution's certification shows 4 ayes, none opposed, and Councilmember Rak absent.
- Sec. 3.18.070 (use tax, p. 9) says the use tax runs "until said tax is repealed by a vote of the electorate of the City", while Sec. 3.18.050 and Sec. 3.18.150 set the March 31, 2041 end. Reported as printed.
- The argument against's "$84,000,000 total" is the arguers' own arithmetic ($6,000,000 x 14 years), not an official estimate.
