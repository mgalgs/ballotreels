Argument Against Measure __

[start of argument]

Stop this huge tax increase!

Sales tax hikes disproportionately hurt families with fewer resources.

By law, City Hall already receives 1% of every local taxable sale* — whether online, in local stores, or on residents’ vehicle purchases.

Inflation means families pay more. Then inflation also “gifts” City Hall with automatic windfalls, through its 1% share of every price increase!

Although proponents downplay this tax increase as just “one-half cent” more, it’s a massive 50% boost to City Hall’s take, with every purchase!

In May, San Carlos’s Administrative Services Director told the Palo Alto Daily Post that San Carlos faces an estimated annual deficit of $500,000 to $1,000,000.

Then why are councilmembers pushing for another $6,000,000 annually for 14 years ($84,000,000 total!)?

Palo Alto Daily Post’s article answered that:
Funding projects like Highway 101 bicycle bridge, whose cost ballooned to $112,000,000 (80% over budget!).

Plus, City Hall’s June mailer states they’ve “identified” $200,000,000 in unfunded capital improvement projects.

Although this measure touts essential services, its text admits this tax increase is for “general government use.”

That’s a legal blank check! Funds could feed bloated payrolls and councilmembers’ pet projects, instead of vital services we’d favor.

Designating Measure __ as a general sales tax instead of a special tax (or bond measure) is a bait-and-switch allowing City Hall to bypass normal expenditure guardrails and the taxpayer protection of a 2/3 supermajority requirement for such projects. We’re talking $84 million!

So-called “independent audits” only track spending after the fact; they don’t prevent waste.

Worse, Measure __ is stacked upon the separate 0.5% regional sales tax measure, threatening to skyrocket San Carlos’s rate to 10.375% — all piled on top of another $489/year parcel tax measure!

Ordinary families must stick to their budget — despite inflation.

Force City Hall to do the same with our tax money.

Vote NO!

*Bradley-Burns rate

Visit: SVTaxpayers.org

[end of argument]
