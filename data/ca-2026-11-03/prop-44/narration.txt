Proposition 44 is about how community health clinics spend money.
About 2,000 safety net clinics in California mainly serve low-income and uninsured people.
On average, nonprofit clinics report spending about 80 percent of revenue on health care.
Proposition 44 would set a minimum of 90 percent.
Clinics falling short would pay a penalty, refunded if they comply within five years.
The Legislative Analyst's Office says enforcement would cost the state low tens of millions a year, less than 1 percent of this year's General Fund spending, paid by clinic fees.
The Legislative Analyst's Office says other state and local costs are uncertain.
Opponents say Proposition 44 forces clinics to cut services or close, ending care for millions.
Supporters say Proposition 44 directs clinic money to patient care, not executive pay and overhead.
A yes vote means certain clinics would have to spend at least 90 percent on health care.
A no vote means the new requirement would not go into effect.
