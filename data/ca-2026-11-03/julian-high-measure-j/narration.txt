Julian Union High School District Measure J asks the district's voters whether to approve $9 million in school bonds.
The bonds would be repaid, with interest, by a yearly tax on the assessed value of property in the district.
Measure J needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would modernize classrooms and career facilities for agricultural science and skilled trades, and fix roofs and plumbing.
The impartial analysis says approval does not guarantee that the listed projects will be funded by the bond sales.
The district's tax rate statement estimates $30 a year per $100,000 of assessed value, through fiscal year 2054-2055.
The statement puts the total repaid, with interest, at about $18.2 million.
Supporters say Measure J prepares Julian students for college, careers and skilled jobs, with money that cannot be redirected to Sacramento.
No argument against Measure J was filed with the Registrar of Voters for the San Diego County Voter Information Guide.
A yes vote is in favor of authorizing the district to issue and sell the $9 million in bonds.
A no vote is against authorizing the district to issue and sell the bonds.
