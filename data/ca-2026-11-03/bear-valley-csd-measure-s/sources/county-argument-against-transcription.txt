Argument Against Measure ____

This measure would repeal a special police tax approved by 89% of voters in the 1997 election. The annual amount of this tax, $80, has not changed since 1997.

There is no need to "modernize" the tax. It serves the same purpose today as it did in 1997. The tax is dedicated to the salaries and benefits of police personnel. The proponents claim that the tax is lacking oversight, audits, and spending limitations. In fact, BVCSD budgets and financial reports are publicly available, transparent, and subject to annual independent audits.

The current budget for police salaries and benefits exceeds $1 million. The tax generates approximately $288,000 per year. Every dollar generated by the tax is used for its lawful purpose.

The operating budget for the police department is $2.2 million. Revenue from the general fund closes the gap between operating costs and special tax revenue.

The adopted CSD budget reduced total operational spending by $1.13 million. The police budget was reduced by 14%. Despite these significant reductions, the budget still exceeds available revenue. If the existing tax is repealed, the CSD will lose $288,000 in annual revenue, forcing added reductions in police service.

In 2026, the CSD commissioned an independent professional review of the level of police service. The review concluded that the existing police budget is necessary to provide a minimally acceptable level of service while meeting best practices for officer safety and supervision. Operating below best-practice standards increases risk, reduces officer safety, and negatively affects crime rates. Today, Bear Valley Springs has a crime rate lower than 93% of cities nationwide.

Without sufficient revenue, the police department will be unable to provide the recommended level of service or maintain acceptable response times.
