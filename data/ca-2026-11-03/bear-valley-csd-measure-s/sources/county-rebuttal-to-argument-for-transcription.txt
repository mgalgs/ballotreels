Bear Valley Community Services District

Rebuttal to argument in favor of measure S

The proponents argue that the existing tax measure is "outdated." Nothing about the existing tax is "outdated," other than the fact that it has not increased in 30 years. It is a legally valid tax used exclusively for its intended purpose.

The proponents claim lack of citizen oversight. In fact, citizens can and do participate in operational and financial oversight via the Public Safety and Finance Committees and the Police Chief's Advisory Panel. BVPD operations and spending are fully transparent. Any additional oversight for a new tax measure will encompass all existing police funding.

The proponents argue the existing tax should be consolidated with a proposed new tax. There is no need or legal requirement to consolidate taxes. The argument assumes that a new tax measure would be approved. If the existing tax is repealed in November 2026, and a new tax measure fails in March 2027, then the District cannot even maintain the status quo. The existing tax revenue would be gone forever; if repealed, it would not come back upon failure of a new tax measure.

If the existing tax is repealed in 2026, a new tax rate brought to the voters in 2027 must increase to maintain an acceptable level of service. A higher proposed rate reduces the likelihood of voter approval.

Measure S, if successful, will severely undermine the District's efforts to sustainably fund the BVPD. Voting NO supports the longstanding tradition of adequately funding the BVPD to maintain a safe community.
