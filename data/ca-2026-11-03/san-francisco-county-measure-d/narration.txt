San Francisco Proposition D asks voters whether to raise the requirements for putting measures on the city ballot.
Today, a measure can reach the ballot by a vote of the Board of Supervisors, by four supervisors, by the mayor alone, or by petition.
A petition needs signatures from 2 percent of registered voters, about 10,600 people.
Proposition D would raise the petition requirement to 8 percent of registered voters, about 42,500 signatures.
Proposition D would end the power of four supervisors, or of the mayor alone, to place a measure on the ballot.
Proposition D would also let petitions be withdrawn, and raise the bar for calling a special election.
The City Controller says Proposition D would have a minimal impact on the cost of government.
Supporters say Proposition D gives more voters a voice, since a minority of supervisors or the mayor alone can put anything on the ballot.
Opponents say Proposition D quadruples the signatures a petition needs, to a limit so high that only the very wealthy can reach it.
A yes vote means you want to make these changes to the ballot process.
A no vote means you do not want to make these changes.
