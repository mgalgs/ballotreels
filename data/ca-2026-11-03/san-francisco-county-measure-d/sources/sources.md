# Proposition D — Changes to Ballot Measure Process

- **Jurisdiction:** City and County of San Francisco
- **Kind:** Charter amendment, placed on the ballot by initiative petition
- **Who votes:** all registered San Francisco voters (consolidated city and county; one electorate)
- **Vote threshold** (VIP PDF p. 86): "This measure requires 50%+1 affirmative votes to pass."

## Ballot question (p. 86)

> Shall the City amend the Charter to increase the requirements for signature initiatives from 2% to 8% of registered voters in San Francisco (currently from about 10,600 to about 42,500 signatures), increase the requirements for calling special elections, allow signature initiatives to be withdrawn before an election, and remove the ability of a minority of Board of Supervisors members or the Mayor to place measures on the ballot?

## Digest by the Ballot Simplification Council (p. 86)

**The Way It Is Now:**
> The San Francisco Board of Supervisors (Board) has 11 members. A measure can be either an ordinance to change the law or a declaration of policy. Under the City Charter, measures can be placed on the ballot in several ways, including:
> • By a majority vote of the Board;
> • By four or more Board members without a Board vote;
> • By the Mayor without a Board vote; or
> • By a signature initiative (petition) signed by at least 2% of the registered voters in San Francisco, currently about 10,600 signatures.
>
> Currently San Francisco has over 530,000 registered voters.
>
> Signature initiatives usually appear on the ballot at the next regularly scheduled election.
>
> Signature initiatives may call for an earlier "special" election on an ordinance if the petition is signed by at least 10% of the votes cast for all candidates for Mayor in the last election. Currently, this is about 39,000 signatures.
>
> Once a signature initiative is submitted to the Department of Elections, it cannot be withdrawn.

**The Proposal:**
> Proposition D would amend the Charter to increase the requirements to put measures on the ballot by:
> • Increasing the number of signatures required to place a signature initiative on the ballot from 2% to 8% (currently from about 10,600 to about 42,500 signatures) of the registered voters in San Francisco.
> • Increasing the number of signatures required to call a special election on an ordinance from 10% of the votes cast for Mayor in the last election to 10% of the registered voters in San Francisco. Currently, this is about 53,100 signatures.
>
> Proposition D would allow signature initiatives to be withdrawn up to 102 days before the election.
>
> Proposition D would also change the requirements to put measures on the ballot by:
> • Removing the ability of four or more Board members to place a measure on the ballot without a Board vote;
> • Removing the ability of the Mayor to independently place a measure on the ballot;
>
> The Board could still place measures on the ballot by a majority vote.

(In the two-column layout the 2%-to-8% bullet ends the left column and the special-election bullet starts the right column; plain `pdftotext` extracts them out of order.)

**A "YES" Vote Means:**
> If you vote "yes," you want to increase the requirements for signature initiatives from 2% to 8% of registered voters (currently from about 10,600 to about 42,500 signatures), increase the requirements for special elections, allow signature initiatives to be withdrawn, and remove the ability of a minority of Board members or the Mayor to place measures on the ballot.

**A "NO" Vote Means:**
> If you vote "no," you do not want to make these changes.

## Controller's Statement on "D" (p. 87)

> City Controller Greg Wagner has issued the following statement on the fiscal impact of Proposition D:
> Should the proposed Charter amendment be approved by the voters, in my opinion, it would have a minimal impact on the cost of government.

Dollar figures:
> The proposed amendment is likely to reduce costs at levels that cannot be determined at this time.

> On average, each page in the VIP costs $11,400 to prepare, print, and distribute to all registered voters. The proposed increase in the voter signature threshold may also reduce the number of special elections called which would result in cost savings for the Department of Elections. The estimated minimum cost for a citywide special election is $5 million with a one-card ballot and less than 50-page VIP.

## How "D" Got on the Ballot (p. 87)

> On July 23, 2026, the Department of Elections certified that the initiative petition calling for Proposition D to be placed on the ballot had a sufficient number of valid signatures to qualify the measure for the ballot.
> 53,076 signatures were required to place an initiative Charter Amendment on the ballot. This number is equal to 10% of the registered voters at the time a "Notice of Intent to Circulate Petition" was published. A random check of the signatures submitted by the proponents of the initiative petition prior to the July 6, 2026, submission deadline showed that the total number of valid signatures was greater than the number required.

## Arguments (pp. 88–89)

Every argument page carries: "Arguments are the opinions of the authors and have not been checked for accuracy by any official agency." and "Arguments are printed as submitted. Spelling and grammatical errors have not been corrected."

**Proponent's Argument** (p. 88). Signers: Mayor Daniel Lurie; Board of Supervisors President Rafael Mandelman; Speaker Emerita Nancy Pelosi; Nancy Tung, San Francisco Democratic Party; Former SF Ethics Chair Ben Hur; Former Deputy City Attorney Jim Lazarus; SFSU Associate Professor of Political Science Jason McDaniel; Former City Controller Ben Rosenfield; Former Fire Chief Joanne Hayes-White; Dan Calamuci, Carpenters Local 22*; Anthony Travis Jr., Laborers Local 261*; Charles Lavery, Operating Engineers Local 3*; Teamsters Local 665; Council of District Merchant Associations; Chinatown History and Culture Association (*"For identification purposes only; author is signing as an individual and not on behalf of an organization.").
> Currently a minority of supervisors, just 2% of voters or the mayor acting alone, can throw anything on the ballot without public input, transparency or proven community support.

> Proposition D requires a public vote of the majority of supervisors or signatures from 8% of voters.

**Rebuttal to Proponent's Argument** (p. 88). Signers: San Francisco Labor Council; San Francisco Senior & Disability Action; United Educators of San Francisco; San Francisco Women's Political Committee; San Francisco Tenants Union; Indivisible SF; Coleman Advocates for Children & Youth; San Francisco Rising Action Fund.
> Quadrupling signature requirements from 10,600 to 42,500 will require hiring paid signature firms to qualify and cost one million dollars or more.

**Opponent's Argument** (p. 89). Signers: San Francisco Labor Council; San Francisco Senior & Disability Action; United Educators of San Francisco; San Francisco Women's Political Committee; San Francisco Tenants Union; Indivisible SF; Community Tenants Association; Coleman Advocates for Children & Youth; San Francisco Rising Action Fund.
> Prop D takes away rights San Franciscans have held for 125 years: to put citizen initiatives on the ballot.

**Rebuttal to Opponent's Argument** (p. 89). Signers: Mayor Daniel Lurie; Board of Supervisors President Rafael Mandelman; Former Controller Ben Rosenfield.
> With D, placing a measure on the ballot requires a public vote of a majority of the Supervisors or a modest amount of signatures. D also removes the mayor's sole authority to place measures on the ballot, ensuring a fair balance of power.
