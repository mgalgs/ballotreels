Measure HT asks the voters of this city whether to apply the hotel tax to more kinds of lodging and to longer stays.
Today, hotel and motel guests pay a 12 percent tax on their room rent, for stays of 21 days or less.
The tax covers only motels with six or more rooms and hotels with services such as restaurants or meeting rooms.
The City Council placed Measure HT on the ballot.
Measure HT would apply the tax to any place offering temporary lodging, including short-term rentals of homes.
Measure HT would also cover stays of up to 30 days, as state law allows.
The 12 percent rate would not change.
As a general tax, the money could fund any lawful city service, and the tax would last until voters repeal it.
Measure HT needs a majority yes vote to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis says the change would raise about $134,000 a year.
Supporters say short-term rental guests use the same city services as hotel guests, and should pay the same tax.
The official county list of measures, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure HT was submitted.
A yes vote extends the 12 percent tax to short-term rentals and stays up to 30 days.
A no vote keeps the tax limited to hotels and motels, for stays up to 21 days.
