## Ballot question (guide p. 933)

> Shall the measure to amend Oakland's City Charter to, among other things, (1) make the Mayor the City's chief executive with a legislative veto, (2) empower the Council to override vetoes, confirm appointments of certain directors, and appoint an Independent Budget and Legislative Analyst, (3) affirm Councilmembers' right to acquire information from other City officials, (4) require Councilmembers to work full-time, and (5) empower the Public Ethics Commission to set salaries for all City elected officials using similar formulas, be adopted?

Transcribed from the guide; line-break hyphens rejoined and the YES/NO box labels omitted.

## Who votes on it

Voters in the City of Oakland. The guide section is headed "CITY OF OAKLAND MEASURE EE", and the Registrar's measure list (<https://alamedacountyca.gov/rov_app/measures/election/260>) files it under that jurisdiction. Vote threshold: see the analysis quotes below.

## Quoted passages

Quotes are from the guide's text layer (decoded; some glyphs in the source fonts lack a Unicode map). Where a quote and the PDF page differ, the PDF page governs.

Quotes for this measure were transcribed from the rendered page image of p. 933 (City Attorney's Impartial Analysis, signed s/RYAN RICHARDSON, City Attorney):

- p. 933: "This measure would amend the Oakland City Charter to, among other things:"
- p. 933: "Make the Mayor the City's chief executive. The Mayor, rather than the City Administrator, would manage most departments and appoint most department heads, except that the City Administrator would maintain authority over the departments of Human Resources and Finance."
- p. 933: "Empower the Council to create an Office of the Independent Budget and Legislative Analyst, which would provide independent fiscal, policy, and feasibility analysis to the Council."
- p. 933: "Provide the Mayor a line-item veto over the annual budget, whereby the Mayor could veto any budget item adopted by Council and change it to the amount the Mayor originally proposed or to an amount somewhere between what the Council adopted and what the Mayor originally proposed."
- p. 933: "Require the Oakland Public Ethics Commission to set the salaries of the Mayor and the councilmembers, and require those salaries to be comparable to the salaries of similar full-time public officials in California."
- p. 933: "This measure was placed on the ballot by the City Council and will take effect only if a majority of voters voting on the measure vote “Yes.”"


## Fiscal analysis (City Auditor)

The guide also prints a **City Auditor's Impartial Analysis of Measure EE** (a financial analysis, signed by the City Auditor, Michael C. Houston) on p. 934:

- p. 934: "our Office estimates an increase of $36,000 to $108,000 in salary and benefits per Councilmember, resulting in an increase of $288,000 to $867,000 for the entire City Council in the first year."
- p. 934: "the Independent Budget and Legislative Analyst Office would cost approximately $1.7 to $1.8 million in staffing and overhead."
- p. 934: "We estimate that if this ballot measure passes, the overall cost to the City ranges from $2.0 million to $2.6 million for the first year, with ongoing costs subject to inflation."
