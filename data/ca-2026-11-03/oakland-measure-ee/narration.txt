City of Oakland Measure EE asks voters in the city whether to change the City Charter to make the Mayor the chief executive of the city.
Today, the city administrator manages most departments, and the Mayor can break ties on the Council.
The City Council placed Measure EE on the ballot, and it needs a simple majority to pass.
The Mayor would manage most departments and could veto Council laws and budget items, but would no longer break ties.
Six members of the Council could override a veto.
The Council could create an independent budget analyst office and confirm some department heads.
Council members would work full time, with pay set by the Public Ethics Commission.
The City Auditor estimates a first-year cost to the city between $2 million and $2.6 million, including the analyst office and higher Council pay.
Supporters say department heads would answer to one leader, the Mayor, and voters could keep or replace that leader based on results.
Opponents say the measure makes government less transparent, raises the risk of corruption, and lets the Mayor block almost any Council action.
A yes vote means the Mayor would become the chief executive of the city, with a veto.
A no vote means the City Charter stays as it is.
