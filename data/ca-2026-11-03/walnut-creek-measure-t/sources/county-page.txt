Measure T

MEASURE T

CITY OF WALNUT CREEK

Shall the office of the City Treasurer be appointive?

CITY ATTORNEY’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF

MEASURE T

Measure T presents to the voters of Walnut Creek the question of whether the office of City Treasurer should be made an appointed position, with the City Manager having the authority to appoint the City Treasurer. Currently, the office of City Treasurer is an elected office. State law authorizes a city election on the issue of whether to make the office of city treasurer an appointed position. Measure T was placed on the ballot by the City Council through Resolution No. 26-31.

The office of City Treasurer is one of the offices that make up the government of general law cities in California. City treasurers are responsible for performing the duties set forth in Government Code Section 41001 et seq., including but not limited to receiving and safely keeping city funds, complying with laws governing the deposit and securing of public and city trust funds, implementing a system of accounting and auditing of bond funds held by the city, paying out of City money based on properly executed warrants, submitting monthly financial reports showing receipts, disbursements and fund balances, and performing the duties related to the collection of City taxes and license fees as prescribed by ordinance. In 2026, the Walnut Creek City Treasurer is responsible for management of approximately 145 million dollars in average daily funds.

City treasurers may be either elected or appointed. By state law, elected city treasurers must reside and be a registered voter in the city in which they hold office. There are no additional minimum qualifications required under state law for the position of an elected city treasurer. The city may approve job specifications and minimum educational and professional qualifications for an appointed city treasurer.

If a simple majority of voters vote “YES” on this measure, the office of City Treasurer in the City of Walnut Creek will become appointive and will no longer be elected by voters. The City Manager, as authorized by the Walnut Creek Municipal Code, would appoint the City Treasurer when the term of the current incumbent expires in December 2026 or if the voters elect a City Treasurer on November 3, 2026, in December 2030, or earlier if a vacancy in the office occurs. If this measure is approved, this measure would save future costs associated with candidate elections related the position of City Treasurer.

If a simple majority of voters vote “NO” on this measure, the office of City Treasurer in the City of Walnut Creek will continue to be elected by voters.

The above statement is an impartial analysis of Ordinance or Measure T. If you desire a copy of the ordinance or measure, please call the elections official’s office at (925) 943-5818 and a copy will be mailed at no cost to you.

/s/ Steve Mattas,

City Attorney, City of Walnut Creek

ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF

MEASURE T

Vote Yes on Measure T to improve the vital role of the Walnut Creek City Treasurer. The Walnut Creek City Council and currently elected City Treasurer, among others, urge your support.

Currently, the only requirement to be elected Walnut Creek City Treasurer, is to be “a registered voter” in our city. Measure T improves accountability and protects the City’s future by changing this position from an elected to an appointed position, ensuring that future city treasurers are qualified to serve, subject to proper criteria, screening and background checks.

Our city is in strong financial condition. And, it’s also true that Walnut Creek’s City Treasurer responsibilities have expanded significantly and become more complex, including today overseeing approximately $145 million in municipal investments. The City Treasurer should be, at a minimum, a finance professional with a college degree, preferably a CPA, experienced in standard government accounting procedures, automated accounting systems, records management, and state and federal finance compliance laws.

Per the California Municipal Treasurers Association, fewer than 10% of California’s 483 cities still locally elect their city treasurers. Voting Yes on T brings Walnut Creek in line with modern practices and over 90% of California cities that employ appointed city treasurers.

Passage of Measure T will:

• Authorize a smooth transition from an elected to an appointed City Treasurer by 2030.

• Ensure that future Walnut Creek City Treasurers are appointed by the City Manager based on established professional qualifications.

• Provide procedures so future Walnut Creek City Treasurers are qualified for and capably perform required duties of the position.

• Require close collaboration between the City Treasurer, Finance Department, City Manager, elected City Council and City Council Finance Committee, to ensure financial transparency and accountability.

The City has a long history of sound financial management, and a professionally appointed City Treasurer will help protect that. Please join the Walnut Creek City Council, current elected (30-year) City Treasurer Ron Cassano, advocates of quality local government, and residents across Walnut Creek Voting Yes on T to help keep our city strong.

REBUTTAL TO ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF

MEASURE T

Measure T is not needed to ensure that Walnut Creek has a qualified City Treasurer. It is a proposal to take the choice away from voters and give it to the City Council—the same body whose spending, borrowing, budgeting, and investment decisions the Treasurer is expected to review.

The argument in favor suggests that election is merely a popularity contest and that appointment would guarantee professional qualifications. But voters can evaluate candidates’ experience, credentials, and qualifications at the ballot box. We could still choose if the Council established qualifications to run.

An elected Treasurer provides an independent check on City Hall because the Treasurer answers directly to residents, not to the officials whose financial decisions are subject to review. Under Measure T, the Council would appoint and remove the Treasurer; would that person hesitate to question council decisions or raise concerns about spending.

The Treasurer is responsible for important public duties, including overseeing the handling of municipal funds, managing the City’s investment portfolio under Council-adopted policy, and participating in budget and audit review; he/she must be independent

Measure T would also produce no meaningful savings and would not take effect until 2030 unless the current Treasurer leaves office earlier. In return for no demonstrated financial benefit, voters would lose their direct authority to select the City’s independent financial officer.

Keep the choice of City Treasurer with the voters. Vote No on Measure T.

Contra Costa Taxpayers Assn, Denise P Kalm, Vice President

Robert Marshall, Retired Businessman

ARGUMENT AGAINST

MEASURE T

Measure T asks Walnut Creek voters to give up their right to elect the City Treasurer and hand that choice to the City Council. Vote no.

The Council argues that an appointed office would be less political, but appointment would place the choice in the hands of the body the Treasurer is meant to oversee. The Treasurer is the only financial officer who answers to residents rather than to the officials who authorize the spending. State law charges the Treasurer with assuring the proper handling of municipal funds, managing the city’s multimillion-dollar investment portfolio under a policy the Council adopts each year, and sitting on the committee that reviews the budget and the audit. Making the office appointive would let the

Council hires the person assigned to review the Council’s own accounts, and a Council that prefers a Treasurer who approves what it brings forward has every reason to hire one.

Walnut Creek voters have weighed this question before and rejected it, in 1956 and again in 1996. The Council notes that most other cities in the county appoint their treasurers, though common practice elsewhere is not evidence that the change would serve Walnut Creek taxpayers.

The change would also produce no meaningful savings. The seat sits on a ballot residents are already casting, and the Treasurer’s modest part-time stipend is unchanged whether the office is filled by election or by appointment. It would, however, weaken accountability. An elected Treasurer can question spending, borrowing, or investment risk and answers only to voters. An appointed Treasurer serves at the Council’s pleasure and can be replaced for raising concerns the Council would rather not hear. Keep the choice of Treasurer with the voters.

Vote no on Measure T.

Contra Costa Taxpayers Assn, Denise P Kalm, Vice President

MEASURE T
