Measure T asks the voters of this city whether the office of city treasurer should be appointed instead of elected.
Today the treasurer, who oversees cash, investments and financial reporting for the city, is elected by voters.
The City Council voted unanimously in May to place Measure T on the ballot.
The treasurer elected on this same ballot would serve a full term; the office would become appointed when that term ends, or sooner if it falls vacant.
The city manager would then appoint the treasurer, who would not need to live in the city.
A city staff report says voters turned down making the office appointed in 1956 and again in 1996.
Measure T needs a majority of the vote to pass.
Neither the resolution placing Measure T on the ballot nor the staff report puts a dollar figure on what the measure would cost or save.
Supporters say the only requirement to be elected treasurer is being a registered voter, and appointment would make sure future treasurers are qualified.
Opponents say voters would give up their right to elect the treasurer, and an appointed one could be replaced for raising unwelcome concerns.
A yes vote makes the office of city treasurer appointed.
A no vote keeps the office elected.
