Measure Y - City of Woodland

Last Content Update: 2 months ago

City of Woodland Sales Tax Measure

To rebuild roads and repair potholes, increase police and fire protection, recruit and retain public safety personnel, maintain parks and trails, and clean and maintain public spaces, shall the City of Woodland measure enacting a one cent transactions and use tax (sales tax) providing approximately 16 million dollars annually for general government services, until ended by voters, with all funds locally controlled and subject to independent financial audits and public spending disclosures, be adopted?

To pass, a majority (50% + 1) of the votes cast by City of Woodland voters must approve the measure.

If you desire a copy of the ordinance, the Ballot Ordinance and/or the full text of Measure Y, please contact the following elections official, and a copy will be mailed at no cost to you:

  • City of Woodland City Clerk office: Call 530-661-5806 or Email City of Woodland City Clerk
  1. Impartial Analysis of Measure Y

CITY OF WOODLAND
CITY ATTORNEY’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE “Y” 
ONE CENT TRANSACTIONS AND USE (SALES) TAX

 On June 16, 2026, the City Council voted to place Measure “Y” on the November 3, 2026 general municipal election ballot.  By placing Measure “Y” on the ballot, the City complies with Article XIII C of the California Constitution (Proposition 218), which requires a majority of the voters to approve an ordinance which establishes a general tax.

If approved by a majority of Woodland voters, this Measure would authorize a second supplemental one cent ($0.01), or one percent (1%), retail transactions and use (sales) tax within the City of Woodland, which would remain in effect unless and until the ordinance is repealed by the voters.  A one cent, or one percent, tax rate equates to a tax of $0.01 per $1.00 purchased.  It is estimated that Measure “Y” will provide an additional $16,000,000 in annual local funding that could be used to maintain and improve City services.  This tax would be a “general tax,” meaning that revenues raised from the tax would go into the City’s general fund to pay for any lawful City program, improvement, or service.

California Revenue and Taxation Code section 7285.9 authorizes the City to levy this second supplemental general transactions and use (sales) tax at a rate of one percent (1%) so long as the tax is approved by a majority of the voters voting in an election on that issue.  

The tax would be paid in addition to current sales taxes and would be collected at the same time and in the same manner as existing sales taxes.  All revenues raised by the tax would remain in the City and would not be shared with the State, County or any other agency.

A "yes" vote on Measure “Y” will authorize the one cent transactions and use (sales) tax.

A "no" vote on Measure “Y” will not authorize the one cent transactions and use (sales) tax.          

If Measure “Y” is not approved, the General Fund will not receive the sales tax revenues identified in the Measure.  

The above statement is an impartial analysis of Measure “Y”.   If you desire a copy of the Measure, please call the Office of the City Clerk at (530) 661-5806 and a copy will be mailed at no cost to you.

 /s/   Ethan Walsh            
ETHAN WALSH
City Attorney

  1. Argument In Favor Of Measure Y

Vote YES on Measure Y: Investing in Woodland’s Future

Woodland is growing rapidly, but our vital city services are falling behind. Calls for police and fire protection have surged, while staffing, equipment, and infrastructure struggle to keep pace. Voting YES on Measure Y provides a stable, locally controlled revenue source to ensure our city can protect neighborhoods, improve emergency response times, repair potholes and sidewalks, and keep parks clean and safe.

Without this crucial investment, the essential services we rely on every day will continue to strain under the weight of our community's growth.

Measure Y will help Woodland:

• Strengthen fire and police protection
 • Improve emergency response times
 • Repair deteriorating roads, potholes, and sidewalks
 • Maintain critical infrastructure
 • Keep parks and public spaces clean, safe, and well maintained

Fiscal Accountability and Local Control:

• Every Penny Stays Local: Measure Y funds remain under local control and directly benefit Woodland residents.
• Transparency Ensures Accountability: Independent audits and public disclosure of spending will help ensure funds are used properly.
• Protects Essential Purchases: Exemptions for essential purchases, including groceries and medicine, help protect residents on fixed incomes.

Measure Y is designed with fiscal responsibility and transparency in mind. Residents will be able to see how funds are spent and how they benefit Woodland.

Our neighborhoods, families, and first responders deserve the resources necessary to maintain a safe, thriving community. Don't leave Woodland's future to chance. Protect our quality of life and ensure our city has the tools to meet tomorrow's challenges.

Vote YES on Measure Y.
