Proposition 1 is about borrowing money for housing.
Most new homes in California are built with private money alone.
The state helps pay for some homes, usually for people with low incomes.
Today, the state often pays its share with bonds: borrowing now and repaying with interest over time.
Proposition 1 would let the state borrow $11.25 billion for housing programs.
The Legislative Analyst's Office says repaying the bond would cost $500 million to $600 million a year for about 25 years, about one-quarter of 1 percent of the state's General Fund.
It says the money would help fund up to 40,000 rental homes and help up to 40,000 households own homes.
Supporters say Proposition 1 builds affordable homes for veterans, seniors and working families.
No argument against Proposition 1 was submitted for the state's official voter guide.
A yes vote means the state could borrow the money.
A no vote means the state could not borrow the money.
