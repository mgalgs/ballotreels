## Facts and quotes

**Ballot question (both files):** "SHALL THE OFFICE OF CITY TREASURER BE APPOINTIVE?"

**Impartial analysis** (file 1, p1; signed Scott G. Drexel, City Attorney, City of Clearlake), key statements:
- "Measure D is placed on the ballot by the City Council of the City of Clearlake and asks voters whether the office of City Treasurer should be converted from an elected to an appointed position. A "yes" vote adopts Ordinance No. 287-2026, which makes the office appointive and provides transition rules."
- "State law (Government Code Section 36501) requires general law cities like Clearlake to have a City Treasurer. The office may be elected or appointed, but conversion from elected to appointed requires voter approval (Government Code Section 36508)."
- "If a majority of voters approve Measure D, the office would become appointive ... and the City Council would appoint the City Treasurer. The appointee would serve at the pleasure of the City Council, need not be a City resident or registered voter, and could be required to meet professional qualifications."
- "The office of City Treasurer appears on this same November 3, 2026 ballot. Measure D's transition rules provide that if it passes, no person shall be declared elected to, assume, or qualify for the office of City Treasurer by virtue of votes cast at this election".

**Who votes / vote needed:** voters of the City of Clearlake; argument in favor: "Measure D requires a simple majority of the votes cast to pass." Analysis: "If a majority of voters approve Measure D".

**Argument in favor (file 1, p2):** signed "s/Dirk Slooten, Mayor, City of Clearlake". States: "The position has not been filled by an elected City Treasurer in nearly two decades."

**Argument against:** "NO ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE D WAS SUBMITTED." (file 1, p2)
