Measure D asks the voters of this city whether the city treasurer should be appointed instead of elected.
Today, voters elect the treasurer, who receives, safeguards and pays out the public money of the city.
If Measure D passes, the City Council would appoint the treasurer, who would no longer have to live in the city.
The council could require professional qualifications, or let the city manager make the appointment.
No treasurer elected on this same ballot would take office.
Measure D needs a majority vote to pass.
The county voter guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say the city cannot require training or financial experience of an elected treasurer, and city staff already do the work.
No argument against Measure D was submitted, so the county voter guide prints only the argument in favor of it.
A yes vote makes the city treasurer an appointed position.
A no vote means the city treasurer would remain an elected office.
