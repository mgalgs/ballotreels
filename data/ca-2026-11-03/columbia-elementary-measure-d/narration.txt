Measure D asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $11.5 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them from a yearly tax on property in the district.
Measure D needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay only for the projects the measure lists.
Those include heating and air conditioning, electrical, plumbing and sewer systems, and safety and security improvements.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $23.49 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $24.
The statement expects the tax to be collected until the 2060-2061 fiscal year.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $24 million.
Supporters say many classrooms are outdated and inadequate, and a citizens' oversight committee must review and audit the spending.
No argument against Measure D was filed, so the county voter guide prints only the argument of the supporters.
A yes vote is to authorize the issuance of the bonds.
A no vote is against authorizing the issuance of the bonds.
