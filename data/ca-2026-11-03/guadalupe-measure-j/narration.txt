Measure J asks the voters of this city whether to raise its tax on short lodging stays.
Today, guests of hotels, motels and similar lodging pay a 6 percent tax on stays of 30 days or less.
Measure J would raise the rate to 12 percent of the rent.
The tax would also cover RV parks and campgrounds, and exempt six kinds of stays, such as state park campsites.
As a general tax, the money could pay for any city purpose, until voters repeal the tax.
Measure J needs a simple majority to pass.
The ballot question estimates up to $20,000 a year, and more if new lodging opens in the city.
Supporters say visitors, not residents, would pay for vital city services, and 12 percent is still lower than most nearby.
No argument against Measure J was filed, so the county voter guide prints only the argument in favor of the measure.
A yes vote raises the rate and applies the tax to more kinds of lodging.
A no vote does not raise the rate or apply the tax to more kinds of lodging.
