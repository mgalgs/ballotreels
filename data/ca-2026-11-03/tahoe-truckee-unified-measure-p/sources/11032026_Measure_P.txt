                                      ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE P
Vote YES on P to protect quality education in Tahoe Truckee Schools by renewing expiring local school funding.
Thanks to great teachers, innovative academic programs, and strong support from the community, Tahoe Truckee Unified
School District is recognized for success in preparing local students for college and good jobs.
Since 1989, TTUSD has relied upon local school parcel tax funding that represents over 6% of the school district budget.
For over 45 years, this funding has supplemented unstable and inadequate funding from the State that only supports basic
education. Unless renewed by voters, this funding will expire soon and over $5,000,000 will need to be cut, forcing teacher
layoffs, academic program reductions, and increased class sizes.
Voting YES on P renews expiring school funding to continue:
  •   Supporting programs in science, technology, engineering and math
  •   Attracting and retaining highly qualified teachers
  •   Maintaining career training programs for in-demand fields like health sciences and skilled trades
  •   Maintaining manageable class sizes
Measure P also continues strict fiscal accountability, oversight, and transparency protections, including:
  •   All money raised would stay local and could not be taken by the county, state, or federal governments
  •   An independent oversight committee, annual audits, and public spending disclosure would continue to ensure funds
      are spent as promised
  •   All funds go straight to teaching and classroom instruction, and no funds can be used for administrator salaries
  •   Homeowners age 65 and older would continue to be eligible for an exemption and do not need to reapply
Even if you don’t have school-age children, supporting quality education in local schools is a wise investment. Good schools
improve the quality of life in our community and protect the value of our homes.
Please join local parents, teachers, business leaders, and elected leaders in voting Yes on P for Tahoe Truckee schools!
Signed,
Dr. David Steakley, NTHS Science Teacher

                                         ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE P
Tahoe Truckee families deserve excellent schools—and they deserve transparency, accountability, and responsible
stewardship of taxpayer dollars.
Measure P is described as a renewal, but it also raises the existing parcel tax from $148 to $188 per parcel, authorizes
automatic annual inflation increases, and extends the tax for another nine years.
The district’s own documents acknowledge that the current Measure AA parcel tax remains in effect through the 2027–2028
fiscal year if Measure P is not approved. Voters should ask why they are being asked to approve a higher tax years before
the current one expires.
Measure P is projected to generate approximately $7 million annually, yet the district states that failing to renew the tax
would require “over $5 million” in budget reductions. Taxpayers deserve a detailed explanation of the difference and a clear,
public spending plan before being asked to approve millions in additional revenue.
The measure permits spending across numerous broad categories, including academic programs, technology, equipment,
counseling, libraries, arts, and other services, but it does not establish binding funding levels or measurable performance
goals for any of them.
Everyone wants strong public schools. The question is whether this proposal provides sufficient specificity and accountability
for a long-term tax commitment.
Before approving higher taxes with automatic increases, voters should insist on greater transparency, clearer priorities, and
a proposal that more directly explains how every additional dollar will improve student outcomes.
The district has two more years before Measure AA sunsets – send this excessive tax increase that hits working class
families the hardest back to the drawing board so that we can come up with a plan that works for everyone.
Vote NO on Measure P.

Mark Wright, Chairman, Placer County Republican Party

                             REBUTTAL TO THE ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE P
None of the Measure P opponents are residents, voters, or taxpayers in our school district. They don’t understand
the needs of our schools and our communities.
By contrast, Measure P is supported by trusted local educators, business, and community leaders.
Don’t be misled and remember these facts:
40 current teaching positions in our schools depend on Measure P. Tahoe-Truckee schools have relied on locally
controlled parcel tax funding for decades and this funding expires next school year. Without Measure P, teachers, academic
programs, career training, and manageable class sizes will be cut.
Measure P renews current funding to protect quality education. The current parcel tax rate has not increased since
2018, while the cost of providing quality education has increased substantially. The current parcel tax provides over
$5,000,000 annually. Measure P provides approximately $7,000,000 because it renews current funding and adjusts the rate
by $40 (~$3 per month) to account for inflation.
Measure P continues exemptions for seniors. Under Measure P, seniors and low-income people with disabilities are
eligible for an exemption so it’s not a burden to those on fixed or limited incomes.
Measure P requires strict taxpayer protections:
  •   Local control: By law, every penny must be used in local schools and cannot be taken away
  •   Classrooms, not administration: All funds go straight to the classroom and cannot be used for administrator salaries
  •   Accountability: An independent oversight committee reviews all spending quarterly and annual audits/public
      disclosure ensure funds are spent properly
Join trusted local leaders and vote YES on P to protect Tahoe-Truckee schools.

ACCOUNTABILITY AND COMPLIANCE MEASURES
In accordance with Government Code sections 50075.1
and 50075.3, the following accountability measures,
among others, shall apply to the Qualified Special Tax
levied in accordance with this Measure:
1) Proceeds of the Qualified Special Tax shall be
   deposited into a fund which shall be kept separate and
   apart from other funds in the District and shall be
   applied only as set forth in this Measure.
2) An annual report, including the revenues generated by
   the Qualified Special Tax and the expenditures of
   these monies and the status of any projects funded by
   these monies, shall be filed by the chief fiscal officer of
   the District with the Board no later than January 1 of
   the year following the fiscal year during which the
   monies were expended.
3) The Board shall provide for the creation of an
   independent oversight committee to oversee
   expenditure of the funds collected pursuant to the
   Measure to ensure that moneys raised under this
   Measure are spent only for the purposes described in
   this Measure. The Board shall provide for the
   composition, duties, and other necessary information
   regarding the committee's formation and operation.
   The Board shall have the option to extend any current
   oversight committee and its membership to serve as
   the oversight committee for this Measure.
PROTECTION OF FUNDING
    Current law forbids any decrease in State or Federal
funding to the District because of the District's adoption of
a qualified special tax. However, if any such funds are
reduced because of the adoption of this qualified special
tax, then the amount of the qualified special tax will be
reduced annually as necessary in order to restore such
State or Federal funding.
APPROPRIATIONS LIMIT
    Pursuant to California Constitution Article XIIIB and
applicable laws, the appropriations limit for the District will
be adjusted periodically by the aggregate sum collected by
levy of this qualified special tax. This provision shall be
deemed to be declaratory of existing procedures
governing District expenditures.
