## Facts and quoted passages

- **Ballot question** (Resolution No. 2026-016, Section 1, packet p. 28):
  > Shall the measure enacting a ten percent (10%) Transient Occupancy Tax to be paid by hotel guests in the City of Farmersville, providing funding for general purposes such as city services, programs, economic development, and capital improvements, which is expected to generate an estimated $250,000 annually per hotel and remain in effect until repealed by the voters, be adopted?
- **Who votes:** Section 1: "the following question shall be submitted to the voters of the City of Farmersville at the General Election to be held on Tuesday, November 3, 2026".
- **Vote needed:** Section 3: "The vote requirement for passage of the measure shall be a majority (50% plus one) of the votes cast."
- **Adopted:** minutes of June 8, 2026, Consent Calendar: "2. Second Reading and Adoption of Resolution 2026-016 Calling for the Submission to the Voters a Measure Relating to the Enactment of a Ten Percent (10%) Transient Occupancy Tax November 3, 2026, General Municipal Election. Council Action: It was motioned by Councilmember Gomez, seconded by Mayor Pro Tem Ochoa and approved with a 4-1 vote." (The resolution copy in the packet is unsigned with blank vote lines, p. 29.)
- **Staff report** (p. 14): "The proposed measure would establish a ten percent (10%) tax on hotel, motel, and other short-term lodging stays within the City for occupancies of thirty (30) consecutive days or less." And: "Revenue generated from the tax would be deposited into the City's General Fund and may be used for general governmental purposes, including public safety, roadway maintenance, parks, recreation, and other City services." Fiscal impact (p. 15): "There is no immediate fiscal impact associated with adopting the Resolution."
- **Impartial analysis — draft printed in the council packet** (p. 16, headed "Impartial Analysis from the City Attorney"; this is the city's packet copy, not the county voter guide):
  > The City currently does not collect a transient occupancy tax ("TOT") from transients, who are defined as those individuals occupying hotel rooms within the City for fewer than thirty (30) days. ... Although the City does not currently have any hotels, the City anticipates that a 10% TOT will generate $250,000 annually once a moderate sized facility is operational.
  > The TOT will be deposited into the General Fund and as such will be a general tax.
- **Argument in favor — draft printed in the council packet** (pp. 16-17, "Arguments for Measure __"): opens "Measure ___ enables voters to establish a 10% tax rate on hotel/motel occupancy, called the Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT). It is NOT a new tax." Ends "The City Council and community leaders urge you to vote "YES" on Measure __." Signed "s/Tina Hernandez, Mayor; s/Araceli Ochoa, Mayor Pro Tem; s/Paul Boyr, City Council Member; s/Gregorio Gomez, City Council Member; s/Danny Valdovinos, City Council Member". The packet then prints "(No arguments against Measure ___ were submitted)" — as of the June 8 packet, before the county's argument deadline. Whether the county guide prints an argument against is NOT confirmed.
- **Full text** (pp. 17-27): "ORDINANCE NO. 538 ... Chapter 3.44 TRANSIENT OCCUPANCY TAX". Section 3.44.020 Tax imposed (p. 19): "For the privilege of occupancy in any hotel, each transient is subject to and shall pay a tax in the amount of ten percent of the rent paid by the transient." Effective date (p. 27): "This Ordinance shall take effect only if approved by a majority of the eligible voters of the City of Farmersville voting at the General Municipal Election to be held on November 3, 2026."
