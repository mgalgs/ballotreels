Measure N asks the voters of Zone 1 of this community services district whether to add a yearly tax of $250 per parcel.
Every property in the district pays a $200 yearly assessment, unchanged since 1987.
Zone 1 is the whole district except one gated subdivision that keeps up its own infrastructure through a homeowners' association.
The district's board of directors placed Measure N on the ballot.
Measure N needs two-thirds of the vote to pass.
The new tax would come on top of the $200 assessment, which would not change.
The tax could rise each year with inflation, by no more than 3 percent.
The money could pay only for district services in Zone 1, such as fire mitigation, roads, drainage, lighting and parks.
The tax would last until voters change or repeal it, and the district's spending limit would rise to match.
The county attorney's impartial analysis expects the tax to raise about $87,250 a year.
Supporters say the assessment has not kept pace with repair costs, and upkeep now will head off costlier repairs later.
No argument against Measure N was submitted, the voter pamphlet of this county says, and it prints no rebuttals.
A yes vote authorizes the tax of $250 per parcel a year in Zone 1.
A no vote is against the tax, and the $200 assessment stays.
