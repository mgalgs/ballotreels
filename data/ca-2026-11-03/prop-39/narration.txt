Proposition 39 is about how voters prove who they are.
In 2024, about 13 million of the 16 million Californians who voted did so by mail.
Today, voters identify themselves by signing: a list at the polls, or the mail ballot envelope.
Proposition 39 would require voters at the polls to show government-issued identification.
Voters who mail their ballots would write the last four digits of their selected ID number on the envelope.
The Legislative Analyst's Office puts yearly state and local costs at tens to low hundreds of millions, less than one-quarter of 1 percent of the state's General Fund.
Supporters say Proposition 39 would increase trust in elections, while keeping voting accessible.
Opponents say Proposition 39 adds costly hurdles that could block eligible California voters.
A yes vote means voters would present additional identifying information each time they vote.
A no vote means a voter's identity would continue to be established with their signature.
