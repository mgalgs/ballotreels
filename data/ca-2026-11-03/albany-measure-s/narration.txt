City of Albany Measure S asks Albany voters whether to change the tax paid when property is sold, so the rate depends on the price.
Since 2020, Albany has taxed property sales at a single rate of 1.5 percent of the price, or $15 for each $1,000.
The money goes to the General Fund and may pay for any city expense.
Measure S would set five rates, from 1 percent for the lowest-priced quarter of sales to 3 percent for the top tenth.
The new rates would start in 2027, and Measure S needs a majority of Albany voters to pass.
The City Attorney's analysis estimates the new rates would raise an additional $1,000,000 a year.
Opponents say Measure S adds thousands of dollars to the cost of buying or selling a home, making Albany even less affordable.
Supporters say Measure S keeps funding city services, cutting the rate on the lowest-priced quarter of sales and raising it above the median.
A yes vote adopts the five rates, from $10 to $30 for each $1,000 of the price.
A no vote keeps today's single rate of $15 for each $1,000 of the price.
