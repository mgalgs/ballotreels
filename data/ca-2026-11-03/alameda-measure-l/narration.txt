City of Alameda Measure L asks voters in the city whether to issue $300 million in bonds for flood, earthquake and street projects.
The City Council placed Measure L on the ballot, and it needs a yes vote from two-thirds of voters to pass.
The money could pay for sea walls and levees, storm drains and pumps, and other flood projects.
It could also pay for earthquake retrofits of fire stations, a new fire station, and street paving and pothole repair.
Property owners in the city would repay the bonds through a tax based on assessed value.
The tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $47.97 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $49.49, through 2061.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $662.2 million.
Supporters say the island is on the front lines of sea-level rise, and fire stations and storm sewers do not meet earthquake standards.
Opponents say the Council should name specific projects, where they are and what each costs, instead of sending voters broad funding categories.
A yes vote allows the city to issue $300 million in general obligation bonds for these projects.
A no vote does not allow the bonds in any amount.
