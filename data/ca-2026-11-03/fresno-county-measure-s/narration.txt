Measure S asks voters across this county whether to adopt a new half-cent sales tax for transportation, for 30 years.
Measure S is a citizens' initiative, which qualified for the ballot by a petition signed by voters.
The tax would begin on July 1, 2027, when the existing transportation tax, Measure C, expires.
Sixty-five percent would go to the county and cities for local streets and roads, and 25 percent to public transit.
Four percent would go first to free transit for seniors, veterans, students and people with disabilities.
A regional council of the county and its cities would hand out the money, with an 11-member citizens' committee overseeing it.
The tax would end after 30 years unless voters extend it, and needs a simple majority to pass.
The auditor-controller's fiscal impact statement expects about $120 million in the 2027-2028 budget year; the ballot question says about $113 million.
If Measure S fails, the statement says, projects that Measure C pays for now could need other funding, possibly from the county.
Opponents say Measure S is not a continuation of Measure C, but a transit tax disguised as a roads tax.
Supporters say Measure S fixes roads in every city and town at the same half-cent rate, with stricter oversight.
A yes vote approves the half-cent transportation sales tax.
A no vote rejects the half-cent transportation sales tax.
