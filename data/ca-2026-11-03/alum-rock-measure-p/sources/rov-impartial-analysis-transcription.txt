Impartial Analysis – Measure P – Alum Rock Union Elementary School District - Bonds

California law permits school districts to issue bonds with the approval of 55% of district voters. Such bonds may be used only for construction, reconstruction, rehabilitation, or replacement of school facilities, including furnishing and equipping those facilities, or the acquisition or lease of real property for school facilities.

The Board of Trustees (Board) of the Alum Rock Union Elementary School District (District) has placed on the ballot a Measure authorizing the issuance and sale of up to $100 million in such bonds.

As identified in the Measure, projects may include but are not limited to:

• Replacing, repairing, and/or modernizing roofs, plumbing, sewer and gas lines, and heating and cooling systems;
• Acquiring, installing, and/or upgrading campus safety and security systems, including emergency communication systems;
• Constructing, expanding, equipping classrooms and student support facilities;
• Constructing, renovating, and equipping classrooms and labs supporting science, technology, engineering, arts, and mathematics instruction;
• Installing and modernizing restrooms and drinking fountains;
• Constructing and modernizing learning and play areas for preschool, transitional kindergarten, and kindergarten;
• Improving paved surfaces;
• Modernizing and retrofitting cafeterias and multipurpose rooms;
• Constructing athletic fields, gymnasiums, and locker rooms;
• Improving, upgrading, and expanding technology infrastructure;
• Making health and safety improvements; and
• Making accessibility upgrades.

Bond proceeds may be spent on eligible project costs, such as design, engineering, architectural, and other professional services; construction management, administration, and other planning and permitting, legal, and accounting services; and other costs incidental but directly related to the projects. In accordance with state law, the Measure states that bond proceeds would not be used for teacher or administrator salaries or other school operating expenses.

These general obligation bonds will be payable from ad valorem taxes levied on taxable real property within the District. The Measure states that the best estimate of the average annual tax rate to be levied to repay the proposed bonds is $25.52 per $100,000 of assessed value, that the final fiscal year in which the tax is anticipated to be collected will be 2053-2054, and that the best estimate of the total debt service during the life of the bond, including principal and interest, is $170 million.

State law requires the District to take certain steps to account for the bond proceeds. The District has listed in the Measure the specific projects to be funded, including a certification that, in developing the list, the Board evaluated safety, class size reduction, enrollment growth, and information technology needs. Additionally, the District will appoint an independent citizens' oversight committee, conduct annual independent performance and financial audits, deposit the bond proceeds into a fund held by the Santa Clara County Treasurer, and file an annual report with the Board containing information regarding the amount of funds collected and expended and the status of projects listed in the Measure.

A "yes" vote is a vote to authorize the issuance and sale of up to $100 million of general obligation bonds to fund school facilities projects as described in the Measure.

A "no" vote is a vote not to authorize the issuance and sale of the bonds.
