Measure P asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $100 million in school bonds.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
Measure P needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The listed projects include roofs, plumbing, heating and cooling, safety and emergency systems, classrooms and labs, and play areas for the youngest students.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries or other operating costs.
The district's tax statement estimates an average tax of $25.52 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $30, through 2053-2054.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $170 million.
Supporters say many schools are over 60 years old and urgently need repairs, and Measure P funds them with no increase in the current tax rate.
No argument against Measure P was filed, so the list of local measures from the county shows only the argument in favor of the bonds.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue and sell up to $100 million in bonds for school facilities.
A no vote does not authorize the district to issue and sell the bonds.
