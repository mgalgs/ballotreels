CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE PS

City of San Gabriel Public Safety/Local Control Measure

Measure PS, if approved by the voters at the November 3rd, 2026 general election, would amend Chapter 35 (Taxation) of Title III (Administration) of the San Gabriel Municipal Code by the addition of new sections 35.200 through 35.213 which shall appear under the heading “Transactions and Use Tax 2026” and which will, if adopted by the voters impose a local retail transactions and use tax in accordance with the provisions of Part 1.6 (commencing with Section 7251) of Division 2 of the Revenue and Taxation Code and Section 7285.9 of Part 1.7 of Division 2.

Specifically, Measure PS places a tax of 1.00% (one cent per $1.00) upon retailers’ gross receipts from the retail sale of most tangible personal property sold within the City, and an excise tax upon the storage, use or other consumption of tangible personal property purchased from any retailer for storage, use or other consumption within the City at a rate of 1.00% of the sales price of the property, where “sales price” includes delivery charges subject to State sales or use tax, regardless of delivery destination.

It is estimated that revenues from this tax will generate approximately $7,000,000 annually for the funding of general City services. Funds generated by the proposed tax are not restricted and may be used for any lawful City general fund expenditures, including, without limitation, on projects and services such as:
- Protection of local drinking water sources
- fire services
- crime prevention
- maintaining safe schools
- earthquake, fire and other disaster preparedness
- maintaining medical and police 911 response times
- retaining qualified firefighters
- other general City services.

Measure PS was placed on the ballot by the City of San Gabriel City Council. If approved by voters, the sales tax will apply to everyone who purchases retail goods within City limits. Food purchased as groceries and prescription medication will not be taxed under this measure. Visitors to San Gabriel will bear part of the tax burden imposed by their local activities and thus contribute to the City’s ability to maintain general public services they use while visiting. Residents and other purchasers of goods in the City will also be subject to the tax.

The tax would be administered by the California Board of Equalization under contract with the City of San Gabriel.

A “YES” vote is a vote to approve a 1.00% (one cent per $1.00) retail transactions and use general tax. A “NO” vote is a vote against a 1.00% retail transactions and use general tax. The transactions and use tax in Measure PS would take effect only if it receives a majority “YES” vote at the November 3, 2026, consolidated general election.
