Argument Against Measure ___

This measure would repeal a lawful rate increase that was based on an independent professional analysis of the five-year operational and capital investment needs of the water utility. The rate increase was implemented after public notice and opportunity for protest. Only 18 percent of customers submitted a protest, far less than the 50 percent required to stop the increase.

Safe and reliable water supply is often taken for granted. However, the delivery of water in Bear Valley Springs depends upon a very complex system that is over 50 years old and has received very little upgrade or replacement over those years.

Recent projects, including a booster station, a water main, and a water master plan, show CSD commitment to investment. However, if the approved rates are repealed, revenue would be reduced by approximately $10 million through 2031. This would make it more difficult or impossible to obtain project financing and cause deferment or cancellation of essential projects- projects needed to continue rebuilding aging water mains, wells, booster stations and tanks.

Water loss through leakage from aging mains will continue to increase. Reliability of water supply will continue to decline and heavy dependence on imported rather than local supply will continue. Imported supply incurs high purchase and pumping costs and its reliability is subject to many factors beyond local control.

With less revenue to support operations, service reductions such as longer response time for leak repair and elimination of a planned fire hydrant testing program may be necessary. As leakage continues and proactive upgrades are deferred, operational costs and the need for costly emergency repairs will rise. Water main leaks and breaks also damage roads and sometimes damage private property.

Absent sufficient revenue for operations, maintenance, and investment, a locally operated and controlled water utility will eventually become unsustainable.
