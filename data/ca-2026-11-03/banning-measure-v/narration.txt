City of Banning Measure V asks voters in the city whether to rezone 41 acres for very high density housing.
State law requires the city to rezone other land when it approves the Sunset Crossroads project, so the city keeps its room for new homes.
The City Council adopted this rezoning in April 2026.
Voters then signed a referendum petition, so the rezoning takes effect only if voters approve it.
The site is zoned for a school today, and would be zoned for 24 to 30 homes per acre.
That would make room for at least 1,146 homes.
The Riverside County guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say Measure V preserves the planned housing capacity of the city, and will help Sunset Crossroads move forward.
Opponents say Measure V crams homes onto less land, so that warehouses can take the place of housing elsewhere.
A yes vote means Ordinance 1619 will take effect.
A no vote means Ordinance 1619 will not take effect, and the site stays zoned for a school.
