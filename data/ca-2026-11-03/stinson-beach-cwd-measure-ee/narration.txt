Measure EE asks the voters of this water district whether to keep its current spending limit for another four years.
The state constitution sets a yearly limit on the tax money a local government may spend, and lets voters change the limit for up to four years.
The board of directors placed Measure EE on the ballot, and it needs a simple majority to pass.
If Measure EE passes, the district could keep spending tax revenue above its base limit through 2029-2030, to meet its current operating costs.
If Measure EE fails, the impartial analysis says, the spending limits of the district would be reduced, leaving less money for its current services.
The analysis says Measure EE does not allow a new assessment or an increase in any property tax rate.
The measure page from the county election office does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
No argument against Measure EE was filed, according to the page for the measure from the county, so only the argument in favor appears there.
Supporters say Measure EE lets the district fully use property tax it already receives, and otherwise water rates would rise to make up the loss.
A yes vote continues the current spending limit of the district for four more years.
A no vote does not continue the current limit.
