# Measure D — City of Burlingame (transient occupancy "hotel" tax, 12% to 15%, general tax)

- **Jurisdiction:** City of Burlingame.
- **Kind:** General tax: amends Municipal Code Sec. 4.09.025 to raise the transient occupancy tax (TOT) from 12% to 15%.
- **Who votes:** the voters of the City of Burlingame. Resolution No. 104-2026, Sec. 1 (resolution-full-text.pdf p. 2): "the City Council hereby calls an election at which it shall submit to the qualified voters of the City of Burlingame a measure that, if approved, would increase the Transient Occupancy Tax from twelve percent (12%) to fifteen percent (15%) to fund general services in the City."
- **Vote threshold:** majority. Recital (p. 2): "Article XIIIC, section 2(b) of the California Constitution requires that any general tax, such as the proposed TOT measure, be approved by a majority vote of the voters voting on the issue". Ordinance Sec. 6 (p. 8): effective "only if approved by a majority of the eligible voters of the City of Burlingame voting thereon".
- **Placed by:** the City Council.
- **Election:** November 3, 2026.

## Ballot question

> To support city services such as: maintaining city streets/sidewalks and repairing potholes; maintaining parks and trees; enhancing emergency response, preparedness, and wildfire prevention; and for general government use; shall the City of Burlingame measure be adopted raising the transient occupancy (hotel) tax from 12% to 15%, paid only by hotel and lodging guests, until ended by voters, providing an estimated $5,700,000 annually, all funds locally controlled, with independent citizen oversight?

(resolution-full-text.pdf p. 2, Sec. 2 "Ballot language"; read from the page image.)

## Full text (resolution and ordinance)

- **Resolution No. 104-2026**, City Council of the City of Burlingame, signed by Michael Brownrigg, Mayor. The City Clerk, Meaghan Hassel-Shearer, certifies that it "was introduced at a regular meeting of the City Council held on the 15th day of June, 2026, and was adopted thereafter by the following vote": AYES: Brownrigg, Colson, Pappajohn, Stevenson, Thayer. NOES: None. ABSENT: None (pp. 4-5).
- Sec. 3 (p. 2): "The City Council hereby approves the proposed Ordinance, in the form thereof, and its submission to the voters of the City at the November 3, 2026, election."
- **Exhibit A, Ordinance Number 2052** (pp. 6-8). Sec. 1 (p. 7) replaces Sec. 4.09.025 to read: "For the privilege of occupancy in any hotel or short-term rental, each transient is subject to and shall pay a tax in the amount of 15% of the rent charged by the operator."
- Recital (p. 6): the current code requires each transient to "pay the TOT rate of twelve percent (12%) for the privilege of occupying the hotel (BMC Section 4.09.025)".
- Sec. 2, Audits (p. 7): "Proceeds resulting from the TOT shall be deposited into the City's General Fund and become subject to the same audit requirements as other General Fund revenues."
- Sec. 6 (p. 8): the Ordinance "shall take effect ten (10) days after the City Council has certified the results of that election by resolution."

## Impartial analysis

"City Attorney Impartial Analysis – Measure D", signed Michael Guina, City Attorney (impartial-analysis.pdf p. 1).

- "Measure D would amend Chapter 4.09.025, "Tax Rate," of Chapter 4.09 of Title 4 of the Burlingame Municipal Code, to increase Burlingame's Transient Occupancy Tax rate by three percentage points, from 12% to 15%."
- "The hotel guest is responsible for paying the tax."
- "The City of Burlingame received approximately $22 million in TOT revenue in Fiscal Year 2025-2026. If adopted, Measure D would generate an estimated additional $5.7 million annually, based on existing occupancy rates, room rates, and total number of hotel rooms in Burlingame."
- "Measure D is a general tax. If the measure is adopted, the proceeds would go into the General Fund. Those revenues may be used for unrestricted governmental purposes".
- "As a general tax, Measure D requires a simple majority for approval, which is 50% + 1 of votes cast in favor. The tax would remain in place until amended or repealed by the voters."
- "A "Yes" vote would approve amending the Transient Occupancy Tax rate from 12% to 15%. A "No" vote would reject the measure and keep the current 12% tax rate."

## Tax rate statement / fiscal

General tax; no tax rate statement applies. Rate: 15% of rent (Ordinance 2052 Sec. 1, p. 7), up from 12%. Duration: "until ended by voters" (ballot question, p. 2); the impartial analysis says "The tax would remain in place until amended or repealed by the voters." Revenue: "an estimated $5,700,000 annually" (ballot question); "an estimated additional $5.7 million annually" on top of "approximately $22 million in TOT revenue in Fiscal Year 2025-2026" (impartial analysis). No separate fiscal analysis was found among ACRE's files.

## Notes

- Resolution Sec. 5 (p. 3) prints stale dates: the certified copy is to be filed "on or before August 9, 2024", and the election "shall be held ... on November 5, 2024". The rest of the resolution and the ordinance say November 3, 2026. Reported as printed.
- The certification gives only the introduction date ("the 15th day of June, 2026") and "was adopted thereafter"; the adoption date itself is not printed.
- The argument in favor and the argument against were written before the letter was assigned ("Measure __", "Measure_"). The rebuttals say "Measure D". The printed guide presumably substitutes "D"; unconfirmed, since the per-voter sample ballot pamphlet was not checked.
- The argument against ends: "Silicon Valley Taxpayers Association's volunteers advocate for taxpayers throughout Santa Clara County." The rebuttal to the argument in favor ends "throughout San Mateo County." The rebuttal to the argument against cites the first wording.
- The ballot question says "with independent citizen oversight". The ordinance's only related text is Sec. 2 (p. 7): the auditor's report "shall be presented to applicable Committee members and the City Council annually and made available for public review." It does not name the committee.
