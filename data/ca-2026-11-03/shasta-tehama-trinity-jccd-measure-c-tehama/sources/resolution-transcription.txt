BOND PROJECT LIST

Scope of Projects.

Specific School Facility Project List. The items presented on the following list provide the specific school facilities projects authorized to be financed with voter-approved bond proceeds. Examples provided in each category does not limit the broader meaning of the type of project authorized. Authorized projects are:

- Upgrade, expand and construct facilities for career training supporting successful careers and transfer to universities.
- Construct classrooms and training facilities including all related improvements supporting career technical education, including careers in welding, public safety, agriculture and nursing.
- Develop through new construction and/or facilities modernization a regional training center to support community education and regional educational collaboratives.
- Provide new science and math labs and expand College facilities providing instruction in Science, Technology, Engineering and Math (STEM).
- Expand, modernize and construct simulation and specialized labs for career training and preparation including careers in public safety and medicine.
- Expand and modernize the Regional Public Safety Training Center and related facilities to provide needed educational programs for fire, law enforcement and emergency medical training.
- Upgrade and replace existing information technology infrastructure and network systems to improve efficiency and increase capacity.
- Upgrade and modernize old restrooms and replace deteriorating plumbing and sewer systems.
- Upgrade outdated and inadequate electrical systems.
- Renovate, repair or replace outdated laboratories, classrooms, and support facilities, including all improvements such as windows, doors, flooring, roofing, lighting, security systems, instructional and learning equipment and devices.
- Improve student safety, disabled access and accommodations to meet all legal and regulatory requirements, emergency preparedness, and security systems, including security cameras, emergency mass announcement

[PDF page 12]

systems, keying systems, lighting, fencing, smoke detectors, fire alarms and sprinklers.
- Expand sustainability through stormwater/rainwater capture and reuse systems, solar initiatives, water reduction programs including but not limited to artificial turf installation, and wastewater facilities improvements.
- Construct new student housing and/or renovate/expand/modernize current student housing.
- Upgrade, modernize, expand and construct indoor and outdoor athletic and recreation facilities including tracks, fields, stadiums and pools and all related facilities such as changing rooms, restrooms, seating, announcement systems and scoreboards, food service and parking, including replacing outdated equipment and renovating facilities for regional competition use, and including all types of indoor and outdoor lighting.
- Construct, update and/or modernize facilities supporting performing arts including instruction and performance spaces, including for music, dance, comedy, and drama/theater, and for other community events.
- Modernize and construct student and staff meeting and gathering spaces including improvements to food service, libraries, study spaces and related areas.
- Improvements to exteriors including ensuring walkways, pathways, ramps and parking lots are repaired and safe and expanded, installing retaining walls as needed, improving grading, adding signage, parking, railings, landscaping, irrigation and exterior lighting.
- Health and safety upgrades Including all upgrades required by laws, regulations and building codes, including the Field Act, to provide a safe and healthy environments including improvements to improve air quality and seismic improvements as recommended, and includes addressing unforeseen conditions revealed in connection with construction such as dry rot or other structural issues.
- Furniture, equipment including technology equipment, and all work necessary and incidental to specific projects described above as more particularly described below.

APPENDIX C

TAX RATE STATEMENT
REGARDING PROPOSED
SHASTA-TEHAMA-TRINITY JOINT COMMUNITY COLLEGE DISTRICT
GENERAL OBLIGATION BONDS

An election will be held in the Shasta-Tehama-Trinity Joint Community College District (the "District") of the counties of Shasta, Tehama, Trinity, Lassen, Humboldt and Modoc, California, on November 3, 2026, to authorize the sale of up to $129,630,000 in bonds of the District to finance school facility improvements as described in the measure. If such bonds are authorized and sold, principal and interest on the bonds will be payable only from the proceeds of ad valorem property tax levies made upon the taxable property in the District. The following information is provided in compliance with Sections 9400-9404 of the Elections Code of the State of California. Such information is based upon the best estimates and projections presently available from official sources, upon experience within the District, and other demonstrable factors.

Based upon the foregoing and projections of the District's assessed valuation, the following information is provided:

1. The best estimate of the average annual tax rate which would be required to be levied to fund this bond issue over the entire duration of the bond debt service, based on a projection of assessed valuations available at the time of filing of this statement, is $7.76 per $100,000 of assessed value. The final fiscal year in which the tax is anticipated to be collected is 2060-61.

2. The best estimate of the highest tax rate which would be required to be levied to fund this bond issue, based on a projection of assessed valuations available at the time of filing of this statement, is $15.29 per $100,000 of assessed value. It is estimated that such rate would be levied in fiscal years 2031-32 through 2042-43.

3. The best estimate of the total debt service, including the principal and interest, that would be required to be repaid if all the bonds are issued and sold is approximately $264,800,000.

Voters should note the estimated tax rate is based on the assessed value (not market value) of taxable property on the applicable county's official tax rolls. In addition, taxpayers eligible for a property tax exemption such as the homeowner's exemption will have a lower effective tax rate than described above. Property owners should consult their own property tax bills and tax advisors to determine their property's assessed value and any applicable tax exemptions.

The attention of all voters is directed to the fact that the foregoing information is based upon projections and estimates only, which amounts are not maximum amounts and are not binding upon the District. The actual debt service, tax rates and the years in which they will apply may vary from those used to provide the estimates set forth above, due to factors such as variations in the timing of bond sales, the par amount of bonds sold and market interest rates available at the time of each sale, actual assessed valuations over
