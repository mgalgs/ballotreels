Full text: the measure enacts a transactions and use tax ordinance; `resolution-21-2026.pdf` is the resolution placing it (I did not check whether the ordinance is attached as an exhibit). No tax rate statement applies (sales tax, not a bond).

## Ballot question

From `notice-of-measures.pdf`, p. 1:

> CITY OF CLAYTON SALES TAX MEASURE. To provide local funding to maintain City of Clayton programs, services, and facilities, including: police services, traffic enforcement and road safety, road and sidewalk improvements, enhance library and recreation opportunities, park maintenance, recruitment and retention of city employees and other city services, shall the City of Clayton levy a 1% sales tax until ended by voters generating approximately $1,000,000 annually, subject to public review, the annual audit and all funds benefitting Clayton?

## Who votes on it

- Notice, p. 1: "NOTICE IS HEREBY GIVEN to the qualified electors of the City of Clayton, located in the County of Contra Costa, State of California, that in accordance with law, an election will be held on Tuesday, the 3rd day of November, 2026"
- Impartial analysis: "If approved by a majority of Clayton voters, this Measure would authorize a one percent (1%) retail transactions and use (sales) tax within the City of Clayton."

## Impartial analysis — key statements (signed /s/ Malathy Subramanian, City Attorney)

- "On June 16, 2026, the City Council voted unanimously to place Measure M on the November 3, 2026 General Municipal Election ballot."
- "It is estimated that Measure M will provide an additional $1,000,000 in annual local funding that could be used for City of Clayton programs, services and facilities"
- "This tax would be a “general tax,” meaning that revenues raised from the tax would go into the City’s general fund to pay for any lawful City program, improvement, or service."
- "If approved, the tax would become operative on April 1, 2027 and remain in effect until repealed by voters at a subsequent election."
- "A “yes” vote on Measure M will authorize the 1% transactions and use (sales) tax."
- "A “no” vote on Measure M will not authorize the 1% transactions and use (sales) tax."
