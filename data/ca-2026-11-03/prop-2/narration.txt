Proposition 2 is about California's Rainy Day Fund.
California's tax money rises and falls with the stock market, but its budget has to balance every year.
So the state puts money into the fund in good years, to use in bad years.
The trade-off is that money in the fund isn't spent on programs that year.
Today, required deposits stop once the fund reaches 10 percent.
Under Proposition 2, deposits would continue until the fund reaches 20 percent.
The Legislative Analyst's Office says budgets would be somewhat easier to balance in downturns.
Opponents say Proposition 2 weakens the spending limit and makes tax rebates less likely.
Supporters say Proposition 2's bigger fund would protect schools and services in a downturn.
A yes vote means the rules would change.
A no vote means the rules would not change.
