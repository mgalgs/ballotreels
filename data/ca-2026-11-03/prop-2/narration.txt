Proposition 2 changes how much California must save in its Rainy Day Fund, the state's main budget reserve.
The Legislature placed this constitutional amendment on the ballot.
Today, the state must deposit part of its tax revenue in the fund each year, until the fund reaches 10 percent of General Fund taxes.
The state must also make extra payments toward pension and retiree health debts, until 2030.
Proposition 2 raises the fund's target to 20 percent, with larger deposits when revenue from investment gains is very high.
It extends the extra debt payments to 2040, for a wider set of debts.
And deposits into reserves would count toward the state's spending limit when they are spent, not when they are saved.
The Legislative Analyst's Office estimates that state reserves would be higher, and that the state might make more debt payments.
The arguments that follow are their authors' opinions. No official agency checked them for accuracy.
Opponents say it weakens the spending limit voters approved in 1978, making the tax rebates that limit can trigger less likely.
They say it adds no new money for schools, health care, or public safety, and the state should follow its existing rules.
And they say its backers soon voted to suspend over 5 billion dollars in deposits.
Their argument is signed by leaders of the Assembly Budget Committee, the Senate Elections and Constitutional Amendments Committee, and Reform California.
Supporters say a larger reserve would protect schools, health care, and public safety from cuts in the next downturn.
They say it makes the state save more of a revenue spike instead of spending it, and that the Legislative Analyst's Office recommends saving more.
And they say the rebate rule opponents cite rewards spending over saving.
Their argument is signed by leaders of the California Professional Firefighters, the Assembly Budget Committee, and the Los Angeles Area Chamber of Commerce.
A yes vote means reserves would be higher, and extra debt payments would continue for longer.
A no vote means the current rules would not change.
