                                       ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE D
Vote Yes on D to support quality education in Rocklin Unified School District WITHOUT INCREASING EXISTING TAX
RATES.
Rocklin-Unified-School-District has been educating generations of Rocklin families for 160 years. Today, many schools are
old. The District has exhausted its resources for major upgrades and renovations to our schools, leaving many classrooms
and school facilities outdated and in need of improvements.
Without increasing existing tax rates, Measure D will upgrade and renovate schools in the District to better educate local
children and provide a safe learning environment. Measure D will fund projects to support current and future education
programs that ensure all students receive the college preparation and career training they need to compete for good-paying
jobs.
Voting YES on D will also qualify our schools to receive $82 million in state matching funds, bringing taxpayer dollars
back to Rocklin.
Vote Yes on D to:
 • Expand and modernize career technical education (CTE) facilities to prepare students for future careers
 • Repair or replace leaky roofs and deteriorating plumbing and sewer systems
 • Make health, safety, and security improvements throughout Rocklin Unified School District classrooms and
     campuses
 • Replace outdated heating and air-conditioning systems and inadequate electrical systems
 • Construct new classrooms to replace outdated portable classrooms
Measure D has taxpayer safeguards:
 • Will not increase existing tax rates
 • Independent citizens’ oversight and annual audits
 • All funds stay local and must be spent on RUSD classrooms and school facilities
 • Funds cannot be spent on teachers’, staff or administrators’ salaries, benefits or pensions
Good schools benefit us all. They improve property values, provide a better learning environment for students,
increase safety, and educate tomorrow’s community leaders and service providers.
Join parents, teachers, local elected officials, and leaders and vote YES on D to support outstanding education in
Rocklin schools.

                          REBUTTAL TO THE ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE D
Measure D is not “free money,” and voters deserve to understand what they are approving.
Proponents say Measure D will provide $288,000,000 in school improvements without increasing existing tax rates. What
they don’t emphasize is that Measure D would continue a property-tax obligation scheduled to expire in 2028. As assessed
property values increase, the amount taxpayers pay also increases.
Proponents highlight $82,000,000 in potential State matching funds, if the State will provide $82,000,000 for eligible projects.
Voters should ask: Why does RUSD need $288,000,000in bonds and where will the remaining $206,000,000 go?
The District states bond funds can not be used for teachers,staff or administrator salaries,benefits or pensions. However,
that does not prevent the District from redirecting other unrestricted funds. If bond money pays for projects otherwise funded
with existing revenues, those revenues could be redirected to other expenses as 6 assistant-superintendents.
Voters deserve a clear, project-by-project accounting of the $288,000,000, including State funds and existing funds currently
budgeted for facilities.
Facilities maintenance and safety are important, but are projects such as $159,042.56 for seating at Rocklin High School
are a higher priority than leaky roofs, outdated plumbing,wiring and other safety needs.
The citizens’ oversight committee is advisory only, so who selects its members and who ultimately controls the
expenditures?
VOTE NO ON MEASURE D until taxpayers receive a clear accounting of total costs,State matching funds, project
expenditures, the impact on existing District finances and your increased property taxes.
Signed,
William J McEnroe
                                                            MD2
                                          ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE D
Before voting on Measure D, voters should carefully consider all of the available information-not just the information provided
in the campaign material.
According to the correspondence from the Placer County Auditor, “even though there is enough funds in reserves for the 2
existing bonds to pay them off, there may be factors that can affect whether a rate would be needed to be levied for either
bonds 2027/2028 and there is no way to determine that at this stage.” Therefore, claims that taxpayers will not see increased
tax rates deserve careful scrutiny.
The District states the proposed bond would begin at approximately the same tax rate as the 2002 bonds-about
$60/$100,000 of assessed value. Under Prop 13, assessed property values normally increase yearly, meaning as the value
goes up, so does the tax. The value of homes in 2002 were less expensive then.
District data shows enrollment has declined by 578 students over the past 5 years to 11,598, compared to a capacity of
more than 13,400. Given this available capacity, voters should ask whether borrowing an additional $288,000,000 is
necessary. The project list includes items that some taxpayers may consider low priorities, such as outdoor learning centers
which the weather may only allow limited use and other campus enhancements.
This is not a vote against education or maintaining our schools. It is a question of whether this is the right bond, at the right
time, and whether the District has demonstrated the need for additional long-term borrowing, given declining enrollment,
available facilities capacity and uncertainty about future taxpayers costs.
Please vote NO on Measure D
Signed,
William J McEnroe

                             REBUTTAL TO THE ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE D
Quality schools are key to providing an excellent education, preserving property values, and maintaining our great
community.
The argument against Measure D has some inaccuracies and misleading statements that merit your consideration.
Please consider the facts:
    FACT: Rocklin voters approved three measures in the past 35 years to improve our schools. Both the 1991 and 1998
    measures are fully paid off, and the outstanding 2002 measure will be paid off in 2028. The time has come, 24
    years later, to invest again in our neighborhood schools.
    FACT: Measure D makes financial sense. Measure D will replace the expiring 2002 tax measure but with a lower
    tax rate. Measure D’s tax rate will be lower and will not be levied until the last payment from 2002 has been made.
    FACT: Enrollment has nothing to do with Measure D. Whether there are 25 students in a classroom or 22, if the roof
    leaks, it must be replaced. Whether there are 500 students at a school or 400, if the plumbing and sewer systems are
    old, they need renovation.
    FACT: Measure D has strict accountability and taxpayer safeguards. Funds must be spent locally in Rocklin
    schools, and an independent citizens’ oversight committee of local residents must annually audit and review spending
    by law.
Measure D is a carefully considered measure that will upgrade safety, improve the quality of education for Rocklin students,
and benefit our community all without increasing tax rates.
We can’t afford to wait.
Join us and Vote YES on Measure D!

Gary Stevens, Rocklin Unified Audit Committee
