Measure K — November 3, 2026

Local Sales Tax Ballot Measure

On July 9, 2026, the Weed City Council voted unanimously to place Measure K on the November 3, 2026, ballot for consideration by City voters.

Measure K proposes a 1.5% local transactions and use tax, commonly called a sales tax. If approved by a majority of voters, the revenue would be deposited into the City of Weed's General Fund to support municipal services and operations.

This page provides factual information about the proposal. The City does not advocate a vote for or against Measure K.

Measure K at a Glance

Election Date November 3, 2026
Proposed Tax 1.5% additional local sales tax
Current Sales Tax 7.50%
Rate if Approved 9.00%
Estimated Annual Revenue Approximately $1.7 million
Revenue Fund City General Fund
Approval Requirement Majority of votes cast on Measure K
Expiration No automatic expiration; remains until repealed by voters
Financial Oversight Regular audits and public financial reporting

** The $1.7 million figure is a City staff estimate. Actual revenue would depend on taxable sales, economic conditions, collection costs, and the date the tax takes effect.

What Would Measure K Change?

If Measure K is approved, the combined sales tax rate in Weed would increase from 7.50% to 9.00%.

For example, on a $100 taxable purchase:

  • Current sales tax: $7.50
  • Sales tax if approved: $9.00
  • Additional tax: $1.50

The tax would apply to qualifying taxable transactions within the City, including purchases by residents, visitors, and travelers.

Certain purchases are exempt from sales tax under California law, including most groceries, prescription medications, qualifying medical supplies, and purchases made with CalFresh benefits. Residential rent and many services are not subject to sales tax.

California Sales and Use Tax Information

How Could Measure K Revenue Be Used?

Measure K is a general tax, meaning its revenue would be deposited into the City's General Fund rather than legally restricted to specific departments or projects.

General Fund revenue may support lawful municipal purposes, including:

  • Police protection and emergency response
  • Fire protection and wildfire preparedness
  • Emergency medical response
  • Building safety and inspection
  • Street, road, and sidewalk maintenance
  • Snow removal
  • Public infrastructure and facilities
  • Other municipal operations and financial obligations

The City Council would determine actual expenditures through the public budget process.

Approval of Measure K would not automatically fund a particular project, establish 24-hour police patrols, or guarantee a specified service level. Those decisions would remain subject to City Council appropriations, staffing, available resources, and operational requirements.

Why Was Measure K Placed on the Ballot?

The City Council considered Measure K following discussions about the City's financial position, increasing operating costs, and ongoing infrastructure and service needs. The City maintains approximately 25 miles of roadway, water and wastewater systems, public facilities, and emergency-response services for residents, businesses, and visitors.

Financial issues identified during the City's 2026 public presentations included:

  • Rising personnel, insurance, equipment, and maintenance costs
  • Roadway and infrastructure repair needs
  • Aging public facilities
  • Public-safety staffing and operational requirements
  • Long-term financial sustainability

The City has also utilized grants, deferred projects, staffing vacancies, and operational adjustments to manage costs.

Measure K was placed on the ballot so voters could decide whether to establish an additional local revenue source. These circumstances provide background for the proposal but do not restrict how General Fund revenue could ultimately be spent.

Financial Accountability

If approved, Measure K revenue would be collected and administered by the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration and returned to the City. The measure provides for regular audits. City revenues and expenditures are also reviewed through public budgets, financial reports, and independent auditing. Because Measure K is a general tax, its revenue would not be legally reserved for the specific services mentioned in the ballot question.

Finance Department

How Will the Election Work?

Only voters registered within the City of Weed may vote on Measure K.

  • YES: Approves the proposed 1.5% local transactions and use tax.
  • NO: Rejects the proposed tax.

A majority of votes cast on Measure K is required for approval.

If voters approve the measure, the tax would take effect in accordance with the implementation provisions of the adopted ordinance and applicable state requirements.

If voters reject the measure, the proposed additional 1.5% tax would not take effect.
