Sacramento City Unified School District Measure M asks the district's voters whether to adopt a $95 yearly tax on each parcel of property for special education.
The county would collect the tax with property taxes from July 1, 2027, until voters amend or repeal it.
Measure M needs two-thirds of the vote to pass.
From the second year, the tax would rise with inflation, by at most 3 percent a year.
Owners who live in their homes and are 65 or older, or receive certain disability benefits, could apply to be exempt.
The money could be spent only on special education and early intervention programs.
The ballot question says the tax would raise $10 million a year.
Opponents say the district should repair its finances before seeking more money, and the tax never expires and can rise annually.
Supporters say the federal government broke its promise to fund special education, and early intervention in 10 schools is succeeding.
A yes vote enacts the special tax.
A no vote does not enact the special tax.
