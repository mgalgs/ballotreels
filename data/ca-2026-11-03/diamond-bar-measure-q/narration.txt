Measure Q asks the voters of this city whether to add a 1 percent local sales tax for general city services.
Four of the five members of the City Council voted to place Measure Q on the ballot.
The 1 percent tax would be paid on top of current sales taxes, and collected the same way.
Sales taxes do not apply to groceries, prescription medicine, utilities and several kinds of services.
As a general tax, the money would go to the general fund, for any general city purpose.
Annual audits would be required, and the tax would last until ended by voters.
Measure Q needs a majority yes vote to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis says the city estimates $6 million more a year, varying with retail sales.
The official county list of measures, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure Q was submitted.
Two supporters filed an argument for Measure Q; its text is not among the official documents gathered for this video.
A yes vote authorizes the 1 percent sales tax.
A no vote does not authorize the sales tax.
