Measure Z asks the voters of this city whether to set a new tax that is charged when real property is sold.
Measure Z can take effect only if voters also approve Measure Y, a limited city charter.
Today, the city charges a property transfer tax at a rate that state law authorizes.
The tax would be $4 per $1,000 of the price for sales up to $2 million, $6 up to $10 million, and $8 above that.
Homes built before 2027 that sell for $2 million or less would not pay the tax.
The money could pay for any city purpose, and Measure Z needs a simple majority.
The city attorney's impartial analysis estimates about $230,000 a year, growing to between $3.3 million and $3.8 million by 2050.
Opponents say the city can later seek to cut the home exemption, and inflation will push more homes over the threshold.
Supporters say Measure Z protects existing homeowners, and asks new development and higher-value sales to help pay for city services.
A yes vote adopts the transfer tax ordinance.
A no vote rejects the ordinance, and the current transfer tax stays in effect.
