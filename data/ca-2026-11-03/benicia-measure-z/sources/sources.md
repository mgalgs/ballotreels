## Facts and quotes

**Ballot question** (Correction to Notice of Measures – Benicia Measures, 8/10/2026, https://content.solanocounty.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/Corrected_Notice_of_Measures_Benicia_XYZ.pdf): "Shall the Measure, to primarily ensure newly constructed property sales contribute to maintaining city services and infrastructure, establish a real property transfer tax that will • not apply to existing homes that sell for $2 million or less, but apply to other property sales at rates of • 0.004 for sales $2,000,000 or less, • 0.006 for sales $2,000,001 - $10,000,000, and • 0.008 for sales over $10,000,000, providing approximately $230,000 annually, until ended by voters, be adopted?"

Note: the original 8/5 Notice printed a different wording (under the heading "Business License Tax", with "not apply to immediate-family transfers" and "with citizen oversight"). The 8/10 correction supersedes it; the printed ballot was not checked.

**Who votes / vote needed (analysis):** "Measure Z asks the electorate of the City of Benicia ("City") to consider the adoption of the City of Benicia Real Property Transfer Tax Ordinance". "Measure Z will be adopted and the Ordinance will become effective only if both Measure Y and Z are approved by a majority vote."

**Impartial analysis** ("City Attorney's Impartial Analysis of Measure Z", submitted August 11, 2026, Benjamin Stock), key statements:
- "Measure Z can only be approved if the voters approve Measure Y."
- "...it is classified as a "general tax," not a "special tax.""
- Current rate: "California Revenue and Taxation Code Section 11911, authorized the City's current real property transfer tax rate of $0.275 for each $500.00 or fractional part of $500.00."
- New rates: "Sales $2,000,000 or less: $4 per $1000"; "Sales over $2,000,000 and up to $10,000,000: $6 per $1,000"; "Sales over $10,000,000: $8 per $1,000".
- "The Ordinance contains certain exemptions, including sales of residential homes built before January 1, 2027 and that sells for $2,000,000 or less. The Ordinance further allows buyers and sellers to negotiate among themselves as to who pays the tax."
- "The tax is estimated to generate approximately $230,000 annually, growing to between $3.3 million and $3.8 million by 2050."
- "A "no" vote on Measure Z rejects the Ordinance; however, the current tax authorized by California Revenue and Taxation Code Section 11911 would remain in effect."
