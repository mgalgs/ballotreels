IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE [TBD]

The California Constitution and Education Code authorize a school district to issue bonds for specified purposes if approved at an election by 55% of those voting on issuance of the bonds. [29]

The Board of Trustees of the Burlingame Elementary School District (District) proposes this Measure, which would authorize the District to issue bonds in a principal amount not to exceed $100 million. The bonds will have an interest rate not exceeding the legal maximum and will be repaid within the time permitted by law. The Tax Rate Statement printed in this pamphlet contains the District's best estimates of tax rates required to service the bond debt during the life of the bonds, which is anticipated to last until June 2058. The District's best estimate of the average annual tax rate levy to fund this bond is $16.00 per $100,000 ($0.016/$100) of assessed valuation. The District estimates that the total amount repayable during the life of the bond, including principal and interest, will be approximately $195.5 million. [125]

The California Constitution requires the listing of specific projects to be funded from the bond revenue and certification that the District Board of Trustees has evaluated safety, class size reduction, and information technology needs in the development of that list. The Bond Project List can be found in the full text of the Measure, and includes without limitation: [53]

- Construct, acquire, renovate, and equip classrooms, science labs, libraries, and other instructional facilities;
- Repair or replace leaky roofs, aging exterior walls, flooring, and ceilings;
- Replace aging and inefficient plumbing, electrical, and HVAC systems;
- Replace portable classrooms with permanent classrooms;
- Upgrade technology, computers, and network infrastructure;
- Upgrade security, fire, and safety systems, including security cameras and access controls;
- Complete accessibility improvements required by law;
- Repair, replace, or upgrade paved surfaces, parking, and outdoor facilities; and
- Make other repairs and improvements to modernize school facilities. [92]

The Measure authorizes equipment acquisition, upgrades, repairs, services, construction, and other items related to the listed projects. The Bond Project List does not imply a specific prioritization among the projects, and should be reviewed for further details. [35]

No proceeds from the bonds shall be used for teacher or administrator salaries or other school operating expenses, except to reimburse the District for the cost of staff time spent working on the bond projects themselves. [36]

State law requires that the District take certain steps to account for the proceeds from the bonds. Accordingly, the District will appoint an independent citizens' oversight committee, establish a special account for deposit of the bond proceeds, and conduct annual independent performance and financial audits to ensure that bond funds are spent only for the purposes listed in the Bond Project List and for no other purposes. [65]

A "yes" vote on this Measure would authorize the District to issue bonds in a principal amount not to exceed $100 million for the purposes listed in the Bond Project List. [28]

A "no" vote would prevent the District from issuing the bonds. [11]

This Measure passes if at least 55% of those voting on the Measure vote "yes." [15]
