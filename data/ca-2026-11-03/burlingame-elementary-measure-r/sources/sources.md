# Measure R — Burlingame School District (general obligation bond, $100,000,000)

- **Jurisdiction:** Burlingame School District. The resolution calls it "Burlingame Elementary School District"; ACRE's link titles call it "Burlingame School District".
- **Kind:** School facilities general obligation bond (Proposition 39), $100,000,000.
- **Who votes:** the qualified voters of the District. Section 2 (resolution p. 2): "a favorable vote of no less than 55% of the qualified voters voting at the election shall constitute approval thereof". Section 4 (p. 2) asks the Elections Officer to consolidate "the Bond Election with the general election within the territory of the District being conducted on the Election Date."
- **Vote threshold:** 55%. Section 3 (p. 2): "the foregoing Bond Measure shall become effective upon the favorable vote of no less than 55% of those qualified voters voting on the proposition." Impartial analysis p. 2: "This Measure passes if at least 55% of those voting on the Measure vote "yes.""
- **Placed by:** Board of Trustees, Resolution No. 2025/2026-25.
- **Election:** November 3, 2026.

## Ballot question

> To repair aging Burlingame schools and maintain academic excellence by fixing leaky roofs; removing dry rot, lead/asbestos; replacing aging plumbing/electrical systems; updating science, technology/engineering labs; and providing teachers with instructional tools to support student success, shall Burlingame Elementary School District's measure authorizing $100,000,000 in bonds at legal interest rates be adopted, at $16.00 per $100,000 of assessed value (approximately $6,567,830 annually) while bonds are outstanding, with oversight, audits, no funds for administrators, and local control?

(resolution-full-text.pdf p. 11, Exhibit B "Bond Measure (Abbreviated Form)"; read from the scan. Answer labels "Bonds- YES" / "Bonds- NO".)

## Full text (resolution)

- Resolution No. 2025/2026-25, "RESOLUTION CALLING FOR AN ELECTION AUTHORIZING THE ISSUANCE OF GENERAL OBLIGATION BONDS OF THE DISTRICT AT AN ELECTION TO BE HELD NOVEMBER 3, 2026" (p. 1).
- Adopted: "PASSED AND ADOPTED this 9th day of June, 2026, by the Board of Trustees of Burlingame Elementary School District, at Burlingame, California, by the following vote: AYES: 5 NOES: 0 ABSENT: 0 ABSTAIN: 0" (p. 6; numbers handwritten). Signatures redacted by ACRE.
- Section 3 (p. 2): the Board asks the Elections Officer to "call an election ... to consider the proposition attached hereto as **Exhibit A** on November 3, 2026 (the "Bond Measure")", and "to reprint **Exhibit A** in its entirety in the voter information pamphlet".
- Recital (p. 1): "some of our school facilities at our campuses – some of which are over 100 years old – need repairs and upgrades".
- Recital (p. 1): the measure "would include accountability safeguards, including an independent citizens' oversight committee, mandatory annual audits to ensure all funds are spent as promised, a project list outlining specific uses of funds, a requirement that no money could be taken by the State of California (the "State"), and a prohibition on the use of funds for administrators' or teachers' salaries".
- Exhibit A (pp. 7-10, the full bond measure and project list) was not transcribed here.

## Impartial analysis

Headed "IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE [TBD]". **No author's name or office is printed** on either page, so the author is unconfirmed from this file. Bracketed numbers are per-paragraph word counts in the original.

- p. 1: "The Board of Trustees of the Burlingame Elementary School District (District) proposes this Measure, which would authorize the District to issue bonds in a principal amount not to exceed $100 million."
- p. 1: the bond debt "is anticipated to last until June 2058. The District's best estimate of the average annual tax rate levy to fund this bond is $16.00 per $100,000 ($0.016/$100) of assessed valuation. The District estimates that the total amount repayable during the life of the bond, including principal and interest, will be approximately $195.5 million."
- p. 1: "No proceeds from the bonds shall be used for teacher or administrator salaries or other school operating expenses, except to reimburse the District for the cost of staff time spent working on the bond projects themselves."
- p. 1: "the District will appoint an independent citizens' oversight committee, establish a special account for deposit of the bond proceeds, and conduct annual independent performance and financial audits".
- p. 2: "A "yes" vote on this Measure would authorize the District to issue bonds in a principal amount not to exceed $100 million for the purposes listed in the Bond Project List." / "A "no" vote would prevent the District from issuing the bonds."

## Tax rate statement (resolution-full-text.pdf p. 12, Exhibit "C")

Dated "June 9, 2026" (handwritten); the signer's name is redacted (the visible part reads "Su[...]strict").

- "The best estimate of the average annual tax rate which would be required to be levied to fund this bond issue over the entire duration of the bond debt service, based on a projection of assessed valuations available at the time of filing of this statement, is 1.60 cents per $100 of assessed valuation (or $16.00 per $100,000 of assessed value). The final fiscal year in which the tax is anticipated to be collected is 2057-58."
- "The best estimate of the highest tax rate which would be required to be levied to fund this bond issue, based on a projection of assessed valuations available at the time of filing of this statement, is 1.60 cents per $100 of assessed valuation (or $16.00 per $100,000 of assessed value). It is estimated that such rate would be levied starting in fiscal year 2027-28 and following."
- "The best estimate of the total debt service, including the principal and interest, that would be required to be repaid if all the bonds are issued and sold is approximately $195,482,493."

## Notes

- The opponents' argument and rebuttal both describe a "$97M Bond Issue" / "$97,000,000 debt". The resolution, ballot question, impartial analysis and tax rate statement all say $100,000,000. The figure is the opponents' as filed.
- The argument against says "Measure Ø" in its heading and closing and "measure K" once ("the mountain of debt that is measure K"); K is the San Carlos School District parcel tax. The rebuttal to the argument in favor uses "Measure R". The argument in favor says "Measure __". Whether the printed guide corrects these is unconfirmed.
- The impartial analysis says the debt lasts "until June 2058"; the tax rate statement says the final collection year is fiscal year 2057-58. These agree (FY 2057-58 ends June 2058).
- Handwritten names and titles are my reading of the scans; the printed pamphlet was not checked.
