Measure C asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $38,000,000 in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds over time and repay them from a tax on property in the district.
Measure C needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money could go to projects such as repairing roofs, replacing heating and cooling systems, and building science, agriculture and career classrooms.
The district's tax statement estimates $47.33 a year per $100,000 of assessed value, on average, through 2061-2062.
The statement estimates the highest rate at $49 per $100,000, in 2026-2027.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $77.6 million.
Supporters say many classrooms and school facilities are outdated, and the measure protects taxpayers with audits and citizens' oversight.
No argument against Measure C was submitted, so the county voter guide prints only the argument in favor of it.
A yes vote supports selling up to $38,000,000 in bonds for the listed school projects.
A no vote is a vote against selling the bonds.
