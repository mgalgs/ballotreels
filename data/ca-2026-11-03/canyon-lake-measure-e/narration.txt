City of Canyon Lake Measure E asks voters in the city whether to add a one-cent sales tax for police, fire and paramedic services.
Measure E reached the ballot by a voter petition, and it needs a simple majority to pass.
Measure E would be a special tax, restricted to set purposes, and would stay until voters repeal it.
The tax and some existing city revenues, including utility tax, fire and emergency medical money, would go into a separate public safety fund.
That fund could pay only for public safety, including police, fire and paramedic staff, equipment, training and emergency medical costs.
The City Attorney's impartial analysis says the tax is estimated to provide about $1.14 million a year.
No argument against Measure E was filed, and none is in the Riverside County Voter Information Guide.
Supporters say Measure E pays firefighters and police officers, and every surrounding city has this tax rate.
A yes vote will adopt Measure E and authorize the 1 percent sales tax.
A no vote will not adopt Measure E or authorize the 1 percent sales tax.
