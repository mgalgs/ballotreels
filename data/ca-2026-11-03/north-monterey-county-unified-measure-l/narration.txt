Measure L asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $40 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them with a yearly tax on property in the district.
Measure L needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The ballot question lists projects such as fixing roofs, plumbing, heating and electrical systems, and upgrading classrooms.
The impartial analysis, citing the district's tax rate statement, estimates the highest rate at about $32 a year per $100,000 of assessed value.
The analysis says the tax is expected to be collected through 2054.
Supporters say nearly all district schools were built over 60 years ago and don't meet safety codes, and some have asbestos and lead pipes.
Opponents say school bonds are like credit cards, repaid with interest over 30 years, and the district should fund upkeep within its existing budget.
A yes vote is in favor of issuing $40 million in school bonds.
A no vote is against issuing $40 million in school bonds.
