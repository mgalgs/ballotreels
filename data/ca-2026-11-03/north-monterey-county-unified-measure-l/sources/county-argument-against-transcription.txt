Argument Against Bond Measure L

BOND DEBT IS NOT A LONG-TERM SOLUTION
School bonds are like credit cards. The repairs are charged today, but you repay the debt with interest at unknown rates over 30 years. Using a credit card for regular maintenance and long-term improvements, is reckless.

WHAT WILL YOU PAY IF MEASURE L passes?
NMCUSD's own projections show combined tax rates reaching $65 per $100,000 in 2029 and remaining above $50 through 2044.The past 12-year average tax rate has been $58.55 per $100,000.
When costs to landlords, apartment owners, and businesses go up, they are often passed on to tenants and consumers.

TRUST IS EARNED: NMCUSD doesn't guarantee any project will be completed.
That disclaimer is stated on the district website. Past experience demonstrates that:

1. Measure E (2002) promised a middle school. It was never built.
2. Measure H (2013) authorized $23.8M for projects that appear again in Measure L.

THE SOLUTION
When money is tight, responsible leaders make hard choices and stretch every dollar. NMCUSD should prioritize maintenance within its existing budget, pursue matching grants, fundraise, and save for major improvements. More than $120,000 in documented catering expenditures during one school year raises legitimate questions about spending priorities. Beyond bond, state, and federal funding, Monterey County School receive 61% of local property-tax revenue.

WHAT'S THE REAL PROBLEM?
The MCOE letter to the District dated 04/20/2026, re: State mandated Williams Act Report: "no findings" of facility conditions posing an emergency or urgent threat to student health or safety.

CLASSROOM INSTRUCTION SHOULD COME FIRST
Bond funds cannot pay teacher salaries, instructional programs, or ordinary operating expenses. Despite spending approximately $23,000 per student annually, only 7.2% of incoming ninth graders were proficient in math and 26.3% in reading.

VOTE NO ON MEASURE L

The District must manage their annual budget, just like you do.
