Measure E asks the voters of this county whether to add a limit on eminent domain to the county charter.
Eminent domain lets a government take private property for public use, paying the owner just compensation.
In 2005, the United States Supreme Court confirmed that a government may take property for economic development and transfer it to a different private owner.
Measure E would bar the county from taking property from a private owner, without the owner's agreement, to convey it to another private party.
Measure E needs a majority of the vote to pass.
The county voter guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Opponents say state law already tightly controls eminent domain, and county voters rejected this same measure two years ago.
Supporters say the amendment would protect local private property owners from having their property taken for another private party.
A yes vote means the board of supervisors cannot authorize eminent domain to take property from a private owner for another private party.
A no vote means the board of supervisors will keep its current ability to authorize taking property for another private party.
