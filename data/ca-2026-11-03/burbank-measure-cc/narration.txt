Measure CC asks the voters of this city whether to drop the charter rule that council members be elected citywide.
Today, every voter can vote for as many council candidates as there are open seats, and candidates run citywide.
Only the voters can change that method, by amending the charter.
The City Council placed Measure CC on the ballot under the settlement of a lawsuit brought under the state's voting rights law.
Measure CC would let an ordinance, passed by the City Council or by the voters, set how council members are elected.
A separate measure on this ballot would create council districts, but could take effect only if Measure CC passes too.
If that district measure fails, Measure CC would still let the council or the voters adopt a different district plan later.
Measure CC needs a majority yes vote to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
The official county list of measures, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure CC was submitted.
Supporters say Measure CC lets the city adapt its elections to changing law and reduces the risk of costly lawsuits.
A yes vote removes the citywide election rule, so an ordinance would set how council members are elected.
A no vote keeps the charter rule that council members be elected citywide.
