Measure I asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $250 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them from a yearly tax on property in the district.
Measure I needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay only for the projects the measure lists.
Those include replacing aging roofs and heating, plumbing and electrical systems, and replacing aging portables with permanent classrooms.
The district's tax rate statement estimates a tax of $36 per $100,000 of assessed value, through 2053-2054.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $482.1 million.
Supporters say many schools are nearly 90 years old with failing systems, and citizen oversight and audits would ensure the money is used as intended.
No argument against Measure I appears among the official documents gathered for this video; the county list of measures links only the argument in favor.
A yes vote is in favor of issuing $250 million in general obligation bonds for the purposes the measure sets out.
A no vote is against issuing the bonds.
