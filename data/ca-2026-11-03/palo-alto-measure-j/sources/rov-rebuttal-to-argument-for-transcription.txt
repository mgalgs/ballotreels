REBUTTAL TO THE ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE J

THE ARGUMENT FOR RAISING THE SALES TAX TO 10.25% IS DELIBERATELY DECEPTIVE.

The Argument pretends the money raised by increasing the sales tax is specifically earmarked for Cubberley. NOT TRUE.

It is a general tax. City Hall can spend the money on anything. Measure J hands the City a blank check.

Why should taxpayers pay $65.5 million to transfer LAND WE ALREADY OWN from the School District to the City itself? The Argument in Favor offers no explanation.

Instead, it is stuffed with unsupported assertions (e.g., renovations will be "lower-cost") and misleading statements (e.g., it implies the Measure specifically exempts prescription drugs ... but they're already tax exempt).

More generally, it paints a sophomorically utopian picture of a future Cubberley that is all things to all people and where money is no object.

If the supporters of Measure J were genuinely interested only in purchasing the Cubberley property and refurbishing it, they would have proposed a one-time bond issue to raise the money, not a forever sales tax the proceeds from which can be used for any purpose.

Palo Alto, with only 67,000 people, has an annual budget of $1.4 BILLION! Our City needs more common sense. What it doesn't need is a new forever sales tax that will take the tax rate up to 10.25%.

Ask yourself: How much do you want a sandwich in this town to cost?

Vote No on Measure J.
