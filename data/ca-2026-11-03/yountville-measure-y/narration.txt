Town of Yountville Measure Y asks Yountville voters whether to raise the yearly limit on how much tax revenue the town may spend.
Since 1979, the state constitution has required local governments to adopt a yearly limit on the tax revenue they may spend.
If the town takes in more than its limit over two years, it must return the excess by revising tax rates or fees.
Voters may raise the limit for up to four years at a time, and the Town Council placed Measure Y on the ballot.
Measure Y would raise the limit by $3 million plus one-fourth of the prior year's transient occupancy tax revenue, each year for four years.
It needs a simple majority; if it fails, the limit stays as it is, adjusted each year under state law.
The town attorney's impartial analysis says Measure Y approves no new taxes and raises no existing ones.
No argument against Measure Y appears among the documents for the measure that the Town of Yountville posted online.
Supporters say Measure Y keeps already collected taxes funding police, fire and parks, instead of returning them under state law.
A yes vote increases the yearly limit for four years.
A no vote rejects the increase and keeps the limit as adjusted under state law.
