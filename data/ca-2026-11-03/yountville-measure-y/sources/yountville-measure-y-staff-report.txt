DISCUSSION/BACKGROUND
The Town Council adopted Ordinance Number 340-04 changing the Town’s General Municipal Election date to
the Tuesday after the first Monday in November of even-numbered years to coincide with the elections of the
other jurisdictions within the County and to be part of the Consolidated General Municipal Elections beginning
with the November 2006 election.

On June 5, 2012, the Town Council adopted Resolution Number 3026-12 recognizing changes to candidate
statements related to foreign language requirements based on the 2010 Census results requiring Napa County
to publish voter election materials in English and Spanish. There is no cost to the candidate for translation. The
adopted budget for Fiscal Year 2026/2027 includes funding to cover translation costs for candidate statements.

Yountville Municipal Code Section 2.04.070 states the general municipal election for the Town shall be held on
the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November of even-numbered years beginning November 2006.
Pursuant to the Elections Code, the Town of Yountville must call and give notice of a General Municipal Election
to be held Tuesday, November 3, 2026, for the purpose of electing one mayor, two (2) council members, and
voting on a proposed ballot measure related to an increase in the Town’s appropriations (GANN) limit.

In addition, the Town Council shall submit to the Napa County Board of Supervisors a resolution requesting that
the General Municipal Election be consolidated with the Statewide General Election to be held on the same date
and that, within the Town, the precincts, polling places, and election for the above-referenced officers and
measure be the same; that the Napa County Elections Division canvass the returns of the General Municipal
Election; and that the election be held in all respects as if there were only one election.

The attached resolution also provides for additional procedural requirements related to the election process,
including but not limited to the posting of a Notice of Election and Notice to Voters of the Date After Which
Arguments For or Against Town Ballot Measures Must Be Filed.

The proposed resolution authorizes the inclusion on the November 3, 2026, ballot of a measure necessary to
increase the appropriations limit for the Town. The Administrative Services Director has reviewed the Town’s
limit and projections of proceeds of taxes for the next four years and recommends that a voter-approved override
be re-established.

In summary, due to the Town’s economic growth, it is necessary to increase the current appropriations limit as
required by state law by $3,000,000 plus a portion of actual TOT revenue in the prior fiscal year to ensure these
revenues can be spent on essential services. This approach provides a smaller initial flat increase with a portion
of actual revenues based on the Town’s most direct tourism-related revenue source. This option allows the
annual override amount to increase or decrease based on changes in TOT revenues from the prior year. This
methodology will provide the Town greater flexibility and certainty in utilizing projected tax revenues for
community purposes while remaining below the appropriations limit.

The Town of Yountville has a unique financial structure, receiving approximately 88% of its revenues from TOT,
property tax, and sales tax. TOT represents approximately 58% of General Fund revenues, while sales tax
accounts for approximately 12%, both of which are primarily paid by visitors to the community. Local property
tax revenues account for approximately 18% and are currently insufficient to cover the costs of contracted law
enforcement, fire, and emergency medical services.

An override is necessary to allow the Town to continue utilizing its tax revenues for the provision of core public
services such as law enforcement, fire and emergency medical services, street maintenance, parks
maintenance, and maintaining the level of services currently provided to residents as outlined in the adopted
budget. The Town will not be able to maintain this level of public service unless it is able to fully utilize revenues
currently received and projected to be received in the future, the majority of which are generated through TOT
and sales taxes.

This will be the eleventh override presented to voters since 1985. Voters have approved all prior measures.

Determination Whether to Participate in Ballot Arguments
The Town Council has prepared arguments in favor of prior ballot measures related to increases in
appropriations (GANN) limits. The information below outlines the Elections Code requirements for preparing
arguments and rebuttals.

The Elections Code provides that any “member or members of the legislative body authorized by that body, or
an individual voter who is eligible to vote on the measure, or bona fide association of citizens, or a combination
of voters and associations, may file written argument for or against any city measure.” (Elections Code, §
9282(b).) No primary argument may exceed 300 words and must include the printed name and signature of the
author or authors submitting it. If submitted on behalf of an organization, the argument must include the name
of the organization and the printed name and signature of at least one principal officer who is an author of the
argument. No more than five (5) signatures may appear on any argument. (Elections Code Section 9283)
Rebuttal arguments may also be submitted in response to arguments either in favor of or against the measure.
Rebuttal arguments are limited to 250 words and may contain no more than five (5) signatures. (Elections Code
Section 9285)

Generally, the Elections Code provides that primary arguments are due fourteen (14) days from the date the
Council calls the election on the measure. However, this deadline does not apply when the election is
consolidated with another election. Accordingly, the deadline for primary arguments is subject to the discretion
of the Elections Official/Town Clerk. The deadline for rebuttal arguments is established by state law and must
be no later than ten (10) days after the final filing date for primary arguments.

If more than one argument is submitted for or against the measure, the Elections Official must select the
arguments to be printed and published using the following order of preference:
    1. The legislative body or members of the legislative body authorized by that body.
    2. The individual voter, bona fide association of citizens, or combination of voters and associations who
       are bona fide sponsors or proponents of the measure.
    3. Bona fide associations of citizens.
    4. Individual voters who are eligible to vote on the measure.

Council should determine whether it wishes to participate in the ballot arguments or allow an individual or
organization to author the primary and/or rebuttal arguments. The following may assist the Council in making
these decisions:
   1. Decide whether to author a primary argument in support of the measure as a whole Council or allow an
       organization or individual to author the primary argument.
   2. If the Council decides to author the primary argument, decide whether to delegate drafting
       responsibilities to one or more Council members.
   3. Decide whether to author a rebuttal argument as a whole Council or allow an organization or individual
       to author the rebuttal argument.
   4. If the Council decides to author the rebuttal argument, decide whether to delegate drafting
       responsibilities to one or more Council members.

Town Attorney’s Impartial Analysis
Finally, Elections Code section 9280 provides that whenever a local measure is placed on the ballot, the Council
may direct the Elections Official to transmit a copy of the measure to the Town Attorney to prepare an impartial
analysis showing the effect of the measure on existing law and the operation of the measure. The analysis may
not exceed 500 words and is printed preceding the arguments for and against the measure.

Consolidated General Municipal Election Update
The Town Council must adopt a resolution calling and giving notice of the holding of a General Municipal Election
to be held on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, and requesting the Board of Supervisors of the County of Napa to
consolidate and render specified services to the Town relating to the conduct of the Statewide General Municipal
Election to be held on the same date. The deadline to submit the request to the Board of Supervisors
consolidating the General Municipal Election with Napa County is July 5, 2026.

The nomination period for candidates opens at 8:00 a.m. on July 13, 2026, and closes at 5:00 p.m. on August
7, 2026. In accordance with California Elections Code sections 10220-10230, this is the period during which
individuals officially receive, circulate, and file nomination papers for candidacy. During this period, nomination
papers may be issued by Town Clerk staff to any eligible candidate during regular business hours. Town Clerk
staff will verify potential candidates’ eligibility with the County prior to releasing nomination papers. If an
incumbent fails to submit nomination papers by the deadline, non-incumbent candidates will have until 5:00 p.m.
on August 12, 2026, to file nomination papers.

Candidates are encouraged to contact Town Clerk staff to schedule an appointment to take out and/or file
nomination papers. Appointments may be made by contacting the Town Clerk’s Office at (707) 944-8851 or via
email at townclerk@yville.com.

The Town’s official 2026 Elections webpage is available as a reference. Additional information regarding the
General Municipal Election, including candidate and voter assistance information, will be posted on the website
as it becomes available throughout the election season.
