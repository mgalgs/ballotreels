Measure Y asks the voters of this high school district whether to approve $17,500,000 in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them from property taxes.
Measure Y needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money could go to projects such as renovating classrooms and restrooms, repairing heating and cooling, safety upgrades, and new career, agriculture and science classrooms.
The district's tax rate statement estimates $29.55 a year per $100,000 of assessed value, on average, through 2060-2061.
The statement estimates the highest rate at $30 per $100,000, from 2027-2028 through 2058-2059.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $36.8 million.
No argument against Measure Y was submitted, so the county voter guide prints only the argument in favor of it.
Supporters say many school facilities are aging and need major improvements, and the bond would strengthen career and technical education.
A yes vote would approve the bond measure and authorize the bonds.
A no vote would disapprove the bond measure.
