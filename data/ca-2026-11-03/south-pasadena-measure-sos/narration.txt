Measure SOS asks the voters of this city whether to issue $80 million in bonds to repair streets, sidewalks and storm drains.
The City Council placed Measure SOS on the ballot, and it needs two-thirds of voters to pass.
The bonds could pay for streets, sidewalks, curbs and gutters, evacuation routes and storm drains.
Property owners in the city would repay the bonds through a tax based on assessed value.
The money must stay in a separate account, with a yearly public report on how it is spent.
The tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $32.33 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $54.64, through 2063.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $154.8 million.
No argument against Measure SOS was submitted, according to the county Registrar's official list of ballot measures.
Supporters say delaying maintenance makes repairs more expensive, so local streets should be fixed before they fail.
A yes vote allows the city to issue the bonds for these repairs.
A no vote is against issuing the bonds.
