Measure TCS asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $146 million in school bonds.
The district's governing board placed Measure TCS on the ballot, and it needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay to upgrade safety, security and heating systems, remove asbestos and lead pipes, and repair classrooms and labs.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would be required.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $42.25 per $100,000 of assessed value, and a highest of $43, through 2063-2064.
The statement puts the total to repay, with interest, at about $319.1 million, and says its figures are estimates only, which may vary.
Supporters say many buildings are over 70 years old, with asbestos and lead pipes, and the measure will not increase taxes.
The official list of measures from the county, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure TCS was submitted.
A yes vote approves the $146 million in school bonds.
A no vote rejects the $146 million in school bonds.
