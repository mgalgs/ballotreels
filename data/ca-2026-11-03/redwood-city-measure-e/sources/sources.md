# Measure E — City of Redwood City — Initiative ordinance (rent stabilization and just-cause eviction)

- **Jurisdiction:** City of Redwood City.
- **Kind:** citizen initiative ordinance, the "Redwood City Fair and Affordable Housing Ordinance", repealing Municipal Code Chapter 42 (and, per the initiative's title, Chapter 42A) and adopting a new Chapter 42. Submitted to the voters by City Council Resolution No. 16425.
- **Who votes:** Redwood City voters. Resolution Sec. 1, PDF p. 2: the election is called "for the purpose of submitting the initiative measure described below and set forth in Exhibit A (attached hereto), to a vote of the qualified voters of the City of Redwood City".
- **Threshold:** simple majority. Resolution Sec. 9, PDF p. 3: "This measure proposes to amend the Redwood City Code and shall be approved if a majority (50% +1) of those voting on the measure approve it."

## Ballot question (verbatim)

Resolution Sec. 4, PDF p. 2 (text layer):

> Shall the measure repealing Redwood City Municipal Code Chapter 42 and replacing it with a new Chapter 42 that (1) limits rent increases on multifamily housing built before February 1, 1995; (2) expands just-cause eviction protections to most rental housing, including single-family homes, ADUs, and affordable housing; (3) requires new City administration estimated to cost $5 million to $11 million annually, funded by annual rental property owner fees; and (4) increases relocation assistance for no-fault evictions, be adopted?

## Impartial analysis

`impartial-analysis.pdf`: "CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE E", "(Word Count: 496)"; received by the City Clerk AUG 18 2026. Signed (signature redacted) "Veronica Ramirez, City Attorney, City of Redwood City" (p. 2).

- "This measure was placed on the ballot by an initiative petition signed by the requisite number of Redwood City registered voters. It requires a majority for passage." (p. 1)
- "Current law caps annual rent increases to between 5% and 10%, depending on the percentage change in local inflation, on specified rental units 15 years old or older. Current law also exempts single-family homes, condominiums, and housing built after February 1, 1995, from local rent control." (p. 1)
- "This initiative would impose rent control on specified units built before February 1, 1995, by capping the base rent rate to the amount paid on October 29, 2025 (for units occupied on that date) or to the initial rent rate (for units occupied after that date). Annual base rent rate increases would be capped at the rate equal to 60% of the percentage increase in local inflation and no more than 5%." (p. 1)
- "This initiative would extend just cause protections to single-family homes, ADUs, and deed-restricted affordable housing, and mobile homes." (p. 1)
- "for no-fault evictions, the initiative would require landlords to provide relocation assistance equal to 4 times the monthly U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development fair market rent or $12,000, whichever is greater, while elderly and disabled tenants would receive an additional $6,000." (p. 1)
- "The initiative would require the City to set annual rent increases; hear rent petitions, review tenant safety plans; issue regulations; and set and collect an annual fee on landlords to pay for administering the initiative." (p. 2)

## Tax rate statement / fiscal

No tax rate statement (this is not a tax measure). Fiscal figures in the official files: the ballot question's "new City administration estimated to cost $5 million to $11 million annually, funded by annual rental property owner fees" (resolution p. 2). The impartial analysis states no dollar cost. The Elections Code § 9212 supplemental report the Council requested (resolution p. 1) is not among ACRE's files; the arguments' "$11 million per year", "18 new full-time employees" and "$757 per rental unit" figures come from the opponents, not from an official analysis on file.
