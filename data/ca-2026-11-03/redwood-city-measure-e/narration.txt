City of Redwood City Measure E asks voters in the city whether to adopt new limits on rents and evictions.
Today, rent increases on certain older units are capped at between 5 and 10 percent a year, depending on inflation.
Measure E reached the ballot by voter petition and needs a simple majority to pass.
For certain units built before 1995, the cap would be 60 percent of inflation, and no more than 5 percent.
Just-cause eviction rules would cover single-family homes, accessory dwelling units and affordable housing.
The ballot question puts the yearly cost of new city administration at between $5 million and $11 million, paid by fees on rental property owners.
Opponents say Measure E is costly to run, forcing service cuts or new taxes, and burdens nonprofit housing groups.
Supporters say rent control keeps the city affordable and stable, funded by a small fee on landlords, not by renters.
A yes vote approves Measure E and its new rent rules.
A no vote rejects Measure E and its new rent rules.
