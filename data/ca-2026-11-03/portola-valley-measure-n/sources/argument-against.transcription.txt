Vote NO.

This 1% real property transfer tax is unfair and inequitable across taxpayers.

Only the 2-3% of residents selling properties each year would pay tax of $45,000 and up on an average-priced home sale, while the 97-98% of non-selling residents and renters get a free pass. Worse, if sellers leave town, they get nothing for those taxes. If they buy a replacement property here, they pay a second transfer tax, plus suffer a regular property tax reassessment up to full purchase price. How is any of this remotely fair or consistent with our Town’s embraced value of “community?”

Town Council failed to provide exemptions from multiple, separate transfer taxes for those moving within town, or for distressed sale due to fire or natural disaster.

This tax is bad economics. A transfer tax, like a tariff, reduces net gain to seller and/or increases price to buyer, thereby limiting both the number of potential buyers and realized prices. Our home sellers compete for buyers against sellers in neighboring communities unburdened by high transfer taxes.

Enacting this tax requires conversion to a Charter Town, which is promoted as maintaining “autonomy” and “independent status.” But that’s a false excuse, and unnecessary. There’s no demonstration that the Town’s existing autonomy and independence ever have or will be compromised.

Spending from this tax can’t be restricted to voters’ highest priority preferences, like enhanced wildfire protection, public safety, critical infrastructure, or any specific voter priority. Council sacrificed such guarantees so they could lower the approval threshold for passage to 50% +1 versus a two-thirds majority.

Council neither asked for nor received an endorsement from its own Finance Committee to advance this measure to voters.

There are better, fairer alternatives for additional revenue, and expense management, and an imperative to explore them.

Vote NO.
