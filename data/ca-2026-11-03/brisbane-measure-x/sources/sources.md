# Measure X — City of Brisbane (business license tax update, general tax)

- Jurisdiction: City of Brisbane.
- Kind: general tax; ordinance amending the Business License Tax (Brisbane Municipal Code Title 5, Division I).
- Who votes: the voters of the City of Brisbane. Resolution, p. 2, Sec. 2: the Council "approves the ballot language, and the Ordinance attached hereto as Attachment A, to be placed on the ballot and submitted to the voters of the City at the November 3, 2026 election."
- Vote threshold: majority. Resolution, p. 5, Sec. 11: "This Business License Tax is a general tax requiring the approval of a majority of qualified electors casting votes." Ordinance Sec. 7 (p. 7): effective "If this Ordinance is approved by a majority vote of the City of Brisbane electorate voting on the measure at the November 3, 2026 election".
- Placed by: the City Council, Resolution No. 2026-28.
- Election: November 3, 2026.

## Ballot question

> Shall the measure updating the City's Business License Tax by replacing the current tiered structure with a $50 tax for businesses with annual gross receipts of $25,000 or less and $50 plus $1.00 per $1,000 of gross receipts above $25,000 for most other businesses; establishing a minimum tax base equal to the cost of operations for businesses whose operating costs exceed gross receipts; and raising approximately $1,000,000 annually, until repealed by voters, be adopted?

(resolution-full-text.pdf p. 2, Sec. 1; from the OCR layer, with OCR spacing such as "$25, 000" closed up.)

## Full text (resolution / ordinance)

- Resolution No. 2026-28, "Resolution of the City Council of the City of Brisbane Submitting to the Electors in the November 3, 2026 Election a Ballot Measure to Update the City's Business License Tax by Amending Sections of Title 5, Division I of the Brisbane Municipal Code" (p. 1).
- Adopted: the City Clerk (Ingrid Padilla) certifies it "was duly and regularly passed and adopted at a meeting of said City Council on the 16th day of July, 2026, by the following votes: AYES: Councilmembers Davis, Kern, Lentz, O'Connell and Mayor Mackin NOES: None ABSENT:None ABSTAIN:None" (p. 5).
- Recital, p. 1: "the Tax is a general tax, as all revenues are deposited into the Brisbane General Fund and used for general services"; "the proposed revisions in this measure replace the tiered rate structure with a gross-receipts rate and modify the definition of "gross receipts,"".
- Sec. 6, p. 4: "Pursuant to Elections Code section 9223, the full text of the measure will not be printed in the voter information pamphlet." It is on the City's website.
- Exhibit A ordinance recital, p. 6: the Business License Tax Study "found that the City's current tax structure has not been significantly updated since 1983, that the top 100 businesses account for approximately 95 percent of reported gross receipts and 90 percent of total tax assessed".
- New gross-receipts definition (Attachment 1, BMC 5.04.110): "Where the gross receipts are less than the cost of maintaining the operations of the Licensee, then the Licensee shall be deemed to produce gross receipts in an amount at least equal to the cost of operations." (read from the underlined text on the page image; the OCR layer drops letters here).

## Impartial analysis

"MEASURE X CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS", signed "Samantha W. Zutler, City of Brisbane City Attorney", "DATED: August 14, 2026" (impartial-analysis.pdf pp. 1-2).

- "The Brisbane City Council placed this Measure on the ballot." (p. 1)
- "The Business License Tax is a general tax, meaning revenues are deposited into the General Fund and used for general City services. Passage requires approval by a simple majority of voters voting on it." (p. 1)
- "Under the current tax structure, most businesses pay multi-tiered rates ranging from $50 for businesses with gross receipts of $50,000 or less, to $1,300 plus $0.04 per $1,000 of the business's gross receipts for businesses with gross receipts of $10,000,000 or more." (p. 1)
- "A business with gross receipts of $25,000 or less would pay a tax of $50. A business with more than $25,000 in gross receipts would pay $50 plus $1.00 for every $1,000 in gross receipts above $25,000." (p. 1)
- "Measure X deletes existing tax exemptions for newspapers, charitable or nonprofit organizations, café musicians, real estate auctioneers, veterans, and residential rentals of less than five units." (p. 1)
- "The City estimates Measure X would generate approximately $1,000,000 in additional annual revenue." / "If the Measure is not approved, the current tax structure will remain in place." (p. 1)

## Tax rate statement / fiscal

General tax, not a bond; no tax rate statement or separate fiscal analysis is among ACRE's files. Rate, from the new "Rates" text in Attachment 1 (BMC 5.20.010; resolution-full-text.pdf pp. 14-15, underlined additions read from the page images):
- "(b) If those gross receipts are $25,000 or less, the licensee must pay a flat license tax of $50." (p. 14)
- "(c) If the gross receipts are greater than $25,000, then the license tax is calculated as follows: a. Take the total gross receipts and subtract $25,000. b. Multiply the remaining gross receipts by $1.00 per $1,000 of gross receipts above $25,000. c. Add $50 to the sum in step b to account for the first $25,000 in gross receipts." (pp. 14-15)
- "The rate applied is the maximum rate unless lowered by appropriate Council action." (p. 15)
- Duration: the ballot question says "until repealed by voters". Revenue: "approximately $1,000,000 annually" (question); the impartial analysis says "approximately $1,000,000 in additional annual revenue".

## Notes

- **Conflict inside the official record on the nonprofit exemption.** The ordinance's own recital (p. 6) says "the proposed measure would maintain existing exemptions and special tax provisions, including those applicable to nonprofit organizations". But Attachment 1 shows BMC 5.12.040 "Exemption — Charitable and nonprofit organizations" entirely in strikethrough (p. 11, checked on the page image), and the impartial analysis says the measure "deletes existing tax exemptions for ... charitable or nonprofit organizations". Do not state either without this caveat.
- The ordinance is headed "ORDINANCE NO. XXXX" (p. 6), unnumbered until adopted.
- Sec. 5 of the ordinance (p. 7) lets the Council amend it "in any manner that does not increase a tax rate".
