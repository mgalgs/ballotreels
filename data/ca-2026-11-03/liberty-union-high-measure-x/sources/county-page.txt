MEASURE X

MEASURE X

LIBERTY UNION HIGH SCHOOL DISTRICT

In order to repair and improve aging classrooms, fix deteriorating roofs, plumbing, heating, ventilation and electrical systems, remove asbestos/ lead pipes, upgrade science labs, libraries, technology and other facilities to support college/ career readiness in math, science, health, engineering, arts and skilled trades; shall Liberty Union High School District’s measure be adopted authorizing $165,000,000 in bonds at legal rates, levying $20 per $100,000 assessed value (raising $11,300,000 annually) while bonds are outstanding and all funds benefiting local schools?

COUNTY COUNSEL’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF

MEASURE X

The California Constitution provides that school districts may issue bonds for the construction, reconstruction, rehabilitation or replacement of school facilities, including the furnishing and equipping of school facilities or the acquisition or lease of real property for school facilities, with the approval of 55% of the voters voting at an election for that purpose.

By resolution, the Liberty Union High School District has proposed that bonds of the District be issued in an amount up to $165,000,000. The measure provides that proceeds from the sale of the bonds will be used to “repair and improve aging classrooms, fix deteriorating roofs, plumbing, heating, ventilation, and electrical systems, remove asbestos/lead pipes, upgrade science labs, libraries, technology and other facilities to support college/career readiness in math, science, health, engineering, arts and skilled trades.” The specific projects are set forth in the bond project list within the measure. The measure provides that a citizens’ oversight committee will be established to ensure that bond proceeds are properly expended and that annual performance and financial audits will be conducted. The measure further provides that bond proceeds will only be used for the purposes specified in the measure and not for any other purpose.

Approval of this measure authorizes levy upon taxable property in the district to repay the bonded indebtedness, both principal and interest, in each year that the bonds are outstanding. The Liberty Union High School District has prepared a Tax Rate Statement, which represents the District’s best estimates of the property tax rates required to service the bonds. The estimated highest annual tax rate required to be levied to fund the bonds is expected to be $20 per $100,000 of assessed valuation.

Approval of the measure does not guarantee that the proposed project or projects in the District that are the subject of the bonds under the measure will be funded beyond the local revenues generated by the measure. The proposed project or projects may assume receipt of matching state funds, which could be subject to appropriation by the Legislature or approval of a statewide bond measure.

Of those voting on the measure, 55% must approve the measure for it to pass.

A ”yes” vote is in favor of authorizing the bonded indebtedness.

A “no” vote is against authorizing the bonded indebtedness.

TAX RATE STATEMENT FOR

MEASURE X

TAX RATE STATEMENT REGARDING PROPOSED

LIBERTY UNION HIGH SCHOOL DISTRICT

GENERAL OBLIGATION BONDS

An election will be held in the Liberty Union High School District (the “District”) on November 3, 2026, to authorize the sale of up to $165,000,000 in bonds of the District to finance school facilities as described in the measure. If such bonds are authorized and sold, principal and interest on the bonds will be payable only from the proceeds of ad valorem tax levies made upon the taxable property in the District. The following information is provided in compliance with Sections 9400-9404 of the Elections Code of the State of California. Such information is based upon the best estimates and projections presently available from official sources, upon experience within the District, and other demonstrable factors.

Based upon the foregoing and projections of the District’s assessed valuation, the following information is provided:

The best estimate of the average annual tax rate which would be required to be levied to fund this bond issue over the entire duration of the bond debt service, based on a projection of assessed valuations available at the time of filing of this statement, is $20 per $100,000 (2 cents per $100) of assessed value. The final fiscal year in which the tax is anticipated to be collected is 2057-58.

The best estimate of the highest tax rate which would be required to be levied to fund this bond issue, based on a projection of assessed valuations available at the time of filing of this statement, is $20 per $100,000 (2 cents per $100) of assessed value. It is estimated that such rate would be levied in all fiscal years in which the bonds are outstanding.

The best estimate of the total debt service, including the principal and interest, that would be required to be repaid if all the bonds are issued and sold is approximately $350 million.

Voters should note the estimated tax rate is based on the assessed value (not market value) of taxable property on the Contra Costa County’s official tax rolls. In addition, taxpayers eligible for a property tax exemption such as the homeowner’s exemption will have a lower effective tax rate than described above. Property owners should consult their own property tax bills and tax advisors to determine their property’s assessed value and any applicable tax exemptions.

The attention of all voters is directed to the fact that the foregoing information is based upon projections and estimates only, which amounts are not maximum amounts and are not binding upon the District. The actual debt service, tax rates and the years in which they will apply may vary from those performed as of a certain date in time and used to provide the estimates set forth above, due to factors such as variations in the timing of bond sales, the par amount of bonds sold and market interest rates available at the time of each sale, actual assessed valuations over the term of the bonds, and other factors. The date and amount of bonds sold at any given time will be determined by the District based on the need for project funds and other considerations. The actual interest rates at which the bonds will be sold will depend on conditions in the bond market at the time of sale. Actual future assessed valuations will depend upon the amount and value of taxable property within the District as determined by the Contra Costa County Assessor in the annual assessment and the equalization process.

s/ Denise Rugani

ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF

MEASURE X

Our high schools are among the best in the region. Liberty, Freedom, and Heritage High Schools provide outstanding instruction that prepares students for college, careers, and lucrative jobs. All three schools have earned recognition as California Distinguished Schools and California Gold Ribbon Schools.

To maintain that standard, Measure X will fund critical repairs and upgrades needed for student success. Many facilities are more than 60 years old. Aging infrastructure has caused gas leaks, water leaks, sewage backups, and more than 150 roof leaks in the past two years alone. These problems must be addressed.

Top Priority projects have been identified at each school, including:

Removing hazardous materials such as asbestos and lead pipes from older schools

Upgrading campuses to meet current health, safety, and accessibility standards

Repairing or replacing deteriorating roofs, plumbing, gas lines, sewer lines, heating, ventilation, electrical systems, and aging portables

Providing modern classrooms, technology, and facilities that support instruction in math, science, engineering, and technology

Expanding career technical education with updated labs and equipment for programs such as health sciences, agriculture, skilled trades, law enforcement, and fire safety

Improving student safety through upgraded fencing, cameras, emergency communication systems, smoke detectors, fire alarms, and sprinklers

Every penny from Measure X will stay in our community and cannot be used for salaries. Independent citizens’ oversight and annual audits are required to ensure funds are spent as promised.

Whether or not you have children in our schools, strong local schools protect our quality of life and support property values. If approved, Measure X will bring up to $35 million in state matching funds to our district that would otherwise go elsewhere, maximizing the value of local taxpayer dollars.

Measure X will protect our schools, improve student safety, modernize aging facilities, and secure additional state funding for our community.

Please vote Yes on Measure X.

REBUTTAL TO ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF

MEASURE X

Vote No on Measure X.

Ten years ago, Liberty Union High School District (LUHSD) received approval to borrow $122 million with Measure U. This money ought to have been spent on the “Top Priority” projects that are now listed as problems “… that must be addressed.”

Why was the asbestos and lead piping not removed? Why weren’t the leaking roofs repaired, or the sewer lines replaced, or the gas lines fixed, with Measure U’s $122 million? Why wasn’t student safety improved with “…upgraded fencing, cameras, emergency communication systems, smoke detectors, fire alarms and sprinklers” using Measure U’s $122 million?

Instead, LUHSD built a new Aquatic Center and Café at Liberty High School. LUHSD built an Auxiliary Gym at Freedom High School, and a Culinary Arts Building at Heritage High School.

Measure X increases the property tax rate by 76% over the two existing LUHSD bonds that homeowners now pay.

Measure X would add $20 per $100,000, while property owners already now pay $10.50 for Measure A and $15.50 for Measure U, per $100,000 of assessed valuation.

Plus, Measure X has no senior exemption.

Flat Enrollment, Poor Timing….

Since 2016 — over the last ten years — LUHSD has increased enrollment by only 28 additional students (0.34%), according to the district’s website.

Adding new facilities during today’s uncertain economic times is ill-advised. The Brentwood Union School District passed a bond resolution in July, then rescinded it a week later, because economic conditions are not promising.

However, LUHSD ignores the economic conditions, requiring homeowners to repay $350 million in additional principal and interest.

Vote No on Measure X.

ARGUMENT AGAINST

MEASURE X

Vote No on Measure X because now is not the time to worsen East Contra Costa affordability.

Measure X increases costs for residents by $350 million of total debt service through 2058. Liberty Union High School District (LUHSD) is still paying off existing construction/maintenance bonds of Measure U (2016, $122 million, payoff 2047) and Measure A (2001, $65.4 million, payoff 2030).

A $700,000 assessed value home now pays $178.50 annually for these two bonds. Measure X’s payoff, adding another $140.00 assessment, makes the total $318.50.

LUHSD enrollment is flat, just 0.34% higher than 2016’s. So new bond funds would be used for maintenance and nice-to-have enhancements, not increased instructional capacity.

Vote No on Measure X because the expenditures are unaccountable.

In 2016 voters approved Measure U, borrowing $122 million (plus interest) to include maintenance issues similar to those cited now. Measure U was supposed to fix deteriorating roofs, plumbing, HVAC, electrical wiring, etc. Yet Measure X repeats those fixes now.

Measure X’s project list is a catch-all, not a plan. “Repair and improve aging classrooms,” “upgrade… other facilities,” and college/career readiness “in math, science, health, engineering, arts and skilled trades” covers virtually any expenditure. Voters are being asked to approve $165 million (plus $185 million interest) without a prioritized project list.

Measure X’s cited costs are “based upon projections and estimates only… and are not binding upon the District.” If home values drop, bond-payback tax rates could increase.

Vote No because Measure X’s project list isn’t sufficiently specific.

California’s Constitution requires “A list of the specific school facilities projects to be funded….” Measure X ‘s Bond Project List states “The items presented on the following list provide are (sic) the types of projects authorized…. Examples included… are not intended to limit the broader types of projects described and authorized by this measure.” Sorry, that’s not specific.

REBUTTAL TO ARGUMENT AGAINST

MEASURE X

Measure X is not about extras. It focuses on practical, needed improvements to improve the quality of education. The specific project list includes replacement of aging classrooms at Liberty High School, modernizing science labs and the Liberty library, replacing 21 outdated portable classrooms at Freedom High School, constructing a Career Technical Education Center, and building additional classrooms and an auxiliary gymnasium at Heritage High School. It also upgrades roofs, plumbing, electrical, heating, ventilation, and campus safety systems.

Measure X includes strong taxpayer protections. By law, bond funds cannot be used for administrator salaries, pensions, or day-to-day operating expenses. Measure X includes independent citizen oversight, annual financial audits, and public reporting to ensure accountability and transparency.

Opponents suggest improvements are unnecessary because enrollment is stable. Yet school facilities must be maintained and modernized to provide quality classrooms for current and future high school students. Children in our community deserve safe classrooms, updated science labs, modern technology, and workforce training facilities to prepare them for college and high-demand careers.

Measure X also positions Liberty Union High School District to compete for approximately $35 million in state matching funds, bringing additional investment into our community that could otherwise go elsewhere.

Most importantly, Measure X contains specific projects for Liberty, Freedom, and Heritage High Schools that address student safety, aging facilities, and educational opportunities. It represents a responsible investment in our schools, our students, and our community’s future.

Vote Yes on Measure X.
