    • Repairing & Upgrading our Local Schools

    Hello Visalia Community

    We are committed to ensuring Every Student is Learning Every Day. That begins with the quality of learning environments.

    Schools that have served generations of Visalia families are now showing their age. Over 70% of our schools are over 50 years old and 87% of our portable classrooms having outlived their useful lifespan.

    How do we continue to keep pace with infrastructure that is breaking down and still provide students with the tools and resources they need for the jobs of tomorrow?

    We patched 383 roof leaks last year, and while our maintenance budget will continue to support patches and small repairs, our schools’ infrastructure needs attention.

    Our Board recognizes the importance of making this decision now for our future, and that is why they unanimously approved to place a bond Measure on the November ballot. This bond would be used to specifically continue to work on our infrastructure, like replacing roofs, modernizing classrooms, and replacing portables, and creating spaces where students and teachers are proud to work and learn in, and where our community can see results.

    Great schools strengthen the entire community and preserve the quality of life we want in Visalia. This is about more than buildings. It is about providing every student, regardless of the school they attend, with a high-quality education, because when they succeed, our community succeeds.

    Whether you’re sending your child to school for the first time or the last time, thank you for entrusting us with their education. Together, we have made meaningful progress, and I hope we can do more of it together.

    We look forward to hosting community meetings and campus tours to show you first-hand the needs of our schools, so please stay tuned.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      What is Measure Z?

      On July 23, 2026 the Board of Education unanimously placed Measure Z on the November 2026 ballot to address health and safety repairs at our local schools.

      Why was Measure Z placed on the ballot?

      Most of our schools were built over 50 years ago and don’t meet modern health and safety codes. Some buildings aren’t accessible for students with disabilities, and others have hazardous materials such as asbestos and lead pipes. If enacted, Measure Z provides local funding to fix these problems to keep all students safe.

      What have previous bond measures accomplished, and why is additional funding needed?

      Measures E and A funded significant improvements across VUSD, including new classrooms, school modernizations, safety upgrades, Ridgeview Middle School, and Redwood High School’s two-story academic building. When Measure A was approved, enrollment projections showed a need for another comprehensive high school; updated projections now show 921 fewer students by 2035–36, making a new high school no longer a fiscally responsible investment. Instead, VUSD has adjusted its facilities plan to maximize the community’s investment while addressing needs at existing schools, where a third-party facilities assessment identified more than $636 million in critical repairs and improvements still to be addressed.

      What is the most significant priority identified by residents that would be addressed by Measure Z?

      We’ve heard about the need to repair leaky roofs, and last year alone, there were 383 roof leaks. If approved by voters, Measure Z will repair aging infrastructure that’s breaking down, fix water leaks, and sewage backups.

      Why is the District still using portable classrooms?

      The District does not have the resources to replace our 430 portable classrooms that are more than 20 years old. These portable classrooms have structural damage and water leaks that lead to emergency repairs. If enacted, Measure Z allows the District to repair and replace decaying portables with modern, permanent classrooms.

      Does Measure Z continue the District’s conservative standards for accountability?

      Yes. All Measure Z money must stay local to support our students and requires accountability and transparency, including a project list describing how money will be used, a Citizens’ Oversight Committee, independent audits, and public disclosure of all spending. By law, no money from Measure Z can be taken by the State or used for administrators’ salaries or benefits.

      What would Measure Z do?

      If approved by voters, Measure Z funds would be used to address high-priority facility needs across VUSD schools, including:

      Health & Safety: Remove asbestos and lead pipes and improve accessibility.

      Critical Infrastructure: Replace aging roofs, plumbing, HVAC, electrical, gas, and sewer systems.

      Modern Learning Spaces: Upgrade classrooms, labs, technology, and career technical education spaces.

      Aging Portables: Replace outdated portables with safer, more durable classrooms.

      What is the cost to property owners?

      If passed, Measure Z would provide $158 million for local school facility improvements at a cost of $40 per $100,000 of assessed value (not market value).

      What happens if the bond measure doesn’t pass?

      VUSD will have to rely on portables, which are a costly short-term fix. Staff will have to prioritize needs to keep the schools functioning. Ultimately, Visalia will need a new school bond because this is the only way to generate the funds needed to create equitable, modern facilities for all students.

      What facility needs would Measure Z address and is there? How does VUSD decide which schools/needs should be addressed first?

      A long-range facilities assessment identified more than $636 million in facility needs across Visalia Unified, including:

      Aging and leaking roofs

      Heating and air-conditioning systems

      Electrical and plumbing infrastructure

      Accessibility and safety improvements

      Aging portable classrooms

      Our maintenance and operations teams work hard to extend the life of our facilities, but there comes a point when replacement is more responsible than continued repairs.

      If approved by voters, Measure Z would focus on Priority 1 and Priority 2 facility needs—not cosmetic improvements, but necessary investments in aging school infrastructure.

      How can VUSD be sure that another high school is not necessary?

      When Measure A was approved, enrollment projections showed a need for another comprehensive high school; updated projections now show 921 fewer students by 2035–36, making a new high school no longer a fiscally responsible investment. Instead, VUSD has adjusted its facilities plan to maximize the community’s investment while addressing needs at existing schools, where a third-party facilities assessment identified more than $636 million in critical repairs and improvements still to be addressed.
