Measure CD asks the voters of this city whether to elect council members by district, instead of citywide.
The City Council placed Measure CD on the ballot under the terms of a settlement of a voting rights lawsuit against the city.
Measure CD can take effect only if voters also approve a separate charter amendment on this ballot, removing the requirement for citywide elections.
Today, every voter can vote for as many council candidates as there are open seats: three this year, and two in 2028.
The city would be split into five districts, each electing one council member to a four-year term.
Only the residents of a district could run for its seat, or vote for it, once every four years.
Districts 2 and 4 would vote first, in November 2028, and districts 1, 3 and 5 in 2030.
Council members already in office would serve out their terms.
The county Registrar's list of measures and the impartial analysis do not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Opponents say voters would choose one council member every four years, losing their vote on the other four, who decide for the whole city.
The official county list of measures for this election says that no argument for Measure CD was submitted, by any group or person.
A yes vote adopts the change from citywide to district elections for the City Council.
A no vote rejects the change from citywide to district elections for the City Council.
