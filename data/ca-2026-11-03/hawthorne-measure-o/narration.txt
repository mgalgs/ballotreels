Measure O asks the voters of this city whether to raise the tax on hotel stays from 12 to 17 percent.
Today, guests who stay 30 days or less at a hotel, motel or inn pay a 12 percent tax on their room rent.
The City Council placed Measure O on the ballot.
Measure O would raise that tax to 17 percent.
Measure O would also make clear that the tax applies to short-term rentals, such as homes rented through an online platform.
The added money would go to the general fund, for purposes such as police, fire, parks and streets.
As a general tax, Measure O needs a majority yes vote to pass.
The ballot question says the tax would raise about $3.1 million a year; the impartial analysis gives no estimate.
The official county list of measures, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure O was submitted.
Supporters say the tax is paid mainly by visitors, not residents, and gives the city reliable local funding for essential services.
A yes vote raises the hotel tax to 17 percent.
A no vote keeps the hotel tax at 12 percent.
