Measure C asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $3.4 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them from a yearly tax on property in the district.
Measure C needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay only for repairing, rebuilding and equipping the district's school facilities.
The district's examples include repairing classrooms, restrooms, plumbing and sewer lines, replacing leaky roofs, and upgrading fire alarms.
The district's tax rate statement estimates a tax of $30 per $100,000 of assessed value, collected until the 2053-2054 fiscal year.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $6.9 million.
No argument against Measure C was filed, so the county voter guide prints only the argument of the supporters.
Supporters say the school is over 60 years old with deteriorating infrastructure, and citizens' oversight and yearly audits are required.
A yes vote is a vote to authorize $3.4 million in bonds, financed by an annual property tax.
A no vote is a vote against issuing the proposed bonds.
