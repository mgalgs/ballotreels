    Measure U: San Marino Service Preservation Measure Info



    Join us for a Town Hall on Measure U
    Residents are invited to attend a Measure U Town Hall regarding the proposed renewal of San Marino’s existing Utility Users Tax (UUT) on Tuesday, September 29, from 6:00–7:00 PM at the San Marino Community Center. The Town Hall will provide an opportunity for residents to learn more about Measure U and participate in a community discussion. Join via Zoom. Mandarin Interpretation Available ( 中文口譯，可透過 Zoom 提供).

    About Measure U:
    At the July 8, 2026, City Council Meeting, the City Council unanimously approved the submission of a ballot measure for the November 3, 2026 General Election that would ask voters to renew the City’s existing Utility Users Tax (UUT). This thoughtfully considered decision marks an important step in the City’s roadmap to create a more fiscally stable San Marino and continue to fund critical City services at their current level. 

    What this action did: 
    • This action continues the Council's efforts to address the City's long-term financial needs by placing the renewal of San Marino's longstanding Utility Users Tax (UUT) before voters. First adopted in 1992 and approved by voters three times since, the measure, Measure U, proposes restoring the UUT from 5% to its previously approved 6% rate.
    • Despite the City's ongoing cost-control efforts, including freezing positions, eliminating programs, and implementing one-time budget reductions, San Marino is still facing ongoing structural deficits. City projections show annual shortfalls growing from roughly $2.1 million to more than $2.8 million over the next several years if the UUT is not renewed. At the same time, the City carries nearly $50.7 million in unfunded infrastructure needs, including streets, sidewalks, storm drains, and public facilities.
    • The Council's action ensures residents have the opportunity to vote on Measure U: the renewal of the Utility Users Tax to preserve essential current public services before the current tax expires on March 31, 2027.
    What will this measure do?
    • If approved by voters, Measure U would renew San Marino's longstanding Utility Users Tax (UUT) at 6%, restoring it to the rate previously approved by voters before it was reduced to 5% in 2015. The measure would provide a stable, locally controlled source of revenue to help fund essential City services, including police, fire, emergency preparedness, street and sidewalk maintenance, and community and recreation programs for youth and seniors. Every dollar generated by the UUT would remain in San Marino to support the community.
    • Measure U would help maintain a critical source of General Fund revenue that supports the City's day-to-day operations and long-term financial planning. San Marino’s existing UUT represents approximately 6% of the City’s General Fund and is essential to maintain existing services that make San Marino special.
    • The City has taken steps to control costs, including freezing positions, eliminating programs, and implementing one-time budget reduction measures, to name a few. By renewing the UUT, the measure would help safeguard the services, public safety, infrastructure, and community character that residents rely on while supporting the City's ability to address current financial needs and continue investing in San Marino's long-term future.
    Why is this measure on the November 2026 ballot?
    • The City's existing Utility Users Tax expires on March 31, 2027. By placing the measure on the November 3, 2026 General Election ballot, the City is providing voters the opportunity to decide whether to renew the tax before it expires, helping support long-term financial planning and continuity of essential City services.
    Who can I ask additional questions about this measure to?
    • Please contact the City Manager's Office at CityManagerOffice@SanMarinoCA.gov

    Additional FAQ's can be found at the bottom of this page.

    The Financial Reasons For The City Council's Direction To Pursue This Measure
    The City’s Five-Year Financial Forecast projects a structural deficit ranging from a low of $2,132,275 to a high of $2,804,101 absent revenue from the City’s longstanding UUT. Despite ongoing cost containment measures and significant budget cuts in the previous two budget cycles, expenditures are expected to grow faster than revenues due to rising costs such as insurance, equipment, fuel, utilities, and more. Without renewing or expanding local revenue sources, the City will not be able to sustain the current level of services the community expects or fund capital investment and maintenance of critical infrastructure. The City faces $50.7 million in unfunded capital projects and a continuing structural imbalance, requiring further local revenue solutions.

    Utility Users Tax Rates in Nearby Cities

For that reason, voters are being asked to consider renewing the Utility Users Tax (UUT) at rate of 6%. San Marino's current 5% UUT is the lowest among nearby cities with a Utility Users Tax.     FAQ

    What Will Voters Be Asked to Decide?
    Voters will be asked whether to renew the City's Utility Users Tax (UUT) at a rate of 6%. The current UUT, which has supported San Marino services since 1992, is scheduled to expire on March 31, 2027. If approved, the measure would continue this locally controlled source of funding for essential City services.

    How Would Funds Be Used If Approved?
    If approved by voters, revenue from the UUT would continue to support essential City services, including police, fire, emergency preparedness, street and sidewalk maintenance, library services, and community and recreation programs. The UUT helps fund day-to-day City operations, infrastructure repairs, and supports long-term financial planning. Every dollar generated by the UUT stays in San Marino.

    What Has the City Done to Balance the Budget Already?
    The City has taken a number of steps to address ongoing budget challenges, including freezing vacant positions, eliminating programs, implementing one-time budget reductions, and authorizing a withdrawal from the California Employers' Pension Prefunding Trust Fund (CEPPT). Despite these efforts, the City's long-term financial forecast continues to project structural deficits, making stable local revenue sources increasingly important.

    Who Pays the Utility Users Tax?
    The Utility Users Tax is paid by customers of taxable utility services, including residents, businesses, and property owners, as a percentage of their monthly utility bills. Utility providers collect the tax and remit it to the City.

    Am I Already Paying a Utility Users Tax?
    Yes. San Marino currently has a 5% Utility Users Tax that was approved by voters in 2015. Prior to that, voters had approved a rate of up to 6%. This measure asks voters whether to renew the UUT at 6% after the current tax expires on March 31, 2027.

    How Much Would the Measure Cost?
    If approved, the Utility Users Tax rate would increase from 5% to 6% on taxable utility services. The amount paid would depend on an individual's monthly utility usage and charges.

    How Will the City Ensure Transparency and Accountability?
    The UUT is proposed as a general tax, meaning revenue would be deposited into the City's General Fund and allocated through the annual public budget process. The City Council reviews and adopts the budget during public meetings, providing residents with opportunities to participate in decisions about funding priorities.

    Does This Replace Other Taxes or Fees?
    No. This measure would renew the City's existing Utility Users Tax, which is scheduled to expire on March 31, 2027. It does not replace other local taxes or fees.

    Can Anyone Take This Utility Users Tax From San Marino?
    No. Revenue generated by the Utility Users Tax remains with the City of San Marino and cannot be redirected by the State or Los Angeles County. The funds are used to support local City services.

    How Was the Estimated Annual Revenue Calculated?
    The annual revenue estimate is based on the proposed 6% Utility Users Tax rate and the City's projected utility tax revenues. At the proposed rate, the measure is estimated to generate approximately $2.62 million annually.
