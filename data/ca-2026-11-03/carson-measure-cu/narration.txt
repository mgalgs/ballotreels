Measure CU asks the voters of this city whether to amend several sections of the city charter.
Today, the charter limits council members to three four-year terms.
Putting a tax before voters takes a two-thirds vote of the council.
The City Council placed Measure CU on the ballot.
Under Measure CU, no elected city office would have term limits.
Under Measure CU, a majority of the council could put a tax before voters.
Measure CU would remove charter limits on added taxes and certain environmental rules for oil refineries and fuel storage, but would not itself impose or increase any tax.
The state's campaign contribution disclosure rules would be written into the charter.
Smaller changes cover council election districts, the city manager's contract powers and two treasurer duties.
Measure CU needs a majority yes vote to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say Measure CU lets voters choose who they want on the council, and it does not create a tax.
The official county list of measures, which names who filed each argument, says that no argument against Measure CU was submitted.
A yes vote is a vote in favor of the proposed charter amendments.
A no vote is a vote against the proposed charter amendments.
