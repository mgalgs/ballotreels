Measure N asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $67 million in bonds for school repairs and upgrades.
The board of trustees placed Measure N on the ballot, and it needs 55 percent of the vote.
The money could pay only for listed projects, such as bathrooms, plumbing, roofs, heating and cooling, and classrooms.
The bond money could not pay salaries or other operating costs.
A citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would review the spending.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average tax of $27.55 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $30, through 2060-2061.
For the average home in the district, the statement estimates a tax of about $245.
The statement puts the total to repay, with interest, at about $132.5 million.
Supporters say most district schools were built 60 to 70 years ago, and Measure N helps qualify for about $7 million in state matching funds.
No argument against Measure N was filed, according to the page for the measure from the county, so only the argument in favor appears there.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue up to $67 million in bonds.
A no vote does not authorize the bonds.
