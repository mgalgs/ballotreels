Information on Measure N

Measure N: Upgrading Classrooms and School Facilities

Many local school facilities are aging and need repairs and upgrades to meet today’s standards. Essential infrastructure like bathrooms, plumbing, roofs, heating and cooling, and electrical systems are deteriorating or failing. Updating classrooms and modernizing campuses are critical to supporting student achievement and keeping schools safe, modern, and accessible for all students.

Local Funding for School Repairs

To repair and upgrade aging schools, the MCSD Board of Trustees voted unanimously to place Measure N, a $67 million school improvement bond, on the November 3, 2026, ballot. If approved by local voters, Measure N would levy $30 per $100,000 of assessed (not market) value and provide funding to:

  • Fix deteriorating bathrooms, plumbing, roofs, HVAC, and electrical systems
  • Upgrade aging classrooms to support student achievement in math, science, technology, reading, writing, arts, and music
  • Complete needed repair projects to provide safe, well-maintained classrooms and meet current educational standards
  • Ensure all classrooms, playgrounds, and campuses are accessible for students, staff, and community members with disabilities
  • Improve school security, including classroom locks, lighting, emergency communications, and cameras

Local Control and Fiscal Accountability

If approved by voters, Measure N would be subject to fiscal oversight as mandated by law:

  • All money would be controlled locally and could not be taken away by the state or federal government
  • No money raised could be used for administrators’ salaries or pensions
  • Independent oversight and mandatory annual audits are required

Measure N FAQ

How are schools in Miller Creek School District doing?
Miller Creek School District (MCSD) is known for strong, well-rounded elementary and middle school education for students in our community. Our goal is to support every child’s growth from their earliest years through middle school, preparing them for success in high school and beyond. The District includes Miller Creek Middle School, a California Distinguished School, Lucas Valley Elementary School, Mary E. Silveira Elementary School, and Vallecito Elementary School.


What is the state of MCSD’s facilities? Why did the Miller Creek School District place a bond measure on the November ballot?
While MCSD strives to maintain an excellent education for students, classrooms across the District's campuses are aging and need repairs and upgrades to meet modern academic and safety standards. Aging infrastructure, including bathrooms, plumbing, roofs, electrical, heating and cooling systems is in need of repairs and upgrades. If approved by local voters, a school improvement bond will provide dedicated local funding for upgrades and repairs to provide modern classrooms to support quality education in Miller Creek schools.


How is the MCSD planning to address these needs?
The MCSD Board of Trustees voted unanimously to place Measure N, a school improvement bond measure, on the November 3, 2026 ballot. The measure will provide dedicated funding for repairs and upgrades to aging school facilities.


What types of projects would a Measure N fund?
If approved by local voters, Measure N funds would be used to:
● Fix deteriorating bathrooms, plumbing, roofs, HVAC, and electrical systems
● Upgrade aging classrooms to support student achievement in math, science, technology, reading, writing, arts, and music
● Complete needed repair projects to provide safe, well-maintained classrooms and meet current educational standards
● Ensure all classrooms, playgrounds, and campuses are accessible for students, staff, and community members with disabilities
● Improve school security, including classroom locks, lighting, emergency communications, and cameras


What has MCSD done to assess the condition of local school campuses and develop a plan for repairs?
MCSD is currently completing a detailed assessment of all classrooms, school facilities and grounds in the District. School facility experts are evaluating school site conditions and providing recommendations on the priority repairs, upgrades, and modernization projects needed to support safe, functional, and modern learning environments for all students.


How do we know funds from Measure N would be spent wisely?
By law, Measure N includes strict fiscal accountability protections to ensure responsible use of funds. All funds would only be used to repair, upgrade, and modernize MCSD classrooms, facilities, and campuses. No funds could be taken away by the state or federal governments. All funds would be subject to strict fiscal accountability protections including an independent oversight committee, mandatory annual audits, and public disclosure of all spending.
 

Could bond measure funding be taken by the county, state or federal governments?
No, funds could not be taken by the county, state or federal governments, or used for other purposes. All funds from the bond measure will benefit MCSD schools only.


How much would Measure N cost?
Because the State does not provide dedicated or adequate funding for facility repairs, these improvements must be funded locally. The Board of Trustees voted unanimously to place Measure N, a bond measure of up to $67 million, on the November 3, 2026 ballot to fund needed repairs and upgrades. If approved by local voters, the bond would cost taxpayers no more than $30 per $100,000 of assessed (not market) value, annually, while bonds are outstanding.


What is the difference between assessed value and market value?
The cost of bond measures is based on the assessed value of properties. The assessed value of a property is based on the original purchase price and may not increase by more than 2% a year, while the market value tends to grow at a much faster rate based on market conditions. Thus, the assessed value of a property is usually much lower than the market value, especially if a property was purchased long ago at a much lower price than it could be purchased for today. It is this lower assessed value upon which the cost of a bond measure is based.


What would happen if Measure N doesn’t pass?
There are no other practical funding sources to address identified repairs and upgrades at local schools. Without Measure N, MCSD would have to divert funding from teaching and classroom programs to address urgent and unavoidable facility repairs needed to keep aging schools functioning. This would require cuts to academic programs and increases in class sizes. Waiting to address school facility repairs and upgrades would make the projects more expensive to complete.


I don’t have children attending local schools. How would this impact me?
Even if you do not have school-age children, funding quality education can help improve the quality of life in our community and protect home values.


Is there a senior exemption?
By state law, exemptions of any kind are not allowed for school facility improvement bond measures. However, the cost of any bond is based on the assessed value of taxpayers’ properties. Seniors and other residents who have lived in their homes for a long time have the lowest assessed values and would pay the least.


Didn't we already pass a bond for MCSD?
Miller Creek has not proposed a bond measure for over a decade. In 2014, voters approved Measure C to address facility improvements identified at that time. Since then, facilities have continued to age and new needs have been identified. The independent oversight committee and annual audits confirm that Measure C funding was spent as promised and projects were completed on time. Audit and oversight committee reports can be found on the MCSD website.


Don't we already pay parcel tax for MCSD?
Yes, like most school districts in Marin County, MCSD benefits from voter-approved parcel tax funding that supports teachers and academic programs. Parcel tax funding supports ongoing expenditures rather than facility improvements. A bond measure would provide dedicated funding for updates, modernization, and repairs to school facilities.


Will any of these funds go to Terra Linda High School?
No. Terra Linda High School is part of San Rafael City Schools, a separate school district with a separate budget and funding sources. Measure N is just for MCSD, which includes three elementary schools and one middle school. Recent improvements to TLHS were funded by San Rafael City Schools.


Who is eligible to vote on Measure N?
All registered voters in the Miller Creek School District will be eligible to vote on Measure N.
 

What level of support is required to pass Measure N?
At least 55% of voters who cast a ballot on Measure N must vote “Yes” in order for it to be approved.


When will I be able to vote on Measure N?
Measure N will be on the November 3, 2026, ballot.

What if I have more questions?
The District values questions, comments, and feedback from parents and community members. If you have questions or would like to provide feedback, please contact MCSD Superintendent Kristy Treewater at superintendent@millercreeksd.org or 415-492-3706.
