City of Alameda Measure N asks voters in the city whether to change how the City Council approves sales and leases of city property.
Today, a city property deal longer than one year needs an ordinance adopted at two meetings, four of the five Council votes, and a 30-day wait.
The City Council placed Measure N on the ballot, and it needs a simple majority to pass.
The Council could approve these deals by resolution at one meeting, taking effect right away, about 45 days sooner.
Leases of 10 years or less would need three Council votes instead of four.
Sales and longer leases would still need four votes, and selling a park would still need voters' approval.
The Alameda County Composite Voter Information Guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say today's extra steps can delay negotiations and discourage tenants, and that a more efficient process would help fill vacant city properties.
Opponents say the four-vote rule keeps the Council from moving so fast it serves only developers or commercial tenants, and has not slowed progress.
A yes vote means approval by resolution, and a simple majority of three votes for leases of up to 10 years.
A no vote means approval stays by ordinance, with a supermajority of four votes for these leases.
