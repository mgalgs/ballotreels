## Key facts, quoted

- **Ballot question:** "Shall Resolution No. 26-052 be adopted to amend the Davis General Plan to approve a residential development to include parks, trails, open space, and neighborhood retail areas, by changing the land use designations for the Willowgrove property from Agriculture to Urban Agriculture Transition Area, Park/Recreation, Neighborhood Greenbelt, Neighborhood Retail, and Residential Low, Medium, and High Density, and by establishing the Baseline Project Features?"
- **Who votes / threshold:** "To pass, a majority (50% + 1) of the votes cast by City of Davis voters must approve the measure." Analysis: "Measure W requires approval by a majority of the voters in the City voting on it to become effective."
- **Placed by / effect:** "Measure W was placed on the ballot by the City of Davis City Council. If adopted, Measure W would amend the City of Davis General Plan to allow for development of the Willowgrove project. The project site is approximately 232 acres, bounded by East Covell Boulevard to the south, and the Wildhorse and Palomino subdivisions to the west. If Measure W is approved, annexation of the site into the City could proceed."
- **Why a vote:** "The Davis Municipal Code requires voter approval of General Plan amendments that change the land use designation from agricultural or open space to urban uses. Voter approval of the General Plan amendments includes approval of "baseline project features" that cannot be significantly modified later without voter approval."
- **Project features (analysis):** "Zoning of the site to allow 1,250 residential units … including at least 250 deed-restricted affordable housing units. Of the 250 affordable units, at least 20 units will be reserved for individuals with intellectual and/or developmental disabilities." "Donation of a minimum $6.8 million from the developer to the City to facilitate development of affordable housing". A community park "that will include an indoor community gym with a basketball court, three lighted playfields, and an inclusive playground." "At least 44 acres of urban agricultural transition area … in addition to providing mitigation for the loss of agricultural land at a 2:1 ratio". "over three miles of new bicycle and pedestrian trails". "Planting of at least 2,500 trees". "Residential units will be all-electric with no natural gas connections."
- **Yes/No:** "A "yes" vote favors the General Plan amendments and baseline project features, and a "no" vote opposes them."
- **Analysis signed:** "/s/ Inder Khalsa, Davis City Attorney".
- **Argument in favor signers:** Donna Neville, Mayor, City of Davis; Hiram Jackson, President, Davis Joint Unified School District Board of Trustees; Lucas Frerichs, Yolo County Supervisor; Beth Foraker, Director of the UC Davis Redwood SEED Scholars Program; Chris Sinclair, Davis Youth Softball Association Board Chair. (Claims in it — e.g. "$21+ million regional public park", "first project in Davis history with an approved Subdivision Map before the public votes" — are the authors'.)
- **Argument against:** "No arguments were filed AGAINST Measure W by the 5:00 p.m. July 24, 2026 deadline."
- **Rebuttals:** "If only an Argument IN FAVOR OF is filed, there is no Rebuttal period."
