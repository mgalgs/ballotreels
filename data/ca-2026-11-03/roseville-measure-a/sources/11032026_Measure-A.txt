                                       ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE A
For 26 years, Roseville City Council compensation has remained frozen at $600 per month, with the Mayor receiving just
$50 more. Since then, Roseville has grown into one of California’s most operationally complex full-service cities, with a
population approaching 170,000 residents, a workforce of approximately 1,400 employees, and oversight of about $1 billion
in municipal operations.
This measure updates compensation to $2,550 per month, the amount authorized under state law for a city of Roseville’s
size. Roseville council members don’t receive City-paid health care or other employee benefits, and that won’t change.
Future increases would remain limited to inflationary adjustments capped at 5% annually, ensuring accountability and
predictability for taxpayers. At about $153,000 annually for the entire Council, this is a modest investment that reflects 26
years of inaction on compensation.
Roseville is unique in the breadth and complexity of its services. Unlike many cities that outsource electric, water,
wastewater, fire, parks, transit, libraries, and housing services, Roseville operates these in-house. That model delivers local
control and high-quality service, and requires substantial time, expertise, preparation, and oversight from elected officials
making decisions that directly affect residents’ daily lives and the city’s long-term financial stability.
Updating Council compensation helps ensure working professionals, parents, and community members from more
backgrounds can realistically consider serving Roseville, easing the financial strain public service requires.
Importantly, this measure isn’t designed to benefit the current Council. With election cycles and term limits, two current
members will leave office before the adjustment takes effect, and two more two years later.
This measure modernizes a compensation structure that hasn’t changed since 2000 while maintaining voter oversight and
fiscal restraint. It supports good governance, broadens access to public service, and helps ensure Roseville continues
attracting qualified leaders for one of California’s premier full-service cities.
Signed,
Ananda Rochita, President and CEO, Roseville Area Chamber of Commerce
David A. Breninger, Past President, Sun City Roseville
Carol C. Garcia, Past Mayor, City of Roseville

                          REBUTTAL TO THE ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE A
Roseville is indeed a well-run city by any metrics. As a lifelong resident, I agree. Having our own police, fire, utilities and
close to 100 parks has made us very attractive to many families who have moved here or chosen to remain.
This hasn’t happened by accident. Our status as a charter city has provided opportunities that allow us to prosper. One is
determining council pay. Let’s not make it automatic and place the city on auto pilot as is the case with general law cities.
We have always been independent and fiscally responsible; let’s keep it that way.
The city has prospered because of dedicated council members. They didn’t run for council because of pay or benefits but
because they love this city. The council members have been quite diverse. Many have served while fully employed, part
time employed, retired, professionals, parents, homemakers and teachers. They wanted to give back to the community and
become true public servants.
What we don’t need for Roseville’s future are people considering a second job or looking to supplement their retirement.
We already have enough of those career politicians serving at the state and federal government. In addition, this amendment
does not broaden access to public services or provide for better governance. It’s about salary increases.
The amendment has two parts. A pay increase which I support and automatic pay increases which I do not support. Send
this back to separate the two proposals. Let the public decide them separately.
Signed,
Richard Roccucci, Former Roseville Mayor

                                         ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE A
This ballot measure is simple. It contains two parts. Do you want to change council member’s compensation and do you
want to approve an automatic increase?
For the last several years I have supported a modest salary increase almost to the level proposed by this charter
amendment. However I have always opposed an automatic increase.
The proposal has been brought forward several times in the past 10-15 years and the council has determined that the time
was not right because of economic concerns. Is this time any better? I believe not.
In addition, a charter revision commission is formed every 10 years to review the charter and make recommendations to be
placed on the ballot. Why this stand-alone charter amendment at this time? It should come forward in 2030 with the other
recommended changes. Two separate proposals; increase salary and automatic increase.
Why should we have an automatic salary increase for council member compensation when all other city employees do not
and are governed by labor agreements which are reviewed and adjusted by the council every couple of years?
Do you want to surrender your rights as a charter city to state standards for general law cities?
You decide if you want to vote yes for both parts of this proposal at this time.
Signed,
Richard Roccucci, Former Roseville Mayor

                             REBUTTAL TO THE ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE A
Roseville’s voters deserve a City Council focused on public safety, infrastructure, utilities, parks, and the issues that
matter most to our community—not returning to the ballot every few years to debate modest cost-of-living adjustments for
elected officials.
That’s why this measure does two things: it updates Council compensation for the first time in 26 years and establishes
clear, voter-approved limits on future inflation adjustments.
Any future adjustment is tied to the Consumer Price Index, capped at 5% annually, and can never exceed inflation. It is
not a discretionary pay raise. It creates a predictable, transparent process that avoids repeated elections and political
debates over small inflation adjustments, allowing the City Council to stay focused on the services and decisions that
matter most to Roseville residents.
Even the opposition agrees the current compensation is outdated and supports increasing it. The only question is whether
Roseville should solve the problem now with clear safeguards or continue revisiting the same issue every few years.
Waiting until 2030 only extends a compensation structure adopted in 2000, despite Roseville’s tremendous growth and
the increasing responsibilities of governing one of California’s most complex full-service cities.
This measure preserves voter control because only Roseville voters can approve this Charter amendment. It provides a
fiscally responsible approach that keeps future adjustments modest, transparent, and tied solely to inflation.
I encourage my fellow Roseville residents to vote Yes on Measure A.
Signed,
Carol C. Garcia, Past Mayor, City of Roseville
