## Key facts, quoted

- **Ballot question** (MA1): "Shall the Charter of the City of Roseville be amended to adjust councilmember compensation from the current $600 a month that was established by voters 26 years ago, to $2,550 a month, with annual incremental adjustments tied to inflation not to exceed 5%, which is less than allowed under State law?" (The county's list PDF prints "council member"; the measure PDF and the resolution print "councilmember".)
- **Who votes / threshold:** Resolution 26-040 submits the measure "to the voters of the City of Roseville at the next General Municipal Election to be held on November 3, 2026"; analysis: "Measure A will be approved if it receives a majority of "yes" votes."
- **Placed by:** "This measure is being placed on the ballot by the City Council for the City of Roseville, after receiving input from the public". Resolution adopted "this 18th day of February 2026" — "AYES COUNCILMEMBERS: Alvord, Bernasconi, Houdesheldt, Mendonsa / NOES COUNCILMEMBERS: Roccucci".
- **Current law** (analysis): "Currently, the Charter sets councilmember compensation at $600 per month, with the Mayor receiving an additional $50 per month. These amounts were established by voters in 2000 … council compensation has remained unchanged for the past 26 years. Roseville's current population exceeds 165,000 people."
- **State law reference** (analysis): "State law says a city with a population between 150,001 and 250,000 may set council compensation at $2,550 per month, with annual adjustments tied to inflation. These limits do not apply unless voters amend the Charter."
- **What it changes** (analysis): "Set the maximum salary for each councilmember at $2,550 per month, which is $30,600 per year, with no additional monthly amount for the Mayor." "Provide for annual inflation adjustments based on the California Consumer Price Index since January 1, 2026, with any increases capped at no more than 5 percent per year". "Allow reimbursement for reasonable and necessary expenses incurred in the service of the City. Expense reimbursements would not be considered part of salary." "The measure does not authorize medical, dental, vision, or deferred compensation benefits". "This measure also requires future voter approval for any change to this Charter section."
- **Yes/No** (analysis): "A "yes" vote is a vote in favor of the proposed amendment to the Charter." / "A "no" vote is a vote against the proposed amendment to the Charter."
- **Analysis signed:** "Michelle Sheidenberger, Roseville City Attorney".
- **Argument in favor signers** (MA2): Ananda Rochita, President and CEO, Roseville Area Chamber of Commerce; David A. Breninger, Past President, Sun City Roseville; Carol C. Garcia, Past Mayor, City of Roseville.
- **Rebuttal to argument in favor** and **argument against** (MA2–MA3): Richard Roccucci, Former Roseville Mayor.
- **Rebuttal to argument against** (MA3): Carol C. Garcia, Past Mayor, City of Roseville.
