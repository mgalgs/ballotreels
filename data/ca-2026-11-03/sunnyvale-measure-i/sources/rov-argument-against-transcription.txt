Argument Against Measure ☒ (box marked by hand)

[Pursuant to §9282: Maximum 300 words]

Vote NO to avoid 2+ year appointments and avoid greater appointee incumbency advantage. Preserve earlier opportunities to vote.

If passed, this will cut the eligible elections to fill vacancies by at least half. This is done by eliminating the existing requirement to use an earlier election consolidated with a statewide election, a primary election for example. Longer appointments and appointee incumbency advantage allows council to handpick successors. While you may like the current city council, that may change in 15-20 years. Do you want that council making extended appointments?

Primary election voters are well engaged and informed. Larger turnout in even-year November elections does not justify sacrificing earlier opportunities to vote. Money spent on elections can be a very good investment.

This measure raises significant concerns:

- ALLOWS LONGER APPOINTMENTS, UP TO 2+ YEARS. Chances for simultaneous appointees increase.
- EXPANDS PATH TO ARRANGED APPOINTMENTS. Including bestowing incumbency advantage. By 1975 four of seven sitting council members had originally been appointed.
- APPOINTMENT POWERS CAN BE ABUSED. As shown by Sunnyvale’s history. A 2009-2010 County Civil Grand Jury Report criticized Sunnyvale regarding the 2009 interim council appointment.

The Sunnyvale 2018 charter update already produced major cost savings in filling a vacancy by avoiding most stand-alone elections. There is no need to go too far. A district election costs only a fraction compared to one citywide.

Sunnyvale’s 2020 shift to districts and a separate mayor increased the chances for council vacancies. Three district council member’s terms don’t align with the directly elected mayor’s. If one of those council members successfully runs for mayor, a two-year vacancy is generated. This measure distracts from deeper issues.

Vote NO to prevent excessively long council appointments. Protect voter rights to democratically choose our elected officials at the earliest statewide election.
