[Pursuant to §9285: Maximum 250 words]

Vote NO on Measure I. Save our elections.

A current office holder often wins an election, enjoying name recognition and more resources than competitors. Measure I deepens this incumbency advantage.

The importance of an earlier election to fill a vacancy outweighs possible concerns over two elections for the same seat in the same even-year.

Measure I primarily benefits the appointee by eliminating the earlier opportunity for election competition.

- ODD-YEAR STATEWIDE SPECIAL ELECTIONS FURTHER REDUCE possibility for two elections in the same even-year. Since 1990, 33% of the odd-years (6 of 18) had California statewide special elections.
- IT’S BEEN ONLY 8 YEARS since voters approved the charter language in 2018 for filling vacancies. It balanced costs and democratic ideals.
Measure I upsets this balance.
- APPOINTMENT POWERS CAN BE ABUSED. From 1913 to 1975, repeated resignation generated vacancies were mostly filled by appointments. Incumbents were rarely defeated due to Incumbency advantage.

Elections are an investment in a representative Sunnyvale City Council.

Filling a mayor vacancy with a district council member creates another vacancy. Currently filling that new City Council vacancy by appointment means two simultaneous appointments on Sunnyvale City Council. Many voters are unaware of this possibility. This measure’s longer appointments will make this worse.

Shown below, two district elections cost about 1/3 as much as one citywide election. The expense for a primary election is reasonable:

Estimated potential at-large vacancy election (6 district approx.) June 7, 2022 $227,188
Districts 3 and 5 (2 districts expense) Nov. 8, 2022 $76,727

More information: https://tinyurl.com/NO-on-Measure-I

Take charge. NO on Measure I.
