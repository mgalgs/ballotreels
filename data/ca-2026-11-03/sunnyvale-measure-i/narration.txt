Measure I asks the voters of this city how long a person appointed to fill a council vacancy should serve before an election.
When a council seat becomes vacant, the council may call a special election, or appoint someone to fill the seat.
Today an appointee serves until the next November city election, or an earlier special election held with a statewide one, whichever is sooner.
Measure I would have an appointee serve until the next November city election, held in even-numbered years.
Depending on when a seat opens, an appointee could serve longer, but the city would avoid a special election at a spring primary followed by a November election in the same year.
The city attorney's impartial analysis does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Opponents say it allows appointments of two years or more, and cuts the elections that can fill a vacancy by at least half.
Supporters say the charter can require two elections for the same seat in one year, and November elections draw more voters than primaries.
A yes vote sends an appointed seat to the voters at the next November election in an even-numbered year.
A no vote keeps the rule of the next statewide or November city election, whichever is sooner.
