City of Piedmont Measure GG asks Piedmont voters whether to raise the tax paid when property is sold, from 1.3 percent to 1.75 percent.
Today, when property is sold or transferred, Piedmont charges a tax of $13 for each $1,000 of the price.
The money pays for the usual and current expenses of the city.
Measure GG would raise the tax to $17.50 for each $1,000, and rename it the real estate transfer tax.
Transfers from a public agency to a nonprofit using certain tax-exempt bonds would no longer be exempt.
Measure GG needs a majority of the votes cast to pass.
The impartial analysis estimates the increase would raise about $1,521,000 a year, which may be used for any general city purpose.
The Alameda County Composite Voter Information Guide says that no argument against Measure GG was submitted for this election.
Supporters say the tax, last updated over 30 years ago, funds city services and would be closer to neighboring cities.
A yes vote is in favor of Measure GG and its 1.75 percent transfer tax.
A no vote is against Measure GG and its 1.75 percent transfer tax.
