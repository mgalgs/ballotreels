Measure PVE asks the voters of this city whether to extend and raise the parcel tax that pays for police services, for six more years.
Voters approved the current police parcel tax in 2018, and it expires on June 30, 2027.
The City Council placed Measure PVE on the ballot in July; as a special tax, it needs two-thirds of the vote to pass.
Today, each parcel pays $342 a year, plus a charge per square foot of buildings, and the money goes only to police services.
From July 2027, the base would rise to $1,025 a parcel, with the same square-foot charge, then go up 2 percent a year.
The City Council could set a lower rate in any year.
The first $1 million each year would pay down the unfunded pension debt of the city; the rest would fund police services.
The money could not buy land or build new police buildings, and a finance committee would review the spending every year.
The tax would end on June 30, 2033.
The impartial analysis estimates the tax would raise $8.7 million a year until it expires.
Supporters say the measure protects local police services, and without it the city faces a yearly deficit that would drain its emergency reserves.
The official county list of measures for this election says that no argument against Measure PVE was submitted, by any group or person.
A yes vote approves the parcel tax.
A no vote does not approve the parcel tax.
