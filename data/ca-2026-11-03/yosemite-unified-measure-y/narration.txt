Measure Y asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $61.2 million in bonds for high school projects.
The district would borrow the money and repay it from a tax on property in the district.
Measure Y needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would repair or replace roofs and heating and cooling systems, renovate career training classrooms, and update fire alarms.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would check the spending.
The district's tax statement estimates a tax of $59.98 per $100,000 of assessed value, from 2027-2028 through 2047-2048.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $84.9 million.
No argument against Measure Y appears among the filings that the county elections office posted for this election.
Supporters say the high school is aging, needs major repairs to stay safe, and delaying will cost more.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue $61.2 million in bonds, repaid by a yearly property tax.
A no vote does not authorize the district to issue the bonds.
