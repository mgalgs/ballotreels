Measure Z asks the voters of this county whether to add a sales tax of one-eighth of 1 percent to fund the California Living Museum zoo.
Measure Z is a citizens' initiative, placed on the ballot by the Board of Supervisors after its petition was certified.
The zoo is operated by the county superintendent of schools.
The tax would apply in every city and the unincorporated areas, on top of current sales taxes.
The tax would start no earlier than April 1, 2027, and remain until repealed by voters.
A spending plan would split the money: 40 percent for animal care and habitats, 25 percent for education, 20 percent for infrastructure, and 15 percent for conservation.
Measure Z needs a majority of the vote to pass.
The impartial analysis estimates the tax would raise about $24 million a year.
Supporters say many exhibits are out of date and too small, and the measure keeps entrance fees low for families.
No argument against Measure Z appears among the official documents gathered for this video; only the argument in favor does.
A yes vote authorizes the county to levy the sales tax of one-eighth of 1 percent for the zoo.
A no vote rejects the tax.
