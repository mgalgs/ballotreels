Measure P asks the voters of this city whether to add a 1 percent sales tax for city services.
The City Council placed Measure P on the ballot in June, with one member voting no.
The sales tax rate in the city would rise from 8.375 percent to 9.375 percent.
The money would go into the general fund of the city, for any lawful purpose.
A citizens' oversight committee would review the spending each year.
The tax would last until ended by voters, and needs a simple majority to pass.
The city attorney's impartial analysis estimates that Measure P would raise about $9.4 million a year.
The Stanislaus County voter guide says that no argument against Measure P was filed by anyone in this election.
Supporters say Measure P keeps 911 response times fast, and prevents any further budget cuts that threaten public safety.
A yes vote on Measure P authorizes the 1 percent sales tax.
A no vote on Measure P does not authorize the 1 percent sales tax.
