City of Half Moon Bay Measure I asks the voters of Half Moon Bay whether to redraw the Measure D downtown area.
Measure D, adopted by voters in 1999, limits new homes each year to 1 percent growth in population.
Measure D steers most new homes downtown, where the City Council may allow 0.5 percent more.
Today, the downtown area is the redevelopment survey area adopted in 1998.
Measure I would make the downtown area the Town Center on the land use plan map.
The impartial analysis says 286 residential parcels would join the downtown area, and 40 would leave it.
The new map would take effect only once the California Coastal Commission certifies it.
The published measure documents of San Mateo County do not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say the decades-old map includes outlying farmland but omits land nearly surrounded by downtown.
No argument against Measure I appears in the published measure documents of San Mateo County.
A yes vote means the Measure D downtown area becomes the Town Center.
A no vote means the downtown area stays the survey area from 1998.
