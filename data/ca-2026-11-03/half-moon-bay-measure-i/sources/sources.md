# Measure I — City of Half Moon Bay (land use plan / zoning amendment: Measure D "Downtown Area")

- Jurisdiction: City of Half Moon Bay.
- Kind: ordinance amending the Local Coastal Land Use Plan (Policy 2-16) and Municipal Code Titles 17 and 18, to redefine the 1999 Measure D "Downtown Area" as the "Town Center" (Figure 2-2).
- Who votes: the voters of the City of Half Moon Bay. Resolution, p. 2, Sec. 1: "The City Council hereby approves the submission of the proposed Ordinance, as set forth in the form attached hereto as Exhibit A, to the voters of the City of Half Moon Bay at the November 3, 2026, general municipal election."
- Vote threshold: majority. Resolution, p. 3, Sec. 4: "the vote requirement for the Measure to pass is a majority (50%+1) of the votes cast."
- Placed by: the City Council (Resolution No. 2026-39). Recital, p. 1: "on January 13, 2026, the City Council directed staff to prepare a ballot measure".
- Election: November 3, 2026.

## Ballot question

> Shall the Measure, amending the City of Half Moon Bay Local Coastal Land Use Plan, Title 17 (Subdivisions) and Title 18 (Zoning) of the Half Moon Bay Municipal Code, and other conforming edits so that the Measure D "Downtown Area" is redefined as the area designated as the "Town Center" (Figure 2-2) of the Local Coastal Land Use Plan, be adopted?

(resolution-full-text.pdf p. 3, Sec. 3(a); lead-in at the bottom of p. 2; transcribed from the scan.)

## Full text (resolution / ordinance)

- Resolution No. 2026-39, "A Resolution of the Council of the City of Half Moon Bay Approving a Proposed Ordinance to Amend the City's Local Coastal Land Use Plan, Policy 2-16, and Half Moon Bay Municipal Code ... and Placing a Measure on the November 3, 2026 General Election Ballot" (p. 1). Stamped AUG 06 2026 (p. 1).
- Adopted: "I, the undersigned, hereby certify that the foregoing Resolution was duly passed and adopted on the 2nd day of June 2026, by the City Council of Half Moon Bay by the following vote: AYES, Councilmembers: BROWNSTONE, JONSSON, NAGENAST, PENROSE, RUDDOCK" (p. 5). NOES, ABSENT, ABSTAIN: blank. Attest: Maggie Rodriguez, Interim City Clerk; approved: Debbie Ruddock, Mayor.
- Recital, p. 2: "the proposed Measure will not change the location, density or intensity of development permitted under the 2020 LCLUP or the General Plan including the 2023 - 2031 Housing Element".
- Recital, p. 2: "if approved by the voters, the amendments will be subject to review by the California Coastal Commission and the proposed Ordinance would not become effective until certified by the California Coastal Commission."
- Sec. 2, p. 2: the Council finds the ordinance exempt from CEQA "under CEQA Guidelines section 15265 (adoption of coastal plans and programs)".
- Exhibit A ordinance, Sec. 1(C), p. 6: "The purpose of this Ordinance is to align the Measure D "Downtown Area" with the City's Town Center boundaries as depicted in Attachment A to this Ordinance, which includes neighborhoods traditionally considered a part of the downtown."
- Exhibit A, Sec. 2, p. 7 (amended Policy 2-16(f)): "The Downtown Area is the area designated as the Town Center in Figure 2-2 of this Local Coastal Land Use Plan." (replacing "the area designated as the Downtown Half Moon Bay Redevelopment Survey Area in City Resolution No. C-91-98, November 3, 1998", struck through).

## Impartial analysis

"CITY ATTORNEY IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE I", signed "Denise S. Bazzano, City Attorney, City of Half Moon Bay" (impartial-analysis.pdf p. 1).

- "The City Council placed Measure I on the November 3, 2026, ballot."
- "Measure D provides that the number of dwelling units for new residential development that the City may authorize each calendar year may not exceed the number of units which would result in a growth of 1% in the City's population as of January 1st of that year."
- "Measure D currently defines the "Downtown Area" as the "Half Moon Bay Redevelopment Survey Area" that was adopted in November 1998."
- "This proposed revision would affect 491 total parcels, either being added to or subtracted from the mapped Downtown Area. Of those 491 parcels, 326 are residential (R-1, R-2, R-3). Of those 326 residential parcels, 286 parcels would be added to the "Downtown Area," and 40 parcels would be subtracted."
- "Measure I, if approved by the voters, would need to be certified by the California Coastal Commission before it can become effective."
- "A "YES" vote is a vote in favor of Measure I. A "NO" vote is a vote against Measure I." / "This Measure will be effective only if a majority of voters voting on the Measure vote in its favor."
