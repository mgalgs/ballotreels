Measure P asks the voters of this county whether to adopt a 15-year parcel tax on buildings for child care and early education.
Measure P is a citizens' initiative, placed on the ballot by a voter petition.
The tax would be charged each year on each square foot of building floor area, and the Board of Supervisors could adjust it yearly for inflation.
The money could pay for child care and early education for low- and middle-income families, and for providers' wages and facilities.
A county commission would plan the spending, which the Board of Supervisors would approve each year, with a yearly independent audit.
As an initiative, Measure P needs a simple majority to pass.
The county estimates the tax would raise about $12.5 million a year on average.
Opponents say Measure P locks taxpayers into a 15-year tax with no defined spending rules, no defined eligibility, and no way to judge results.
Supporters say Measure P will make early education more affordable, create more child care spaces, help keep educators, and let more parents work.
A yes vote adopts the parcel tax for 15 years.
A no vote does not adopt the tax.
