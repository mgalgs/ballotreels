Measure T asks the voters of this city whether to raise the tax that hotel guests pay on their rooms.
Hotel guests staying 30 days or less pay 14 percent of the room rate, a rate set in 2012.
Measure T would raise the rate to 15 percent in March 2027, then to 16 percent in March 2028.
The money would go to the general fund, which pays for services such as emergency response, streets and parks.
Measure T needs a simple majority to pass.
The impartial analysis says the city expects up to about $1 million the first year, and $2 million a year after.
No argument against Measure T was submitted, according to the county Registrar's official list of ballot measures.
Supporters say visitors, not residents, pay for services they use, and the state cannot take the money.
A yes vote raises the hotel tax to 16 percent over two years.
A no vote keeps the hotel tax at 14 percent.
