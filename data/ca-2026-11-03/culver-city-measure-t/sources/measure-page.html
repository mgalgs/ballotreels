Impartial Analysis - CCUSD Parcel Tax (Measure AA)

CITY ATTORNEY’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE AA

The City Council of the City of Culver City (City) placed Measure AA on the ballot to amend the Culver City Municipal Code to add a new Parcel Tax to provide additional funding to the Culver City Unified School District.

The Measure would add a new Chapter 3.08 to the Culver City Municipal Code to create a new tax on all parcels of taxable real property in the City. The tax is calculated at the rate of forty-eight cents ($0.48) per square foot on all improvements on the parcel up to a maximum of $15,000 per parcel. The tax would be collected annually and paid by the owner of each parcel in the same way as other property taxes. The tax will expire after 10 years.

The proposed tax would not apply to persons 65 years or older, or anyone who receives Supplemental Security Income for a disability, or those who receive Social Security Disability Insurance benefits. The tax also would not apply to an Accessory Dwelling Unit occupied by a person meeting the exemption standards listed above.

The Measure requires the City and District jointly form an oversight board to monitor the use of the funds raised, to ensure the funds are being used for educational purposes. The Board must consist of Culver City residents including parents or guardians of District Students of various grade levels, representatives of District employee bargaining units, and members of the community at large. Proper uses of funds include salaries and professional development for staff, maintaining STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) programs, art, music, visual and performing arts, technology, counseling, health, and extra-curricular activities. Funds may not be used for administrative salaries.

The City will collect the tax revenues and transmit them to the District. The City’s costs for administering the tax will be reimbursed by the District.

To be adopted, State law requires this Measure be approved by a majority of Culver City voters. A “Yes” vote on the Measure would approve of the tax, amend the Municipal Code, and provide the resulting funding to the District. A “No” vote would mean no new parcel tax would be established.

Dated:  August 13, 2026                                                                                       

Heather Baker, City Attorney
City of Culver City

Impartial Analysis - TOT Increase (Measure T)

CITY ATTORNEY’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE T

Measure T, which was placed on the ballot by the City Council, would increase the City of Culver City’s current Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) from 14% to 16% in two phases.  Effective March 1, 2027, the TOT would increase from 14% to 15%.  Effective March 1, 2028, the TOT would increase from 15% to 16%.  If passed, the Measure would amend Culver City Municipal Code (CCMC) Section 11.02.105, which establishes the TOT rate. 

The City’s existing TOT rate of 14% has been in effect since 2012.  The TOT is a percentage of the room rate charged to a guest who stays in a “hotel” within the City for a period of 30 days or less.  The TOT is collected from guests by the hotel operators and then remitted to the City.  CCMC Section 11.02.100 defines a hotel as “[a]ny structure, or any portion of any structure, which is occupied or intended or designed for occupancy by transients for dwelling, lodging or sleeping purposes, and includes any hotel, inn, tourist home or house, motel, studio hotel, bachelor hotel, lodging house, rooming house, apartment house, dormitory, public or private club, mobile home or house trailer at a fixed location, or other similar structure or portion thereof.”

The Measure proposes to increase only the rate of the existing TOT.  It does not change the method of collection of the TOT, and all other related regulations shall remain the same, if the Measure is approved by a majority of the voters. 

The TOT provides revenue for the City’s General Fund, which is primarily used for basic City services, such as emergency response; firefighter, paramedic and police staffing; affordable housing, mental health and homeless reduction programs; improvements to streets, sidewalks, public transportation, infrastructure and parks; and other general services.  The City anticipates the proposed increase would be expected to generate up to an additional $1,000,000, approximately, in the first year of its implementation and up to approximately $2,000,000 per year thereafter, in General Fund revenue to fund City services.

State law requires that this measure be approved by a majority of the voters to be enacted.  A “Yes” vote for the Measure will increase the existing TOT from 14% to 16% over a two-year phase in period.  A “No” vote against the Measure will leave in place the existing TOT at 14%.

Dated:  August 13, 2026                                                                           

Heather Baker, City Attorney
City of Culver City

Argument in Favor - TOT Increase (Measure T)

ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF BALLOT MEASURE T
ARGUMENT TO INCREASE TRANSIENT OCCUPANCY TAX (TOT)

Measure T asks visitors — not Culver City residents — to help pay for the services they use while they’re here.

Culver City’s Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT), the “hotel tax,” is paid by guests staying in our hotels, motels, and short-term rentals for 30 days or less. Residents don’t pay it. The rate hasn’t changed since April 2012, when 83.3% of voters approved it. Costs have risen ever since.

This measure raises the TOT one percent at a time — to 15% in 2027, then 16% in 2028 — generating an estimated $2 million annually. Those dollars stay here, maintaining 9-1-1 emergency response, firefighter, paramedic and police staffing; affordable housing, mental health and homelessness reduction programs; and repairs to streets, sidewalks, storm drains and parks.

The phase-in matters. Increasing gradually gives our 17 tax-paying hotels time to adjust before the 2028 Olympics bring record visitor demand to the Westside.

At 16%, Culver City is no outlier. Counting tourism district assessments, our neighbors already charge as much or more: Los Angeles 16% (14% plus a 2% Tourism Marketing District assessment), Anaheim 17% (15% plus 2%), Santa Monica roughly 16–18%, West Hollywood 15.5% (12.5% plus 3%). Inglewood charges 15.5% today. We would sit squarely within the regional market — competitive for our hotels, fair to our residents.

Unlike other revenue, this local tax cannot be taken by Sacramento. It requires annual audits and public disclosure of spending, and continues only until voters end it.

Visitors use our streets, our parks and our emergency services. This measure simply asks them to pay their share.

Please join us in voting YES on Measure T.

Impartial Analysis - Vote 16 (Measure YV)

CITY ATTORNEY’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE YV

The City Council of the City of Culver City (City) placed Measure YV on the ballot to amend the City Charter (Charter) by allowing City residents aged 16 and 17 to vote for City Council candidates, City ballot measures, and Culver City Unified School District (CCUSD) Board candidates and measures once certain conditions are met by the City and CCUSD Board.

The City Charter states that “unless otherwise provided by this Charter,” state law will control the procedures for holding all City elections and elections for members of the CCUSD school board.  See Charter Sections 1500 and 1503. California Constitution Article II section 2 states that “United States citizens 18 years of age and resident of this state may vote.”  The California Elections Code also requires that a valid registered voter must be a California resident, a United States citizen, and at least 18 years of age.

The California Constitution, Article XI Section 5, grants charter cities the right to govern their municipal affairs, which includes the conduct of city elections. The Measure proposes to add Section 1506, “Voting Age”, to the Charter, which would allow 16- and 17-year-old City residents, who are otherwise eligible under state and local law, to vote in City and CCUSD elections. The Measure also amends Section 1500 regarding School Board elections, to refer to the provisions of the new Section 1506. 

The Measure provides that in order for the voting age to be lowered to 16, the City Council and the CCUSD Board must each decide to do the following for their respective elections: (1) approve the budgetary funds to cover any additional election costs that may be incurred; (2) determine that logistical systems are in place to conduct the election; and (3) determine that including 16- and 17-year-olds in the election would not prevent consolidation of the election with the County election, which has occurred since 2020.

The conditions described in the preceding paragraph include having equipment, software, and procedures in place for voter registration and for casting votes during the election. These systems and logistics must be technically capable and ready to handle accommodating 16- and 17-year-old voting. Additionally, the Measure allows the City and the CCUSD to independently decide, as to their respective elections, the manner, method and times at which 16- and 17-year-olds may cast their votes, which do not need to be the same as those for persons 18 and older.

The requirements of this Measure, if approved, would apply to the City and the CCUSD independently of each other.

To be adopted, State law requires this Measure be approved by a majority of Culver City voters. A “Yes” vote on Measure YV would lower the age requirement to 16 for City and CCUSD elections, but before the Measure is implemented, the City Council and the CCUSD Board must determine that the conditions listed in the Measure are met. A “No” vote on the Measure would not lower the voting age to 16.

Dated:  August 13, 2026                                                                                                   

Heather Baker, City Attorney
City of Culver City

Argument in Favor - Vote 16 (Measure YV)

ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF BALLOT MEASURE YV
ARGUMENT TO LOWER THE CULVER CITY VOTING AGE TO 16

Measure YV is an investment in Culver City’s future, strengthening local democracy by welcoming 16- and 17-year-olds into the civic process. Young people don’t have to wait until 18 before local government begins shaping their lives. Decisions by the Mayor, City Council, and School Board affect their schools, parks, transportation, public safety, recreation programs, and future opportunities.

Every day, Culver City’s 16- and 17-year-olds contribute to the community by working, paying taxes, driving, volunteering, leading student organizations, and serving as active neighbors. They have demonstrated the responsibility to participate in decisions that directly affect them. A YES vote on Measure YV gives them a voice in local elections where they already have a meaningful stake.

This proposal is not new to Culver City voters. In 2022, the same idea appeared on the ballot as Measure VY and fell just 16 votes short of passage—the narrowest margin of any contest on the county’s ballot that year. It also received more votes than the highest vote getting City Council candidate, demonstrating broad community engagement with the issue.

Measure YV builds on a growing movement. Communities across California and the nation have expanded local voting rights to 16- and 17-year-olds, with encouraging results. Research shows that people who vote when they first become eligible are more likely to remain engaged throughout their lives. 16- and 17-year-olds are especially well positioned to cast their first ballot while supported by their families, schools, and community. Communities that have adopted Vote16 have reported strong youth turnout, increased civic knowledge, and deeper connections between young people and local government.

A YES vote on Measure YV is about more than one election. It prepares the next generation of informed voters, strengthens local democracy, and ensures more residents with a stake in Culver City’s future have the opportunity to help shape it for generations to come.

Argument in Opposition - Vote 16 (Measure YV)

Direct Argument Against Vote16

High school has been a formative time in all our lives. It was our first introduction to politics and culture. The Vote16 amendment makes promises to engage Culver City High School students in our civic culture by granting them the right to decide who our next councilmembers will be.

The reality is a bit different.

For a while now, activists have had an eye to campaigning in our school district. They've pressured Council to approve this measure on the ballot - for a second time.

Let's set aside how this is going to work. Let's assume the proponents thought of all that-how those under 18 get registered, whether outside students can vote, whether students can run for Council, etc.

The fact is, this measure makes our students a captive, impressionable voting bloc. The candidates with the best access to the school district will have an advantage over outsiders. That makes our Council less accessible or accountable.

And a voter is public record. Their name, address, and contact info would be published by the election administrator, available to anyone who requests it. It's a necessary part of elections. That means your 16-year old child would be getting contacted - and courted - by strangers with political motives.

Is that the school district you want? Do you want it to become a political football, with your children being contacted by competing strangers, pressured to vote for their favored candidates? Or do you feel like your children should be shielded from the political winds of our city?

If you feel like our Council elections should leave our kids alone, vote NO on Vote16.
