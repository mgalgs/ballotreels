Measure S asks the voters of this county whether to add a half percent sales tax for public safety.
Voters placed Measure S on the ballot by signing a petition.
The state would collect the tax along with existing sales taxes, for 20 years.
The money would go to a public safety fund for fire stations, sheriff and coroner buildings, and fire equipment.
A citizens' committee and a yearly independent audit would check the spending.
The measure's own text says the tax takes effect with a majority vote.
The ballot question estimates about $20 million a year in revenue.
Supporters say Measure S would improve emergency response and upgrade deteriorated fire stations and equipment.
The posted pages for Measure S print one argument in favor, and none against it.
A yes vote raises the sales tax by a half percent for 20 years.
A no vote does not raise the sales tax.
