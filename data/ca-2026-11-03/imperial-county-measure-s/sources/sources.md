**Argument against / rebuttals:** the Registrar's Measure S packet prints an argument in favor only. No argument against and no rebuttals appear in it. The full county voter information guide is not posted on the Registrar's site, so I could not check it.

**Vote threshold:** the initiative's own text says majority — Section 3(c): "a temporary special retail transactions and use tax ... which shall be operative for a period of 20 years if a majority of electors voting on the measure vote to approve the imposition of the tax"; and "This ordinance shall take effect immediately upon its adoption by a majority of voters voting on the ordinance." County Counsel's analysis does not state the threshold. I found no county record stating which threshold the county will apply; do not state one beyond quoting the measure text.

## Quoted facts

- **Ballot question** (packet p. 1): "IMPERIAL COUNTY 911 RESPONSE/PUBLIC SAFETY PROTECTION. To maintain/improve emergency/911 response, fire/paramedic/sheriff services; prepare/respond to earthquakes/disasters; reduce crime; retain/attract qualified firefighters/paramedics/sheriff's deputies; address homelessness; upgrade aging lifesaving fire equipment, fire stations, emergency facilities; shall Imperial County's locally-controlled measure establishing a ½-cent transactions and use (sales) tax, generating approximately $20,000,000 annually, expiring in 20 years, be adopted; requiring audits, spending disclosure, funds cannot be diverted for other purposes?"
- **Who votes:** July 20 notice: "the following measure will be submitted to the voters of Imperial County".
- **Full text:** "This ordinance shall be known, and may be cited as, the "Imperial County Public Safety Ordinance."" Findings include: "Sixty percent (60%) of all Imperial County 911 calls are medical emergencies"; "Imperial County is huge, spanning nearly 4,500 square miles."
- **Impartial analysis (County Counsel Geoffrey P. Holbrook; Deputy Faye Winkler):**
  - "is an initiative that was placed on the ballot by a petition signed by the requisite number of voters."
  - "proposes the adoption of a sales tax upon all retailers of tangible personal property in the incorporated and unincorporated Imperial County at a rate of 0.5 cent and a use tax ... at a rate of 0.5 cent".
  - "funds in the Public Safety Fund may only be used for the construction, improvement, and maintenance of public safety facilities, such as fire stations, law enforcement facilities, and a Coroner's Facility, in El Centro, Brawley Salton City, Ocotillo, and Calexico; the purchase of fire safety vehicles, equipment and supplies; and the funding of ongoing operational maintenance and repair costs".
  - "establishes a 7-member Citizen's Oversight Committee".
  - "effective on the first calendar quarter commencing more than 110 days after the adoption of this ordinance and shall cease to be operative 20 years from that date, unless subsequently extended by the voters."
  - "A vote "yes" would increase the applicable sales and use tax each by 0.5 cent for 20 years."
- **Argument in favor:** "VOTE YES on Measure S to improve PUBLIC SAFETY across Imperial County." "To fund these dire needs, Measure S authorizes a modest, locally controlled ½-cent sales tax, countywide, expiring in 20 years." Signers: Fred Miramontes (Imperial Co. Sheriff), Martha Cardenas-Singh (Supervisor, District 2), Mark McBroom, Norma Aguilar, David Lantzer (Imperial Co. Fire Chief).
