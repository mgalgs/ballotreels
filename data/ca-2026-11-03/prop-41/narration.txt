Proposition 41 is about special taxes, which fund one set purpose.
Today, the state's yearly spending limit leaves out some spending, like infrastructure.
Special tax programs would be audited before an initiative's vote and every four years.
New special tax spending might count toward the limit.
Conflicting measures on this ballot would be void if Proposition 41 got more yes votes.
The Legislative Analyst's Office expects audit costs of low millions a year, less than 1 percent of this year's General Fund spending, mostly paid by new taxes.
The office says savings are possible and the net effect unknown.
Supporters say public audits of programs funded by new taxes bring transparency and accountability.
Opponents say Proposition 41 is designed specifically to undo the Billionaire Tax, Proposition 40.
A yes vote means the State Auditor would review special tax programs.
A no vote means the State Auditor's reviews would not change.
