Measure K asks the voters of this city whether to replace the business tax with one based on gross receipts.
The business tax today dates from 1976, and charges each business by its industry and number of employees.
The City Council placed Measure K on the ballot, and it needs a majority to pass.
Gross receipts are all the money a business takes in, before expenses.
Starting in 2028, each business would pay a flat amount from $25 to $400 a year, rising with inflation.
Businesses taking in more than $700,000 would also pay a tax on part of their receipts.
The ballot question estimates about $3 million a year for general city services.
Supporters say small businesses now pay a higher tax rate than large corporations, in some cases over 70 times more.
Opponents say businesses must pass taxes on to customers through higher prices, and living costs here are already too high.
A yes vote adopts the new gross receipts tax to replace the existing business tax.
A no vote rejects the new tax and keeps the existing one.
