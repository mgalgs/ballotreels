# Measure K — City of Milpitas — Business tax (gross receipts), "City of Milpitas Local Funding Measure"

- **Letter:** K, confirmed by the City Clerk's "Final Notice of Nominees and Measure" (p. 1: "MEASURE K") and the city's 2026 Election page.
- **Jurisdiction:** City of Milpitas.
- **Kind:** ordinance repealing the business license tax (Municipal Code Title III, Chapter 1) and adding a gross-receipts business tax (Title III, Chapter 10); general tax, placed by the City Council.
- **Who votes:** City of Milpitas voters. Impartial analysis: "This measure was placed on the ballot by the City Council of the City of Milpitas."
- **Threshold:** the ordinance (resolution PDF p. 28): "If approved by a majority of the voters at the November 2026 general election, the provisions of ..." (sentence continues in the PDF).
- **Status:** adopted. Resolution No. 9533, adopted August 3, 2026; the City Clerk's certification (PDF p. 1, dated August 4, 2026) states it was "duly adopted by the Council of the City of Milpitas at a regular meeting of said Council held on the 3rd day of August, 2026".

## Ballot question (Final Notice, p. 1)

> City of Milpitas Local Funding Measure
> Shall an ordinance to update City of Milpitas's 50-year-old business tax be adopted, with no tax increase to residents, so small businesses pay less than larger businesses, with all paying an annual flat rate, plus 0.025% to 0.035% of gross receipts for larger businesses, providing approximately $3,000,000 annually for general city services such as maintaining fiscal stability, neighborhood safety/cleanliness; requiring public disclosure, all funds invested locally, until ended by voters?

The notice is signed "Suzanne Guzzetta, MMC, Milpitas City Clerk/Elections Official" and "Dated and Posted: October 21, 2026".

## Full text

Ordinance title (resolution PDF): "AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY OF MILPITAS REPEALING MILPITAS MUNICIPAL CODE TITLE III ("BUSINESS AND PROFESSIONS"), CHAPTER 1 ("BUSINESS LICENSES") AND ADDING TITLE III, CHAPTER 10 ("BUSINESS TAX") TO MODERNIZE AND MODIFY THE METHOD OF LEVYING THE BUSINESS TAX". ## Impartial analysis

"CITY ATTORNEYS' IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE K", signed "/s Christian M. Curtis, City Attorney". Key statements:

> Measure K seeks voter approval of an ordinance that replaces the City's current business tax with a new tax based on gross receipts.

> Currently, the City's uses a business tax formula that was adopted in 1976. It requires each business to pay an amount based on its relevant industry ... and its number of employees.

> Each business would pay a flat rate ranging from $25 to $400 based on the tier in which its gross receipts fall. Additionally, businesses making more than $700,000 would pay a rate ranging from 0.025% to 0.035% of that portion of their gross receipts falling in the top three tiers. The flat rate would increase automatically each year with inflation.

> If approved, the new tax would go into effect January 1, 2028.

> Proceeds of the new tax would continue to be (i) deposited in the City's general fund, (ii) annually audited and publicly reported in the City's annual audit, and (iii) available for any lawful municipal purpose.

> A "yes" vote adopts the new gross receipts business tax to replace the existing tax. A "no" vote rejects the new gross receipts business tax and keeps the existing tax.

The analysis prints a schedule of seven gross-receipts tiers, from "Under $25,000 — $25" up to "Over $25,000,000 — $400 + $0.35 per $1,000".

## Arguments and rebuttals
