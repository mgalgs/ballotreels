Measure O asks voters in one area of this school district whether to approve $6,000,000 in bonds for two high schools.
Only voters in that area vote, and the bonds would be repaid from a yearly tax on property there.
Measure O needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money would build and equip career training classrooms at the two high schools, for skills such as welding, carpentry and automotive technology.
The money would also improve school safety systems, such as cameras and communication systems.
The district's tax rate statement estimates $19 a year per $100,000 of assessed value, each year the bonds are outstanding, through 2054-2055.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $11.3 million.
No argument against Measure O was filed, so the county voter guide prints only the argument in favor of the measure.
Supporters say many skilled jobs in the region go unfilled, and Measure O would prepare students to fill jobs at local businesses.
A yes vote is a vote to authorize the bonds.
A no vote is a vote against authorizing the bonds.
