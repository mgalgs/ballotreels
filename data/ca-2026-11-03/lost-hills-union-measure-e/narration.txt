Measure E asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $9 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them from a yearly tax on property in the district.
Measure E needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay only for the school facility projects the measure lists.
The ballot question names classrooms and bathrooms, fire, electrical, lighting and security systems, and air conditioning.
The impartial analysis says the district estimates an average tax of $30 per $100,000 of assessed value.
The district expects the tax to be collected until the 2056-2057 fiscal year.
The ballot question puts the yearly levy at $720,000 while the bonds are outstanding.
Supporters say voters last invested in these schools a decade ago, and state money has not been enough for repairs.
No argument against Measure E appears among the official documents gathered for this video; only the argument in favor does.
A yes vote authorizes the district to issue $9 million in bonds.
A no vote does not authorize the bonds.
