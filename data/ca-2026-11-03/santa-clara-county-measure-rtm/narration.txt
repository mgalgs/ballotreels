Measure RTM asks the voters of this county, one of five in a regional transit district, whether to add a sales tax for public transit.
A 2025 state law created the five-county district, and lets its voters approve a sales tax for transit.
Voters placed Measure RTM on the ballot by petition.
The tax would be 0.5 percent in this county and three others, and 1 percent in the fifth, for 14 years.
After election and administrative costs, the measure splits the money among transit agencies, by shares it sets for each county.
Measure RTM needs a majority of all the votes cast in the five counties combined, not in each county.
The impartial analysis estimates the tax would raise about $980 million a year across the five counties.
Opponents say it is a $14 billion bailout for a broken system, and most tax dollars go to the other four counties.
Supporters say that if it fails, BART could close 15 stations and cut service by 70 percent, and it invests locally.
A yes vote is a vote to impose the sales tax for 14 years.
A no vote is a vote against imposing the tax.
