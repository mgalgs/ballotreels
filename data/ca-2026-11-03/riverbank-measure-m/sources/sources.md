## Quoted facts

- Ballot question: "CITIZEN INITIATIVE MEASURE TO REQUIRE VOTER APPROVAL OF URBAN DEVELOPMENT ON AGRICULTURAL OR OPEN SPACE LANDS LOCATED WEST OF RIVERBANK CITY LIMITS. Shall the measure, to require that a majority of city voters approve at an election any decision of the Riverbank City Council that would authorize or facilitate development of urban uses on agricultural or open space lands located west of the current city limits of the City of Riverbank, be adopted?"
- Who votes: notice, "the following measure will be submitted to the voters of the City of Riverbank".
- Origin: analysis, "Measure M was placed on the ballot by a citizen petition."
- Effect: "Measure M would amend the Riverbank General Plan to require majority voter approval at an election of City Council decisions allowing urban development on agricultural or open space land west of the City limits."
- City Attorney's view: "In the City Attorney’s opinion, Measure M is likely unconstitutional because of conflicts with state housing and annexation laws." The analysis names the State Housing Element Law, the Housing Crisis Act (SB 330), the Housing Accountability Act, and state annexation law, and cites L.I.F.E. Committee v. City of Lodi (1989) 213 Cal.App.3d 1139.
- Consequence stated: "If approved, the City may be sued by both the California Attorney General and private land developers, resulting in large legal costs to the City."
- Vote: "A “yes” vote is in favor of adopting Measure M. A “no” vote is against adopting Measure M."
- Full text: the policy applies to land west of city limits "including the area(s) north of Patterson Road to the Stanislaus River"; "This voter-approval policy may not be amended or repealed without a vote of the people".

Note: the impartial analysis is unusually argumentative for an impartial analysis; quote it as the City Attorney's stated opinion.
