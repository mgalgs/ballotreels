Measure M asks the voters of this city whether city voters must approve urban development on farmland and open space west of the city.
Measure M was placed on the ballot by a citizen petition.
Measure M would amend the General Plan of the city, so a City Council decision allowing urban development there would need a majority vote of city voters.
The measure makes exceptions, such as land the state certifies the city needs for its required share of housing.
The city attorney's impartial analysis says that, in the city attorney's opinion, Measure M is likely unconstitutional, because of conflicts with state housing and annexation laws.
The Stanislaus County Voter Information Guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Opponents say Measure M lets a neighboring city control land west of this city, and loses revenue for a new fire station.
Supporters say Measure M protects the most valuable farmland west of the city, and does not cap growth or block housing.
A yes vote adopts the requirement that city voters approve that development at an election.
A no vote is against adopting Measure M, and its requirement for a vote.
