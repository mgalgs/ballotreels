## Facts and quotes

**Ballot question (p1):** "City of Santa Rosa Community Safety/Financial Stability Measure. Shall the measure maintaining Santa Rosa's quality of life, 911 emergency medical, police response to violent crimes; keeping all city fire stations open and public places safe/clean; fixing streets/repairing potholes; addressing homeless encampments; maintaining police, fire protection, other services, by reauthorizing the existing, voter approved sales tax at an updated 1¢ rate, providing approximately $46,000,000 annually until ended by voters; requiring audits, public disclosure, local control, be adopted?"

**Vote needed (p1):** "Simple Majority Needed to Pass". Analysis: "Because Measure L does not limit the use of tax revenue, it is a "general tax." ... the measure only requires approval by a simple majority of voters voting on the measure."

**Who votes:** voters of the City of Santa Rosa ("increase to the transactions and use (sales) tax within the City").

**Impartial analysis (p2), key statements:**
- "If passed by the voters, Measure L would authorize a one-half of one percent (0.50%) increase to the transactions and use (sales) tax within the City."
- "Part of the City's share is the 0.50% tax approved by voters in 2020 as Measure Q, which is scheduled to expire on March 31, 2031. Measure L would replace Measure Q with a 1.00% tax that continues unless and until ended by the voters. Measure L would increase the total sales tax rate in Santa Rosa by 0.50%, to 10.50%."
- "State law caps the local sales tax that a city may impose. The current cap applicable to Santa Rosa allows for a 0.25% increase. Measure L tracks Senate Bill 762 ... As of the date of this analysis, the Governor has not acted on SB 762. ... If the Governor vetoes SB 762 and Measure L passes, Measure L's full 0.50% increase may not take effect."
- "Measure L is estimated to produce $46 million annually, including approximately $23 million in additional annual revenue compared to the existing one-half cent tax."
- Signed: Ashle Crocker, City Attorney.

**Argument in favor (p2):** signed Mark Stapp (Santa Rosa Mayor), Sal Rosano (Santa Rosa Police Chief Retired), Anthony G. Gossner (Retired Santa Rosa Fire Chief), Hector Velazquez (Local business owner), Thea Hensel (Santa Rosa Homeowner and Taxpayer).

**Argument against:** "No Argument Was Submitted Against Measure L" (p2).

Open point: whether SB 762 was signed is not stated in any document archived here.
