Measure J asks the voters of this road zone whether to replace its $325 yearly road assessment with a $525 tax per parcel.
The county formed the zone in 1985 to pay for road improvements and maintenance.
The county counts 162 registered voters living in the zone.
The Board of Supervisors placed Measure J on the ballot at the request of the zone's advisory committee.
Measure J needs two-thirds of the vote to pass.
The new tax would apply to every parcel, built on or not, starting in the 2027-2028 fiscal year.
The money could pay only for the roads in the zone, and the tax would last until voters change or repeal it.
If Measure J fails, the $325 assessment stays.
The county attorney's impartial analysis expects the tax to raise about $39,375 a year.
No argument against Measure J was submitted, the voter pamphlet of this county says, and it prints no rebuttals.
Supporters say the $325 has not risen since 1997, and current funds cover only the most critical road repairs.
A yes vote authorizes the tax of $525 per parcel a year.
A no vote is against the tax, and the $325 assessment stays.
