Measure W

MEASURE W

ACALANES UNION HIGH SCHOOL DISTRICT

Shall the measure supporting high-quality education in local high schools by:

• Attracting/retaining highly qualified teachers

• continuing advanced academic programs in science, technology, engineering, math, and arts that prepare students for success in college and careers

• maintaining manageable class sizes

with Acalanes Union High School District levying a $168 parcel tax for eight years, with senior exemptions, citizens’ oversight and independent audits, providing $6,000,000 annually in local school funding that cannot be taken by the State, be adopted?

COUNTY COUNSEL’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF

MEASURE W

The governing board of the Acalanes Union High School District has adopted a resolution proposing a parcel tax (a qualified special tax).

If this measure passes, beginning on July 1, 2027, the current parcel tax would be increased by $168 annually on parcels of taxable real property within the District for eight years, expiring on June 30, 2035. Based on information from the County Auditor-Controller’s Office, there are two existing parcel taxes totaling $301 per parcel annually. If this measure passes, the total amount of annual parcel taxes would be $469 per parcel.

The parcel tax would apply to each parcel of taxable real property in the District. A parcel of taxable real property is any unit of real property in the District that receives a separate tax bill for property taxes from the Contra Costa County Treasurer-Tax Collector’s Office.

The ballot measure includes certain exemptions. Upon proper application to the District, the District may exempt any parcel from the parcel tax that is owned and occupied as a principal residence by (1) a person 65 years of age or older; (2) a person receiving Supplemental Security Income for a disability; or (3) a person receiving Social Security Disability Insurance benefits, whose yearly income does not exceed 250 percent of the 2012 federal poverty guidelines issued by the United States Department of Health and Human Services. Additionally, property that is otherwise exempt from property taxes shall also be exempt from the parcel tax.

The ballot measure states that the proceeds of the parcel tax will be used to support “high-quality education in local high schools by attracting/retaining highly qualified teachers; continuing advanced academic programs in science, technology, engineering, math and arts that prepare students for success in college and careers; [and] maintaining manageable class sizes.” Proceeds from the parcel tax may be used only for the specific purposes set forth in the ballot measure and according to constitutional and statutory provisions.

State law requires the District’s chief fiscal officer to file an annual report with the District’s governing board that states the amount of funds received and expended in each year and the status of any projects required or authorized to be funded from the parcel tax proceeds. State law also requires the proceeds from the parcel tax to be deposited into a separate designated account. The measure also would require the District to have an appointed citizens’ oversight committee to oversee the use of parcel tax proceeds.

Of those voting on the measure, two-thirds must approve the measure for it to pass.

A ”yes” vote is in favor of authorizing the parcel tax for an eight-year period.

A “no” vote is against authorizing the parcel tax for an eight-year period.

ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF

MEASURE W

Vote Yes on Measure W to protect the outstanding quality of education local high school students receive from Acalanes Union High School District schools.

Today’s high school graduates must be prepared to compete in a highly technical and competitive global workforce. Our four public high schools—Acalanes High School, Campolindo High School, Las Lomas High School, and Miramonte High School—have answered the educational call, today ranking among the top 5% of all high schools nationwide.

Here’s the problem: years of inadequate state funding have taken their toll. This is not an expense problem; it’s a revenue problem.

AUHSD’s last new funding measure was 16 years ago. While educational costs continue rising, AUHSD has cut $4.2M in the last 2 years alone! Without Measure W, drastic cuts will continue. Our students will suffer in academic program losses, increasing class sizes, and layoffs of valued teachers, counselors, and support staff.

Measure W will:

● Protect advanced science, technology, engineering, and math programs that help students get into college

● Retain/attract highly-qualified teachers

● Maintain manageable class sizes

● Preserve high-quality music, visual, and performing arts programs

● Continue Career Technical Education for in-demand fields like auto mechanics, fabrication, skilled trades, biotech, medical and health sciences

● Keep classroom technology/instructional materials current

● Provide academic intervention for students in need

● Restore positions lost to budget cuts – especially teachers, counselors, and school librarians.

Senior Citizens are eligible for exemption.

Mandatory Taxpayer Protections: No funds for administrators’ salaries; Citizens’ Oversight and independent audits ensure proper spending; Measure W lasts 8 years only; all funds stay local, benefitting AUHSD only.

Maintaining high-quality schools is a wise investment. Great schools protect property values and quality of life.

Teachers, parents, seniors, civic leaders and residents across our community are voting Yes. They know, as we do, the need is great and our kids are worth it!

Join us! Vote Yes on W!

REBUTTAL TO ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF

MEASURE W

Proponents say Measure W solves a revenue problem. It’s a spending problem.

• Acalanes Union High School District already collects $301 on top of our regular property tax. While paying off AUHSD bonds, we also pay parcel taxes to each TK-8 “feeder” district.

• Voters already rejected a smaller version of this tax last year.

• The district itself spent $834,000 on the standalone special election it lost, plus opinion surveys and consultants. Now, over $68,000 has already gone to Measure W polling and consulting.

• The separate “Yes” committee selling you Measure T raised $210,000 — money that could have been spent in classrooms. A majority of that “Yes on T” funding came from tax-exempt, AUHSD school-related organizations.

• During 2025-26 the Acalanes board approved $2.1 million in special education contracts with Utah residential facilities, including $436,360 for a single placement at Crimson Heights, a program decertified by Utah regulators.

• Taxpayers already pay $378,000 per AUHSD teacher; yet only about $150,000 (on average) reaches the teacher in the form of salary and benefits. Where does the rest go?

By proponents’ own account, AUHSD schools rank in the nation’s top 5 percent — on the taxes we pay now. Yet their historic academic focus is now often eclipsed by politically driven, nonacademic priorities.

Fewer than one in ten District households has a child enrolled in AUHSD. A parcel tax ignores ability to pay: a small condo is charged the same as a mansion. Combined with other new and proposed taxes, Measure W makes our area even more unaffordable.

Vote No on Measure W. StopMeasureW.com.

ARGUMENT AGAINST

MEASURE W

Just last year, the Acalanes Union High School District (AUHSD) spent $834,000 of taxpayer money on Measure T, a special election for a $130 parcel tax.

We rejected it.

The board’s response? Return to the ballot with a bigger tax, $168 per parcel for eight years, adding to the existing $301 parcel tax: same district, eighteen months later, demanding nearly 30% more than in Measure T.

But AUHSD doesn’t have a revenue problem; AUHSD has a spending problem.

State data show the District’s cost per student essentially doubling from 2012-13 to 2024-25, from $10,305 to $20,508 — a 99% jump, while Bay Area CPI inflation was only 45%. That’s unsustainable, and much of the increase never reached the classroom.

Where did it go?

• Wellness Centers on each high-school campus;

• A costly Central Administration;

• Non-academic Diversity, Equity, Inclusion and Belonging programming.

• AUHSD teaches seniors an elective Deconstructing Race course, grounded in Critical Race Theory, that could put federal funding at risk. And AUHSD voluntarily requires Ethnic Studies for graduation, though it is neither funded nor required by the state.

Such choices are unrelated to math, science, or teacher pay.

AUHSD is financed mainly by local property taxes that keep climbing with home values.

And California’s new budget handed AUHSD a one-time grant of nearly $5 million.

The money is there. The priorities are wrong.

AUHSD already collects two permanent parcel taxes, atop the hefty TK-8 parcel taxes we also pay.

Adding a third AUHSD tax rewards the same runaway spending.

Rather than cut administrative overhead or consolidate high schools with AUHSD’s feeder districts (as other communities have done to reduce spending), AUHSD’s Board is doubling down on duplicate bureaucracy.

Homeowner budgets are stressed enough already.

Don’t reward AUHSD’s runaway spending. Vote No on Measure W.

See StopMeasureW.com.
