Measure W asks the voters of this high school district whether to levy a parcel tax of $168 a parcel for eight years, beginning with the 2027-2028 fiscal year.
The district's voters already approved two parcel taxes, totaling $301 a parcel each year, and this measure would not affect them.
Measure W needs two-thirds of the vote to pass.
The money could pay for listed purposes, such as keeping qualified teachers, class sizes, library services and counseling.
It could not pay administrators' salaries or benefits, and a citizens' oversight committee would report yearly on spending.
Owners aged 65 or older who live in their home could apply for an exemption, and so could some residents with disabilities.
The ballot question says the tax would provide $6 million a year in local school funding.
Supporters say the tax protects the quality of education after years of inadequate state funding, and without it, cuts to programs and teachers continue.
Opponents say the district has a spending problem, not a revenue problem, and is back with a bigger tax after voters rejected one last year.
A yes vote levies the $168 parcel tax for eight years.
A no vote does not levy the tax.
