Argument Against Measure J

A NO vote prevents dissolution of King City Union School District (KCUSD) preserving local control of King City's elementary schools. Dissolving KCUSD and two other local districts while creating two new unified districts carries sweeping logistical, governance, financial, and educational consequences for the affected districts, their students, and the broader community. Action to dilute local control, shift focus from elementary students, and create fiscal risks deserves careful, community-specific scrutiny and solutions that this proposal does not provide.

No guarantee that actual educational experience improves for our children. Under the proposal students would continue attending the same schools; what changes is who governs and how resources are prioritized. There is no reason to believe a combined TK-12 board would prioritize student achievement more than an elementary-focused board. KCUSD's programs are tailored to support children during their developmental stages. In unified districts, athletics and secondary demands can dominate attention and budgets. Under the proposal, elementary programs—such as KCUSD's Dual Language Immersion program—could be overlooked or cut to meet high school priorities.

The Proposal was intended to benefit Greenfield, not King City. The original goal of this effort was to unify Greenfield, King City did not seek unification and its students were not the focus. The proposal links the two districts and does not permit Greenfield to unify without simultaneously dissolving KCUSD. Five neighboring districts were asked and opted out of the reorganization. King City was not given this choice and is forced to surrender its independent elementary district to accommodate Greenfield's unification.

The proposal to dissolve KCUSD and create a new, larger unified district, is not in the best interest of King City's students, families, and taxpayers.
