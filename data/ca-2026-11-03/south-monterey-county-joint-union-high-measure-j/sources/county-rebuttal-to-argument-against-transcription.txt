Rebuttal to Argument Against Measure J

The opposition asks voters to preserve separate elementary and high school districts in the name of "local control." But Measure J does not eliminate local control – it enhances it. It creates locally governed unified school districts in both King City and Greenfield, each with one elected board accountable to local voters for the success of each community's students from TK through grade 12.

Measure J creates a continuous educational system that aligns curriculum, student support, and academic pathways from the earliest grades through graduation. KCUSD's Dual Language Immersion program is not at risk under Measure J. Claims that elementary students will lose resources to high-school programs are speculation, not requirements of the measure.

The proposed reorganization has undergone extensive review – with participation from KCUSD and its representatives – and received unanimous approval from the Monterey County Committee on School District Organization and the California State Board of Education. A joint MOU supporting plans related to unification developed collaboratively has been adopted by GUSD, KCUSD, and SMCJUHSD.

Voters should also consider the perspectives supporting each position. The supporting statement was signed by the presidents of the Greenfield Elementary and South Monterey County Joint Union High School District Boards, both of which unanimously support the reorganization, putting community identity, local accountability, and a seamless experience for students and families ahead of their own institutional interests. The mayors of King City and Greenfield and the local Monterey County Supervisor also signed the supporting statement, demonstrating broad community support across the communities the measure would serve.

Measure J is about creating locally accountable TK–12 school districts designed to better serve students throughout their educational journey.
