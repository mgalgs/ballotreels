Measure M asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $19.8 million in school bonds.
The district would sell the bonds and repay them, with interest, from a tax on property in the district.
Measure M needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The money could repair roofs, heating and cooling, plumbing and water systems, and modernize classrooms, restrooms and playgrounds.
The district's tax rate statement estimates a tax of $22.50 per $100,000 of assessed value each year, until 2052-2053.
The statement estimates the total to repay, with interest, at about $37 million.
Supporters say schools over 50 years old need basic repairs, and Measure M qualifies the district for state matching funds.
No argument against Measure M was submitted for the voter guide that the county election office printed on this measure.
A yes vote is in favor of letting the district issue and sell up to $19.8 million in bonds.
A no vote is against letting the district issue those bonds.
