City of Albany Measure P asks Albany voters whether to update the parcel tax that pays for street paving and storm drains.
Albany voters approved the tax in 2006; it is now $167.65 a year per residential unit, and very low-income homeowners are exempt.
Measure P would cut the tax in half for low-income homeowners, and give rebates to low-income and very low-income renters.
Yearly increases for inflation would be capped at 3 percent, down from 5.
If any parcel of 500,000 square feet or more became tax exempt, the City Council could instead charge by the square foot of land, at a rate the measure sets.
Leaseholders on exempt land would pay the tax, and Measure P needs two-thirds of Albany voters to pass.
The City Attorney's analysis estimates the tax would raise $1,400,000 a year, kept in a fund for streets and storm drains.
Supporters say Measure P protects street funding if Golden Gate Fields becomes a park, and closes a tax loophole.
The Alameda County Composite Voter Information Guide says that no argument against Measure P was submitted for this election.
A yes vote approves these changes to the street paving and storm drain parcel tax.
A no vote does not approve these changes to the street paving and storm drain parcel tax.
