Measure S asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $75 million in school bonds.
The district's governing board placed Measure S on the ballot, and it needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay to upgrade classrooms and science labs, fix roofs, plumbing and electrical systems, and remove asbestos and lead pipes.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would be required.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average and a highest tax of $40 per $100,000 of assessed value, through 2061-2062.
The statement puts the total to repay, with interest, at about $157 million, and says its figures are estimates, not maximum amounts.
The official list of measures from the county, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure S was submitted.
Supporters say many schools are more than 60 years old, and every dollar stays local and cannot be taken by the state.
A yes vote approves the $75 million in school bonds.
A no vote rejects the $75 million in school bonds.
