City of Berkeley Measure Z asks voters in the city whether to add a parcel tax to fund a public bank.
A public bank is a nonprofit bank owned by local governments, and needs state approval to open.
Voters signed a petition to put Measure Z on the ballot, and it needs a majority vote to pass.
Homes would pay a yearly tax on each square foot of buildings, and other property a higher one, from 2027, for six years, rising each year with inflation.
The money would go to a fund to support a public bank investing in affordable housing, green energy and small businesses.
The City Attorney's impartial analysis says the city estimates the tax would raise $9.2 million in its first year, and $58.3 million in all.
Opponents say Berkeley would pay $58.3 million for an experimental bank that may never be authorized, and would subsidize other cities.
Supporters say Measure Z keeps Berkeley money local, investing in housing, green energy and small businesses, with profits going to the city.
A yes vote creates the parcel tax for a public bank.
A no vote does not create the parcel tax.
