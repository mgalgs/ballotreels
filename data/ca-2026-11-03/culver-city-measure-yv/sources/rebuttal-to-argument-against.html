REBUTTAL TO ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE YV

Culver City's 16- and 17-year-olds already work, pay taxes, drive, and lead their communities. Yet opponents claim they're too impressionable to be trusted with a ballot on the local decisions that shape their mobility, education, services, and safety. Denying them a vote is arbitrarily unjust and counter to raising capable, self-advocating young adults.

A few misleading claims deserve correction:

Measure YV returns because Council listened when voters asked. In 2022 it fell just 16 votes short - the closest contest on the entire county ballot - and outpolled the top Council candidate.

California has let 16- and 17-year-olds pre-register to vote since 2016. Our teenagers are already in the system, and none of the harms opponents predict have materialized here or in any of the twelve Vote16 cities nationwide.

The School District is a separate government with its own rules. No candidate gets special access to students, and campuses aren't campaign offices. Meanwhile, seniors, homeowners, and union members are campaigned at senior centers, neighborhood meetings, and union halls. We don't strip their votes because someone might ask for one. In fact, being asked is what having a voice looks like.

California voter information isn't handed to "anyone who requests it." State law limits access to electoral, governmental, scholarly, journalistic, and political purposes, and misuse is a crime - the same protection every voter receives.

Our students are neighbors with real stakes in this city's mobility, education, services, and safety. They've earned a say in it. Vote YES on Measure YV.
