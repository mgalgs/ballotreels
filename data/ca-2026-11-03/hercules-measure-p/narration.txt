Measure P asks the voters of this city whether to add a 1 percent sales tax that would last until voters repeal it.
The city council placed Measure P on the ballot on July 28, 2026.
State law caps the combined rate of these local taxes at 2 percent in any county.
The tax would add 1 percent to retail sales in the city.
If the cap kept the full 1 percent from being collected, the rate would drop to the most allowed, while that lasts.
It is a general tax, for any lawful government purpose.
The impartial analysis by the City Attorney says the revenue would be audited every year by an independent accountant, and the audit reviewed at a public council meeting.
Measure P needs a majority of the vote to pass.
The ballot question says the tax would raise about $4 million a year.
Supporters say the tax keeps fast 911 response and police patrols, and keeps the city financially stable as state and federal funding falls.
Opponents say the tax would raise the price of gas, school supplies, clothes and other essentials, and the real problem is spending, not revenue.
A yes vote adds the 1 percent sales tax, until voters repeal it.
A no vote does not add the tax.
