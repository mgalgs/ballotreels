Measure P

MEASURE P

CITY OF HERCULES

Shall the measure providing Hercules with locally controlled funding to maintain financial stability/general city services, such as fast 911 response; neighborhood police patrols; water quality standards; keeping public areas/parks safe/ clean; repairing deteriorating streets/potholes; and retaining/attracting local businesses, by establishing a one-cent (1%) sales tax generating approximately $4,000,000 annually until ended by voters; requiring audits, public disclosure, and funds only for Hercules, be adopted?

CITY ATTORNEY’S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF

MEASURE P

This measure (the “Hercules Public Safety/ Financial Stability Measure”) would add Chapter 11 to Title 8 to the Hercules Municipal Code and would impose a transactions and use tax, commonly known as a sales tax, of up to 1% in the City of Hercules.

The tax would be imposed on the sale or use of tangible personal property in the City. The tax would be administered by the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration, which administers all sales and use taxes and transactions and use taxes in California.

The tax would be collected in addition to the existing sales and use and transactions and use taxes already imposed by the State of California, Contra Costa County, and BART in the City of Hercules. It would also be collected in addition to the City’s existing transactions and use tax in Chapter 9 of Title 8 of the Hercules Municipal Code. The current combined rate for all sales and use and transactions and use taxes in Hercules is 9.25%.

Groceries and prescription medicines would be exempt from the tax.

The proposed tax is estimated to generate $4,000,000 per year, and the California Constitution prohibits the State from taking this revenue from the City.

The proposed tax is a general tax, which means the revenues could be used by the City for any lawful municipal purpose, such as 911 response, neighborhood police patrols, water quality standards, keeping public areas and parks safe and clean, repairing deteriorating streets and fixing potholes, and retaining and attracting local businesses.

If approved, the tax would begin to be collected on April 1, 2027 and would continue to be collected until repealed.

The tax would be imposed at a rate of 1%, unless state law limits the tax rate to a lower rate, in which case it would be imposed at the maximum rate allowed under the law, but no more than 1%

Revenues collected under this tax will be subject to an independent financial audit conducted annually by a certified public accountant, and the expenditure of the revenues will be stated in the annual budget. Both the annual audit and the budget will be presented and reviewed by the Hercules City Council in an open public meeting.

The measure was placed on the ballot by the Hercules City Council. The measure will take effect if a majority (50% plus 1) of voters voting on the measure vote “Yes.”

s/Christine Crowl, City Attorney, City of Hercules

REBUTTAL TO ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF

MEASURE P

Public safety deserves reliable funding, but supporters use police and 911 response to sell another tax without explaining why existing revenues are not enough.

Hercules already has a half-cent Measure B sales tax that was extended indefinitely, plus an 8% utility users tax continued indefinitely by Measure N. Before asking families, seniors, and local businesses to pay more, City Hall should show what changed, what savings were pursued, and exactly how much additional police staffing Measure P will buy.

The State Controller reports that in 2025 Hercules had 34 Public Safety employees receiving $5,158,622 in wages plus $1,195,041 in benefits.

Some employees receive over $300,000 in total compensation, and retired police officers can get up to $6000 per year for health insurance on top of their pensions.

This is not an argument against police officers. It is a reason to demand a clear compensation, staffing, and long-term cost plan before approving another tax.

Supporters also point to audits and annual reports. Audits tell voters whether money was accounted for; they do not prove a tax is necessary, prevent future spending increases, or guarantee faster 911 response.

Exemptions for unprepared food and prescriptions do not make the tax painless. Working families still pay more for gas, clothes, school supplies, and new cars.

Remember, if both Measure P and the transit tax pass, we will be looking at a 10.75% total rate. How much is too much?

Protect public safety with responsible budgeting, not another tax increase.

Vote No on Measure P.

Contra Costa Taxpayers Association, Marc D. Joffe, President

ARGUMENT AGAINST

MEASURE P

Vote No on the Measure P tax hike!

Everything in and around Hercules costs more, and now the City Council wants to add another 1% to the price of gas, school supplies, clothes, and other essentials.

A higher tax on nearly everything you buy:

• Measure P raises Hercules’ sales tax from 9.25% to 10.25%. Your ballot also carries a separate 0.5% regional transit tax. If both pass, Hercules shoppers will pay 10.75%, tying El

Cerrito and Pinole for the highest sales tax in Contra Costa County, and well above the national and California averages.

• It even raises the price of a new car. California charges vehicle tax at the buyer’s home address, so Hercules residents would pay 10.75% no matter where they buy, e.g., $5,375 on a

$50,000 vehicle. You can’t escape it by shopping out of town.

• Sales taxes hit low- and middle-income families hardest. If Hercules truly values equity, why penalize those who can least afford it? If Measure P passes, shoppers can save 1.5%

on necessities simply by buying in unincorporated Rodeo or El Sobrante.

The real problem is spending, not revenue:

• Hercules’ own audited statements show General Fund spending more than doubled in a decade, from $10.2 million in 2015 to $20.7 million in 2025. Where is the money going?

• Nine city employees each received more than $200,000 in wages in 2025 according to State Controller data.

• Staff admit the retail tax base is “not robust enough to fund anything more than basic service levels.” If spending can double while services stay “basic,” the problem isn’t too little

tax money; it’s how the money is managed.

Before charging residents the highest sales tax in the county, the Council should show real spending restraint.

The sales tax in Hercules is high enough already.

Vote No on Measure P.

Contra Costa Taxpayers Association, Marc D. Joffe, President

Michael Hernandez, Hercules Resident

Michael Letcher, Hercules Resident
