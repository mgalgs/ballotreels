Measure Q asks the voters of this city whether to borrow up to $120 million in bonds to repair and rebuild fire stations.
The City Council placed Measure Q on the ballot; it needs two-thirds of the vote to pass.
The bonds could pay to fix or rebuild fire stations and firefighter living quarters, including their roofs, wiring and plumbing.
The council would decide which projects come first, and completing every listed project is not guaranteed.
The Finance Director's statement estimates an average tax of $22.15 per $100,000 of assessed value, and at most $24.06.
The statement says a property tax, not the general fund, would repay about $249.7 million with interest; the City Attorney's analysis says through 2060.
Supporters say aging, undersized stations slow the response to emergencies, and that some firefighters work in unsafe buildings with mold and leaky roofs.
Opponents say the city need not borrow, because settlement money already in hand could fix the stations, with no new tax and no interest.
A yes vote authorizes the city to issue the bonds.
A no vote does not authorize the city to issue the bonds.
