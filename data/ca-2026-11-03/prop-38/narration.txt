Proposition 38 is about borrowing to pay for immunology medical research.
Today, companies and government agencies are the main funders of medical research.
Proposition 38 would let the state borrow $8.4 billion for research on the immune system.
About half would go to one research institute, and half to research grants.
The Legislative Analyst's Office says repaying the bonds would cost $500 million to $600 million a year for about 20 years, about one-quarter of 1 percent of the state's General Fund.
It says research discoveries could repay part or all of that cost in later decades.
Opponents say Proposition 38 locks taxpayers into decades of debt for one field of research.
Supporters say Proposition 38 funds research to cure and prevent diseases like cancer and Alzheimer's.
A yes vote means the state could borrow $8.4 billion.
A no vote means the state could not borrow that money.
