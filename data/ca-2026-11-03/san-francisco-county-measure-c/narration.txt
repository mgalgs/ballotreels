San Francisco Proposition C asks voters whether to put more city money into the Housing Trust Fund, and extend it to 2058.
The fund pays for affordable housing and down payment loans.
Today the yearly payment into the fund rises or falls with General Fund revenue.
Last year it was $50.8 million, less than 1 percent of the General Fund of about $7 billion.
Proposition C would make the payment grow faster, to $125 million, likely by 2034-2035.
The fund could also pay for streets and sidewalks serving affordable housing.
The City Controller puts the extra cost at about $60 million a year by 2034-2035; the General Fund today is about $7 billion.
Opponents say the trust fund has failed at its job, and Proposition C doubles its size without requiring a single additional home.
Supporters say Proposition C renews the main source of money for affordable housing in San Francisco, and raises no one's taxes.
A yes vote means you want these changes to the fund.
A no vote means you do not want these changes.
