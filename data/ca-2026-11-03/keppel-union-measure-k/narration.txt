Measure K asks the voters of this school district whether to approve $27 million in school bonds.
The district's governing board placed Measure K on the ballot, and it needs 55 percent of the vote to pass.
The bond money could pay to repair or replace school buildings, such as roofs, plumbing, gas lines, and fire and security systems.
The money could not pay teacher or administrator salaries, and a citizens' oversight committee and yearly audits would be required.
The district's tax rate statement estimates an average and a highest tax of $30 per $100,000 of assessed value, through 2065-2066.
The statement puts the total to repay, with interest, at about $67.8 million, and says its figures are estimates, not maximum amounts.
The official list of measures from the county, which names who filed each argument, says no argument against Measure K was submitted.
Supporters say the district's schools are aging and in dire need of repairs, and the state cannot take this funding away.
A yes vote approves the $27 million in school bonds.
A no vote rejects the $27 million in school bonds.
