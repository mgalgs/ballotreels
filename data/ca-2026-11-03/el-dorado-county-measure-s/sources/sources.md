## Key facts, quoted

- **Ballot question** (p. 56): "To update hospital facilities, equipment, and technologies and maintain local access to emergency rooms; reduce wildfire risk by supporting local fire protection/prevention such as removing dry brush and other fire hazards; and provide rapid 9-1-1 emergency response to fires, accidents, and medical emergencies, shall the measure enacting a 3/8% sales tax for 30 years in El Dorado County, providing approximately $13,000,000 annually, with independent oversight, audits, and local control, be adopted?"
- **Who votes / threshold** (analysis, p. 61): "Measure S (the "Measure"), if approved by a majority of the voters voting thereon, would impose a special retail transactions and use tax, commonly referred to as a sales tax, within the County of El Dorado for a period of thirty (30) years at the rate of three-eighths (3/8) of one percent. If approved, the tax would apply throughout El Dorado County, including in the cities of Placerville and South Lake Tahoe."
- **How it got on the ballot** (analysis): "This Measure was placed on the ballot by a petition signed by the requisite number of voters." Full text, Section 8: "This measure is an exercise of the initiative power of the People of the County of El Dorado to implement a special tax".
- **First-year allocation** (analysis): "In the first year after the Measure is approved, the Board of Supervisors ("Board") would be required to allocate fifty percent (50%) of the proceeds of the tax to eligible local hospitals … forty-five percent (45%) of the proceeds of the tax will be allocated to local fire agencies and five percent (5%) of the proceeds of the tax will be allocated to eligible local non-profit organizations for wildfire prevention and fire services in El Dorado County."
- **Later years** (analysis): "the Measure requires the Board to allocate tax revenues first to eligible local hospitals for the purpose of providing payment of debt service on bonds or other debt obligations incurred to finance capital projects for updated healthcare facilities. … Five percent (5%) … to eligible local nonprofit organizations … The remaining tax revenues would be allocated to local fire agencies within the County in specified proportions that are based on the number of full-time fire engines operated by each fire agency."
- **Oversight** (analysis): "The Measure requires the creation of an Independent Citizens' Oversight Committee ("Committee") to review and report on expenditures annually … The Measure also requires annual independent financial audits".
- **Yes/No** (analysis): "A "YES" vote is a vote to authorize the imposition of a three-eighths (3/8) of one percent sales tax within El Dorado County." / "A "NO" vote is a vote against the imposition of a three-eighths (3/8) of one percent sales tax within El Dorado County."
- **Analysis signed:** "David A. Livingston, El Dorado County, County Counsel".
- **Argument in favor signers** (p. 57): Tim Cordero, Retired Fire Chief, El Dorado County Fire; Laurel Brent Bumb, CEO El Dorado County Chamber; Rubeal Mann, Vice President of Medical Affairs, and Emergency Dept. Medical Director; David Zelinsky.
- **Argument against and rebuttal to argument in favor signers** (pp. 58–59): Raymond J. Nutting and Dan Dellinger, County Co-Chairmen, California Taxpayer Protection Committee.
- **Rebuttal to argument against signers** (p. 60): George Nielsen, Chief of Police, Retired; Chelsey Shanks, RN, Director, Emergency Department; John Pang, Retired Fire Chief, Meeks Bay Fire/Founding Chair Tahoe Fires and Fuels Team; Robert P. Hess, Chair, El Dorado County Fire Safe Council; Christa K. Campbell, Owner Rainbow Orchards.
- Arguments are printed as submitted; claims in them (e.g. "raise our sales tax to 7.625 cents", "Essential purchases like groceries and prescriptions are exempt") are the authors' statements, not findings of the county.
