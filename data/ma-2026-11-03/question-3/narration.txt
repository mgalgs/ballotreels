Question 3 is about how state primary elections work.
Today, each party holds its own primary for state offices.
The question would put every candidate, of any party, on one primary ballot.
The top two candidates would advance to the general election.
The change would take effect on January 1, 2028.
The Executive Office of Administration and Finance finds no discernible material fiscal consequences.
Supporters say two-thirds of the Legislature is running unopposed, and all-party primaries would bring more competition and force politicians to listen.
Opponents say similar top-two systems have favored wealthy, self-funded candidates, and only two candidates, even from the same party, could reach November.
A yes vote would replace party primaries with one primary, where the top two advance.
A no vote would make no change to the laws governing primary elections.
