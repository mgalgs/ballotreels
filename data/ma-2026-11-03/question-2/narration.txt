Question 2 is about union rights for employees of a state agency known as CPCS.
Today, the public employee labor law does not name CPCS as an employer.
The question would let CPCS employees form a union and bargain with their employer.
CPCS would then have to ask the Governor to fund any contract.
The Executive Office of Administration and Finance says the fiscal impact is difficult to project.
The argument against says bargaining could disrupt how the agency is run, and limit its flexibility with caseloads and courts.
Supporters say CPCS employees, unlike other state employees, cannot choose whether to unionize, and the question gives them that choice.
A yes vote would specify that CPCS employees may form a union.
A no vote would make no change to the labor law for CPCS employees.
