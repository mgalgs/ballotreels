Constitutional Amendment 4 is about salaries for legislators.
Today, New Mexico's legislators get no salary, only per diem and mileage.
Amendment 4 would let the Legislature pay each member the state's median household income, approximately $68,000 in 2026.
Pay could begin in 2029, rising or falling with that income.
The 2026 General Election Voter Guide does not include a cost estimate for the amendment.
The Legislative Finance Committee's analysis put the general fund cost at about $7.6 million a year, before payroll taxes and benefits.
Opponents say salaries could give incumbents an edge, and be out of line with most other states.
Supporters say pay could give legislators more time for their work, and let more New Mexicans run.
A vote for the amendment allows legislator salaries equal to the median household income.
A vote against the amendment means legislators would still receive no salary.
