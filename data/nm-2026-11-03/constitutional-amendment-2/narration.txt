Constitutional Amendment 2 is about when school elections can be held.
Today, the state constitution requires school elections to be held at different times from partisan elections.
Amendment 2 would let school districts put bond and property tax questions on a partisan ballot, like the general election.
The Legislature could also let school board elections be held with partisan elections.
The 2026 General Election Voter Guide does not include a cost estimate for the amendment.
Opponents say school questions could cause voter fatigue and higher ballot costs, and could politicize school elections.
Supporters say school districts would save the cost of all-mail elections, and more voters would take part.
A vote for means school questions could go on partisan ballots.
A vote against means school elections stay separate from partisan elections.
