Constitutional Amendment 1 is about the governor's pocket veto.
Today, a bill sent in a session's last three days becomes law only if the governor signs it.
From 2014 through 2026, governors pocket vetoed 166 bills and vetoed 180.
Under Amendment 1, a bill not vetoed within 20 days would become law.
Every veto would also need a written, substantive explanation.
The 2026 General Election Voter Guide does not include a cost estimate for the amendment.
Opponents say the amendment is not needed, could be a burden, and leaves the explanation undefined.
Supporters say the amendment shows the public why bills are vetoed, and could balance the branches.
A vote for means bills sent in a session's last three days and not acted on would become law.
A vote against means bills sent in a session's last three days and not acted on would not become law.
