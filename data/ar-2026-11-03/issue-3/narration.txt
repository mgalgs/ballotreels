Issue 3 is about economic development in Arkansas.
Today, cities and counties may fund economic development projects.
The amendment would let lawmakers create development programs, loans, and grants.
Lawmakers could allow economic development districts in cities, counties, or cooperative areas.
District property would be exempt from other taxes, but taxed by the district.
Arkansas does not publish a cost estimate for the amendment.
Supporters say, according to the University of Arkansas guide, Arkansas alone in its region lacks these districts, and they would help communities compete without raising taxes.
Opponents say, according to the University of Arkansas guide, district property leaves the tax rolls, shifting costs to everyone else, and government picks which businesses benefit.
A vote for means lawmakers could create these programs and districts.
A vote against means the current wording would stay.
