Public Question 2 is about where city and town court judges may live.
Today, Indiana's Constitution requires county, township and town officers to live within their county, township or town.
The amendment would let a city or town court judge live in the court's county, or the closest bordering Indiana county.
The closest county is found by the shortest distance from the city or town limits to the county line.
Indiana's election summary does not include a cost estimate for the amendment.
Indiana publishes no official argument against the amendment.
Indiana publishes no official argument for the amendment.
A yes vote means city and town court judges could live in either of those two counties.
A no vote keeps today's residence rule in the Constitution unchanged.
