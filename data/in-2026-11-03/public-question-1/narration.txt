Public Question 1 is about bail for people charged with crimes.
Today, Indiana's Constitution allows bail for every offense except murder or treason, when the proof is evident or the presumption strong.
The amendment would let courts restrict bail when the accused poses a substantial risk to others or the community.
It would also deny bail when two things are true.
First, the proof is evident or the presumption strong.
Second, the state proves by clear and convincing evidence that no release conditions will reasonably protect others or the community.
Indiana's election summary does not include a cost estimate for the amendment.
Indiana publishes no official argument for the amendment.
Indiana publishes no official argument against the amendment.
A yes vote adds these limits on bail.
A no vote keeps bail as it is today.
