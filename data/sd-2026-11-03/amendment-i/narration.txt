Amendment I is about what happens to Medicaid expansion if federal funding falls.
Medicaid provides medical coverage, paid for by the state and the federal government.
In 2022, voters expanded it to lower-income adults over 18 and under 65.
Today, federal law sets the federal share at 90 percent.
Amendment I would end the expanded coverage if that share drops below 90 percent.
Medicaid would then cover only low-income children, pregnant women, disabled people and the elderly.
South Dakota's ballot question pamphlet does not include a cost estimate for the amendment.
Supporters say nothing changes while Washington pays its share; if not, voters and lawmakers decide.
Opponents say a no vote keeps voter-approved expansion, so funding changes cannot automatically end coverage.
A yes vote adopts the amendment.
A no vote leaves the Constitution as it is.
