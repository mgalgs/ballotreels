Amendment J is about citizenship and who may vote in South Dakota.
Today, the state constitution lists United States citizenship among the qualifications to vote.
The constitution also lets a person be disqualified for mental incompetence or a felony conviction.
Amendment J would add people who are not citizens to the list of those disqualified from voting.
The Attorney General's explanation says this clarifies the existing requirement that only citizens may vote.
South Dakota's ballot question pamphlet does not include a cost estimate for the amendment.
Supporters say Amendment J closes a loophole, making it clear that only United States citizens may vote.
Opponents say Amendment J solves no problem, but could let a challenge keep a citizen from voting.
A yes vote adopts the amendment.
A no vote leaves the Constitution as it is.
