Amendment L is about how many votes it takes to change South Dakota's Constitution.
Today, an amendment or revision joins the Constitution if a majority of the votes cast approve it.
Amendment L would require 60 percent of the votes cast instead.
The rule would cover every later amendment or revision, including those started by citizen petition.
Amendment L itself needs only a majority, under today's rule.
South Dakota's ballot question pamphlet does not include a cost estimate for the amendment.
Supporters say a higher bar protects the Constitution, so only changes with broad support become part of it.
Opponents say Amendment L creates minority rule, making it much harder for the people to have their say.
A yes vote adopts the amendment.
A no vote leaves the Constitution as it is.
