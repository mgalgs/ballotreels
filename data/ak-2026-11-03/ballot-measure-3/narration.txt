Ballot Measure 3 is a proposed law about who may vote in Alaska elections.
Today, a voter must be a United States citizen, at least 18, a 30-day resident, and registered.
The measure would reword the law to say only a person who meets all four requirements may vote.
The Legislative Affairs Agency Summary says the requirements to vote would not change.
The state's statement of costs estimates $114,050 to implement the measure, for public hearings, petition review and translations.
The pamphlet says the Division of Elections did not receive a statement in opposition.
Supporters say the measure closes a gap, making clear that only citizens may vote.
A yes vote means the law would say only citizens meeting the other requirements may vote.
A no vote keeps today's wording: a voter must be a citizen and meet other requirements.
