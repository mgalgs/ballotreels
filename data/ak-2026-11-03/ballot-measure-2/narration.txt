Ballot Measure 2 would change Alaska's elections and campaign finance rules.
Since a 2020 vote, all candidates run in one open primary, and the general election uses ranked-choice voting.
The measure would bring back party primaries, and general elections where the most votes win.
The measure would also repeal some 2020 limits and disclosure rules on donations.
The state's statement of costs estimates about $2.6 million to implement the measure, mostly for voter education.
Opponents say the measure would flood Alaska with untraceable campaign cash, and take away freedom to vote for anyone.
Supporters say the measure returns Alaska to simple, one person, one vote elections, with primaries open to all Alaskans.
A yes vote brings back party primaries and repeals some 2020 campaign finance rules.
A no vote keeps the all-candidate primary, ranked-choice voting and 2020 campaign finance rules.
