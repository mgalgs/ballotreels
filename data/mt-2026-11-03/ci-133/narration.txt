CI-133 is about initiatives and referendums in Montana's Constitution.
Today, Montana's Constitution reserves the powers of initiative and referendum to the people.
They would become a fundamental right, burdened only for a compelling government interest.
The government would have 90 days to approve petitions, which could not be disqualified over minor or technical issues.
Government resources could not be used to support or oppose a ballot measure.
Montana's Voter Information Pamphlet does not include a cost estimate for the amendment.
Opponents say the amendment restricts elected officials and courts in checking flawed measures, and helps special interests purchase laws.
Supporters say lobbyists have too much influence in Helena, and ballot measures let citizens veto laws when government overreaches.
A yes vote would approve amending the Montana Constitution as proposed.
A no vote would make no change to the current Montana Constitution.
