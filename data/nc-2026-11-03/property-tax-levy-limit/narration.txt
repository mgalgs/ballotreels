This amendment is about how much local property taxes may go up in North Carolina.
Counties, cities and towns collect property taxes for purposes state law allows, or that their own voters approve.
The amendment would require the General Assembly to pass laws limiting how much local property tax collections may increase.
The amendment sets no limit itself; those later laws would, and they may include exceptions.
The Fiscal Research Division found no fiscal impact for the state or local governments, because the specifics would come in later laws.
North Carolina publishes no official argument for the amendment.
North Carolina publishes no official argument against the amendment.
A vote for the amendment requires the General Assembly to set limits on local property tax increases.
A vote against the amendment leaves the constitution without a requirement for such limits.
