Proposal 26-1 asks whether Michigan should hold a convention to revise its constitution.
The constitution puts this question to voters every 16 years.
A convention could rewrite the constitution, change parts, or change nothing.
Today's constitution came from the last convention, in 1961.
A yes vote would mean electing 148 delegates in 2027 to draft a revision.
Voters would then approve or reject the revision.
The Senate Fiscal Agency estimates about $10.2 million for the convention, based on the 1961 convention's costs.
The House Fiscal Agency estimates about $78.3 million in all, including about $56 million for two elections of delegates.
No official argument for the proposal was published.
No official argument against the proposal was published.
A yes vote means delegates would draft a general revision.
A no vote means no convention in 2027.
