Proposal 26-2 would change Michigan's campaign finance law.
Today, ads that do not directly urge a vote need not report their donors.
The proposal would bar regulated electric and gas utilities, and contractors with over $250,000 annually in government contracts, from giving to campaigns for offices that affect them.
Disclosure laws would also cover ads that clearly identify a candidate or ballot question.
Paid internet ads would have to say who paid for them.
The Senate Fiscal Agency expects minor state costs, perhaps more staff.
It sees new local costs for law enforcement, courts, community supervision and jails, and fine revenue for libraries.
No official argument for the proposal was published.
No official argument against the proposal was published.
A yes vote adopts these contribution bans and disclosure rules.
A no vote keeps current campaign finance laws as they are.
