- Passages (bill history header and SECTION 1):
  - "CHAPTER 904 / FILED WITH SECRETARY OF STATE SEPTEMBER 29, 2004 / APPROVED BY GOVERNOR SEPTEMBER 29, 2004"
  - "An act to add Chapter 13.4 (commencing with Section 25980) to Division 20 of the Health and Safety Code, relating to force fed birds."
  - § 25980(b): "Force feeding a bird means a process that causes the bird to consume more food than a typical bird of the same species would consume voluntarily.   - § 25984(c): "...to allow a seven and one-half year period for persons or entities engaged in agricultural practices that include raising and selling force fed birds to modify their business practices."

  - pp. 6-7: "The district court denied leave to add the new express ingredient preemption claim and dismissed the impossibility preemption and dormant Commerce Clause claims. ... (C.D. Cal. (C.D. Cal. July 14, 2020)."

## Part 2. Per-jurisdiction summary

For comparison, DC Initiative 86 (from `formulation-and-text.pdf` in the repo) bans **both** force-feeding (Sec. 4(a)) **and** sale/distribution (Sec. 4(b)), and also bans importing, transporting or receiving the products "into the District for sale, distribution, or any other commercial purpose" (Sec. 4(c)). No other law below has a separate import/transport ban.

### California: confirmed
- **Law:** SB 1520 (Burton), Stats. 2004, ch. 904; Cal. Health & Safety Code §§ 25980-25984 (C1).
- **What it bans:** both. § 25981 bans force-feeding a bird to enlarge its liver; § 25982 bans selling in California a product that results from it (C1). Penalty: civil penalty up to $1,000 per violation and per day (C1).
- **Dates:** approved by the Governor and filed with the Secretary of State Sept. 29, 2004 (C1); operative July 1, 2012, after a seven-and-a-half-year transition (C1, § 25984).
- **Outcomes:**
  1. July 2, 2012: producers and a restaurant sued, challenging only the sales ban (§ 25982) (C4 pp. 4-5).
  2. Sept. 28, 2012: district court denied a preliminary injunction (C4 p. 9). Aug. 30, 2013: Ninth Circuit affirmed (C2 p. 27). Oct. 14, 2014: Supreme Court denied certiorari (C3).
  3. 2015: district court held the sales ban expressly preempted by the federal Poultry Products Inspection Act and permanently enjoined its enforcement, i.e. **struck it down** (C4 pp. 9-10). The exact 2015 date is not in the archived record.
  4. Sept. 15, 2017: Ninth Circuit **reversed**, vacated the injunction: "California is free to enforce it" (C4 p. 26). Jan. 7, 2019: Supreme Court denied certiorari, after inviting and receiving the U.S. Solicitor General's views (C5).
  5. Jan. 14 and July 14, 2020: on remand, district court dismissed new preemption and dormant Commerce Clause claims, and declared that out-of-state sellers may sell to California buyers by internet, phone or fax when title passes outside the state (C6 pp. 6-7).
  6. May 6, 2022: Ninth Circuit **affirmed**: the sales ban is "neither preempted nor impermissible under the dormant Commerce Clause," and the order permitting those out-of-state sales stands (C6 p. 27). May 22, 2023: Supreme Court denied certiorari (C7).
- **Current status (latest official record found, May 22, 2023):** in force; upheld; sales completed outside California to California buyers (title passing out of state) are not covered (C6).

### New York City: confirmed
- **Law:** Local Law 202 of 2019 (Int. No. 1378-A), N.Y.C. Admin. Code §§ 17-1901 to 17-1903 (N1, N2).
- **What it bans:** sale only. Retail food and food service establishments may not "store, keep, maintain, offer for sale, or sell" any force-fed product; a "foie gras" label or menu listing is presumed force-fed (N1 p. 2). It does **not** ban force-feeding itself. Penalty: $500 to $2,000 per violation (N1 p. 2).
- **Dates:** passed by the Council Oct. 30, 2019; approved by the Mayor Nov. 25, 2019 (N1 p. 3; N2). "This local law takes effect 3 years after it becomes law" (N1 p. 3). (Three years after Nov. 25, 2019 is Nov. 25, 2022; no archived record states that calendar date.)
- **Outcomes:**
  1. Before the law took effect, a New York County Supreme Court justice (Sweeting, J.) entered a preliminary injunction in La Belle Farm, Inc. v City of New York (index No. 656399/2022), which "has prevented [the City] from enforcing the law" (N3 p. 3).
  2. December 2022: the state Commissioner of Agriculture and Markets determined that Local Law 202 violates state Agriculture and Markets Law § 305-a (N3 p. 3). Albany County Supreme Court (Platkin, J.) annulled that determination as arbitrary and capricious and sent it back (N3 p. 3).
  3. The Commissioner issued a second determination against the law. The City challenged it; Supreme Court (Platkin, J.) dismissed the City's case by judgment entered June 21, 2024 (N3 pp. 2, 3-4).
  4. March 12, 2026: the Appellate Division, Third Department, **reversed**: Local Law 202 is not preempted by § 305-a; the Commissioner's determination is annulled (N3 p. 8).
- **Current status (latest official record archived, March 12, 2026):** enacted and, per the Appellate Division, not preempted by state law; as of that decision a preliminary injunction had kept the City from enforcing it (N3 p. 3). Whether a further appeal is pending, or the injunction has been lifted, is not confirmed (see Part 4).

### Chicago: confirmed (passed 2006, repealed 2008)
- **Law:** Ordinance adding Chicago Municipal Code §§ 7-39-001 and 7-39-005, passed April 26, 2006 (H1). The N.D. Ill. court names it Ordinance PO-05-1895 (H3 p. 2).
- **What it bans:** sale only, and only at "food dispensing establishments" (restaurants and the like): they "shall prohibit the sale of foie gras" (H1 p. 75182). It does not ban force-feeding, and does not reach retail stores. Fine: $250 to $500 per offense, each day a separate offense (H1).
- **Dates:** passed 48-1 on April 26, 2006 (H1 p. 75181); in force "ninety (90) days after its passage and publication" (H1 p. 75182). The N.D. Ill. opinion states it "became effective on August 23, 2006," as an allegation accepted for the motion (H3 p. 2).
- **Outcomes:**
  1. Illinois Restaurant Association v. City of Chicago: June 12, 2007, the federal district court **upheld** the ordinance against home-rule and dormant Commerce Clause challenges and dismissed the suit (H3 p. 26).
  2. While the appeal was pending in the Seventh Circuit, the City repealed the ordinance (C2 pp. 23-24).
  3. **Repealed** May 14, 2008, 37-6, effective "upon passage and publication" (H2 p. 28640).
  4. Aug. 7, 2008: the district court's decision was **vacated as moot** (C2 pp. 23-24; D4 n. 26).
- **Current status:** repealed since May 14, 2008 (H2).

### Pittsburgh: confirmed (not named by the reviewer)
- **Law:** Ordinance No. 43 of 2023 (Council File 2023-2291), adding Pittsburgh Code Title 6, Chapter 641, "Force-Fed Products Prohibited" (P1).
- **What it bans:** sale/provision only. Food service and retail establishments may not sell, offer, give away or serve any force-fed product; a "foie gras" item is presumed force-fed (P1, § 641.02). Does not ban force-feeding itself. Fine: up to $500 per violation (P1, § 641.03).
- **Dates:** passed finally, as amended, Dec. 18, 2023; the Mayor returned it unsigned, so it became law without his approval; enacted and effective Dec. 29, 2023 (P1).
- **Outcomes:** no court ruling, amendment or repeal found in official records (see Part 4).
- **Current status (latest official record, the Legistar file):** enacted and effective Dec. 29, 2023.

### Portland, Oregon: confirmed (the reviewer's "Portland")
- **Law:** Ordinance 192190 (Document 2026-149), adding Portland City Code Chapter 17.110 (O1, O2, O3).
- **What it bans:** sale/provision only. Retail and food service establishments may not "provide, sell, or offer for sale or provide free of charge, or serve" any force-fed product or a food containing one; "foie gras" is presumed force-fed; a sale occurs where the recipient takes physical possession in Portland (O2). Does not ban force-feeding itself. Enforcement: written warning first, then $1,000 to $5,000 per violation (O3, § 17.110.050).
- **Dates:** an earlier version (Document 2026-033) failed in committee on Jan. 27, 2026, 2-3 (O5). The reintroduced ordinance passed as amended on June 4, 2026, 7-5 (O1). Ordinance effective July 4, 2026 (O1, O3); the prohibition (§ 17.110.030) takes effect Dec. 31, 2026 (O1, O4).
- **Outcomes:** none found; no court ruling in official records (see Part 4).
- **Current status (as of the City's own pages, retrieved 2026-10-28):** enacted; the sales prohibition is not yet in effect (starts Dec. 31, 2026).

### Brookline, Massachusetts: NOT confirmed (records blocked)
Town Meeting reportedly adopted a foie gras sales-ban bylaw (May 27, 2025 Annual Town Meeting, Article 20), later approved by the Attorney General. The Town's documents are behind a Cloudflare block; see Part 4. Do not use until the official records are archived. (Portland's own impact statement, O1, also says sales bans "have been passed in ... Brookline, Massachusetts," but that is Portland's statement, not Brookline's record.)

## Part 3. Q2: Who would Initiative 86 affect, according to DC's official records?

**No official DC record found says which or how many restaurants or retailers in the District sell foie gras.** Do not state a number.

What the official records do say:

- The CFO's Fiscal Impact Statement for I-86 (repo `sources/fiscal-impact.pdf`, Jan. 21, 2026), p. 1-2: "DOEE has experience inspecting food service businesses and retail establishments under existing non-food product bans such as Styrofoam containers, straws, and plastic shopping bags. The initiative is expected to expand the number and type of establishments that DOEE must inspect. DOEE must also publicize the law and educate relevant businesses on the prohibition. DOEE does not currently and is unlikely to inspect kitchen or other back-of-house operations at any establishment under the initiative."
- Same FIS, p. 2: "The Office of Revenue Analysis is not aware of any bird farms or bird feeding operations in the District and has not included any potential costs related to the inspection or enforcement of live animal establishments." (So the force-feeding ban, Sec. 4(a), has no known operation in DC to apply to, per the CFO.)
- The measure's own text names the kinds of businesses covered: "food service establishment" (restaurants, cafeterias, pushcarts, stands, vehicles) and "retail establishment" (store, shop, sales outlet, farmers' market) (formulation Sec. 3(4), 3(7)); and anyone importing, transporting or receiving the products into DC for commercial purposes (Sec. 4(c)).
- The predecessor I-85 FIS (D1) says the same, word for word in the relevant parts.
- The BOE proper-subject order (D5), the Council General Counsel opinions (D2, D3), the Attorney General opinions (D4) and the BOE notices and petition report (D6) contain no count or list of affected businesses. The OAG says only that the measure "may have an incidental effect on out-of-state businesses by regulating what they may transport into the District" (D4 p. 4).
- No DOEE, DC Health or DC Council record on the subject was found.

Restaurant counts appear only in news and advocacy material (e.g. counts of DC menus listing foie gras). Those are not official and must not be used.
