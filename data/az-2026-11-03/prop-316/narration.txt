Proposition 316 is about local taxes on groceries.
Today, cities and towns may tax groceries, if the tax applies to all food alike.
Under the measure, a new or higher grocery tax would need local voters' approval.
The rate could not exceed 2 percent, and places at 2 percent or more could not raise it.
The measure is retroactive: no grocery tax could be adopted or raised from mid-2025 to mid-2027.
Arizona's guide does not include a cost estimate for the measure.
Supporters say the measure lets cities keep their revenue, while local voters decide any increase.
Opponents say the measure strips cities' local control, risking service cuts and higher property taxes.
A yes vote means no new or higher grocery tax without voter approval.
A no vote means current local tax laws stay the same.
