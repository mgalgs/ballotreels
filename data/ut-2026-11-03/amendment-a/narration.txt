Amendment A is about how proposed changes to Utah's Constitution are published.
Today, each amendment must run in a newspaper in every county for two months.
Amendment A would require 60 days of publication before the election, in a manner set by state law.
Under a law already passed, the full text would go on two state websites.
The Voter Information Pamphlet says the change could reduce state costs by up to $1.4 million every two years; the state's General and Income Tax Funds budget is about $12.4 billion a year.
Supporters say Amendment A updates public notice for how Utahns get information and saves money.
The pamphlet says no argument against was submitted, because the Legislature adopted Amendment A unanimously.
A vote for means amendments would be published as state law sets.
A vote against means amendments would still be published in newspapers.
