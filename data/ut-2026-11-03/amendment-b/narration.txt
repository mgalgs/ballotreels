Amendment B is about passing citizen initiatives that raise taxes.
In Utah, voters can propose and pass a state law through a statewide initiative.
Today, most initiatives need more than half of the votes to pass.
Amendment B would require at least 60 percent for an initiative that raises taxes.
The rule covers new taxes, expanded taxes, higher rates and some property tax changes.
It would not change the vote needed for any other initiative.
The Voter Information Pamphlet says Amendment B likely would not materially affect state revenues or spending.
Supporters say tax increases should need broad agreement from the voters who will pay them.
Opponents say Utah voters should get the same simple majority lawmakers use to raise taxes.
A vote for means tax-raising initiatives would need at least 60 percent.
A vote against means tax-raising initiatives would still need a simple majority.
