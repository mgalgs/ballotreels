Amendment 87 is about Colorado's state income tax.
Today, the constitution requires one flat income tax rate, now 4.4 percent.
The amendment would set graduated rates, lowering taxes on taxable income below $500,000 and raising them above.
The added revenue would go to K-12 education, health care, and early childhood care and education.
If Proposition 136 also passes, the exact outcome is unclear, for the legislature or a court to determine.
Legislative Council Staff estimates income tax revenue of $17 billion in 2028, up $2 billion from current law.
The ballot title's $2.7 billion is a required maximum estimate, about 11 percent of expected state spending without the increase.
Opponents say this multibillion-dollar tax increase will harm the economy, and more taxpayers will pay higher taxes each year.
Supporters say the amendment cuts taxes for 97 percent of taxpayers, and funds education, health care, and child care.
A yes vote creates a graduated state income tax that increases revenue.
A no vote keeps the requirement for a flat state income tax rate.
