Amendment 84 is about how Colorado checks mail ballots.
Today, counties compare the signature on each mail ballot envelope with the signature on file.
The amendment would add the last four digits of a Social Security, driver's license or state ID number.
A ballot without a matching number would count only if the voter fixes it within eight days.
Legislative Council Staff estimates state and local costs of up to $2.7 million the first year, then $1.2 million a year.
Opponents say the amendment burdens voters without meaningfully improving security, and minor mistakes could keep eligible ballots uncounted.
Supporters say identification numbers strengthen election security and are less subjective than signature checks, while keeping voting convenient.
A yes vote requires identification information with mail ballots, plus a signature.
A no vote continues the signature-only system.
