Amendment 81 is about law enforcement notifying federal immigration authorities.
Today, no law requires notifying immigration authorities when someone unlawfully present is charged with a crime.
The amendment covers people charged with a violent crime, or any crime after a prior felony.
If law enforcement cannot determine that such a person is lawfully present, it must notify the Department of Homeland Security within 72 hours.
Legislative Council Staff estimates $60,000 in state costs, for computer updates.
Local law enforcement would have more work, mostly in district attorney offices.
Opponents say reporting people before a court decides undermines due process, and erodes trust in law enforcement.
Supporters say violent and repeat offenders who are here unlawfully should be reported, under one statewide standard.
A yes vote requires notifying federal immigration authorities after certain charges.
A no vote maintains current law.
