Proposition NN is about state revenue above Colorado's constitutional limit.
Today, the Taxpayer's Bill of Rights requires the state to refund revenue above a yearly limit.
The proposition would let the state keep that money instead, up to the amount it spends on public education.
For ten years, the money would fund K-12 education and children's programs, then other purposes too.
Legislative Council Staff expects refunds to fall by $329.9 million this budget year and $521.0 million the next, each about 2 percent of revenue subject to the limit.
That would eliminate refunds in both years.
Opponents say the proposition is essentially a tax increase, letting government keep more of what families earn.
Supporters say the proposition raises teacher pay and reduces class sizes, using money the state already collects.
A yes vote allows the state to keep revenue that otherwise would be refunded to taxpayers.
A no vote maintains current refunds and funding levels.
