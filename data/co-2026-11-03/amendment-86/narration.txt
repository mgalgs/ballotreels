Amendment 86 is about redrawing congressional districts between census years.
Today, an independent commission redraws Colorado's congressional districts every ten years.
The constitution sets no process for redrawing them between those years.
Under the amendment, any change between those years would need approval from the commission and the Colorado Supreme Court.
Those new districts could not intentionally favor a political party or unnecessarily divide communities.
Legislative Council Staff estimates about $370,000 in state costs for each year the commission reviews new maps.
Opponents say the amendment creates an unnecessary constitutional barrier, and voters should always have the final word.
Supporters say the amendment keeps partisan politics out of redistricting, and protects public input in any changes.
A yes vote requires the commission to approve any new districts between scheduled years.
A no vote continues the current rules for changing districts every ten years.
