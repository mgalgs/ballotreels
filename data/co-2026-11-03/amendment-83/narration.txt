Amendment 83 is about hunting and fishing in Colorado.
Today, Colorado Parks and Wildlife regulates hunting and fishing.
The amendment would create a constitutional right to hunt and fish by traditional methods.
Hunting and fishing would become the preferred means of managing wildlife.
The state could still regulate if necessary for sound scientific reasons, public safety, or to preserve future hunting and fishing opportunities.
Legislative Council Staff says existing law would not change at first, though court rulings could lead to changes.
Near-term state spending is not expected to change significantly, though legal costs could rise.
Opponents say the constitution should protect fundamental rights, not recreation, and vague language could prompt costly lawsuits.
Supporters say the amendment protects hunting and fishing from future restrictions, and license revenue aids wildlife statewide.
A yes vote creates a state constitutional right to hunt and fish.
A no vote means hunting and fishing continue under existing law.
