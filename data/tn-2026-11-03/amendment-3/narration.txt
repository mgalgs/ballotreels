Amendment 3 is about the rights of crime victims.
Today, the Constitution lists rights for crime victims; the amendment would keep or clarify them.
New rights would include being treated with fairness, taking part in the parole process, and protection from harassment, intimidation, and abuse.
Victims would learn the minimum sentence and release date, and victim, family and public safety would be weighed before release.
The amendment would not restrict the powers of prosecutors or courts.
The Fiscal Review Committee estimates a one-time local cost of $9,400 to print a longer sample ballot.
Tennessee publishes no official argument for the amendment.
Tennessee publishes no official argument against the amendment.
Yes adopts the proposed language.
No keeps the current language.
To pass, yes must outnumber no and exceed half of all votes cast for governor.
